## Voluntary national defence

![A conscript prepares to defend his positions with an assault rifle.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_6240,h_3510,x_0,y_255/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-943879625ff9ca7e047/1650458515)

Open image viewer

Voluntary national defence may get weapons from the army and the Border Guard already in the summer. Photo: Toni Pitkänen / Yle

The Finnish army, that is, the Defence Forces, will soon be able to give weapons for the use of voluntary national defence. Defence Minister Antti Kaikkonen says that the government is preparing an urgent change of law on the matter.

If the new law is approved, the army and the Border Guard may give weapons and cartridges for the use of voluntary national defence. The aim is for the new law to come into force at the beginning of July.

The government has also decided that national defence organisations will be given millions of euros of extra funding.

## Municipal sector dispute

![KT's Markku Jalonen answered questions from the media.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5000,h_2812,x_0,y_520/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-953328627b99e07ddfe/1652267627)

Managing Director of the Local Government Employers, KT, Markku Jalonen, at the office of the National Conciliator on 11 May. Photo: Silja Viitala / Yle

The negotiations in the municipal sector dispute have stopped. The Local Government Employers and the unions of the employees have not been able to agree in the dispute. It is not yet known when the municipal sector dispute will be conciliated again. The solutions may move past the summer.

The negotiations in the municipal sector dispute got stuck after the nurses' organisations rejected the conciliation proposal about 2 weeks ago.

The municipal sector collective agreement covers about 425 000 workers.

## Long-term unemployed

![A customer steps into the Helsinki Työllisyys service point at the Pasila TE Office on 28 July 2020.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11816179)

Open image viewer

The number of long-term unemployed has grown. Photo: Markku Ulander / Lehtikuva

The number of long-term unemployed is growing. Last year the number of long-term unemployed grew by 16 per cent from the previous year.

Statistics Finland says that at the end of last year there were more than 105 000 long-term unemployed in Finland. That is almost 70 per cent more than in 2019. A long-term unemployed person is a person who has been without work for 1 year without a break.

At the end of last year there were about 272 000 unemployed job seekers. Almost 40 per cent of them were long-term unemployed.
