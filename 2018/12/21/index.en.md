# Friday 21.12.2018 (radio)

The Iso-Syöte hotel has been badly destroyed in a fire. Fewer and fewer new upper secondary graduates continue their studies straight after upper secondary school. The employment rate has risen to the government's target. Finland will take 750 people through the refugee quota next year. School Christmas parties have been held. Holiday traffic has started.

## Fire at the Iso-Syöte hotel

![hotel fire](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10567111)

Open image viewer

The fire at the Iso-Syöte hotel caused big damage. Photo: Yle

The Iso-Syöte hotel has been badly destroyed in a fire. The hotel is located in Pudasjärvi in North Ostrobothnia.

The fire started at night, and it destroyed half of the hotel building. The fire caused damage estimated at hundreds of thousands of euros. The fire stayed in the restaurant areas and did not spread to the hotel rooms.

There were tourists in the hotel from various parts of Europe. All residents were moved to Pikku-Syöte, and some have already gone home.

## Starting studies is still delayed

![Roses and upper secondary school graduation caps](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10541927)

Open image viewer

The attempt to speed up upper secondary graduates' access to further studies has not succeeded. Photo: Matti Myller / Yle

Fewer and fewer new upper secondary graduates continue their studies straight after upper secondary school. Last year only about 28 percent went straight from upper secondary school to further studies.

In 2016 quotas for first-time applicants were introduced. The aim was to make it easier to accept a first university place. However, the quotas are causing confusion. Many do not accept a study place, because they fear losing their first-time applicant status.

The Union of Upper Secondary School Students hopes that study guidance in upper secondary schools will be made more effective.

## The government's employment target has been met

![Tays helicopter picture of a construction site](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10567726)

Open image viewer

Employment has risen. Prime Minister Juha Sipilä says that the next government's target is a 75 percent employment rate. Photo: Jussi Mansikka / Yle

The employment rate has risen to the government's target. Statistics Finland says that the employment rate was 72 percent in November.

There were just over 2.5 million employed people in November. That is 38,000 more than a year earlier.

## Finland takes 750 quota refugees

![South Sudanese refugee children play at the Gambela refugee camp in Ethiopia on 21 June.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9773319)

Open image viewer

Finland receives people in the refugee quota who are presented by the UN refugee agency UNHCR. Photo: Solan Kolli / AFP

Finland will take 750 people next year through the refugee quota. Finland will receive Syrian refugees from Turkey and Congolese people from Zambia.

In Turkey there are more than 3.6 million Syrian refugees because of the Syrian war. More than 800,000 Congolese have fled violence to African countries.

Finland is also prepared to receive 100 emergency cases.

## School Christmas holidays have started

![Christmas tree in the foreground, school pupils behind it.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10560548)

Open image viewer

Schools have had Christmas party days. Many pupils have now started a long Christmas holiday. Photo: Esa Huuhko / Yle

The autumn term of schools has ended in different parts of the country.

For many pupils the Christmas holiday lasts more than 2 weeks. In many places school continues only after Epiphany on Monday. At least in Kokkola, Kuopio and Mikkeli, school work continues right after New Year.

A school can decide independently how working days and holiday days are arranged.

## Christmas holiday traffic has started

![Cars on a road in snowfall.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10566944)

Open image viewer

Christmas holiday traffic has started. At least in Southern Finland the driving conditions may be poor. Photo: Esko Jämsä / AOP

Christmas holiday traffic falls on many days, because Christmas Eve is on Monday. The most traffic has been this afternoon, and it will be tomorrow, Saturday, from the morning on.

At least in the southern parts of the country you need to be prepared for poor driving conditions.

The busiest return day in traffic is probably St Stephen's Day, that is, Wednesday.
