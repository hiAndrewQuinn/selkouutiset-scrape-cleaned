# Tuesday 12.12.2017 (radio)

Snowfall has disrupted traffic. Fewer babies are born in Finland than before. Japan is taking Finland's child health clinics as an example.

## Snow chaos in traffic

![A snowstorm](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9973817)

A lot of snow fell on Tuesday. Photo: Kari Ahotupa / Yle

Snowfall and wind have disrupted traffic on Tuesday. There have been crashes on the roads, and many buses have been late.

Train traffic between Helsinki and Turku was cut for some time, because the snow brought trees down onto the railway. Local trains have also been cancelled.

There have also been delays in air traffic.

## Finnish families

![A baby drinks breast milk](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9969555)

Open image viewer

Few babies are born in Finland. Photo: AOP

Fewer babies are born in Finland than before. Finns are also older than before when they have their first child.

The Family Federation of Finland has studied young adults' thoughts about family. Young people today think that the right age to have children is about 30. Just 50 years ago, a Finnish woman had her first child at the age of 23.

Anna Rotkirch, research professor at the Family Federation, says that there are many reasons for the situation. Many young adults think that family and children bring rush, stress and arguments. That is why having children does not interest young people.

Many put off having children because they want to enjoy life first. Young people think that trips, hobbies and meeting friends will end when children are born into the family.

Young women also think a lot about how children affect work.

Sometimes couples may end up without a child if having a child is put off for a long time. A woman's chance of having a child gets smaller after the age of 30.

## Japan's family centres

![Child health clinic in Lahti, weighing a baby](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8535418)

Open image viewer

The Finnish child health clinic interests the Japanese. Photo: Juha-Petri Koponen / Yle

More about families. Japan is taking Finland's child health clinics as an example. Many Japanese people have visited Finland to learn about the Finnish child health clinic.

The Japanese have admired the fact that in Finland the clinics serve all families. The Japanese also think it is great that the same public health nurse looks after a family for a long time. Often the relationship between the nurse and the family begins in pregnancy and continues until the child goes to school. The Japanese have also been surprised that in Finland the nurse and the family talk a lot.

Japan is now setting up family centres. They work in the same way as Finnish child health clinics.
