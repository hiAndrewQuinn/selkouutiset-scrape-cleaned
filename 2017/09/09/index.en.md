# Saturday 9.9.2017 (radio)

Millions of people are fleeing Hurricane Irma. The Greens will not join the same government as the Finns Party. People have marched in London against leaving the EU. 100-year-old Finland is visible at the BBC's Proms music festival.

## Millions flee storm Irma

![In a NOAA satellite image on 7 September, from left to right: Hurricane Katia, Hurricane Irma and Hurricane Jose.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9824337)

In a NOAA satellite image on 7 September, from left to right: Hurricane Katia, Hurricane Irma and Hurricane José. Photo: Jose Romero / AFP

Millions of people are fleeing Hurricane Irma. The weather forecast says that the hurricane will reach the coast of Florida in the United States on Sunday. For example, most of the over five (5) million residents of greater Miami have fled the storm. There has never before been such a large mass movement because of a storm.

In Cuba, a million people have been evacuated out of the hurricane's path.

In the Caribbean Sea area, at least 19 people have been killed because of the hurricane and thousands of homes have been destroyed. Irma is followed by Hurricane José.

## The Greens reject working in government with the Finns Party

![Touko Aalto.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9781737)

Open image viewer

Touko Aalto was elected chair of the Greens in June 2017. Photo: Jarno Kuusinen / AOP

The chair of the Greens, Touko Aalto, says that the Greens will not join the same government as the Finns Party.

Aalto stressed on TV 1's Ykkösaamu programme that issues will decide whether the Greens take part in the next government. The chair of the Greens says that the Finns Party's line on human rights questions, immigration and the economy is far from the Greens' views.

The Greens are ready to be in government with all other parties. The next parliamentary elections are in 2019. In Yle's newest poll, the Greens were the second largest party after the National Coalition Party.

## Brexit has been opposed in London

![Big Ben and flags.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9817381)

Open image viewer

Britain's exit from the EU is getting closer. Photo: EPA / Will Oliver

People have marched in London against Britain leaving the EU. Opponents of leaving, or Brexit, marched to Parliament.

The organisers of the march claim that the British people's view on Brexit has changed. They say that a majority of the island nation's residents no longer support leaving the EU.

Brexit is due to take effect at the end of March 2019. In practice, the deadline for the Brexit negotiations is next autumn.

## Finland is on show at the Proms festival

![a man and a woman in an opera auditorium](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9823153)

Open image viewer

Lotta Wennäkoski and Sakari Oramo in the Royal Albert Hall. Photo: Janne Koskinen / Yle

Sibelius's Finlandia will play this Saturday evening for millions of listeners. The British public broadcaster BBC's Proms music festival ends with a concert in which Finland plays a visible part.

The last night of the Proms is broadcast around the world on radio and television. The evening is important for Finland, which turns 100. The Finlandia hymn by Finland's national composer Jean Sibelius will be sung in Finnish. Sakari Oramo, chief conductor of the BBC Symphony Orchestra, says that the British choir has practised Finnish pronunciation hard. The BBC has also commissioned a work from the Finnish composer Lotta Wennäkoski, which opens the concert.

In Finland the concert is on during this radio broadcast. In Finland the concert is on Yle Radio 1 and Yle Teema, and on the internet radio Yle Klassinen. The TV broadcast is on Yle Areena for a week and the radio concert for a month.
