# Thursday 10.9.2020 (radio)

The refugees of the year have been chosen. Many Finns living abroad are thinking about moving back. Prime Minister Marin takes a corona test. The sloth Coco at Korkeasaari has died.

## Refugees of the year

![The 2020 refugees of the year Kordnejad Ebrahimi and Farhia Abdi.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11536986)

Kordnejad Ebrahimi and Farhia Abdi. Photo: Finnish Refugee Advice Centre / Julius Töyrylä

The Finnish Refugee Advice Centre has chosen the refugees of the year. The choices are meant to highlight the active and positive work of refugees in everyday life.

Farhia Abdi from Helsinki is the refugee woman of the year. Farhia Abdi came to Finland from Somalia in December 2008.

Farhia Abdi is a practical nurse and entrepreneur. She does multicultural family work and looks after elderly people.

Kordnejad Ebrahimi from Raisio is the refugee man of the year. He is a Kurd and came to Finland from Iran in 2006.

Kordnejad Ebrahimi works as an interpreter for official matters and as a volunteer mediator. He also makes YouTube videos in which he teaches the Finnish language and Finnish customs, and translates corona instructions into Kurdish.

## Finns living abroad moving back

![Emma Winiecki and the children at the airport](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11525166)

Open image viewer

Emma, Lily and Lucas Winiecki moved from Denver in the USA to Finland. Photo: Emma Winiecki home album

The corona pandemic has made many Finns living abroad consider moving back to Finland.

Finns living abroad, that is, Finns who live in other countries, are considering a return, for example because of Finland's good health care. Families with children are also attracted by the fact that schools in Finland are open despite the pandemic. Many are also thinking about returning because the corona situation in Finland is better than in many other countries.

During the start of the year, 1,800 more Finnish citizens came back to Finland than moved away from here. In recent years the situation has been the opposite, that is, more people have left than come back.

About 300,000 Finnish citizens live abroad.

## Prime Minister takes a corona test

![Prime Minister Sanna Marin as a guest of political journalists on 8 September.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11534328)

Prime Minister Sanna Marin is suspected of having been exposed to the coronavirus.

Marin is well, however, and has no symptoms, but she will be tested for the coronavirus. The Prime Minister will work remotely until the test result is confirmed.

Marin got the information about the exposure through the Koronavilkku app on her phone.

## Coco the sloth

![Coco the sloth.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5760,h_3240,x_0,y_297/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-7172255f59ea45e39c4/1599728263)

Open image viewer

Coco the sloth at Korkeasaari. Photo: Korkeasaari Zoo

The oldest animal at Korkeasaari Zoo, a sloth called Coco, has died. Coco died at the age of 47.

Korkeasaari says that Coco was the oldest sloth in Europe and one of the oldest sloths in the whole world.

Coco was born in Panama in 1973. It moved to Korkeasaari in 1985.

Coco spent almost its whole life hanging upside down from a tree branch. Its favourite food was boiled potato and carrot.
