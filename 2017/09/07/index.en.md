# Thursday 7.9.2017 (radio)

Nordea's head office to Helsinki. The Finns Party's popularity has grown. The Sibelius Monument turns 50. School pupils in Mikkeli and Pieksämäki can ride the bus for free.

## Nordea's head office to Helsinki

![The entrance of Nordea's head office in Stockholm.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9819602)

Open image viewer

Nordea's head office is still in Stockholm for now. Photo: Martti Kainulainen / Lehtikuva

The head office of Nordea bank is moving from Sweden to Finland.

Nordea is moving because Finland belongs to the European banking union but Sweden does not. In Finland, Nordea has the same rules and benefits as many European competitor banks. It is also cheaper for Nordea to keep its head office in Finland.

Nordea bank operates in Finland, Sweden, Denmark and Norway. It is the largest bank in these Nordic countries.

It is good advertising for Finland when Nordea's head office moves to Helsinki. It tells other companies too that it is worth being in Finland.

## Support for the Finns Party grew

![Graphic](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9818722)

Open image viewer

The National Coalition Party is again the most popular party. Photo: Yle News Graphics

The Finns Party is now a little more popular than a month ago. In Yle's poll, 10.3 percent of respondents supported the Finns Party. The Finns Party's popularity has perhaps grown because of the stabbings in Turku. The stabber is a foreigner, and the Finns Party opposes immigration.

Blue Reform, which split from the Finns Party in the summer, got only 1.4 percent support in the poll.

In Yle's poll, the National Coalition Party had the most supporters: 20.8 percent, and the Greens had the second most: 17.8 percent. The Greens now have almost 5 percent more support than last spring. The popularity of the Centre Party and the SDP has fallen a little.

## The 50-year-old Sibelius Monument is popular

![Tourists at Sibelius monument.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7304620)

Open image viewer

The Sibelius Monument interests tourists. Photo: Jyki Lyytikkä / Yle

The famous work of art, the Sibelius Monument, turns 50 this Thursday.

The artist Eila Hiltunen designed the monument in Helsinki in honour of the composer Jean Sibelius. The monument has 600 steel pipes and next to them a portrait of Sibelius's face. When the monument was finished in 1967, many Finns hated it. The monument was too modern.

Today the Sibelius Monument is very popular. About half a million people visit the Sibelius Monument every year. It also interests many foreign tourists.

## School pupils ride the bus for free

![School pupils getting on a bus.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9797771)

Open image viewer

For school pupils in Mikkeli, the bus is free in their free time too. Photo: Tarja Nyyssönen / Yle

School pupils can ride the bus for free in Mikkeli and Pieksämäki in Eastern Finland.

In Pieksämäki, local buses have been free for school pupils for four years already. In Mikkeli, free rides are being tried for the first time this autumn. School pupils can also ride the bus for free in their free time.

The municipality saves money when it does not need to use expensive charter buses for school class trips. Local buses get more passengers, and in Pieksämäki the buses have also got more passengers who buy a ticket.
