# Sunday 24.12.2017 (radio)

Turku declared the Christmas peace. Traffic quietens down and many shops close for Christmas. Many Christmas tables also have ready-made food from the shop. The Good Christmas Spirit collection helped poor families with children.

## Turku declared the Christmas peace

![The City of Turku's chief of protocol Mika Akkanen declares the Christmas peace in Turku on Christmas Eve, 24 December 2017.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9993676)

Finland has gone quiet to celebrate Christmas.

Turku in Finland declared the Christmas peace at midday on Christmas Eve.

![The Christmas peace was declared at the Old Great Square in Turku on Christmas Eve, 24 December 2017.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9993678)

15,000 people came to the Old Great Square in Turku to hear the Declaration of Christmas Peace. Photo: Roni Lehti / Lehtikuva

The Declaration of Christmas Peace says that people must behave quietly and peacefully during the Christmas holidays. But if someone disturbs other people's Christmas peace, they will get the punishment that the law sets.

Turku's Declaration of Christmas Peace has a long tradition. The Declaration of Christmas Peace has been read in Turku for 700 years.

For many Finns, Christmas begins with the Declaration of Christmas Peace. People from Turku go to the Old Great Square to listen to the declaration. Other Finns can listen to it on the radio or watch it on television.

Finland goes quiet for Christmas

Many shops closed their doors already in the afternoon on Christmas Eve.

But shops are allowed to be open even for the whole of Christmas if they want to. You can check the shops' opening hours, for example, on their websites.

Traffic has also quietened down because of Christmas. For example, in Turku, Tampere, Jyväskylä and Oulu, local buses will run next only on St Stephen's Day, that is, Boxing Day.

In Helsinki, buses, trams and the metro run on Christmas Day after 11 am.

## Many Christmas tables have swede casserole from the shop

![Swede and potato casseroles. ](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9993209)

Open image viewer

Potato casserole and swede casserole. Photo: Mikko Oksanen / Lehtikuva

Shops sell a lot of ready-made food in December.

About 30 percent more ready-made food is sold in December than in other months. That is because Finns do not make all their Christmas food themselves. They buy part of it from the shop.

People buy especially casseroles for the Christmas table from the shop, for example carrot casserole, swede casserole and potato casserole. Many also buy rosolli from the shop. Rosolli is a salad with, for example, beetroot, apple, pickled gherkin and whipped cream.

According to a survey, only 12 percent of Finns make all their Christmas food themselves from scratch.

## The Good Christmas Spirit collection helped families with children

![The logo of the Good Christmas Spirit collection. ](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9993588)

Open image viewer

The money from the Good Christmas Spirit collection was used to buy gift cards for grocery shops. Photo: Yle News Graphics

The Good Christmas Spirit collection went well this year.

The collection's goal was reached, that is, 30,000 poor families with children got a gift card for a grocery shop. Each gift card was worth 70 euros.

In Finland, every 10th child lives in a family where the parents have a small income.

The collection was now held for the 21st time. The organisers of the collection are the Mannerheim League for Child Welfare, the Finnish Red Cross and Yle.
