# Wednesday 22.11.2017 (radio)

The National Board of Antiquities listed Finnish cultural heritage. Stress weighs on upper secondary school students. The Lapland meteorite will be searched for next summer. The winter's frost record was set in Utsjoki. On Thursday it will snow and blow hard.

## The National Board of Antiquities listed living cultural heritage

![Dancers on the stage at Kuikka.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9745698)

Dance halls are part of Finnish cultural heritage. Photo: Matti Myller / Yle

Cultural heritage that is important to Finns has now been listed. The National Board of Antiquities has chosen 52 things or practices for the list, which are living Finnish cultural heritage.

The list includes, for example, dance halls and pesäpallo, the Finnish Spitz and kalakukko, children's snow games and lighting a candle at graves on Christmas Eve. Of course, the list also includes the sauna and Santa Claus.

The aim of the list is to protect Finnish cultural heritage and to make it familiar.

The National Board of Antiquities' list still has to be approved by the Minister of Culture, Sampo Terho.

## Stress troubles upper secondary school students

![Students browse study material at a table.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9830861)

Open image viewer

Studying at upper secondary school is hard work. Photo: Niko Mannonen / Yle

Upper secondary school students are more exhausted than before. According to a study by the Ministry of Education, school exhaustion among upper secondary school students has increased.

Stress troubles girls in particular. Of the girls, 18 percent are severely exhausted.

Of the boys, 8 percent are severely exhausted. Boys also have a negative attitude towards upper secondary school studies.

The study says that today's upper secondary school students are the first generation in which children have more stress than their parents.

## The Lapland meteorite fell in the wilderness

![Ursa's model of the fireball's path.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9942726)

Open image viewer

The meteorite fell in north-eastern Lapland, near the border of Norway and Russia. Photo: Ursa

The astronomical association Ursa has been investigating where the Lapland meteorite fell. Ursa says that the meteorite probably fell in the Vätsäri wilderness area, near the border of Russia and Norway.

Last Thursday a bright light was seen in the Lapland sky, which was like a fireball. The flight of the fireball lasted about 10 seconds. Bright flashes were also seen during the flight. Ursa says that the flashes were caused by the meteorite breaking into pieces.

Ursa believes that dozens of kilograms of meteorite fragments ended up on the ground.

Ursa plans to search for the fallen pieces next summer.

## The winter's frost record was broken

This winter's frost record was measured last night in Utsjoki. The Finnish Meteorological Institute measured 26.5 degrees of frost at Kevojärvi in Utsjoki.

## A snowstorm sweeps across Finland

![A January snowstorm in Vantaa, 2017.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9400987)

Open image viewer

On Thursday, driving conditions are poor or very poor across almost the whole country. Photo: Markku Ojala / EPA

A storm and a snowstorm are approaching Finland. Snow will fall from Thursday morning.

Yle's meteorologist Marjo Hoikkanen says that in the southern and central parts of the country, more than 10 centimetres of snow may fall, and in the north as much as 20 centimetres.

Motorists must be careful, because driving conditions may be very poor across almost the whole country tomorrow.

The weather is also windy. At sea there will be storms, and on land there may also be strong gusts of wind.
