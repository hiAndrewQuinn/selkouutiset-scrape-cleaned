35,000 Finns have a guardian. Finnish companies want to bring clean technology to market. Easy-to-read language has been on show at the Turku Book Fair. The film The Happiest Day in the Life of Olli Mäki won a prize again.

## 35,000 Finns have a guardian

![An elderly person calculates with a pocket calculator.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9190584)

Open image viewer

A guardian looks after the client's property and money. The guardian must also make sure that the client gets suitable care, looking after or rehabilitation. Photo: Ismo Pekkarinen / AOP

There are 35,000 people in Finland who have a guardian.

Not everyone is able to take care of their own affairs, such as money matters. The reason can be an illness or a disability. There are many hundreds of thousands of such people in Finland.

If a person has no close relatives, a public guardian is appointed as their support person. A guardian is an official who looks after the property and money of the customer, that is, the client.

There are 200 public guardians in Finland. The client is often, for example, a person with an intellectual disability, a mental health patient, a minor or a person with dementia.

## Finnish companies want to bring clean technology to market

![water purification container](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9203018)

Open image viewer

A solar-powered water purifier was tested in Hanko in the summer. Photo: Solar Water Solutions

The Finnish clean tech sector, that is, so-called clean technology, wants to get into world markets. Around the world there are big markets for methods that promote a clean environment.

One of these companies is Solar Water Solutions. The company's plant makes drinking water out of sea water using solar energy. The aim is to replace batteries or generators that use diesel oil. The company has a test plant in Dubai.

There are perhaps 5,000 clean technology companies in Finland. But there are too few pilot projects in the field in Finland. Next week there is an event in Lahti where clean technology companies look for funding.

## More new books and old classics are wanted in easy-to-read language

![Easy-to-read literature at the Turku Book Fair 2016](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9205274)

Open image viewer

Leealaura Leskelä from the Selkokeskus (Centre for Easy Language) interviews author Eppu Nuotio and easy-to-read author Johanna Kartio (right). Photo: Kaisa Kaatra / Selkosanomat

There has been a book fair in Turku at the weekend. Books in easy-to-read language have also been on show at the book fair.

Author Eppu Nuotio hoped for  more easy-to-read books of new Finnish fiction. Easy-to-read author Johanna Kartio also hoped for more old Finnish classics. Such a classic is, for example, Väinö Linna's The Unknown Soldier.

Kartio has written an easy-to-read version of Nuotio's book Peiton paikka.

## The film The Happiest Day in the Life of Olli Mäki got a prize again

![Hymyilevä Mies](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9115442)

Open image viewer

The main roles in Hymyilevä mies are played by Jarkko Lahti, Eero Milonoff and Oona Airola. Photo: Kuokkasen Kuvaamo

The Finnish film Hymyilevä mies (The Happiest Day in the Life of Olli Mäki) has won the main prize at the Zurich Film Festival. The film is directed by Juho Kuosmanen.

The main prize is about 23,000 euros. It also includes a distribution prize. This year, 14 films competed for the main prize.

The film is about boxer Olli Mäki falling in love. The film is set in 1962.

Hymyilevä mies has also won prizes in Macedonia and at the Cannes Film Festival in France. The film is Finland's Oscar candidate. The Oscar awards are given by the American film academy.
