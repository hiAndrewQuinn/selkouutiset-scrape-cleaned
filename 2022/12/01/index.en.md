## Veterans and Lottas at the Presidential Palace

![People around a table at the Presidential Palace.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12683093)

Open image viewer

President Sauli Niinistö has already invited the first guests for Independence Day. Photo: Markku Ulander / Lehtikuva

The celebration of Independence Day has begun. Finland's Independence Day is on 6 December, that is, next Tuesday.

However, President Sauli Niinistö and his wife Jenni Haukio invited the first guests to the Presidential Palace already today, Thursday. Veterans and Lottas came to the Presidential Palace.

Veterans and Lottas are already old. That is why the presidential couple wanted to make sure that they can celebrate without other guests and without fear of coronavirus.

Veterans were soldiers in the Finnish army during the Second World War. Lottas are women who helped the army, for example as nurses or cooks.

The Second World War was about 80 years ago, so the veterans and Lottas are already about 100 years old. Most of the veterans and Lottas have already died.

## Home care support

![A man wipes a baby's feet.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11217697)

Open image viewer

Photo: Tiina Jutila / Yle

Home care support has a big effect on families' lives. A new study says that home care support has a negative effect on families.

Researchers examined the situation in municipalities that pay more home care support to families than other municipalities.

The study says that, with the help of the support, mothers in particular have stayed at home with their children. They work less and earn less income from wages than others, even up to 10 years after the child's birth.

Because of home care support, many children take part in early childhood education less than others. They make more mistakes in child health checks, and later they study less than others.

## Power cuts possible

![Electric heater.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8635843)

Open image viewer

Electric heating uses a lot of electricity. Photo: Jyrki Utriainen / Yle

Finland is preparing for power cuts.

Electricity may run short in winter if there is hard frost across the whole country. Then heating uses a lot of electricity.

The situation is difficult if the weather is at the same time windless and wind turbines stop.

In a bad situation there will not be enough electricity for the whole day. Then there may be power cuts, but attempts will be made to tell people about them in advance.

However, everyone should know what to do if the power goes out. Instructions in Easy Language for power cuts can be found on the website of the Easy Language Centre.
