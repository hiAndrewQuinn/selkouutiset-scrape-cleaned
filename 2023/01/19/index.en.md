## Finland's military intelligence

![The southern tip of Rankki fortress island, with Navy commanders scanning the sea.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_6000,h_3375,x_0,y_469/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6180655de3cb09013d5/1575210690)

Open image viewer

The war in Europe has increased Finland's military intelligence and other countries' intelligence on Finland. Photo: Jani Saikko / Yle

Last year was busy for Finland's military intelligence. The reason is the war in Ukraine. That is what the Defence Command says.

The number of Finland's intelligence permits more than doubled from the year before. Finland was also the target of a lot of military intelligence from abroad, because Finland is applying to join the NATO military alliance.

New intelligence laws were introduced in Finland 4 years ago. The Defence Command says that the new intelligence laws now work well.

## Trans act

![Protesters in Senate Square.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5120,h_2880,x_0,y_448/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-106113063c8e578d0f5e/1675252298)

The trans act has been long awaited. Photo: Vesa Moilanen / Lehtikuva

Parliament's Social and Health Committee has completed its report on reforming the Trans Act. There has been a dispute in the committee about the Trans Act. The completion of the report has been postponed.

The committee approved the report on the Trans Act by 11–5 in a vote. Representatives of the opposition National Coalition Party voted for the bill, even though the National Coalition has criticised the Trans Act.

Representatives of the governing Centre Party voted against the bill. The Social and Health Committee changed the Trans Act so that a person may change their legal gender at most once a year.

## Electricity bills

![Electricity company bill and cash.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4629,h_2591,x_0,y_96/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-1031124636a54c294783/1671528338)

Open image viewer

Electricity prices vary, because some people still have an old electricity contract, in which electricity is cheap. Photo: Matias Väänänen / Yle

Finns' electricity bills rose by more than 50 percent last year. That is what the Energy Authority says.

Electricity bills rose by 53 percent on average. The calculation includes the electricity price, the electricity transmission fee and electricity taxes. The price of electricity energy alone rose by over 140 percent.

There were big differences in the electricity price, however. Some Finns still had an old electricity contract in which electricity was cheap. Many Finns started a new electricity contract last year, in which electricity became very much more expensive.
