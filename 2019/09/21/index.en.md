# Saturday 21.9.2019 (radio)

Fewer and fewer asylum seekers are coming to Finland. There are not enough home care services. Wolves kill quite a lot of dogs in Finland. Saturday was World Cleanup Day.

## Fewer and fewer asylum seekers are coming to Finland

![Some asylum seekers moved to Sweden. ](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9816358)

Some asylum seekers moved from Finland to Sweden at the Tornio border in 2017. Photo: Riikka Rautiainen / Yle

Fewer and fewer asylum seekers are coming to Finland. The Finnish Immigration Service, Migri, says so. This year there may be fewer applicants than at any time since 2010.

About 1,700 first asylum applications have been made this year. Last year there were 2,400 applications.

The number of asylum applications has been falling all the time since 2016. The peak year was 2015. Then more than 32,000 applications came.

The Immigration Service says that moving within Europe is harder than before. There may be border checks at the borders of countries. In addition, Finland is far away.

This year, the most new asylum seekers have come from Turkey, Russia and Iraq. There have been about 80 different nationalities.

## More people are needed for home care of older people

![A home carer helps an elderly person with a shoe on their foot.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10165486)

Open image viewer

Photo: Kalle Parkkinen / Lehtikuva

There are not enough home care services. In Finland there are more and more older people who need support and care.

The biggest difficulties are in the big cities of southern Finland. Earlier, the problems were in the north and east.

A lot has been said in public about care services for older people outside the home. The same problems are also in home care.

The Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare predicts that more than 30,000 workers will be needed for home care in 2030. Last year there were about 21,500 workers.

Half of the clients of older people's services are in home care.

## Wolves kill many dogs

![Wolf bite mark on the neck of a hunting dog](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10976982)

Open image viewer

Tinka the dog has a wound on its neck, which may be a wolf bite mark. A similar mark is on its back near the tail. Photo: Sami Takkinen / Yle

Wolves kill quite a lot of dogs in Finland. This year, almost 40,000 euros in compensation has already been paid. The most damage was compensated in North Karelia.

For example, the compensation for a purebred dog is at least 1,600 euros. The compensation amount goes up if, for example, the dog has been trained as a hunting dog.

Last year, 34 dog damages caused by wolves were recorded in the register.

The Natural Resources Institute says that there are about 200 wolves in Finland.

## Saturday was World Cleanup Day

![Volunteers collected rubbish on World Cleanup Day in Jakarta, Indonesia, on 21 September.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10982448)

Volunteers collected rubbish on World Cleanup Day in Jakarta, Indonesia. Photo: Bay Ismoyo / AFP

On Saturday it was World Cleanup Day. Thousands of activists have cleaned beaches in Asia, for example. In Hanoi, the capital of Vietnam, there were 1,400 volunteer cleaners.

Plastic rubbish is a serious problem in Southeast Asia. The worst situation is in the Philippines, China, Vietnam and Indonesia.

A UN report says that only 9 percent of the world's plastic is recycled for new use.
