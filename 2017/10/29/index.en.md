# Sunday 29.10.2017 (radio)

Finnish children can move around more freely than others. A new way to grow potatoes has been invented in Finland. Many people want to stop turning the clocks. The number of plain-language books is growing.

In Finland, children can move around more freely than children elsewhere in the world, says a new study.

In Finland, parents let their children, for example, go to school alone, cross a big road alone or go outside alone, even when it is dark outside.

The international study compared how children move around in 16 different countries. Finland was number one in almost everything.

Researcher Veera Moll says that Finnish children's great freedom may be because parents think their surroundings are safe. Children also have to learn to get around alone, because usually both parents are at work.

## Potatoes also grow without soil

![The Finnish Seed Potato Centre, Tyrnävä](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9904611)

Open image viewer

The Finnish Seed Potato Centre grows air potatoes. Photo: Paulus Markkula / Yle

A new way to grow potatoes has been developed in Finland.

Potatoes grown in the new way are called air potatoes. The potato does not need to be planted in soil; instead, the new crop grows in a dark, empty box.

The new growing method has been developed at the Finnish Seed Potato Centre in Tyrnävä.

New kinds of growing methods are being developed, for example, in case of climate change or an environmental crisis.

## Winter time has begun

![Alarm clocks.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9906315)

Open image viewer

Many people would like to keep the clocks on winter time, which is Finland's normal time. Photo: Antti Aimo-Koivisto / Lehtikuva

Finland has moved to winter time. The clocks were moved back by one hour last night.

Winter time is Finland's normal time. But in spring the clocks are moved to summer time, because the EU countries have agreed so.

Many people think that moving the clocks should stop. They say that moving the clocks is not good for health.

An initiative demanding that summer time be given up is now being prepared in the European Parliament. All of Finland's MEPs have signed the initiative.

## Plain-language books have readers

![Two women, one holding a bouquet of flowers](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9906931)

Open image viewer

Tiina Aalto and Katja Jalkanen of the publisher Avain received an award for promoting plain-language literature. Photo: Kaisa Kaatra/Selkokeskus

The number of plain-language books is growing little by little.

More than 350 plain-language books have already been published. At least 10 new plain-language books are published every year, often more.

The Plain Language Centre says that perhaps as many as 12 percent of Finns need plain language. For them, reading an ordinary book in standard language can be really difficult. Students of Finnish also benefit from plain-language books.

Every other year at the Helsinki Book Fair, the Plain Language Centre gives an award for promoting plain-language literature. This time, this Seesam award went to the publisher Avain.
