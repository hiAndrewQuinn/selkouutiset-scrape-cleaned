## Coronavirus vaccinations and young people

The National Institute for Health and Welfare also recommends coronavirus vaccinations for all 12–15-year-olds.

THL says that the vaccination protects young people and at the same time the people close to them from the disease. Vaccinations may also reduce restrictions that apply to children and young people.

THL has already earlier recommended coronavirus vaccinations for 12–15-year-olds in risk groups.

## Tournament tourists' coronavirus infections

![Car queue at the Vaalimaa border station.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1600,h_900,x_0,y_245/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-82464960d310ed360fd/1624536182)

Open image viewer

Tournament tourists queued at the border on Tuesday. Photo: Niklas Joki / Yle

Many Finnish football fans have caught coronavirus in Russia. The football European Championship is currently being held in St. Petersburg.

Hundreds of fans came back over the Vaalimaa border on Tuesday and Wednesday. About 90 infections have come to light.

At the border only 900 people had time to be tested on Tuesday. Testing was stopped because of queues.

Now the goal is to reach the 800 people who crossed the border without a test.

## Heat increases electricity use

![Air-source heat pump.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4618,h_2598,x_0,y_260/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-7526685fd1d3e2c7b73/1607587008)

Open image viewer

An air-source heat pump can also be used to cool the home. Photo: Kalle Niskala / Yle

Electricity is now being used a lot in Finland. During the day it has been hot and it has been hot at night too. That is why people use a lot of fans.

Many homes also have a heat pump, which only cools rooms.

Electricity use rises by about 3 percent when the temperature is about 30 degrees.

The share of cooling in the country's electricity use is small. For a resident it costs about one euro more on the electricity bill per hot day.

## Textile waste recycling

![Clothes recycling from a street collection.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9111891)

Open image viewer

About 100,000 tonnes of household textile waste accumulates in Finland every year. Photo: Atte Penttinen / Yle

Textile recycling is improving.

Finland is drafting a new waste act, which concerns waste textiles. These include, for example, old clothes.

The European Union requires member states to collect waste textiles. The requirement comes into force by 2025.

Finland is 2 years ahead of schedule. Here, textile recycling will already start in 2023. Then all municipal waste companies in Finland will recycle textiles.

Used clothing can become, for example, yarn for new clothes.

At present only one percent of all Finnish textile waste ends up for recycling.

Home mortgages

![Floor plan of an apartment at an apartment viewing.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9578839)

Open image viewer

About 30 percent of homes have housing debt. Photo: Tiina Jutila / Yle

Many Finnish homes have housing debt.

The debt was on average 106,000 euros last year. The most housing debt is in households in the capital region, on average about 157,000 euros.

The average housing debt grew by 3.5 percent from the previous year. In the capital region the growth was 5.6 percent.
