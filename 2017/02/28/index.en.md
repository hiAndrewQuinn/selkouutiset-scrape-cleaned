# Tuesday 28.2.2017 (radio)

Employers propose that the child home care allowance be shortened. Most Finns want all young people to go to another school or work training straight after comprehensive school. A new ship terminal was opened in Helsinki. The tarpaulin on the Parliament House is being taken off. In March owls hoot in the forest when they are looking for a mate.

## EK: A shorter home care allowance

![A father and child walking.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8826861)

Open image viewer

A father and child on a walk.  Photo: Olichel / Pixabay

Employers propose that the child home care allowance be shortened.

Nowadays a mother or father can care for a child at home until the age of 3 and get support from the state. The employers' organisation EK proposes that the allowance be paid for only 1 and a half years.

EK also proposes that the parental allowance be paid half to the mother and half to the father. Nowadays mothers use 90 percent of the parental allowance, and that becomes expensive for companies that have a lot of women working. The employers of men get off much more cheaply.

## Young people are wanted in further education, even by force

![Monna Järvinen.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9482665)

Open image viewer

Student Monna Järvinen. Photo: Jaani Lampinen / Yle

Most Finns want all young people to go to another school or work training straight after comprehensive school.

Yle asked Finns for their opinion. 70 percent supported a law that orders young people into further education.

Student Monna Järvinen says that if a law forces people to go to school or to work, nobody will stay at home and be left outside society. And that is a good thing.

Researcher Sanna Aaltonen says that being forced may not be a good thing for all young people. Many young people do not know what profession they want, and being forced may make a young person even more anxious.

## A new ship terminal

![The Jätkäsaari passenger terminal.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9248414)

Open image viewer

Helsinki's new ship terminal. Photo: PES-Arkkitehdit Oy

The new terminal at Helsinki's West Harbour is ready. Ships leave from the new terminal to Tallinn in Estonia.

Over 6 million passengers a year pass through the terminal. From the new terminal you can get on and off the ship quickly.

Some ships still leave from the old terminal. You can get to the front of both terminals by tram.

## The Parliament House tarpaulin comes off

![The Parliament House covered with a tarpaulin in August 2016.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9481331)

Open image viewer

The Parliament House tarpaulin is not easy to notice when you look from far away. Photo: Joni Haavisto / AOP

The tarpaulin on the Parliament House in Helsinki is being taken off.

A big renovation is being done in the Parliament House, and that is why there are tarpaulins over the building. Soon the renovation will be finished.

The first tarpaulin is taken down carefully so that it stays in one piece. The tarpaulin will be used again. For example, bags will be made from it.

The second tarpaulin will be taken off in March. Then everyone will see what the Parliament House looks like after the repair.

## Owls hoot in the spring

![A boreal owl in close-up.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6861331)

Open image viewer

Boreal owl. Photo: Mirva Korpela / Yle

March begins, and it is the first month of spring. One sure sign of spring is when owls start to hoot in the forest.

Owls hoot when they are looking for a mate. If you want to hear an owl, you have to go to the forest or the edge of a field at night, when there is no wind and it is otherwise quiet too.
