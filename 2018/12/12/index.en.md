# Wednesday 12.12.2018 (radio)

A terrorist attack happened in Strasbourg. Emissions from traffic must be reduced. Protests must be reported to the police earlier than now. Several complaints have been made about the seizure of Nazi flags. Finns are good at languages.

## Gunman struck at the Strasbourg Christmas market

![A French police officer stands guard in Strasbourg on the evening of Tuesday 11 December. A multicoloured Christmas tree in the background.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10550061)

A terrorist act has happened in Strasbourg in France.

On Tuesday evening a man attacked a crowd of people and shot at least 2 people dead. In addition, 12 people were wounded. Of them, 6 were seriously wounded.

The attack happened near the Christmas market in Strasbourg.

The shooter escaped after the attack. He is an old acquaintance of the police for several minor crimes. The police also knew that the shooter had become radicalised when he was in prison earlier.

Border checks on the French and German border have been tightened. Security arrangements at Christmas markets in France have also been tightened.

## Traffic emissions can be removed

![Traffic jam in Ruoholahti](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10228439)

Open image viewer

20 percent of Finland's emissions come from traffic. Photo: Esko Jämsä / AOP

A working group of the Ministry of Transport and Communications has considered how Finland can reduce traffic emissions.

The working group says that it is possible for Finland to remove traffic emissions completely by 2045. However, that requires that big decisions are made quickly.

The working group proposes, for example, road tolls in big cities and raising the fuel tax. Today's cars must be replaced with electric cars and biogas cars. In addition, public transport connections must be improved.

20 percent of Finland's emissions come from traffic.

## The law on assemblies is to be changed

![Antti Häkkänen](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9856009)

Open image viewer

Minister of Justice Antti Häkkänen. Photo: Petteri Sopanen / Yle

The government wants to change the law on assemblies so that protests must be reported to the police 24 hours in advance.

Under the current law, the organiser of a protest must notify the police of the protest 6 hours before it starts.

Minister of Justice Antti Häkkänen says that the notice period must be extended, because the police have time to arrive on the spot and make sure everyone is safe.

## Complaints about the seizure of Nazi flags

![Police removed swastika flags from protesters.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10542614)

Open image viewer

The police seized the swastika flags of neo-Nazis on Independence Day. Photo: Martti Kainulainen / Lehtikuva

Several complaints have been made about the Helsinki police. The complaints concern the seizure of swastika flags, which the police carried out at the neo-Nazi march on Independence Day.

4 private individuals ask for an investigation into whether the police's actions were lawful.

The complaints say that the police acted wrongly when they seized the flags, because the use of Nazi symbols is not forbidden in Finland.

## Finns are good at languages

src="https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5286492"

Open image viewer

Most Finns can speak at least one foreign language. Photo: YLE / Eeva Huovinen

Almost every working-age Finn can speak at least one foreign language.

93 percent of working-age Finns say that they can speak some foreign language. The figure is the same as 5 years ago.

Over the last 20 years, the people who have improved their language skills the most are those who have only finished basic comprehensive school.
