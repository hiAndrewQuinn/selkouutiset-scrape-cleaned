# Tuesday 7.4.2020 (radio)

Finland continues movement restrictions at the borders. The coronavirus situation is shrinking Finland's economy. The police are getting more home call-outs because of the coronavirus situation. Grocery shops are prepared for employees falling ill. There is enough food in the shops.

## Finland continues movement restrictions at the borders

![Border Guard carrying out border checks in Tornio.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11295286)

Open image viewer

Border guards are guarding Finland's borders. Photo: Antti Ullakko / Yle

Finland is continuing movement restrictions at its external borders. The restrictions will be extended by a month, so that they are in force until 13 May.

Finland is also tightening border traffic. At Finland's borders with Sweden and Norway, only essential commuting traffic is allowed, for example for hospital staff.

Goods traffic may also cross the border. Cargo may be carried on ships, but not passengers.

## The coronavirus is shrinking the economy

![Bank of Finland.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10775602)

Open image viewer

Bank of Finland. Photo: Jari Kärkkäinen/Yle

The coronavirus situation is greatly shrinking Finland's economy. The Bank of Finland says that Finland's economy may shrink by 13% this year because of the coronavirus situation.

The economy may shrink this much because the restrictions will last for a long time in Finland.

The Bank of Finland says that if Finland takes steps that quickly get rid of the coronavirus, the economy will shrink much less, for example by only 5%.

## Home call-outs are increasing

![A police car.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5568,h_3132,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-4988625b4f10bf6fa65/1583757455)

Open image viewer

A police car. Photo: Tiina Jutila / Yle

The police are getting more home call-outs because of the coronavirus situation. The biggest increase in home call-outs has come from noise or behaviour that disturbs others.

Arguments at home are happening more easily because of the movement restrictions. Children are at home doing distance school, and adults are at home working remotely or laid off. When people are at home a lot, some families may have many arguments and perhaps violence. Then the police are called in to help.

Arguments and violence in homes may increase if the movement restrictions continue for a long time.

## Grocery shops have enough staff

![Shopping baskets next to the checkout at the Prisma in Malmi, Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10911625)

Open image viewer

Grocery shops have prepared to stay open even if employees fall ill. Photo: Jussi Nukari / Lehtikuva

Grocery shops are able to stay open in Finland even if their staff fall ill with the coronavirus.

The big grocery chains S Group and K Group have made a plan for how there will be enough staff for all shops, if many employees fall ill.

For example, employees of one shop can go and help at another shop.

## There is enough food in the shops

![PTT forecasts that the prices of meat and dairy products will rise by more than 20 percent by the end of the year.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10266028)

Open image viewer

There is enough food in the shops. Prices may rise for a while. Photo: Hanna Othman / Yle

There is enough food in the shops in Finland. Researchers say that food will not run out of the shops because of the coronavirus situation.

A lot of food is produced in Finland, and food imports from abroad work well.

Because of the coronavirus situation, food prices may rise for a while. Food prices may rise for a while because people have bought a lot of food, and shops have had to arrange more deliveries, for example.
