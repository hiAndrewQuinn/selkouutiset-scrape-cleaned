# Saturday 7.3.2020 (radio)

Finland's prime minister spoke about the position of women at the UN. The Association of Finnish Local and Regional Authorities and the Ministry of Education disagree about the costs of extending compulsory education. New coronavirus infections have been found in Finland again. Tonight Finland's Eurovision representative will be chosen.

## Finland's prime minister at the UN

![Sanna Marin speaks at a women's day event at the UN on Friday.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11246169)

Finland's Prime Minister Sanna Marin has given a speech at the UN in New York. On Friday Marin spoke about the position of women at the UN's international women's day event.

In her speech Marin said that there are only 21 heads of state in the world who are women. The UN has 193 countries, however, and more than half of the people in the world are women, Marin said.

Marin also told about Finland's achievements in equality. For example, she mentioned childcare and full political rights, which Finnish women were the first in the world to get, in 1906.

Marin also visited Columbia University, where she talked about climate change and the welfare society. Marin's lecture caused long queues at the university.

## Extending compulsory education

![A teacher and pupils in a lesson.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11152549)

Open image viewer

The government wants to extend compulsory education to age 18. Photo: Jari Kovalainen / Yle

The Association of Finnish Local and Regional Authorities is worried about the government's plans to extend compulsory education. The association is worried about the costs and the timetable of the law change.

The government wants to raise the compulsory education age from 16 to 18. A pupil must therefore, after comprehensive school, also complete second-level education, for example upper secondary school or vocational school.

The Ministry of Education and Culture has calculated that extending compulsory education costs over 100 million euros a year. The Association of Finnish Local and Regional Authorities says that much more money is needed.

The government's aim is that the reform comes into force in a year, in the spring.

## Coronavirus infections

![Isolation sign at the HUS Meilahti hospital area in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11243875)

The coronavirus continues to spread in Finland. In total 19 infections have now been found in Finland.

Worldwide, more than 100,000 people have already got the coronavirus infection. The vast majority of the infections are in China.

About 3,500 people have died of the coronavirus. However, tens of thousands of people have also recovered from the disease.
