# Tuesday 28.4.2020 (radio)

Training of corona contact tracers begins in Finland. The National Emergency Supply Agency has ordered protective equipment from China. Yle's online survey shows that most guardians want primary schools to stay closed for the rest of the spring term. Computers are being collected for schoolchildren. The number of bears is growing in Finland.

## Training of corona contact tracers begins

![The picture shows a Norwegian corona app.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11311819)

Training of corona contact tracers begins in Finland.

They find out the chains of coronavirus infection. Their task is to trace people who have been exposed to infection through contact with other people.

An open course will open next month. The course can be completed as online study, according to your own schedule.

The training is organised by the Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare THL and the universities of Tampere and Eastern Finland.

## Protective equipment is coming to Finland from China

![Face protectors the National Emergency Supply Agency obtained from China through the businessman Onni Sarmaste.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11320056)

Open image viewer

Face masks. Photo: Kalle Parkkinen / Lehtikuva

The National Emergency Supply Agency has ordered surgical mouth and nose protectors for Finland.

12.5 million protectors will come from China during May to July. The agreement was made last week with the Chinese company Hengan.

The company's masks are similar to those the company has already delivered to Finland before. The National Emergency Supply Agency says that the masks are suitable for hospital use and their quality has been tested.

Hengan makes tissue paper and hygiene products. The company's turnover last year was 3 billion euros.

## Most guardians would keep schools closed

![A woman works remotely on a laptop.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4445,h_2500,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6523685e68fa0d9d09f/1602832465)

Yle has carried out an online survey about schools.

70 percent of respondents want primary schools to stay closed for the rest of the spring term, that is, until summer. 30 percent of respondents oppose keeping schools closed.

If schools open, almost 70 percent consider it unlikely that their own child will take part in classroom teaching.

The majority of respondents were guardians of primary school-age children.

Yle's online survey received 3,200 answers. The result of the survey does not represent the whole Finnish population.

## Computers are being collected for schoolchildren

![A child doing homework on a laptop.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11325821)

Open image viewer

Not all parents want to send their child back to school yet, even if the corona restrictions are lifted and schools open. Photo: Mikko Ahmajärvi / Yle

Laptop computers have been collected for schoolchildren in Finland.

Already more than 1,000 computers have been obtained for primary school pupils and students. The computers are for distance learning.

Behind the donations is the "A Computer for Everyone" campaign. It is under the patronage of President Sauli Niinistö. Many companies have joined the collection.

Money has also been donated through the charity service Kotimaanapu.

However, the need for computers is great. The Finnish National Agency for Education has received requests for almost 8,000 devices.

## There are more bears than before

![A bear standing in snow.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4606,h_2591,x_0,y_367/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6629635e8dc145564b6/1586348760)

The number of bears is growing in Finland.

The Natural Resources Institute Finland, Luke, estimates that there are about 2,500 bears in Finland. The number has grown by about 14 percent since the previous estimate.

The population has grown, among other reasons, because bears wander into Finland from Russia. In addition, bear sightings have been made more often and from wider areas than before.

The number of bears has grown, even though in recent years more culling permits have been granted than before.
