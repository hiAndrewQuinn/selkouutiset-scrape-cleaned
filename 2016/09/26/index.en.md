# Monday 26.9.2016 (radio)

The Finnish government says that extremist movements can be banned already under the current law. In the United States, the first television election debate is on Tuesday night. Not all schools have enough computers. Many foreign students will have to pay a fee next autumn. Vendace are fished from Lake Kemijärvi in autumn.

## Putting the law to use

![The Resistance Movement at Narinkkatori in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8758507)

A neo-Nazi association called the Nordic Resistance Movement at a demonstration in Helsinki.  Photo: Yle

The Finnish government says that violent extremist organisations can be banned already under Finland's current law.

The law exists, but it has not been used. Now the government wants the law to start being used. An association can be banned if its activities break the law or good morals.

The police have not yet decided whether they will ask the court to ban the neo-Nazi association called the Nordic Resistance Movement.

## The first TV debate

![Donald Trump and Hillary Clinton.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9192452)

Donald Trump and Hillary Clinton. Photo: Michael Reynolds and Shawn Thew / EPA

The US presidential candidates have their first election debate on television on Tuesday at 4 o'clock Finnish time. Perhaps over 100 million Americans will watch the debate.

Before the debate, the Democratic Party's candidate Hillary Clinton is more popular than the Republican Party's Donald Trump. 46.5 percent of those who answered polls support Clinton and 43.7 percent support Trump.

Yle's TV1 and Yle Areena show the debate live. On Tuesday morning it will be shown again, and then the programme will have Finnish subtitles.

## School and computers

![Mankkaa school](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9188033)

Open image viewer

Mankkaa school in Espoo has good equipment.  Photo: Yle

Decision-makers want all children in Finland to learn at school to use computers and the internet well.

But in some schools there are not enough computers for all students. In addition, many teachers have not themselves had training in using computers and the internet in teaching.

There are big differences between municipalities in how much of the teaching is done digitally with computers. That means that Finland's children are not equal in their teaching.

## A fee for foreigners at higher education institutions

![The presentation event in Moscow](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9191127)

Open image viewer

Higher education institutions on show in Moscow. Photo: Erkka Mikkonen/Yle

Finnish higher education institutions presented themselves to young people in Moscow at the weekend. Many Russians want to study in Finland.

Now teaching is free for everyone, but next autumn many foreign students will have to pay a fee. Then one year of teaching will cost about 8,000 euros if the student comes from somewhere other than an EU country, Norway or Iceland.

But some higher education institutions give a foreign student a scholarship with which they get teaching for free.

## Vendace time in Lapland's lakes

![A net full of vendace.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9176638)

Open image viewer

Vendace in a net. Photo: Jorma Korhonen / Yle

The water in Lapland's lakes will soon be cold enough for fishing vendace.

For example, in Lake Kemijärvi a lot of vendace move about when the water gets colder. Fisherman Taisto Törmänen says that there are a lot of vendace in Lake Kemijärvi this autumn. But the fish are small. They have grown slowly because they had less food in the cold summer.

Taisto Törmänen believes that the best vendace catches of the autumn are still to come.
