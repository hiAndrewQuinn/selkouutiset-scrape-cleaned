# Friday 01.05.2020 (radio)

The May Day celebrations in Finland have mostly passed calmly. Political speeches have been given online. The May Day flag-raising is dedicated to the work against the coronavirus. Health is the number one issue for Finns. A huge amount of microplastics was found on the coast of Italy.

## The May Day in the emergency conditions has been calm

![Aleksi on the evening of the May Day eve.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11332295)

The May Day celebrations in Finland have mostly passed calmly.

The authorities' advice was to spend May Day at home and remotely. The police say that this has been followed quite well. The streets have been quieter than usual for a weekend.

The traditional reception for the graduating students is usually held at Ullanlinnanmäki in Helsinki. This time it was held in the morning in the yard of Yle in Pasila, Helsinki.

## Political speeches were given online

![Havis Amanda statue in Helsinki, fenced off with a colourful fence.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11331937)

In Helsinki, the cap-putting on the Havis Amanda statue was also virtual this year, and the statue is behind the fence. Photo: Petteri Bülow / Yle

The political speeches of May Day have been given online.

The chairman of the SDP, the Prime Minister's party, Antti Rinne, thanked the front line of health care in his May Day speech.

The chairman of the Left Alliance, Li Andersson, proposed a May Day hundred, that is, a hundred euros, for supporting all cultural and service sectors.

The chairman of the Greens, Maria Ohisalo, wants to use recovery measures to fight the climate crisis.

The chairman of the Swedish People's Party, RKP, Anna-Maija Henriksson, hopes for safe solutions for older people to meet other people.

From the opposition, the chairman of the Finns Party, Jussi Halla-aho, criticised the use of foreign labour in agriculture in his May Day speech.

The chairs of the Centre Party and the National Coalition Party published their May Day messages already on the eve of May Day, and spoke, among other things, about the economy.

## Now the flag is raised in support of the work against the corona

![The Finnish flag flies on a pole on the 2015 parliamentary election day.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7938320)

Open image viewer

May Day is an official flag day. Photo: Pekka Autio / Yle

The flag-raising on May Day this year is dedicated to those who work against the coronavirus.

The Ministry of the Interior is responsible for flag matters. It stresses that, for example, health care staff work every day on the front line of fighting the virus.

Police officers, teachers, cleaners and checkout workers also do their work in conditions that the coronavirus has made more difficult.

## Health is an important matter for people

![A pedestrian, a cyclist and a bus in Pasila.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11327319)

Open image viewer

In the corona era, people value ordinary, everyday things. Photo: Tiina Jutila / Yle

Health is the number one issue for Finns. This is what a survey carried out by Yle in the middle of the corona crisis shows.

Almost half of the respondents mentioned health. Second most often, people wished for having work.

Next, human relationships were mentioned, and fourth on the list was livelihood.

## There is a lot of microplastic in the sea off Italy

![Microplastics washed ashore near Cosenza in Italy.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11332206)

Open image viewer

Plastic waste thrown onto the coast by a storm in Italy. Photo: Alfonso Di Vincenzo / AOP

A lot of microplastics have been found on the coast of Italy. Microplastic is, for example, plastic that has broken down into microscopic pieces.

Italy's discovery is the largest known accumulation. In an area of 1 square metre there were about 1.9 million microplastic particles.

The study showed that bottom currents can carry plastic waste on the seabed.

Microplastic is harmful to sea nature.

Every year, millions of tonnes of plastic waste end up in the world's seas. It comes from housing, industry, ships and fishing.
