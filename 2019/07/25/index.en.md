# Thursday 25.7.2019 (radio)

The police have continued investigating the Helsinki shooting. Britain's new Prime Minister Boris Johnson promises that Britain will leave the European Union at the end of October. A study says that talking on a mobile phone does not cause headaches or hearing problems. Jaakko has once again thrown a cold stone into the water.

## Police investigate the shooting

The police have continued investigating the Helsinki shooting. The man shot at 3 other men on Tuesday. 2 of the men were injured and had to go to hospital.

The shooter is the director of a building company from Vantaa. He is originally from Kosovo.

The victims also work in the construction sector, but the police say that the incident is not connected to the men's work. It was a dispute.

The police demand that the shooter be taken into custody, because he may have tried to murder the men.

The British voted for EU withdrawal already 3 years ago. Johnson's promise is that the exit will finally become a reality.

## Mobile phones do not cause headaches

![A man speaks into an iPhone.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7462257)

Open image viewer

A mobile phone does not cause headaches or hearing problems. Photo: Julian Stratenschulte / EPA

Many people wonder whether using a mobile phone is harmful.

The study says that talking on a mobile phone does not cause headaches or hearing problems.

More than 24,000 people from Finland and Sweden took part in the study. The study examined concerns related to electromagnetic fields. The result was that electromagnetic fields do not cause headaches or hearing problems.

The research continues. In the next phase, 150,000 people from the Netherlands, Denmark, Britain and France are taking part. The new study will examine whether talking on mobile phones is linked to tumours in the head area.

## Jaakko threw a cold stone

![Jaakonpäivä stone throwing at the Market Square on 25 July 2019.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10893938)

Open image viewer

A cold stone was thrown into the water in Helsinki with a device like this one. Photo: Emmi Korhonen / Lehtikuva

Jaakko has once again thrown a cold stone into the water. An old Finnish folk tradition says that bathing waters start to cool on Jaakko's Day, that is, on 25 July.

In Helsinki the Finnish Unscientific Society threw a cold stone into the water on the shore of the Market Square. The funny event attracted spectators this year too. The Unscientific Society has organised the Jaakko's Day event for more than 20 years.

This year Jaakko's stone takes effect only in 4 days, laughs Yle meteorologist Joonas Koskela. The weather will cool next week, and the bathing waters may then also cool.
