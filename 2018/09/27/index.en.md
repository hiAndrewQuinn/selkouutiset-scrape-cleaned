# Thursday 27.9.2018 (radio)

The Security Police, Supo, help the National Bureau of Investigation in the investigation in the Turku archipelago. Food industry workers are on strike. The State Treasury pays a lot of costs for injuries in the army. The weather is getting colder, and roads may be slippery.

## The Security Police help the National Bureau of Investigation with the Airiston Helmi investigation

![Säkkiluoto, owned by the company Airiston Helmi, photographed from the air.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10421747)

The Security Police, that is, Supo, are helping the National Bureau of Investigation (KRP) with the investigation in the Turku archipelago. At the weekend a large operation was carried out in the Turku archipelago. A lot of material and money was seized.

Supo is helping to examine the material. KRP gets help from Supo, for example, in examining texts in foreign languages. Supo is also examining whether anything seized is linked to the security of the Finnish state.

Authorities suspect that several million euros have been laundered in a company called Airiston Helmi. In money laundering, attempts are made to hide where the money came from. KRP suspects two foreign men linked to the company of financial crimes.

## Part of the food industry goes on strike

![Sanna Pellilä-Laakso working on the production line at Saarioinen's Sahalahti factory.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7969975)

Open image viewer

Making liver casserole at Saarioinen's Sahalahti factory. Photo: Marjut Suomi / Yle

More than 4,500 food industry workers will go on strike next week. The Food Workers' Union opposes the weakening of job protection.

The government is planning a law that would make it easier to dismiss employees in firms with fewer than 20 employees. The strike is next Wednesday, and 15 food industry workplaces will take part. Among those taking part are workers from Atria, Hartwall, Fazer Confectionery and Saarioinen, among others.

Bakeries, dairies and the poultry industry will operate normally.

## Injuries in the army are expensive

![Conscripts.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9944515)

Open image viewer

Conscripts in Upinniemi. Photo: Ari Lahti / AOP

Accidents happen in the army. The State Treasury, that is, the state's cash office, pays a lot of costs for injuries in the army. Conscripts' injuries and illnesses cause costs of over 7 million euros a year. About 1,000 damages are reported to the State Treasury each year.

The office pays only for damages that require treatment even after military service or that cause incapacity to work. Over 95 percent of applications are accepted.

Common injuries are dislocations and sprains. Serious accidents happen rarely. The army tries to reduce accidents, but the goal is still far away. Major Panu Kekoni from the Defence Command says that even one accident is too many.

## Roads are slippery again

![A mechanic changes a car tyre.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9896120)

Open image viewer

Now it is time to change to winter tyres. Photo: Martti Kainulainen / Lehtikuva

The weather is getting colder, and there is night frost across the whole country. Roads may be slippery across the whole country.

Henri Nyman, chief meteorologist at the Finnish Meteorological Institute, says that the first slippery roads are always a surprise to motorists. That is why the Finnish Meteorological Institute wants to warn about slippery conditions more sensitively than before. Nyman reminds that slipperiness is often local, and that is exactly why it is surprising.
