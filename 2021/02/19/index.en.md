# Friday 19.2.2021 (radio)

In Helsinki and Uusimaa, only 6 participants are allowed at public events. The government proposes mandatory coronavirus tests at Finland's borders. The Estonian prime minister hopes that travel between Finland and Estonia will become easier. It will snow at the weekend.

## Coronavirus restrictions in Uusimaa

Coronavirus restrictions are being tightened in Helsinki and Uusimaa.

The new order is that public events may have at most 6 participants. All larger public events are banned, and it is also good to avoid gatherings of fewer than 6 participants.

The order starts on Monday, and it continues until 14 March.

The reason for the order is the poor coronavirus situation in Helsinki and Uusimaa. There are many new coronavirus infections, and among them is a variant of the coronavirus. The variant spreads easily and quickly.

## Coronavirus tests at the borders

![Russian passengers at passport control at Helsinki-Vantaa airport.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3648,h_2052,x_0,y_302/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-76491760099a90ba29d/1612360928)

Open image viewer

Passengers may soon be ordered to take a coronavirus test. Photo: Jani Saikko / Yle

The government proposes mandatory coronavirus tests at Finland's borders. The mandatory coronavirus tests apply to passengers who come to Finland from abroad.

The government proposes that all passengers can be ordered to take a coronavirus test, for example at airports and ports. If a passenger does not go for a test, they can get a fine or even a prison sentence.

However, a passenger does not have to go for a coronavirus test if they have, for example, a certificate of coronavirus vaccination.

Now coronavirus tests are voluntary for passengers. Coronavirus infections come to Finland from abroad, because not all passengers go for a coronavirus test.

Mandatory coronavirus tests will start in early April, once Parliament has dealt with the matter.

## Estonian prime minister

![Finnish Prime Minister Sanna Marin and Estonian Prime Minister Kaja Kallas.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11799108)

Open image viewer

Estonian Prime Minister Kaja Kallas (on the right) met Finnish Prime Minister Sanna Marin in Helsinki. Photo: Pekka Tynell / Yle

Estonian Prime Minister Kaja Kallas hopes that travel between Finland and Estonia will become easier.

Kallas hopes that Estonians can travel to Finland if they have, for example, a certificate of coronavirus vaccination. Now only Estonians who do necessary work in Finland, for example in health care, can come to Finland.

Prime Minister Kallas visited Finland for the first time.
