# Tuesday 22.11.2016 (radio)

Finns have risen to high positions in the Isis extremist organisation. Unemployment is falling. Alcohol sales are being liberalised. There are many drug drivers on the roads. Dolphins are doing well in Greece.

## A Finn in an important position in Isis

![SUPO report.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9308526)

Open image viewer

The Finnish Security Intelligence Service (Supo) spoke about counter-terrorism in Finland. Photo: Yle

The Finnish Security Intelligence Service says that at least 1 Finn has risen to a high position in the terrorist organisation Isis. However, the head of the Finnish Security Intelligence Service, Supo, Antti Pelttari, does not want to say what position the Finn has held.

The Finnish Security Intelligence Service also confirms that a Finnish citizen has committed a suicide bombing in Iraq. Supo says that this is the first time that a person who has left Finland has committed a suicide bombing.

At least 80 people have left Finland to fight in the ranks of the extremist organisations. The Finnish Security Intelligence Service says that people keep leaving, but the number is growing more slowly than before. The Finnish Security Intelligence Service considers people returning to Finland from crisis areas to be the biggest threat to Finland's security.

## Unemployment is falling

![Joensuu TE Office.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9268345)

Open image viewer

TE Offices help with job searching. Photo: Ismo Pekkarinen / AOP

Unemployment has fallen slightly in Finland. There are now about 329,000 unemployed, that is, people who have no job. That is almost 9,000 fewer than a year ago. More new jobs are also on offer than before.

However, the authorities are worried, because there are many long-term unemployed. A long-term unemployed person is someone who has been without work for more than a year. There are now almost 123,000 of them.

## Alcohol law reform is moving forward

![A man sits at a table with a bottle of beer in front of him.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9164537)

Open image viewer

Restaurants will soon be open for drinking until morning. Photo: Tiina Jutila / Yle

Alcohol sales are clearly being liberalised. The government proposes that restaurants may serve alcohol until 5 am. Shops, kiosks and service stations may sell strong beer, cider and long drinks. However, wine will not be brought into grocery stores. Experts fear that the alcohol reform will increase alcohol problems and alcohol-related illnesses.

## Many drug drivers on the roads

![Breathalyser checkpoint.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8270203)

Open image viewer

The police are monitoring traffic because of drunk drivers. Photo: Antti Seppälä / Yle

Over the weekend the police caught many drunk drivers on the roads. More than 170 drunk drivers, that is, drivers under the influence of alcohol or drugs, were caught. The police are worried that the number of drivers who have used drugs has grown.

Most drunk drivers were caught in the Turku area, in Helsinki and in Ostrobothnia.

The police are monitoring traffic a lot right now, because a lot of alcohol and other intoxicants are used during the Christmas party season.

## Dolphins are doing well in Greece

![Greek dolphins.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9306907)

Open image viewer

The dolphins live in Athens. Photo: Robert Gojceta

The dolphins that left Finland are doing well in Greece. The dolphins were moved from Tampere to Athens in August, which caused a lot of discussion about the dolphins' well-being. The dolphins were moved because Finland's only dolphinarium, at the Särkänniemi amusement park, was closed.

Four dolphins left for Greece, namely Veera, Delfi, Leevi and Eevertti. Trainers say that they have adapted well to the Greek heat and to their new pod. The dolphins also perform in shows for the public in Greece.
