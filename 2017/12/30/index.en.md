# Saturday 30.12.2017 (radio)

Power cuts continue in Eastern Finland. The presidents of Finland and Russia talked on the phone. From now on, New Year's tin must not contain lead. Measles is spreading in Gothenburg.

## Power cuts in Eastern Finland

![Heavy snow situation](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9994426)

Open image viewer

Heavy snow on trees is knocking them down onto power lines. Photo: PKS Sähkönsiirto Oy

In Eastern Finland the power cuts have continued for many days already.

Today, on Saturday, the power has been off for more than 10 000 households. The most power cuts have been in Lieksa, Suomussalmi, Ilomantsi and Hyrynsalmi.

The electricity companies are repairing faults all the time, but new faults are arriving at the same pace. The electricity companies say that it may last past the turn of the year before power is back in all homes.

The power cuts are caused by snow cannon snow. Snow cannon snow is hard snow and hoarfrost that builds up on trees. Snow cannon snow is heavy. It can press trees onto power lines. Branches and treetops can also break under the weight of snow cannon snow.

## The presidents' phone call

![Vladimir Putin and Sauli Niinistö](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10000315)

Russian President Vladimir Putin and Finnish President Sauli Niinistö. Photo: AOP

The presidents of Finland and Russia have talked on the phone. Finnish President Sauli Niinistö called Russian President Vladimir Putin.

Niinistö and Putin spoke on the phone about, for example, relations between Finland and Russia and protecting the Arctic region. The presidents also talked about North Korea and the conflict in Ukraine.

During the call, the presidents also wished each other a happy New Year.

## New Year's tin

![Two tin horseshoes.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5694104)

Open image viewer

Traditional New Year's tin contains a lot of lead. Lead is dangerous to people and the environment. Photo: Yle

Sales of traditional New Year's tin will end in shops after this New Year. Traditional tin may no longer be sold, because it contains a lot of lead.

The EU has classified lead as a substance dangerous to people. That is why the sale of lead to consumers will be banned across the whole EU from March onwards.

Already this year, New Year's tin must have a warning label. The label must warn about lead.

The Safety and Chemicals Agency, Tukes, reminds that lead is also dangerous to the environment. That is why New Year's tin must not be thrown into an ordinary rubbish bin. It must be taken to a hazardous waste collection.

## Measles in Gothenburg

![A nurse holds a vaccination syringe at a child health clinic.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7052178)

Open image viewer

In Finland almost all children are vaccinated against measles. Photo: Yle

In the city of Gothenburg in Sweden, many people have fallen ill with measles.

As many as 10 people have already fallen ill with measles during December. Doctors believe that more people will fall ill, because measles spreads easily.

The symptoms of measles include high fever, runny nose, cough and a rash. Measles can also cause serious complications, such as pneumonia or inflammation of the brain.

In Finland, fewer than 5 people fall ill with measles each year. In Finland almost all people are vaccinated against measles.
