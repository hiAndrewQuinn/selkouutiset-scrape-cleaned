## Sami Virta is the Clear Language Person of the Year

![A man with a certificate of honour and a bouquet of flowers.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11057198)

Sami Virta is the Clear Language Person of the Year. Photo: Kaisa Kaatra / Selkokeskus

The Clear Language Person of the Year has been chosen.

The Clear Language Person of the Year is Sami Virta. Virta received the award in Helsinki.

The Clear Language Person of the Year award is given to a person or organisation that has promoted clear language.

![Happy people with bouquets of flowers and certificates of honour.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11057201)

Open image viewer

Candidates for Clear Language Person of the Year (from left to right): Klaara network, Sami Virta, Heureka Science Centre. The winner was chosen by the Non-Discrimination Ombudsman Kirsi Pimiä (in the middle of the picture). Photo: Kaisa Kaatra / Selkokeskus

This year the Non-Discrimination Ombudsman Kirsi Pimiä chose the Clear Language Person of the Year. There were 3 candidates: Heureka Science Centre, the Klaara network of the University of Helsinki and Sami Virta.

The texts of the exhibitions at the Heureka Science Centre are also often in clear language. The Klaara network of the University of Helsinki has promoted cooperation between clear-language researchers in Finland and abroad.

The winner, Sami Virta, is an expert by experience, that is, he himself needs clear language. Virta has talked about clear language, for example, to students of social and health care.

This is the first time that the Clear Language Person of the Year represents the target group of clear language.

## Grounds for residence permits

![Circuit board being cut with pliers.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6008970)

Open image viewer

The technology industry, for example, needs workers. Photo: YLE / Jussi Kallioinen

Many foreigners apply for residence permits in Finland because of work. The number of applications has grown. Last year, more than 10,000 residence permit applications were made on the basis of work.

At the same time, the number of asylum applications has fallen, because fewer asylum seekers are coming to Finland.

The most common reason for moving to Finland is family. Last year, more than 9000 applicants received a residence permit on the basis of family ties. Many of them were Russians.

## Wärtsilä's redundancies

Wärtsilä is a big company. Photo: Yle/Jukka Tyni

Wärtsilä is cutting employees. Wärtsilä is making 92 employees redundant in Finland. Most of them are in Vaasa.

Wärtsilä makes ship engines and power plants. Wärtsilä operates in 70 countries. In Finland, Wärtsilä has almost 4000 employees.

## Postal strike

![Päivi Hallikainen / postal worker / postal delivery / Pasila 11.10.2019](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5202,h_2926,x_0,y_154/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5993145da093e9b2195/1570804754)

Open image viewer

About 9000 Posti employees will start a strike on Monday.

The postal strike begins.

About 9000 Posti employees will stop work on Monday. The strike is meant to last 2 weeks.

The reason for the strike is a dispute between Posti and its employees, for example about pay.

During the strike, the post does not run normally. Parcels are late by many days, and letters even by many weeks. If a customer sends a letter during the strike, it may only arrive in the next year.

At the weekend there is a big difference between the temperatures in the north and the south.

In Northern Finland there is frost, in Lapland even more than 20 degrees. In Southern Finland it is about 5 degrees warm.
