# Wednesday 3.10.2018 (radio)

Many workplaces have had strikes today. Life is becoming harder for people in debt. The Soputan volcano in Sulawesi, Indonesia, has erupted. The S2 teachers of the year say that digital skills are also important for immigrants.

## Strike day

![Labelling line at the Hartwall drinks manufacturer's Lahti unit, on Tuesday 2 October.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10437497)

A strike lasting a day has stopped, for example, factories and food companies. Photo: Samuli Ikäheimo / Lehtikuva

Workers have been on strike at many workplaces today. The strike lasts one day.

There are tens of thousands of workers on strike across the country. The business sector estimates that the strike may cause losses of tens of millions of euros for firms.

The workers are opposing the government's new bill with the strike. The government wants to make it easier to dismiss employees in small firms.

## People in debt

![A woman agonising over a bill.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10365527)

Open image viewer

The Guarantee Foundation says that Finns have more debt than before. Photo: Ville Välimäki / Yle

The Guarantee Foundation says that life has become harder for people in debt.

The Guarantee Foundation helps and advises people who have difficulty coping with their debts.

The Guarantee Foundation says that its advice service has been busier than ever before. About 10,000 people contacted the foundation in August and September.

The Guarantee Foundation says that Finns have more debt than before. Solutions for making the lives of debtors easier are also harder to find.

## Volcanic eruption

![Volcanic ash cloud.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10436259)

Mount Soputan erupted in Sulawesi, Indonesia. Photo: BNPB / EPA-EFE

On Sulawesi island in Indonesia there has been a volcanic eruption.

The Soputan volcano spewed ash up to a height of 4 kilometres. However, no one is known to have been injured.

At the same time, rescue work continues on Sulawesi after Friday's earthquake and tsunami. More than 1,400 dead have already been found.

## S2 teachers of the year

![Two women standing with flower bouquets and certificates in hand.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10437406)

Open image viewer

Marianne Seppä and Réka Aarnos. Photo: Svetlana Ostapuk

Réka Aarnos and Marianne Seppä have been chosen as S2 teachers of the year. S2 teachers teach Finnish to immigrants.

Réka Aarnos works at Omnia, and Marianne Seppä at Luksia.

Réka Aarnos and Marianne Seppä tell what the best way to learn Finnish is:

Réka: The best way to learn Finnish is to practise a lot yourself. You can learn in class too, but you learn the most at home, when you do the work yourself. Marianne: You learn Finnish by using Finnish. Speaking Finnish in shops, banks and offices.

Réka Aarnos and Marianne Seppä say that, besides the Finnish language, it is important for immigrants to learn digital skills too:

Réka: Nowadays almost everyone can use phones and mobile devices, but in my opinion, in Finland, if you go into working life, you must also be able to use a computer. And you can learn that too by practising.

Marianne: Skills are needed, among other things, when applying for education, in job searching and in dealing with offices.

Can these skills be studied before you speak Finnish well?

Réka: Yes. Definitely. No Finnish language skills are needed for that.

Marianne: Good experiences have also been gained from peer tutoring, where an immigrant who is very skilled in IT helps another in their own language.
