# Wednesday 13.12.2017 (radio)

Most Finns do not want Finland to join the military alliance Nato. Lucia Day was celebrated on Wednesday. Students who speak a foreign language experience discrimination in vocational schools. Greetings from the Finnish schools.

## Support for Nato

![The flags of Finland and Nato](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9973905)

Open image viewer

Finns do not want to join Nato. Photo: Yle News Graphics

Most Finns do not want Finland to join the military alliance Nato.

Yle did a survey on support for Nato. Over 50 percent of those who answered said that Finland should not join Nato.

Support for Nato has fallen in Finland in recent years. 3 years ago, 26 percent of Finns thought that Finland should join Nato. Now only 19 percent of Finns support joining.

Most European countries belong to Nato, but Finland and Sweden do not.

Russia does not want the military alliance Nato to grow.

## Lucia Day

![Anna-Kajsa Edström, crowned as Lucia, in Helsinki on Lucia Day, 13 December 2017.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9975973)

Anna-Kajsa Edström is Finland's Lucia 2017. Photo: Heikki Saukkomaa / Lehtikuva

Lucia Day was celebrated on Wednesday.

Finland's Lucia was crowned in Helsinki Cathedral. She got a crown of candles on her head. Lucia brings light to the dark winter.

This year Finland's Lucia is Anna-Kajsa Edström. She walked from the cathedral through the centre of Helsinki in a big procession.

Lucia is important to Swedish-speaking Finns, that is, Finland Swedes. For example, many Swedish-language schools hold a Lucia celebration.

## Discrimination in schools

![A young woman walks up the stairs.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7953044)

Open image viewer

Discrimination is a serious matter for a student. Photo: Tiina Jutila / Yle

Students who speak a foreign language experience discrimination in vocational schools.

Almost 30 percent of students who speak a foreign language think that other students discriminate against them or bully them. Finnish- and Swedish-speaking students experience less bullying.

Many students who speak a foreign language also feel that teachers bully them. This can even give them a wrong picture of Finland.

The information comes from the Vocational Student Barometer, which almost 10,000 students answered.

## The Finnish schools' video project

![A Happy Birthday Finland card](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9974791)

Open image viewer

Greetings from Toronto! Toronto has the oldest Finnish school that is still running. Photo: Yle

This year, Finnish schools have made congratulation videos for 100-year-old Finland.

The pupils of the Finnish schools are Finnish children and young people who live abroad. In the Finnish schools they study the Finnish language and culture.

Suomi-Seura (the Finland Society) coordinates the work of the Finnish schools, and coordinator Maarit Hyvärinen is pleased with the video project.

We got a lot of well-made videos from the Finnish schools, Hyvärinen says.

The last congratulation video comes from **the Finnish school in Toronto**. It is the oldest Finnish school that is still running.

The videos from Toronto and the other Finnish schools can be found at yle.fi/selkouutiset, on the **Finland 100** page.
