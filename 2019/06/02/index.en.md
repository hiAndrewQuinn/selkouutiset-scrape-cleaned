# Sunday 2.6.2019 (radio)

The party leaders have negotiated the ministerial posts. Young people have celebrated the end of school calmly. Finns like salmiakki.

## Ministerial posts

Antti Rinne is the next prime minister. Soon it will be clear who else will join the new government. Photo: Prime Minister's Office

The party leaders have negotiated the ministerial posts. The parties must agree which ministerial jobs the different parties get in the new government.

Finland's government is changing, because Finland held parliamentary elections in April. The party leaders have negotiated the new government in the government negotiations.

5 parties are taking part in the government negotiations. They have already agreed on the government programme. The government programme says what the new government intends to do.

The party leaders will tell about the government programme on Monday. Information about how the parties will share the ministerial posts in the government is also expected soon.

It is already known that the post of prime minister goes to the SDP. The SDP is the largest party in Parliament.

The next prime minister is Antti Rinne. Rinne is the SDP party leader.

Young people have celebrated the end of school calmly. The rainy weather also calmed the outdoor celebrations.

The police say that, for example, the centre of Helsinki was calm on Saturday evening. The Helsinki police were pleased and praised young people on Twitter.

Other cities were also fairly calm. For example, in Tampere young people celebrated at Pyynikki, but nothing serious happened.

## Salmiakki

![Turkish peppers](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10714200)

Open image viewer

Salmiakki has also been made in factories for about 90 years. Photo: Petri Kivimäki/Yle

The Finns' favourite sweet is salmiakki.

A salmiakki sweet is sweet and salty at the same time. And because Finns like salmiakki, salmiakki ice cream and salmiakki yogurt are also made in Finland.

Earlier, salmiakki was used in medicines. Then pharmacies began to sell salmiakki as a treat too. Sweet factories have been making salmiakki for about 90 years.

Many people think salmiakki is good for health. It helps, for example, when your throat is sore.

However, salmiakki also has bad effects. Salmiakki, for example, raises blood pressure.

Doctors also say that women should not eat salmiakki during pregnancy. Children, for example, have more psychiatric problems if their mothers ate a lot of salmiakki during pregnancy.

Finns also sell salmiakki abroad. The Netherlands, Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Iceland and Germany are countries where salmiakki is eaten. Elsewhere salmiakki is not known.
