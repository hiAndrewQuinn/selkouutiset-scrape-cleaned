# Thursday 17.1.2019 (radio)

Pension companies want more immigration. The future of many Finnish companies is uncertain because of Britain's EU exit. 3 victims of an avalanche have been found in Norway. Second-graders in Hämeenlinna are learning a little English, Swedish and German. Puupää hat goes to Tiina Paju and Sari Luhtanen.

## Pension companies want more immigration

![A baby with a name tag on its wrist](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10575219)

Open image viewer

Pension companies hope for more babies or more immigration. Photo: AOP

The pension companies want Finland's immigration to double.

The directors of the pension companies say that immigration must be increased, because very few children are born in Finland. When more immigrants come, there are more people in Finland who work and pay pension contributions.

About 17,000 more people move to Finland in a year than move away from here.

## Britain's EU exit may raise prices in Finland

![Demonstration in front of the Parliament building in London.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10545726)

Open image viewer

Some British people oppose leaving the EU. Photo: Ben Stansall / AFP

The future of many Finnish companies is uncertain if Britain leaves the EU without an agreement.

Thousands of Finnish companies sell goods to Britain or import them from Britain to Finland. If customs duties have to be paid on the goods, the prices of the goods will rise. For example, the price of a car made in Britain may rise by about 10 percent. The prices of goods ordered from British online shops will also rise.

Britain leaves the EU on 29 March.

## One of the avalanche victims is still missing

![A helicopter in the sky above Tamokdalen.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10601086)

Open image viewer

A helicopter searched for the missing people from the fell. Photo: Gustaf Antell / Yle

In Norway, three of the people who were buried by an avalanche 2 weeks ago have been found.

Today the bodies of two victims were brought down from the fell. One victim is still missing. A Swedish woman and three Finnish men were buried by an avalanche when they were skiing on the fell.

The search has lasted two weeks because the weather in the area has been bad. There was also a danger that the searchers would be buried under new avalanches.

## Second-graders are learning languages

![Second-graders at the German lesson at Ruununmylly school, language spraying lesson](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10599664)

Second-graders from Hämeenlinna in a German lesson. Photo: Kati Turtola / Yle

In Hämeenlinna, all second-grade pupils are being taught foreign languages this spring.

The children are taught at least some German, Swedish and English. In one school the children also try Estonian, Spanish and Russian. The teachers say that in second grade the children learn everything easily.

Next autumn, children will start studying a foreign language already in the first grade in all Finnish schools.

## A comic about middle-aged women won an award

![The annual main domestic comics prize, the Puupää hat, was awarded for a lifetime's work to illustrator Tiina Paju (right) and illustrator Sari Luhtanen at the award ceremony in Helsinki on 17 January.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10600943)

Sari Luhtanen (left) and Tiina Paju.  Photo: Antti Aimo-Koivisto / Lehtikuva

The Puupää hat, the comics prize, goes to Tiina Paju and Sari Luhtanen this year.

Paju and Luhtanen made a comic called Maisa and Kaarina for almost 30 years. The comic was published in the Anna magazine. The Maisa and Kaarina comic was about middle-aged women. Maisa was a relaxed artistic type and Kaarina was a busy stay-at-home mother.

Tiina Paju and Sari Luhtanen made the comic until 2016.
