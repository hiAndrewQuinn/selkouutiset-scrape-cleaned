Parliament's alcohol debate. Threshing grain. A centre of excellence in economics. Employment of people over 55. Physical punishment of children.

## Parliament debates alcohol

![Beer bottles on a shelf in Alko.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9644894)

Open image viewer

Strong beers can be bought from Alko. The government proposes that in the future they can also be bought in grocery shops. Photo: Ismo Pekkarinen / AOP

There has been an argument in Parliament today about the new alcohol law.

Politicians argued about how strong an alcoholic drink can be sold in grocery shops.

The government proposes that grocery shops can sell drinks that contain at most 5.5 percent alcohol.

Now beers, ciders and other drinks in shops may contain at most 4.7 percent alcohol.

MPs can vote on raising the percentage limit according to their conscience.

That means that an MP does not have to vote the same way as the rest of the party.

The vote will probably be in November.

## The harvest is late

![Winter wheat](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9808053)

Open image viewer

The winter wheat is in danger of spoiling in the field. Photo: Mikko Savolainen / Yle

On farms the harvest is still badly unfinished.

It is difficult to bring in the grain because of the bad weather.

Farmers may have losses of up to 200 million euros, because a large part of the spring grains will not be harvested.

The grain is also in bad condition, and it has to be dried for a long time. Drying costs a lot of money.

The harvest work can be done this autumn if the weather stays warm and dry for another month.

## A centre of excellence in economics for Finland

![Minister of Education Sanni Grahn-Laasonen, Prime Minister Juha Sipilä and Professor Bengt Holmström at a press conference on 19 September.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9840166)

Open image viewer

The centre of excellence in economics was presented by Minister of Education Sanni Grahn-Laasonen, Prime Minister Juha Sipilä and Nobel Prize winner in economics Bengt Holmström. Photo: Jarno Kuusinen / AOP

A new centre of excellence in economics will be set up in Finland, says Prime Minister Juha Sipilä.

Important economic research will be done at the centre of excellence.

According to Sipilä, Finland needs more economic expertise. Politicians also need more information about economic matters that is based on research.

The centre of excellence may get as many as 15 new professors.

The name of the centre of excellence is Helsinki Graduate School of Economics.

## More people over 55 are working

![A picture of hands.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7485711)

Open image viewer

Older workers are also wanted for expert jobs. Photo: Jukka-Pekka Tyhtilä / Yle

Working has increased among people over 55.

More and more people over 55 are now working.

Over half of 60–64-year-olds are working.

There is work for them especially in shops, construction, care work and expert jobs.

## Physical punishment of children is not accepted

![a person pulls a child's hair](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9090183)

Open image viewer

Many parents have sometimes pulled a child's hair, even though it is not allowed. Photo: Yle

Most Finns do not accept physical punishment of a child, for example spanking or pulling hair.

Almost all Finns also know that physical punishment of children is forbidden by law.

Still, about 25 percent of Finns said in a survey that they have sometimes pulled a child's hair.

13 percent of Finns said that they accept physical punishment in some situations.

The survey was done by the Central Union for Child Welfare.
