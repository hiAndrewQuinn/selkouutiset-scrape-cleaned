## Children are being born to Finns in ISIS

![A mural showing the victories of ISIS in the city of Mosul in Iraq.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9449167)

Open image viewer

Children may become violent when they grow up in the violent environment of a terrorist organisation. Photo: Osie Greenway / AOP

The authorities are worried about the children of Finns who have joined the terrorist organisation ISIS.

About 20 Finnish women have joined ISIS. They have given birth to more than 10 children.

The Finnish authorities are thinking about how the children can be helped if they come to Finland.
Children may become violent when they grow up in the violent environment of a terrorist organisation.

## Money obtained by fraud with an asylum application

![money](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9974093)

Open image viewer

The man received the reception allowance meant for asylum seekers for more than a year. Photo: Yle News Graphics

It has come to light in Finland that money has been obtained by fraud with an asylum application. The asylum seeker was a man who got Finnish citizenship more than 20 years ago. Even so, he applied for asylum in Kotka more than a year ago. The man received the reception allowance meant for asylum seekers for more than a year before he was caught.

The man was born in Iraq. The man is suspected of forging an asylum application and of fraud.

## Crowdfunding is becoming more popular

![The computing power of the computer unit in Nvidia's basement in Ruoholahti is the biggest in Finland.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10116886)

Open image viewer

The investor hopes that the company will be successful. Photo: Tanja Heino / Yle

Crowdfunding has quickly become more popular. Many new companies get money from people.

A small investor can buy a share in a new company. The investor hopes that the company will be successful and later bring the investor money. The amount of crowdfunding in growth companies more than doubled last year.

## Merja Ylä-Anttila is Yle's new CEO

![Merja Ylä-Anttila](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10163214)

Open image viewer

Merja Ylä-Anttila will become Yle's CEO in September. Photo: Jyrki Lyytikkä / Yle

Merja Ylä-Anttila has been chosen as the new CEO of Yle, the Finnish Broadcasting Company. She has long been the editor-in-chief of MTV's news. Merja Ylä-Anttila says that Finland needs both a strong public service and commercial media.

Ylä-Anttila will become CEO of Yle in September. Lauri Kivinen will leave the post then.

## Finland's oldest funfair is 130 years old

![Tivoli Sariola](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10161983)

Open image viewer

Ville Sariola is the new CEO of Tivoli Sariola. Photo: Janne Ahjopalo / Yle

Funfairs have set off touring Finland now that winter has ended. Four funfairs tour Finland. Tivoli Sariola is the oldest. It has been running for 130 years already. The funfairs are small. They come to many towns, close to people.

The funfair workers say that they always think it is nice when someone comes to say that the funfair is really fun.
