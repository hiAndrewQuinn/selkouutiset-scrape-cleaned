## The Vastaamo data breach is a major crime

![Logo of the Vastaamo psychotherapy centre.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_6000,h_3375,x_0,y_356/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-7324365f8ff124be19a/1603562108)

Vastaamo is a big company that offers psychotherapy services. Photo: Petteri Bülow / Yle

In recent days a lot has been said about the company called Vastaamo, which provides psychotherapy services.

Vastaamo has become the target of a data breach.

Someone has broken into Vastaamo's customer data and is now blackmailing the company.

An unknown blackmailer has published online the health data and personal identity codes of at least 300 customers. The blackmailer is demanding bitcoins, i.e. digital currency, from Vastaamo.

The blackmailer has also sent blackmail messages directly to customers and demands bitcoins from them.

The psychotherapy centre Vastaamo operates all over Finland. The blackmailer claims that he has the data of 40,000 people.

## The National Bureau of Investigation is investigating the data breach

![Head of the National Bureau of Investigation (KRP) Robin Lardot (left), KRP detective inspector Marko Leponen, KRP detective chief superintendent Tero Muurman and Leena-Kaisa Åberg, managing director of the Crime Victim Support organisation RIKU, at the KRP press conference at the KRP building in Vantaa on Sunday 25 October 2020.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11612544)

The National Bureau of Investigation (KRP) announced a major data breach on Sunday. Photo: Heikki Saukkomaa / Lehtikuva

The National Bureau of Investigation says that a large group of police officers is investigating the Vastaamo data breach.

The police have already received thousands of crime reports from Vastaamo's customers. The police say that the data may concern as many as tens of thousands of people.

This data breach is unusual even internationally, because there are so many victims of it.

The police do not yet know whether the blackmailer is Finnish or from somewhere else.

The National Bureau of Investigation says that no one should under any circumstances agree to the blackmailer's demands.

The police also remind everyone that if you come across someone's health or personal data online, you must not read it.

## Crime Victim Support helps victims of the data breach

![Crime Victim Support material.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7810543)

Open image viewer

Crime Victim Support's duty staff help by phone. Photo: Risto Koskinen / Yle

If you suspect that your personal data has been stolen online, or you have fallen victim to an online blackmailer, make a crime report.

The easiest way to make a crime report is on the police website.

Also contact, for example, Crime Victim Support. Crime Victim Support tells you what you must do, and you can talk about the matter with a duty worker.
