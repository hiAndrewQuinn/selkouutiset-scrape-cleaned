Industrial action against weakening protection from dismissal is spreading. The Ministry of the Interior has suspended the head of the National Bureau of Investigation, Robin Lardot, from office. This week the police are monitoring mobile phone use in traffic.

## Industrial action against weakening protection from dismissal

![A corridor in Meilahti hospital.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10287823)

Open image viewer

Now the care sector is also fighting against weakening protection from dismissal. Photo: Kalevi Rytkölä / Yle

Industrial action against weakening protection from dismissal is spreading.

The Union of Practical Nurses Super and the health and social care trade union Tehy will start 3 days of industrial action next week. The unions will start an overtime ban and a ban on swapping shifts. Today the Industrial Union, the trade union Pro and the Finnish Transport Workers' Union AKT started overtime bans.

Behind the dispute is the government's plan to weaken protection from dismissal in companies with fewer than 20 employees. Then it is easier to dismiss a worker from their job.

## Head of the NBI suspended from office

![Robin Lardot](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10347641)

Open image viewer

Robin Lardot has been suspended from the office of head of the NBI. Photo: Markku Ojala / EPA

The Ministry of the Interior has suspended the head of the National Bureau of Investigation, Robin Lardot, from office.

In mid-August Lardot was charged with misconduct in office. The Ministry of the Interior says that Lardot cannot work while the charge is being dealt with.

The charge concerns the years 2010 - 2013. Lardot was then police director at the National Police Board and head of the crime prevention unit. The charge says that Lardot did not act on wrongdoings at the Helsinki Police Department. However, Lardot knew that the drug crime unit did not record its informants in the register.

## A huge amount of snus has been smuggled from Sweden to Finland

![Snus tins in a cardboard box.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10407919)

Open image viewer

Selling snus is banned in all member states of the European Union except Sweden, which negotiated an exemption in its EU accession treaty. In Finland selling snus was banned in 1995. Photo: Customs

Customs has investigated large-scale smuggling of snus from Sweden to Finland.

About 13,000 kilos of snus have been smuggled over the last 2 years. Almost 5 million euros in taxes have been avoided.

The main suspect is a 50-year-old Finnish citizen who lives in Haparanda in Sweden. Customs suspects that the main suspect has gained as much as 80,000 euros through criminal activity. In total 8 people are suspected of the smuggling

## Strength study of bridges is complete

![A concrete bridge photographed from below](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10408048)

Open image viewer

The study was started because of quality problems found in concrete. Photo: Roni Rekomaa / Lehtikuva

No serious faults have been found in Finland's concrete bridges.

The Finnish Transport Agency says that strength defects were found in only one concrete bridge in the large study. The bridge is the Kuivajoentie underpass bridge in Ii on the Oulu - Kemi railway. The bridge did not meet the strength requirements but is still safe.

The study was done because some of the concrete in Finland has been poor. The study included 90 busy road bridges and railway bridges.

## Police intensive monitoring in traffic

![A police officer at the side door of a police car. ](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8743207)

Open image viewer

In the intensive monitoring the police are also watching seatbelt use. Photo: Juha Kemppainen / Yle

This week, in intensive monitoring, the police are watching for example mobile phone use in traffic.

The European Commission estimates that 10 - 30 percent of traffic accidents are because the driver was not paying attention. The police stress that you must not hold a mobile phone in your hand while driving, even when the car is stopped, for example at traffic lights.
