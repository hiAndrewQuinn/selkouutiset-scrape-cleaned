# Friday 21.10.2016 (radio)

The activities of aid worker Rami Adham are being investigated. EU leaders demand that the ceasefire in Syria continue. Hospitals need blood. Finnish films are not successful in Europe.

## The police are investigating a charity worker

![Rami Adham.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9209874)

Open image viewer

Rami Adham Photo: Yle

The National Bureau of Investigation is investigating the activities of aid worker Rami Adham. Adham is known for taking toys to Syria.

The police are now looking into whether Adham's collection of money for his activities has been legal. Some of the donors suspect that not all the money has been used to help the children of Syria.

The police are also looking into whether Adham has written hate posts on the internet.

The police inquiries will take a few days. After that it will be decided whether an actual criminal investigation into the fundraising and the hate posts will be started.

Adham himself has denied all the accusations.

The Helsinki police have asked the Finland-Syria community to stop the fundraising.

## EU leaders talked about Syria

![The president of France, Francois Hollande.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9243052)

Open image viewer

The EU summit is in Brussels.  Photo: Yoan Valat / EPA

Europe's leaders demand that the Syrian ceasefire continue. The EU countries are ready to use all means if the military operations in Syria continue. However, at the summit of EU countries no decision was made, for example, on new sanctions against Russia.

Russia has promised to stop the bombing of Aleppo for 4 days so that aid can be brought into the city. The UN has not yet got the aid convoys moving. The UN says that it has not got certainty about the safety of the convoys.

## Many blood donors are needed

![A blood donation trolley in Kajaani.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9223041)

Open image viewer

You can donate blood at the Red Cross premises and events.  Photo: Terhi Marjakangas / Yle

In Finland a thousand voluntary blood donors are needed almost every day so that there is enough blood for patients in hospitals.

In Finland you can donate blood, for example, when the blood service comes to visit your home municipality. Then a blood donation event is organised, for example, in a sports hall or a school.

However, you may not donate blood, for example, if the blood is not healthy enough, that is, if the person has some disease in their blood. Medication can also prevent blood donation.

## Finnish films rarely succeed abroad

![A picture from the film The Happiest Day in the Life of Olli Mäki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9141253)

The film The Happiest Day in the Life of Olli Mäki tells about the boxer Olli Mäki.  Photo: Yle

Finnish films do not do very well outside Finland. In 20 years only a few Finnish films have attracted a good audience in Europe.

Only 4 films have got more than a million viewers. Children's films have done best. Niko & the Way to the Stars and its sequel Little Brother, Big Trouble: A Christmas Adventure both attracted more than 2 million viewers in Europe.

At the moment success is hoped for the film The Happiest Day in the Life of Olli Mäki, which tells the story of the boxer Olli Mäki. It won the second most important competition at the Cannes Film Festival. The film will be shown in more than 40 cinemas in France alone this autumn.
