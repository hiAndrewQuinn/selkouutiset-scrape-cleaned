The economic development in Finland looks bad for the end of the year, estimates the financial company OP.

OP estimates that Finland's economy will slide into a mild recession during the next 12 months. We speak of a recession when the economy shrinks for at least half a year.

According to the OP group, industry and construction are in steep decline. Finns' real wages are improving, but employment development is weakening,

## Jukkatalo

![Jukkatalo office in Vantaa. Jukkatalo text logo on the wall.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4852,h_2729,x_0,y_468/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-116367764ec9cd8b28eb/1693228654)

Open image viewer

Jukkatalo has been one of Finland's largest small-house manufacturers. Photo: Petteri Bülow / Yle

Small-house manufacturer Jukkatalo has laid off all its employees. Jukkatalo laid off about 200 people.

Jukkatalo filed for bankruptcy yesterday.

Jukkatalo got into trouble because costs have risen. In addition, fewer houses have been ordered than before.

Jukkatalo has been one of Finland's largest small-house manufacturers. At its peak, the company sold over 500 house packages a year.

## Terror threat

![A person dressed in white stands with one foot on the Swedish flag and the other on the Danish flag.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5291,h_2976,x_14,y_216/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-114966764c67915a2ae1/1691503203)

In January, a demonstration was held in Jakarta, Indonesia, protesting the burning of the Quran in Sweden and Denmark. Photo: Mast Irham / EPA

The Security Police says that Finland does not have the same terror threat as Sweden.

The Security Police says that the terror threat in Finland is at level two on a four-level scale.

In Sweden, the security police has raised the threat level to the second highest level. In Sweden, the terror threat has been increased by several demonstrations in which the Quran was burned.

## Monday's heavy rain

![Water floods the streets leading to a car park in Merihaka.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3024,h_1701,x_0,y_1181/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-116366064ec96824ebe4/1693226763)

Open image viewer

Heavy rain brought water into a car park in Merihaka, Helsinki. Photo: Mikko Sauli

Yesterday's heavy rain and strong wind caused damage in various parts of the country.

Strong and gusty wind broke trees onto power lines, houses and cars. At most, just under 20 000 households were without electricity.

Heavy rain caused floods on roads and in the basements of houses. The most rain fell in Tampere, over 65 millimetres.
