# Sunday 19.12.2021 (radio)

The government is ready to tighten corona restrictions, says Prime Minister Marin. Corona vaccinations for children in risk groups have started. The Finnish-grown forest spruce is the Finns' favourite Christmas tree. Last night the last full moon of the year rose into the sky. The weather is getting colder.

## Corona restrictions may be tightened

![Prime Minister Sanna Marin at a press conference on 8 December.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12220960)

Prime Minister Sanna Marin. Photo: Markku Ulander / Lehtikuva

Prime Minister Sanna Marin says that corona restrictions in Finland may be tightened.

The government will negotiate about corona measures on Tuesday. Marin said on the Prime Minister's interview hour on Radio Suomi that the government is ready for all necessary measures.

Marin said that, for example, rapid corona testing can be expanded, the corona pass can be temporarily taken out of use, or the school Christmas holidays can be lengthened.

The corona situation in Finland has got worse quickly in recent weeks. There are many new infections, and there are many corona patients in hospitals and in intensive care.

## Corona vaccinations expanded

Corona vaccinations have started for children in risk groups in Finland. Children aged 5 to 11 who belong to a risk group receive the vaccine.

For example, in Jätkäsaari in Helsinki the first vaccination point for children in risk groups was opened on Saturday. There was no crowd, but customers kept coming all the time.

Now only children in risk groups receive the vaccine. No decision has yet been made on vaccinations for all 5 to 11-year-old children.

## The Finnish-grown spruce is the most popular

![Christmas tree seller Veikko Lamminaho shows a Christmas tree at Tammela market square in the early morning twilight.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3000,h_1687,x_0,y_75/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-89349761bc511e28691/1639731879)

Open image viewer

Veikko Lamminaho sells Christmas trees at Tammela market square in Tampere. Photo: Miikka Varila / Yle

Many Finnish homes will also get a Christmas tree this year. The Christmas Tree Association estimates that this Christmas Finns will get about 1.4 million Christmas trees in total.

The most common Christmas tree is the domestic farmed forest spruce. About one million of them are sold every year.

Some people fetch a wild spruce home, for example straight from their own forest. The number of wild spruces is not known exactly.

10 per cent of Christmas trees come from abroad. They come mainly from Denmark and Estonia.

## The cold moon climbed into the sky

![The full moon is visible above the Cathedral of the Nativity of Christ in Riga, Latvia.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5526,h_3099,x_0,y_116/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-89407461bed3c9d73d9/1639896269)

Open image viewer

The full moon above the Cathedral of the Holy Trinity in Riga, Latvia. Photo: Toms Kalnins / EPA

Last night the full moon lit up the sky.

The US space agency NASA says that the last full moon of the year has many names. For example, it is called the cold moon, the frost moon or the winter moon. The names come from the fact that here in the northern hemisphere the nights in December are long and cold.

Because of the long night, last night's full moon was also the longest of this year. In Washington in the USA, for example, the moon was visible in the sky for about 15 and a half hours.
