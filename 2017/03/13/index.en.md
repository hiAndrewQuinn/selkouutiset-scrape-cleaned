# Monday 13.3.2017 (radio)

Jussi Halla-aho is trying to become leader of the Finns Party. Scotland may hold a new referendum on independence. A memorial celebration of Finland's Winter War has been held in Stockholm. Finland is discussing the pay gap between women and men. The polar bear cub at Ranua Zoo may come out of the den on Wednesday.

## The Finns Party

![Jussi Halla-Aho](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9426539)

Jussi Halla-aho. Photo: Petteri Paalasmaa / AOP

Jussi Halla-aho is trying to become leader of the Finns Party. Member of the European Parliament Halla-aho announced it on Monday.

Halla-aho said that Finns Party supporters are nationalists. That is why the party must also defend the interests of Finns. Halla-aho did not say whether the Finns Party will stay in the Finnish government if he is chosen as party leader.

There are also three other candidates for leader of the Finns Party. The new party leader will be chosen in summer. The current leader Timo Soini no longer wants to continue as party leader.

## Scotland

Scotland may hold a new referendum on independence.

Scotland is part of Britain, but Britain is leaving the European Union and most Scots want to stay in the union. That is why Scotland is thinking about whether it should become independent.

Scotland had a referendum on independence about 2 years ago. Then 55 percent of Scots wanted to stay part of Britain.

## Memorial celebration

![ President of the Republic Sauli Niinistö and Crown Princess Victoria and Prince Carl Philip in a park.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9507291)

Finnish President Sauli Niinistö and Swedish Crown Princess Victoria.  Photo: Office of the President of the Republic / Lehtikuva

President Sauli Niinistö is taking part today, Monday, in a Winter War memorial celebration in Stockholm, Sweden.

The memorial celebration is held in honour of the Swedes who fought on Finland's side in the war against the Soviet Union. In the wars of the 1930s and 1940s there were about 10,000 Swedish volunteers in the Finnish army.

Swedish Crown Princess Victoria and Prince Carl Philip are also taking part in the Stockholm memorial celebration.

## The pay gap

![A nurse beside the bed of a resident of a service home.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9128719)

Open image viewer

Women are often in fields with low pay. Photo: Ere Ek / Yle

Finland is discussing the pay gap between women and men.

In Finland women earn less than men. When men earn a euro, women earn 83 cents.

The pay gap between women and men has narrowed, but the good development has stopped. The pay gap is no longer narrowing.

There are many reasons why women earn less than men. Women are often in fields where pay is low. Women also take more family leave, and they have fewer working years than men. Men have more time to advance to well-paid jobs.
