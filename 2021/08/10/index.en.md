A new school year starts this week in basic schools.

Most schools start the autumn term on Wednesday. For example, in the Helsinki region the school year starts on Wednesday for most schools.

In some municipalities, for example in Tampere, the autumn term already started today, on Tuesday. In a few schools the autumn term starts only next Monday.

## Children's road safety has improved

![Two children crossing a pedestrian crossing. A car is driving in the background.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12052896)

Open image viewer

Traffic is dangerous for children. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

Children's road safety has improved. Fewer 7–12-year-old children are injured in traffic accidents than before. Injuries to children have fallen by 50 percent in 10 years.

Road safety education for children has been increased. In cities, speed limits have been lowered.

Still, about 150 primary school-age children are injured in traffic accidents every year. Experts say that road safety in cities must be improved further.

## Corona infections

![Test tubes in a nurse's hand.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4718,h_2654,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-83394360f7d87833552/1627907432)

Open image viewer

Corona infections have grown a lot in 2 weeks. Photo: Kalle Niskala / Yle

Today, on Tuesday, many new corona infections have again been found in Finland. There are 781 infections. Most new infections are in Southern Finland, but in Northern Finland too, infections have grown quickly.

In the last 2 weeks there have been about 4,400 more corona infections in Finland than in the previous 2 weeks.

Corona vaccinations are progressing. About 39 percent of Finns have already received both of the 2 vaccine doses. 67 percent of Finns have received the first vaccine dose.

## Grain growth ahead of schedule

![Harvesting grain in a wheat field in Liperi at the beginning of September.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11524181)

Open image viewer

A combine harvester gathers, that is, threshes, grain in the field. Photo: Marja-Liisa Kämppi / Yle

A warm and dry summer has brought forward the threshing of cereals. The cereals have grown about 2 weeks faster than usual in many areas.

Drought has hindered the growth of some cereals. Barley and oats have suffered the most from drought. Their harvest will be smaller than usual.

Rye and winter wheat have grown well. The rye and winter wheat harvest will be good.
