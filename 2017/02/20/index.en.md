# Monday 20.2.2017 (radio)

The Sastamala crash was a suicide attempt. The Finnish Red Cross is sending help to Iraq. Many people with a good education are moving abroad from Finland. 100-year-old Finland gets congratulations from Finnish school pupils in Dubai.

## The Sastamala crash

![The accident site in Sastamala](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9469628)

Open image viewer

People have brought candles to the crash site where two young people died. Photo: Matias Väänänen / Yle

The police have given new information about the Sastamala crash. The police say that the cause of the crash was a suicide attempt.

The crash happened in Sastamala on Saturday. Two 17-year-olds who were driving a moped car died in it. They died when a bigger car crashed into their small moped car.

The driver of the bigger car crashed into the moped car on purpose, because he wanted to kill himself. But the suicide did not succeed, because the driver drove into a small moped car. The police say that the driver did not notice that the other car was a moped car.

## Help to Iraq

![The Finnish Red Cross has tent clinics in two refugee camps in northern Greece.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9392407)

Open image viewer

The Finnish Red Cross is used to taking help abroad. Photo: Jessica Stolzmann / Yle

The aid organisation SPR, or the Finnish Red Cross, is sending help to Iraq. The Red Cross is sending 4 nurses and medical equipment to Iraq.

Help is needed in Iraq because there is fighting in the city of Mosul. So many people have been injured in the fighting that the hospitals in the area cannot treat everyone.

The Iraqi army is fighting against the terrorist organisation Isis in Mosul. The Red Cross says that tens of thousands of people have been injured in the fighting.

## People moving abroad

![The Åbo Akademi laboratory aims for cleaner combustion, presents professor Mikko Hupa.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5497438)

Rector Mikko Hupa is worried that Finnish researchers are moving abroad. Photo: YLE / Maria Puhakka

Many people with a good education are moving abroad from Finland. Many researchers in Finland have been left without work, because the state gives universities less money than before.

Finnish researchers have moved to Sweden, for example. In Sweden there is more work for researchers than in Finland.

Mikko Hupa, rector of the Turku university Åbo Akademi, hopes that the good level of Finnish research will remain. That is why he hopes that at least some of the Finnish researchers will come back to Finland from abroad. In addition, Finland needs foreign researchers, rector Hupa says.

## Greetings from Dubai

![A child in a Finland shirt being interviewed in front of the Finnish flag. ](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9468699)

Open image viewer

A pupil of the Finnish school in Dubai. Photo: Yle

A large number of Finnish children and young people live in different parts of the world and study the Finnish language and Finnish culture in Finnish schools.

Now that independent Finland turns 100, the Finnish schools are sending greetings to Finland on video.

The latest greetings come from the Finnish school in Dubai. The school has 110 pupils. The youngest are small children, the oldest are 12 years old. They go to the Finnish school once a week.

You can watch the greetings from the Dubai Finnish school on the Clear News website. The address is yle.fi/selkouutiset.
