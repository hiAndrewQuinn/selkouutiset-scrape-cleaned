## Damage from the Paula storm

![Damage from the Paula storm in a forest in Taivalkoski.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_960,h_540,x_0,y_49/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-82733160dd66167ddeb/1625123059)

Open image viewer

The Paula storm was the worst storm in Northern Finland in 40 years. Photo: Anu Suomalainen

The traces of the Paula storm in June are being studied from a helicopter in Northern Finland. The last forest destruction like this in Northern Finland was about 40 years ago.

Metsähallitus, the state forest enterprise, assesses the damage from a helicopter in the forests of Koillismaa, Kainuu and North Ostrobothnia.

The state wants to put the trees on state land to commercial use. The storm mostly knocked down pine trees the size of sawlogs. Some of the sawlog wood ends up in a pulp mill.

Some of the trees in the damaged area may be left in the area. Then dead wood will come into commercial forests, which increases biodiversity, that is, the diversity of nature.

## Passport queues

There are queues at the police licence services at least in North Ostrobothnia, Kainuu and Eastern Finland.

Applying for passports and identity cards is busy, because travel restrictions have been reduced.

The approaching hunting season and the many public events after the break also overload the police licence services.

## Customs on cheap goods

![Aleksi Laikari, customs officer, Vantaa.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5472,h_3078,x_0,y_282/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-82981260e6b04878603/1699613658)

Open image viewer

Customs and Posti avoided queues. Photo: Markku Rantala / Yle

The change in the law on international online shopping has not brought a big parcel chaos to Finland.

Even small parcels from outside the EU must now go through customs. VAT must also be paid on them. Previously cheap goods could be ordered tax-free.

Posti and Customs prepared for queues, but online shops reacted to the reform faster than expected.

## Approved mosquito repellents

![Against mosquitoes, with electricity or smoke?](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5582122)

Open image viewer

In mosquito repellents only approved substances and products may be used. This can be checked on the product packaging. Photo: Jari Vesa / YLE

In fighting mosquitoes only products that have been approved may be used. Customs and the Safety and Chemicals Agency Tukes remind of this.

During the early summer Customs has taken over about 4,000 pesticide pillows whose use is banned in Finland. The pillows have come with postal shipments and air freight.

Mosquito repellents and mosquito poisons require approval from Tukes in Finland. An approved product can be recognised from Tukes' licence number.

In addition, the packaging must have instructions in Finnish and Swedish.

## Health benefits of walking

![Mikkeli walking street.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3008,h_1692,x_0,y_310/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-81610160ba276cc78d1/1622812673)

Open image viewer

Walking brings health benefits and costs nothing. The aim is that short car trips are replaced by walking. Photo: Juho Liukkonen / Yle

In Finland they want to promote walking. The aim is to increase the popularity of walking and cycling by 30 percent by 2030.

A similar goal is in the national energy and climate strategy.

The transport authority Traficom grants state aid to municipalities for promoting walking and cycling. Last year aid was granted to many projects, for example in Helsinki, Joensuu, Jyväskylä and Tampere.

Increasing walking can produce health benefits worth a total of 3.3 billion euros.
