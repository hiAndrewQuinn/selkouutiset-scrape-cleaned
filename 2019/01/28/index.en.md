# Monday 28.1.2019 (radio)

Problems continue at Esperi Care's care homes. The chair of the trade union SuPer demands that the government must get elderly care in order. Finland is the only European country where the number of homeless people is falling. Snowfall is hindering traffic.

## Esperi Care's problems continue

![Orange buildings of a care home.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10618197)

Open image viewer

Esperi Care has many care homes. Photo: Miina Sillanpää / Yle

Problems continue at Esperi Care's care homes. Esperi Care is a company that has care homes in different parts of Finland.

Last week the authorities intervened in the operations of an Esperi Care care home in Kristinestad, in Western Finland. The authorities said that the care of the elderly in the care home was poor.

A Yle investigation says that the authorities have demanded that Esperi Care fix problems in almost 20 care homes. The care homes are in 14 different towns. Esperi Care has problems at Tilkka Care Services Centre in Helsinki, for example.

Esperi Care's biggest problem is that it has too few employees.

## The trade union demands change

![An elderly person and a carer.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10401809)

Open image viewer

The trade union reminds us that there must be enough carers. Photo: Mikko Savolainen / Yle

Silja Paavola, chair of the trade union SuPer, says that the government must get elderly care in order. SuPer is the union of practical nurses and basic care workers.

Paavola says that there are problems at many care companies. In Paavola's view, private companies hire too few employees. That is why, Paavola demands, the law must set out how many employees there must be in care homes.

## The number of homeless people is falling

![A man sleeps in a shopping centre corridor.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10574617)

Open image viewer

There are still people in Finland who have no home. Photo: Petri Aaltonen / Yle

There are only a few people in Finland who have no home. Finland is the only European country where the number of homeless people is falling.

Finland's example interests others. For example, the French want to know about Finland's Housing First programme.

The idea of the Housing First programme is that a homeless person is quickly offered a permanent home. That is why cheap rental flats are also being built in Finland.

However, about 7,000 people are still without a home. Among them are young people too. Many of them live with friends or relatives.

## Lots of snow is falling

![Bus operating in a snowstorm](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10600419)

Open image viewer

Snowfall is hindering traffic early in the week. Photo: Marjut Suomi / Yle

Snowfall is hindering traffic. Driving conditions are really poor.

The snowfall continues on Tuesday. In the Helsinki region, as much as 30 centimetres of snow may fall in total during Monday and Tuesday.

Trees are now covered with thick snow. The authorities fear that the heavy snow will break trees. If trees fall onto electric lines, the power may go out.

The authorities warn that trees may also fall on people. People must be careful in nature when the snow weighs down the trees.
