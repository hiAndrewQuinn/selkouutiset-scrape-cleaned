# Friday 3.2.2017 (radio)

Most Finns support dual citizenship. There is a wish to take vocational teaching to workplaces. Reed nests are being built for the Saimaa ringed seal. Photographer Heikki Willamo photographs bears.

## A survey on dual citizenship

![A woman holds Finnish and Russian passports in her hands.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7918034)

Open image viewer

A dual citizen has the passports of 2 countries. Photo: Anna Mattila / Yle

Most Finns think that dual citizenship is a good thing. In a Yle survey, 56 percent of those who answered support dual citizenship. Dual citizens have a Finnish passport and a passport of some other country.

However, most Finns think that dual citizens are not suited to jobs in the army. 66 percent of those who answered the survey do not want dual citizens in tasks related to Finland's security. Many Finns do not want, for example, people who also have Russian citizenship to work in the Finnish army.

## Vocational teaching at workplaces

![Two logistics students drive a forklift in a logistics centre.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9435893)

Open image viewer

Logistics students at Jyväskylä vocational college study at a workplace. Photo: Jani Ilola / Yle

Vocational teaching is being reformed. The government wants teaching to be taken to workplaces. Schools and companies are developing the new way of teaching together.

Student Kaisla Salonen says that you learn well at a workplace. Kaisla studies logistics at Jyväskylä vocational college. The place of study is a real terminal, and Kaisla can practise with real equipment.

The plan is that a student can show what they can do with a demonstration of skills. Someone from the workplace assesses the work together with the teacher.

However, in the education sector people fear that it is difficult to develop the new teaching, because the government is giving less money to education than before.

## Nests for Saimaa ringed seals

![Saimaa ringed seal](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6422697)

Open image viewer

A Saimaa ringed seal on the ice. Photo: Yle

People are helping the Saimaa ringed seal in a new way this winter.

Reed nests have been built for the Saimaa ringed seal. They are needed this winter because there is no snow on Lake Saimaa. Usually the Saimaa ringed seal builds a nest out of snow, where it gives birth to its pups.

Reed is a tall grass plant that is easy to build a nest from for the seal. In the spring we will find out whether the seals accepted the reed nests.

Saimaa ringed seals live only in Finland. There are fewer than 400 of them. That is why Finns are interested in how well the Saimaa ringed seal and its pups are doing.

## Photos of bears

![An exhibition picture from Heikki Willamo's Mythical Journey.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9436677)

Open image viewer

The king of the forest. Photo: Heikki Willamo

Photographer Heikki Willamo photographs bears. Willamo thinks seeing a bear is always wonderful. It is no wonder that Finns have called the bear the king of the forest, Willamo says.

Willamo likes to photograph bears when it is dark. It is difficult, but Willamo thinks the picture becomes soft and beautiful when there is little light.

Willamo's photographs can be seen at the Salo Art Museum. There are also some of Willamo's pictures on the Yle News website.
