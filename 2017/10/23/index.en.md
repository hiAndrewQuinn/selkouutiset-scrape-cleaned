## Finland's arms trade

![A Patria AMV 8x8 armoured vehicle. ](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9895903)

Open image viewer

Finland has sold armoured vehicles like this to the United Arab Emirates. Photo: AOP

Finland sells most of its weapons to the Middle East. Last year Finland sold military equipment worth more than 130 million euros in total, and more than 60 percent of it was sold to the Middle East.

Finland has sold, for example, armoured vehicles to the Middle East. The biggest buyer last year was the United Arab Emirates. Finland has also sold military equipment to Saudi Arabia.

The information comes from a report made by the research institute SaferGlobe.

SaferGlobe researcher Kari Paasonen points out that Middle Eastern countries buy military equipment because there are conflicts in the region. For example, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates are involved in the war in Yemen.

## Expensive diabetes medicines

![A pharmacist at the Kontula pharmacy shows type 2 diabetes medicines whose Kela reimbursement has gone down](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9770178)

Open image viewer

The Kela reimbursement for diabetes medicines has become smaller. Photo: Juha Kivioja / Yle

There are new and effective medicines for treating diabetes. They also prevent, for example, heart diseases, which are common in diabetes patients.

But the problem with the new medicines is the price. The medicines are expensive, and Kela no longer pays as big a share of them as before. Buying the medicines can now cost a patient tens of euros more a month.

Pensioners, for example, often think carefully about whether they can afford to buy the new, expensive diabetes medicines. Some have even cut down on their medicines without a doctor's permission.

## Helsinki, a tourist city

![Toralf Tescher and his daughter Maria are spending their autumn holiday in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9897264)

Open image viewer

Germans Maria and Toralf Tescher are on holiday in Helsinki. Photo: Ronnie Holmberg / Yle

Many tourists are now visiting Helsinki. Clearly more tourists are coming from abroad than before.

Finland has done well in various international comparisons. That is why Finland has been written about in newspapers around the world, and people have noticed Finland and Helsinki.

Helsinki also has things that other cities do not have. Tourists are interested, for example, in Helsinki's city sauna culture.

Americans, Germans and Japanese stay in Helsinki's hotels the most.
