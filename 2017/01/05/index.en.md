# Thursday 5.1.2017 (radio)

Electricity use sets a record, and public transport is struggling because of the frost. Many researchers are leaving for abroad. Fishing fees have fallen sharply. Smith is charged with voyeurism. Russian President Vladimir Putin is visiting Finland's 100th anniversary celebrations.

## Record in electricity use

![Large electricity pylons](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9381847)

Open image viewer

In the frost a lot of electricity is used, especially to heat detached houses. Photo: AOP

Today a winter record has been set for electricity use. The reason is the hard frost.

Finland does not have enough electricity production, so electricity has to be imported, for example from Russia.

The hard frost is also causing problems for public transport.

The trains have been badly delayed because of problems with the tracks.

In the morning some of the buses could not get going, because faults had appeared in them in the frost.

The metro also stopped in the middle of its journey in the morning.

## Researchers are leaving Finland

![A microscope is used to study the H1N1 virus.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5922028)

Open image viewer

Many researchers are leaving for abroad. Photo: AP GraphicsBank

Many highly educated researchers are leaving Finland for other countries.

Many researchers and research groups are going abroad, because Finland no longer has enough money to do research.

The state has cut funding, for example, for universities and state research institutes.

The Union of Science Professionals says that it is mainly bioscientists, social scientists, psychologists and statisticians who are leaving Finland for abroad.

Many of them go to Sweden or Norway, because there more money is given to research work than in Finland.

The Union of Science Professionals hopes that the Finnish government will stop cutting money from universities and research institutes, and will instead give them more money.

## Fishing fees have fallen sharply

![Men fishing with spinning rods from a rock](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9017758)

Open image viewer

Fishing is a popular hobby. People over 65 do not have to pay a fishing fee. Photo: Kare Lehtonen / Yle

Fees for fishing have fallen so much that the authorities are worried.

With fishing fees, the authorities can make sure that there will be enough fish in Finnish waters in the future too.

Fishing fees fell by 2 million euros last year, when the new fishing act came into force.

Fishers aged 18–64 pay a fishing fee, which is, for example, 39 euros a year.

## Smith charged with voyeurism

![Media personality Axl Smith at Helsinki District Court.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8994613)

Open image viewer

Axl Smith in court last May. Photo: Jarno Kuusinen / AOP

Finnish presenter Axl Smith has been charged in court with 32 counts of voyeurism.

Smith had a video camera in his bedroom, with which he secretly filmed sexual partners.

Smith is also charged with three counts of defamation and two counts of spreading information that violates private life.

The Helsinki District Court will hear Smith's charges in a couple of weeks.

## Putin visits Finland's 100th anniversary celebrations

![Russian President Vladimir Putin smiles after casting his vote in the parliamentary elections.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9176396)

Open image viewer

Vladimir Putin was delighted by the invitation to Finland's 100th anniversary celebrations. Photo: Grigory Dukor / EPA

Russian President Vladimir Putin is visiting Finland this year.

President Sauli Niinistö has invited Putin to Finland's 100th anniversary of independence celebrations.

The website of the Russian president says that Putin has accepted the invitation with pleasure.
