## Taxi drivers' work is changing

![Taxis and taxi drivers in front of the railway station in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10134711)

First, news about taxi services.

Taxi services have changed. A new taxi law came into force on Sunday. The taxi law also affects drivers' work.

Now anyone can become a taxi driver. A taxi driver course is no longer compulsory. It is enough for the driver to pass a driving test.

A taxi driver must have a B driving licence. The licence must be from an EU or EEA country, which means almost all countries in Europe.

The authorities also check that the driver has not committed serious crimes. Drivers must also have good enough language skills.

If a taxi driver drives their own car, they need a taxi transport licence. But the licence is easy to get, because the authorities no longer decide how many taxis there can be.

A taxi no longer has to have a yellow taxi light on the roof. A taxi does not even have to be a car; it can be a lorry, for example.

Prices are changing too. Taxis can now decide themselves how much a ride costs. A taximeter is no longer compulsory. If there is no meter, the driver must tell the customer the price of the trip before the trip.

Because of the new law, many new companies and drivers are coming into taxi services.

## Vantaa gives out free contraceptives

![Contraceptives](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10278961)

Open image viewer

Free contraceptives have reduced pregnancies among young people in Vantaa. Photo: AOP

Here in Finland, Vantaa has tried free contraception. Vantaa has given out free contraceptives for 5 years.

The experiences are good. Pregnancies among people under 20 have gone down.

There have been over 30 percent fewer abortions among people under 20 than before.

## July is the warmest month

![A view of the Åland archipelago.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10282706)

Open image viewer

July is usually the warmest month of the year. Photo: AOP

July has begun. July is Finland's warmest month. That is why Finns want to be on holiday in July.

Yle meteorologist Kerttu Kotakorpi says that the highest temperature in July was measured in 2010. Then it was 37.2 degrees at Joensuu airport.

Kotakorpi says that July is also the rainiest month. In July it rains every other day on average.
