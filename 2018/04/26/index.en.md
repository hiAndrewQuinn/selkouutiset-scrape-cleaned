## The health sector's faith in the health and social services reform is fading

![The entrance of North Karelia Central Hospital](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9891106)

The faith of social and health care managers in the government's health and social services reform is going down, says this year's Social Barometer.

45 per cent of managers in the sector say that the health and social services reform is bad. Last year 36 per cent thought so. 32 per cent of health and social services managers say that the reform is good or very good. 97 per cent believe that the position of big companies will improve in the reform. 73 per cent believe that public service providers will get weaker.

The information comes from the preliminary results of the Social Barometer of SOSTE Finnish Federation for Social Affairs and Health. 154 health and social services managers answered the survey. The health and social services reform moves social and health care services from municipalities to regions. Every citizen will also be able to choose for themselves where they get care.

## Wills to charity are becoming more common

![Signing a will.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9269408)

Open image viewer

Photo: Tiina Kokko / Yle

More and more Finns leave their property to charity or to some organisation after their death.

Donations from private people make up about half of the money of non-profit organisations. Many also want to support the well-being of children and women in Finland and in developing countries. Organisations believe that there will be more wills, because more and more Finns die without heirs.

If a person has no heirs and no will, their property goes to the state after their death. Last year the state received almost 26 million euros of such funds.

## In Finland the birth rate was lower than the death rate early in the year

![A baby.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10143765)

The growth of Finland's population slowed down a little at the beginning of the year. Statistics Finland says that the population grew by about 580 people during January to March. That is more than 1,100 fewer than at the beginning of last year.

The reason for the population increase was people moving from abroad. About 2,700 fewer children were born than people died.

## Spring tiredness is not imagination

![A tired woman with a coffee cup in her hand leaning on an exercise ball.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10177258)

Open image viewer

Photo: Rui Santos / AOP

Many people suffer from spring tiredness. You can feel tired right in the morning.

Research professor Timo Partonen says that spring tiredness is a real thing. Spring tiredness may be more common than winter depression, which about 30 per cent of Finns have.

Partonen says that the increase in light makes many people go to bed later. Light can also make sleep broken or too short. Then it is good to think about how you can sleep better. A regular eating rhythm also helps. Partonen reminds that doing more exercise helps with spring tiredness.
