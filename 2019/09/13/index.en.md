# Friday 13.9.2019 (radio)

The governor of the Bank of Finland says that the Finnish state should not stimulate the economy any more. The government and labour market organisations are looking together for ways to create more jobs. The state says that small municipalities should make a safety plan. A book has been written about the life of Vesa-Matti Loiri.

## Rehn: No stimulus with debt

![Governor Olli Rehn, Bank of Finland](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10970790)

The governor of the Bank of Finland, Olli Rehn, says that it is not worth stimulating Finland's economy any more, at least not with borrowed money. Rehn says that the Finnish state already has so much debt that it is not worth taking on more.

Yesterday, the European Central Bank, ECB, hoped that the euro countries would stimulate their economies. The ECB said that economic growth needs more than lowering interest rates. The European Central Bank has been stimulating the economy for ten years, for example by letting people borrow money without interest. Bank of Finland governor Olli Rehn says that this has been a good method. But more methods are needed too.

Many euro countries hope that Germany will start big construction projects. People and companies would get jobs from them, and more money would circulate in the European economy.

## Jobs with wage subsidies?

![A cleaner washing cupboard doors](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10711316)

Open image viewer

A cleaner at work. Photo: AOP

The Finnish government, trade unions and employers are looking together for ways to create new jobs.

One way is to pay more wage subsidies than today. A wage subsidy means that the state pays part of an employee's wage. A company gets a wage subsidy if it hires an unemployed person.

Today the state pays companies a wage subsidy of at most 50 percent of the wage. Now the government is considering whether a company's wage subsidy could be 70 percent of the wage.

The government will decide later which methods will be used next year.

## Hobbies for young people

![Children playing.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10942665)

Open image viewer

Icelandic children playing. Photo: Mike Toivonen

The Icelandic model may come to Finland. The Icelandic model will be tried in Helsinki this autumn.

The Icelandic model means that children can take part in hobbies for free or cheaply. Hobby fees are paid by the municipality from tax money. In Reykjavik, the capital of Iceland, every child gets 360 euros a year from the municipality for hobbies.

Iceland pays for children's hobbies because it wants to offer children suitable things to do. When all children and young people can afford hobbies, they use less alcohol and tobacco than before.

## Book about Vesa-Matti Loiri

![Vesa-Matti Loiri with a cigarette in his hand (1969).](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1600,h_900,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-41934059883a1897684/1502100033)

Vesa-Matti Loiri in 1969. Photo: Yle picture service

A book has been written about the life of actor Vesa-Matti Loiri.

The book was written by author Jari Tervo. Tervo has interviewed Loiri. The book tells about Loiri's work and private life.

Vesa-Matti Loiri became a star at the age of 17, when he acted in the film Pojat. Finns remember Loiri best from the Uuno Turhapuro films. Loiri is also a popular singer.

The book also tells that Loiri had many girlfriends, and that at times he drank too much alcohol.
