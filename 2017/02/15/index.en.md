# Wednesday 15.2.2017 (radio)

Finland's NATO membership does not attract people. Fear of Russia has decreased. More Finnish teaching is wanted for immigrants. The government cancelled the motorcycle and boat tax. Finns have large housing loans. Cancer is also an illness of many children.

## Survey: less than half support NATO membership

![Graphic](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9457188)

Open image viewer

The latest Taloustutkimus survey was carried out on 6-9 February 2017. In 2016 NATO membership was not asked about.  Photo: Yle Uutisgrafiikka

Yle has made a survey about Finland's NATO membership. About a thousand people answered the survey, and more than half of them oppose NATO membership.

Only 21% of respondents want to join the military alliance NATO. 51% do not want Finland to join NATO.

The result of the new survey is almost the same as in earlier surveys.

## Fear of Russia has decreased a little

![Two Russian SU-24 attack aircraft are flying side by side.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9460510)

Open image viewer

Russian fighter jets can be seen, for example, over the Baltic Sea. Photo: EPA/US NAVY

Less than half of Finns say in Yle's survey that they consider Russia a threat.

47 percent of respondents considered Russia a threat. In 2014, as many as 56 percent of respondents considered Russia a threat.

At that time Russia had just taken over the Crimea region in Ukraine.

## Immigrants want more Finnish teaching

![An immigrant doing basic education tasks.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8292509)

Open image viewer

Children are wanted to have good language skills.  Photo: Laura Tolonen / Yle

Immigrants need to be taught more Finnish, says a new report.

The report considers how immigrants can settle into Finland.

The report says that immigrants can take part in and influence matters better if they know Finnish or Swedish.

The report talks a lot about how important education is. For example, the report hopes that all children who speak a foreign language will go to day-care.

Finnish must also be taught enough at school. The report has more than 40 proposals on how the settling of immigrants into Finland can be made easier.

The report has been given to the Minister of Education and Culture.

## Government cancelled the motorcycle and boat tax

![Boats at the quay of Kemi Inner Harbour.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9007033)

Open image viewer

The government planned a tax on boats. Photo: Antti Heikinmatti / Yle

The government says that after all it will not propose a motorcycle tax and a boat tax.

Prime Minister Juha Sipilä says that not enough money is raised from taxes on motorcycles and boats.

The motorcycle and boat tax has been opposed by both the government parties and the opposition parties.

## Finns have large housing loans

![A house made of paper with a roof made of banknotes.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8994256)

Open image viewer

Homes are expensive, so many home buyers have to borrow money from a bank.  Photo: Ismo Pekkarinen / AOP

Finns have a lot of debt for their homes.

On average, a Finn has a debt, that is a housing loan, of 95,000 euros.

About 20% of Finns have a loan that is three times their income for a year.

Finns have much more loans than, for example, 15 years ago.

Young people now have more housing loans than older people.

## Cancer also affects children

![Child patient in bed.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7105837)

Open image viewer

Many children survive cancer, because treatment in Finland is good.  Photo: Petri Aaltonen / Yle

About 150 children get cancer in Finland every year.

The most common cancer among children is leukaemia, that is blood cancer.

The outlook for children's cancer has improved over the years. For example, more than 80 percent of young leukaemia patients recover.

Treatments are long. They can last as long as two years.

Today is International Childhood Cancer Day.
