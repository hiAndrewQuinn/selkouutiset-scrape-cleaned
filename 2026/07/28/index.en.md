## French and Spanish wildfires

First, about wildfires in Europe.

Devastating wildfires are raging in France.

The worst fires are in the Gironde region in southwestern France. The flames have already destroyed 42,000 hectares of land.

Wildfires continue for the seventh day. More than 200,000 people have been forced to leave their homes.

French President Emmanuel Macron held an emergency meeting yesterday and visited the disaster area.

The Minister of the Interior said that the fire created a special thunderstorm over the weekend. It caused strong winds, and burning material flew all over the terrain.

This kind of cloud has never been observed in France before.

In addition to France, wildfires are wreaking havoc in Spain, where the heat is getting worse again. 100,000 people have fled their homes. 77,000 hectares have burned across 3 provinces.

The Finnish Ministry for Foreign Affairs urges Finns to avoid fire areas and follow the instructions of local authorities.

## Scrapping premium

Then about the scrapping fee.

Low-income people will soon be able to receive a subsidy of up to 6,000 euros to purchase a fully electric car. The prerequisite is that they scrap or recycle their old petrol or diesel car.

The scrapping bonus can be used to purchase a new or used electric car.

You can also use the reward to lease or rent a fully electric car for long-term use.

The application for scrapping bonus support will probably start at the end of next year. The income limit for the recipient is approximately 25,000 euros per year.

The matter still requires parliamentary approval.

## Smart mushroom control

Finally, about mushroom picking.

Researchers at the University of Eastern Finland want to make mushroom picking easier with technology.

Smart glasses may in the future be able to distinguish, for example, delicious treats better than with the naked eye.

At least in theory, smart glasses could be equipped with technology that allows you to select the species of mushroom. The technology is spectral imaging.

Researchers at the University of Eastern Finland believe that the technology could triple the efficiency of mushroom cultivation. The research is still ongoing and there is no funding yet.
