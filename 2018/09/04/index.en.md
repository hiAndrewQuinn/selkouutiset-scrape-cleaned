# Tuesday 4.9.2018 (radio)

The Finnish Immigration Service suspends the processing of asylum applications from Afghans. Civil speech is hoped for in Parliament. Cities compete for university students. Few people with a foreign background live in Helsinki. The water is low.

## A pause in processing Afghan asylum applications

![Anonymous young people in the picture.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10250792)

Open image viewer

The Immigration Service is not making any new asylum decisions for Afghans for now. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

The Finnish Immigration Service is suspending the processing of applications from Afghan asylum seekers.

The Immigration Service has received new information about Afghanistan from the UN refugee organisation. The Service will process the information and then decide whether it changes its line on asylum decisions.

For now, the Immigration Service is not making new asylum decisions for Afghans.
The Service will say more about the matter once it has first become familiar with the UN report.

Civil speech is hoped for in Parliament

![Parliament building.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10221832)

Open image viewer

The Speaker is worried about the way people speak in Parliament. Photo: Jyrki Lyytikkä / Yle

Parliament has returned from its summer holiday. Today the first plenary session of the autumn term was held.

The chair of Parliament, Speaker **Paula Risikko**, hopes that MPs will speak in Parliament in a friendly and calm way. The Speaker is worried that the debate in Parliament is sometimes angry and that MPs sometimes say rude things.

Cities compete for university students

![University students at a lecture.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5682603)

Open image viewer

University cities hope that graduates simply stay to work in the place where they studied. Photo: Yle

Cities compete for university students. Many people who graduate from university leave for work in the Helsinki region.

About 40% of all young people with a university degree in Finland live in Uusimaa, that is, in Helsinki or near Helsinki. Only a few who studied in Uusimaa move elsewhere.

Other cities hope that graduates do not leave for Helsinki, but stay to work in firms in the city where they studied.

Few people with a foreign background in Helsinki

![Employee with a foreign background at a computer.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5819560)

Open image viewer

In Stockholm, Gothenburg and Oulu, people with a foreign background make up over 30% of residents. Photo: YLE

Helsinki has far fewer people with a foreign background than other big cities in the Nordic countries. In Helsinki, about 14% of residents have a foreign background.

In Stockholm, Gothenburg and Oulu, people with a foreign background make up over 30% of residents.

Also in Vantaa and Espoo, neighbouring Helsinki, more people with a foreign background live compared with other residents than in Helsinki.

Water levels are low

![Boats on the shore.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10345263)

Open image viewer

Water levels will fall even more this September. Photo: Matti Myller / Yle

There is less water than usual in lakes and rivers now. Groundwater is also low. Water levels are low because the summer has been very warm and little rain has fallen.

Groundwater in Southern and Central Finland has dropped as much as half a metre lower than usual.

Water levels will fall even more this September.
