## Government sounding-out

Government informer Petteri Orpo says that the government base may be clear before May Day. The government base means which parties will join the new government. National Coalition chairman Petteri Orpo is forming the government, because the National Coalition is now the largest party in Parliament.

Orpo says that the talks with the parties are not a matter of a day or two. However, he hoped that the actual negotiations will begin right after May Day. In the government negotiations, it is agreed what kind of policy the government will start to pursue.

## Russian intelligence

![Russian ocean research vessel Admiral Vladimirsky.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_2482,h_1396,x_820,y_117/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-1100861643ebc6b99038/1681891910)

The Nordic documentary Shadow War is shown on TV1. The first episode is already on Yle Areena. Photo: Morten Krüger / DR

Russian military and civilian ships are examining the northern sea areas. The ships are examining the infrastructure of the Baltic and North Seas, for example cables. The Nordic public broadcasters, including Yle, have uncovered Russia's intelligence. Danish broadcaster DR's journalists got onto the trail of the Russians in November.

Russian radio traffic revealed that a 148-metre research vessel was moving in the Kattegat strait.

The Nordic documentary series says that Russia's targets of interest are gas pipelines and telecommunications cables. Wind turbines and their electricity cables also interest them. Experts say that Russia could, for example, prevent electricity transmission from wind turbines in sea areas.

Russia's intelligence activities are dealt with in the three-part documentary Shadow War. The first episode is already on Yle Areena.

## Surnames

![Mailbox with the name Munkki on it.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_6240,h_3510,x_0,y_322/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6004335da5bd46cc850/1571143063)

Open image viewer

There has been a turn in the choice of surname for married couples. Photo: Päivi Meritähti / Yle

Less than half of last year's married couples took the groom's surname. This is reported by the Digital and Population Data Services Agency. This was the first time that a majority of man–woman couples chose an option other than the groom's surname. 41 percent of the couples kept their own surnames. Less than 2 percent of couples chose the woman's surname.

Finnish married couples have been able to choose a surname quite freely since 1986. Earlier the law obliged the woman to take the man's surname.

## Baltic ringed seal

![Baltic ringed seal in the water.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4288,h_2412,x_0,y_302/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5487805c88f00e78741/1650434762)

Open image viewer

The Baltic ringed seal must be protected, because climate change harms its nesting. Photo: Zoo-Life / Alamy

The Baltic ringed seal has accepted an artificial nest in the Archipelago Sea and has given birth to a pup there in February. This happened for the first time.

Climate change has made life harder for the Baltic ringed seal. The animal normally makes its nest in sea ice, but it has had to give birth on land. There the newborn pups are in danger.

Artificial nests are used to try to protect the survival of the species.
