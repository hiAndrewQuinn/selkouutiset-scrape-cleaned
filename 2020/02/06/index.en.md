# Thursday 6.2.2020 (radio)

The government wants to increase the number of nurses in elderly care. The popularity of the Centre Party, a government party, has fallen. The Sámi National Day was celebrated on Thursday. Yle has been making news in Plain Swedish for 2 months.

## The number of nurses in elderly care

![A nurse and an elderly person in a care home.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10731929)

Open image viewer

There may be more nurses in elderly care. Photo: Toni Pitkänen / Yle

The government wants to increase the number of nurses in elderly care. The government proposes a law that says exactly how many nurses there must be.

In the government's view, 10 elderly people need 7 nurses. That is how many nurses must be in care places where the elderly are cared for around the clock.

If Parliament approves the government's bill, the law will come into force within 3 years at the latest. At that point over 4,000 more nurses will also be needed.

## Party support

![Party support graphic](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11194294)

Open image viewer

Photo: Laura Merikalla/Yle

Yle has asked Finns which party they support.

The Finns Party gets the most support. The Finns Party is the only party whose support is over 20 per cent. The National Coalition Party gets the second most support.

The Finns Party and the National Coalition Party are opposition parties, that is, they are not in the government.

The government parties, the SDP and the Greens, have increased their support, but they still trail behind the big opposition parties.

The support of the government party, the Centre Party, has fallen. According to experts, this is the biggest news from the survey. Co-operation between the government parties may suffer if the Centre Party's support does not improve.

The support figures for all parties can be found at yle.fi.

## The Sámi National Day

![Sámi álbmotbeaivi 2020 in Helsinki](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11195391)

Open image viewer

The Sámi flag also flew in Helsinki on Thursday. Photo: Linda Tammela / Yle

The Sámi National Day was celebrated on Thursday. The Sámi National Day is always on 6 February.

The Sámi are the only indigenous people of the European Union. Sámi people live in Lapland, in the areas of Norway, Sweden, Finland and Russia.

About 10,000 Sámi people live in Finland. Many Sámi have moved from Lapland to the big cities in the south.

The Sámi languages are related to the Finnish language. Three Sámi languages are spoken in Finland. The largest of them is North Sámi, which is also spoken in Norway and Sweden.
