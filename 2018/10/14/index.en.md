# Sunday 14.10.2018 (radio)

Prime Minister Sipilä does not want to ban political strikes. There was a big demonstration against racism in Berlin. The colour of a medicine is important to people. On Sunday an October heat record was measured.

## Political strikes

![Prime Minister Juha Sipilä at the Prime Minister's interview hour in Oulu on 14 October.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10456150)

The dispute between the government and the trade unions about the dismissal law continues.

The government has proposed a law that makes it easier to dismiss employees in small firms. The trade unions have organised strikes against the bill. That is why in Finland people have talked about whether it is right to hold political strikes.

On Sunday Prime Minister Juha Sipilä said that he does not want to ban political strikes. Sipilä does say, however, that political strikes must be carefully considered on a matter that Parliament has decided.

On Wednesday Parliament will vote on whether it trusts the government's employment policy. The dismissal law is part of the government's employment policy. Sipilä hopes that the strikes against the dismissal law will end if Parliament supports the government.

## Berlin demonstration

![Alice Weidel, chair of the Alternative for Germany party, speaks at an election event.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10455957)

AfD, or Alternative for Germany, is a party that opposes immigration. At the Berlin demonstration, people called for different politics. Photo: Andreas Gebert / EPA

On Saturday there was a big demonstration in Berlin, the capital of Germany. The organisers say that over 240,000 people took part. They protested against racism.

In Germany support for the far right has risen. Far-right parties do not want immigrants in Germany. That is why people protested in Berlin for an open Germany.

## The colour of a medicine

![Pills of different colours in a hand](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10453924)

Open image viewer

Which of these tablets looks like it will perk you up? Photo: AOP

The colour of a medicine is important to people. People think, for example, that a red medicine perks you up and a blue medicine makes you sleepy. The idea can be so strong that it affects how well the medicine works.

Researcher Ilari Sirenius was surprised by the power of the idea. He has studied how hopes and ideas affect which medicine a person chooses.

Sirenius says that people often want a medicine whose colour is familiar. For many people a good cough medicine is brown, because it has always been that way. And a painkiller works well if it is white.

Sirenius's study shows that reason alone does not decide what we buy from the pharmacy.

## October heat record

![View of the sea on a sunny autumn day at Mustikkamaa in Helsinki on 14 October.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10456241)

Sunday was a warm day. The temperature rose above 20 degrees in many places.

On Sunday a new October heat record was also measured. The October heat record is now 20.9 degrees. It was measured in Northern Finland, at Kokkola-Pietarsaari airport.

The old October heat record was from 1985. Then 19.4 degrees was measured.
