# Tuesday 24.9.2019 (radio)

British Prime Minister Boris Johnson broke the law. The travel agency Tjäreborg will continue its operations. Li Andersson wants to continue as leader of the Left Alliance. Finns are not happy with their bodies.

## Supreme Court: Boris Johnson broke the law

![Boris Johnson](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10954482)

Open image viewer

British Prime Minister Boris Johnson. Photo: Neil Hall / EPA

The UK's Supreme Court has made an important decision.

The decision says that Prime Minister Boris Johnson has broken the law. Johnson broke the law when he ordered that the British Parliament must take a long break.

Boris Johnson ordered that Parliament must stop working from 9 September. The break was supposed to end on 14 October. According to his opponents, Johnson ordered the break because Parliament could not talk about Britain's exit from the EU, that is, Brexit.

Prime Minister Johnson does not intend to resign because of the Supreme Court's decision. The Speaker of Parliament has already called Parliament together for Wednesday.

Britain intends to leave the European Union at the end of October.

## Tjäreborg will continue operating

![Logo of the Tjäreborg travel agency on the wall of the Kamppi shopping centre in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10985237)

Open image viewer

The logo of the Tjäreborg travel agency on the wall of the Kamppi shopping centre in Helsinki. Photo: Markku Ulander / Lehtikuva

The travel agency Tjäreborg will continue operating.

The large British travel company Thomas Cook said on Monday morning that it had gone bankrupt. Thomas Cook owns Tjäreborg.

Tjäreborg's flights will return to normal today, Tuesday. Tjäreborg says that about 2,000 Finns are still on trips it has arranged in different countries. Tjäreborg is trying to get people who are travelling home soon.

Meanwhile, a new owner is being sought for Tjäreborg.

Li Andersson has been leader of the Left Alliance since 2016.

## Finns want a better body

![Gym exercise](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10984766)

Open image viewer

People exercising in a step aerobics class in Essen, Germany. Photo: Ralph Lueger / AOP

Only every third Finn is happy with their own body.

A survey says that other Europeans are happier with their bodies than Finns are. In Germany and Italy, for example, many more people think their body is fine.

Finnish men like their bodies more than Finnish women do. Young people think their bodies are better than older people do.

Many people want to change something about their body. A large part of Finns want to lose weight, that is, weigh less.
