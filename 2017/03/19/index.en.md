# Sunday 19.3.2017 (radio)

Finnish authorities are investigating whether bad meat has been brought to Finland from Brazil. Minna Canth and Equality Day were celebrated on Sunday. Are loanwords a good or a bad thing? An immigrant must be hardworking, says Amy Järvisalo.

## Brazilian meat

Brazilian meat has also been used in restaurants in Finland. Photo: Fotosearch

Finnish authorities are investigating whether bad meat has been brought to Finland from Brazil.

Brazilian meat producers may have sent old and bad meat abroad, including to Europe. The Brazilian police suspect that meat companies have paid bribe money to Brazilian authorities. That is why the authorities have not intervened in the matter.

Finnish shops say that they sell only a little Brazilian meat.

## Minna Canth

![Minna Canth's home, Kanttila. Minna Canth's home, Kanttila, is located in central Kuopio.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7875094)

Minna Canth lived in this house in Kuopio. Photo: Timo Heikura / Yle

Minna Canth and Equality Day was celebrated on Sunday.

Writer Minna Canth lived in the 1800s. Canth was also the first Finnish-language woman journalist.

Minna Canth wrote a lot about the position of women. She wanted to improve girls' education, for example. That is why Minna Canth's Day is also Equality Day.

Minna Canth's Day is a flag day. Minna Canth is the only woman in Finland who has got her own flag day.

## Loanwords

![Selfie stick held up long](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7691356)

Open image viewer

Is "selfiekeppi" (selfie stick) a good Finnish word? Photo: Jari Vesa / Yle

"I'll take a selfie and tweet it to everyone."

Loanwords like these annoy many Finns. Many think that the Finnish language gets worse when words from other languages are taken into it. However, that is not the case, says Professor of Finnish Jaakko Leino.

Leino says that loanwords have always come into the Finnish language. Already more than 100 years ago, many people were worried about the language changing.

Today, loanwords come into Finnish from the field of technology, for example. Many words are translated from English, for example "pilvipalvelu" (cloud service) and "hiiri" (mouse). Sometimes words are not translated, but they are adapted to Finnish. Words such as "selfie", "tviitti" (tweet) and "tviitata" (to tweet) are like that too.

In Professor Leino's opinion, it is good that the language changes. It is a sign that the language is alive and doing well, he says.

An immigrant must be hardworking. That is what Amy Järvisalo says. In Järvisalo's opinion, it is worth an immigrant studying, practising Finnish and meeting Finns.

Amy Järvisalo comes from Thailand, and she has also lived in the USA. Now Järvisalo lives in Finland, in Joensuu. The town is suitably small for a family with children, Järvisalo says.
