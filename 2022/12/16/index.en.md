## Household electricity bills

![Customer checks an electricity bill.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6491978)

Open image viewer

The electricity bill is now unpleasant reading. Photo: Lassi Lähteenmäki / Yle

The Finnish government is considering how household electricity bills can be reduced.

Electricity is now so expensive that many households find it hard to cope with their electricity bills.

The government is trying to find a model with which households can be supported in the best way.

The largest government party, the SDP, has for example proposed an electricity price cap. If the price cap is, for example, 20 cents per kilowatt-hour, households do not need to pay any more than that for electricity themselves.

One option is that households receive direct support to pay their electricity bills.

The government parties discussed the matter on Friday. All parties in Parliament will discuss the price of electricity on Monday.

The government intends to decide during next week which model will be used to start supporting households.

## Rising interest rates

![Home loans. Real estate office. Helsinki. 20.5.2021.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_6016,h_3384,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-80887060a6895212eda/1644294960)

Open image viewer

Interest rates on many housing loans are also rising. Photo: Jorge Gonzalez / Yle

Interest rates are rising. The 12-month Euribor rate is already about 3 percent.

The interest rates on many housing loans follow the 12-month Euribor rate. That is why many people will notice at the latest during next year that the interest rate on their housing loan is rising and that repaying the loan is becoming more expensive.

Interest rates are rising, because the European Central Bank has raised key interest rates. Key interest rates steer how high other interest rates are.

## Church membership

![Interior of the Joensuu Evangelical Lutheran Church.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10197568)

Open image viewer

Church membership is falling, and fewer people attend services than before. Photo: Jouki Väinämö / Yle

The membership of the Finnish Lutheran Church is falling. The church has 64,000 fewer members than last year.

Now about 65 percent of Finland's inhabitants are members of the Lutheran Church.

As late as 2000, 85 percent of Finns were church members, and in 1950 95 percent of Finns belonged to the Lutheran Church.

Finland has 2 folk churches, the Lutheran Church and the Orthodox Church. About 1 percent of Finns belong to the Orthodox Church.

The folk churches have more rights than other religious groups. For example, the folk churches are allowed to collect taxes.

## Flood risk on streets

src="https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5519543"

Open image viewer

Next week water will come onto the streets if the storm drains are blocked. Photo: YLE

In southern Finland a lot of water may come onto the streets next week. The weather is warming, that is, the frost is weakening, and the snow begins to melt. In addition, rain is falling.

Many storm drains are now blocked by snow and ice. That is why water may stay on the streets.

## The weather

On Saturday the weather is cloudy, and in places a little snow may fall. In the southern and central parts of the country the frost is 1 to 6 degrees. In Lapland there is 10 to 20 degrees of frost.

On Sunday the weather warms up. In the south the temperature is already a little above zero. On Sunday there will also be rain in Finland. The precipitation may be snow, sleet or rain.
