## Finland's and Sweden's prime ministers

Finland's and Sweden's prime ministers held a press conference in Stockholm. Photo: Jonas Ekströmer / EPA

Finland's Prime Minister Sanna Marin has visited Sweden. Marin met Sweden's Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson.

The prime ministers said that Finland and Sweden will join the NATO defence alliance together, at the same time.

Finland and Sweden applied for NATO membership last May. The NATO country Turkey has not yet approved them as members of the defence alliance.

Turkey has been especially angry at Sweden, because the Quran was burned at a demonstration in Sweden.

## Helen's electricity price

![Banknotes and bank cards.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12688748)

Open image viewer

The electricity of many Helen customers is getting cheaper. Photo: Risto Degerman / Yle

The Helsinki electricity company Helen is cutting the price of electricity. For many Helen customers the electricity price falls by almost 50 percent.

The electricity price falls for customers whose electricity contract is valid until further notice. For a detached house the electricity bill falls on average by 70 euros a month, and in a flat in a block of flats by about 30 euros a month.

The new price does not apply to Helen customers who have a fixed-term electricity contract or spot electricity. With a fixed-term contract the electricity price does not vary, while the price of spot electricity changes all the time.

## Party support

![Bar chart.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12688750)

Open image viewer

Photo: Eetu-Mikko Pietarinen / Yle

The differences in support between the big parties have shrunk.

That is what Yle's new support poll shows. In it people were asked which party they support, that is, which party they intend to vote for.

The National Coalition is still the largest party in the poll. The National Coalition's support has, however, fallen. Now about 21 percent of poll respondents support the National Coalition.

The second largest party is now the SDP. The SDP's support has risen a little, and it is now about 19 percent.

The Finns Party is in third place. The party's support has fallen. About 18 percent of poll respondents now support the Finns Party.

The differences in support between the big parties have thus shrunk, and competition between the parties is tightening.

Parliamentary elections will be held in Finland in 2 months. After the elections the post of prime minister goes to the largest party in Parliament.

The Prime Minister now is SDP chair Sanna Marin, because the SDP won the previous parliamentary elections.
