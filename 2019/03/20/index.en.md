# Wednesday 20.3.2019 (radio)

Britain asks for a delay to its EU exit. Finland is the happiest country in the world. More than a million schoolchildren protested against climate change. Eevi and Eeli were last year's most popular children's names. The days are getting longer.

## Britain wants to postpone the EU exit

![Theresa May](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10696791)

Open image viewer

British Prime Minister Theresa May. Photo: Andy Rain / EPA

Britain wants to delay its EU exit, that is, Brexit.

Britain has asked the EU to postpone Britain's EU exit until the end of June. Britain was to leave next Friday, that is, on 29 March.

Britain wants more time, because the British parliament has not approved the withdrawal agreement that Prime Minister Theresa May has negotiated with the EU. Parliament has voted on the agreement 2 times and rejected it both times.

Britain's request for a delay will be dealt with at a summit of EU leaders in Brussels tomorrow.

## A happy country

![Two hands form a heart.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10245264)

Open image viewer

Finland has been at the top of the happiness survey for the second year in a row. Photo: Yle

Finland is the happiest country in the world.

In the UN happiness survey, Finland is in first place for the second year in a row, that is, it is the happiest country in the world.

After Finland come Denmark, Norway, Iceland and the Netherlands.

156 countries are included in the UN comparison. The study looks at, for example, a country's gross domestic product, freedom and social security.

Happiness researcher Ilona Suojanen says that the report does not tell about people's happiness, but about how well things are going in different countries.

At the bottom of the list are South Sudan, the Central African Republic and Afghanistan.

## More than a million young people marched for the climate

![Young protesters in front of Parliament House at the international youth climate strike march in Helsinki on Friday 15 March.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10691207)

Open image viewer

Demonstrations for the climate were held in more than 100 countries. Photo: Marja Väänänen / Yle

About 1.3 million young people from different parts of the world took part in the schoolchildren's climate demonstrations.

The schoolchildren were absent from school and demonstrated against climate change last Friday.

Demonstrations were organised in more than 100 countries and about 2,000 cities.

In Finland about 9,000 schoolchildren took part in the demonstrations.

## Eevi and Eeli were last year's favourite names

![baby yawns](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10618673)

Open image viewer

More than 49,000 babies were born in Finland last year. Photo: AOP

The Population Register Centre has said which were the most popular first names for children born last year.

The most popular first name for girl babies last year was Eevi, and for boy babies it was Eeli. About 300 girls were given the first name Eevi last year. About 400 boys were given the first name Eeli.

Among Swedish-language names, the most popular girls' name was Saga and the boys' name was William.

More than 49,000 children were born last year.

## The day is now longer than the night

![Dandelion](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10696923)

Open image viewer

In the north it is still winter, but on the edge of the Vuosaari golf course in Helsinki, dandelions are already flowering. Photo: Antti Kolppo / Yle

Today is the spring equinox. That means that the amount of light now grows quickly.

At the time of the spring equinox, the sun moves from the southern hemisphere to the northern. From now on, in the northern hemisphere, that is also here in Finland, the day is longer than the night.
