# Thursday 8.9.2016 (radio)

The biggest opposition party, the SDP, clearly increased its support. People demonstrated against the Immigration Service. A former national police commissioner fears immigrant gangs. Innocent Anneli Auer will be paid record compensation.

## The SDP's popularity rises

![Party support figures](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9149718)

Open image viewer

Party support poll Photo: Yle News Graphics

The popularity of the opposition party SDP has risen the most in Yle's party support poll. The SDP's support is now exactly 20 percent.

The Centre Party is still the biggest party, but the support of the Prime Minister's party fell to 20.7 percent. The support of the other government party, the National Coalition Party, stayed almost the same at nearly 18 percent. The support of the Finns Party rose to 8.4 percent.

The combined support of the government parties is about 47 percent, and that of the opposition parties is a little over 51 percent.

## Asylum seekers demand changes in how the Immigration Service works

![A demonstration in front of the Immigration Service.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9152143)

Open image viewer

There was a demonstration in front of the Immigration Service. Photo: Pekka Pantsu / Yle

People have demonstrated in front of the Immigration Service against the agency's actions. Asylum seekers and Finnish activists oppose the agency's new policies on asylum decisions. The asylum seekers' signs and sheets said, for example, that Afghanistan is not a safe country.

The organisers of the event demand that the Immigration Service re-examine the negative asylum decisions. In addition, the agency must change its policies on the safety of different countries. The Immigration Service says that the situation has improved in Iraq, Afghanistan and Somalia, and people can be returned there.

## A former national police commissioner fears immigrant gangs

![Retired national police commissioner Mikko Paatero.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9151596)

Open image viewer

Former national police commissioner Mikko Paatero. Photo: Yle

Former national police commissioner, that is, the highest police officer, Mikko Paatero says that there may be immigrant gangs in reception centres. Paatero says that some asylum seekers have already formed groups that are tightly led.

The former national police commissioner fears that some asylum seekers will not leave Finland when they get a negative decision. These asylum seekers may then move into the world of crime. Paatero is also worried because many asylum seekers have military training.

The Ministry of the Interior and the National Police Board disagree with the former national police commissioner. According to them, there are no signs of new gangs, but the police are following the situation closely.

## The state pays huge compensation to an innocent person

![Anneli Auer at the SuomiAreena event in Pori in July.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9151415)

Open image viewer

Anneli Auer gets record compensation Photo: Pasi Murto / AOP

The state is paying the biggest compensation in Finland's criminal history to Anneli Auer, who was in prison for almost 2 years though innocent. Anneli Auer gets almost 500,000 euros in compensation for her suffering from the State Treasury. Auer was in prison for over 600 days for the murder of her husband, but the court later acquitted Auer of the charges.

The State Treasury justifies the big sum of compensation by saying that Auer had to be in prison for a long time and she was suspected of a serious crime.

Auer's lawyer is not happy with the sum. The sum is only 30 percent of what Auer demanded from the state. Lawyer Juha Manner wonders why the great publicity of Auer's criminal case did not affect the amount of compensation.

Professor of criminal law Jussi Tapani also says that the compensation sum may rise, even though it is already large.
