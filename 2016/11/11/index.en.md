## A rare court case

![The school killing suspect at the trial.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9286918)

Open image viewer

The prosecutor says that a woman of about 20 planned to kill people. Photo: Yle

A court in Helsinki is handling a rare case.

The prosecutor says that a young woman intended to kill at least 40 people. The prosecutor says that the woman intended to commit the crime at the school where she used to be a pupil. The school is in Kokkola. Nowadays the woman lives in Helsinki.

The prosecutor also says that the woman wanted to buy weapons for the act. She already paid for one weapon and planned to buy other weapons.

The woman herself says that she did not really intend to kill people.

## The Talvivaara mine

![Terrafame Oy's Talvivaara mine.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9173832)

Open image viewer

The Talvivaara mine is in Sotkamo. Photo: Pasi Peiponen / Yle

The Finnish government intends to give more money to the Talvivaara mine. The government thinks that the mine's operation is still worth continuing.

The plan gives the mine more time to look for private money for its operation. There is time at least until the end of the year.

The Finnish state became the owner of the Talvivaara metal mine because of the mine's problems. The environment around the mine became polluted, and the mining company ran out of money.

The Talvivaara mine is in Sotkamo, in Northern Finland. People in the area hope that the mine's jobs will be kept.

## Russian tourists

![Allegro train.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8812539)

Open image viewer

More Russian train passengers are coming to Helsinki again. Photo: Derrick Frilund / Yle

The number of Russian tourists has started to grow again in Helsinki.

VR says that more train passengers are coming from Moscow and St Petersburg than some time ago. In Helsinki Russian tourists go shopping, for example at the Stockmann department store.

The number of Russian tourists fell a few years ago, when the value of the rouble fell and euro prices were expensive for Russians. Now the value of the rouble has improved.

## Finns and coffee

![A coffee maker is not suitable for boiling water.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5397771)

Open image viewer

Is this the most important machine in a Finnish home? Photo: YLE

More coffee is drunk in Finland than anywhere else in the world.

Finns consume about 12 kilos of coffee per person a year. The average consumption of coffee in the world is about 1.3 kilos per person.

A Finn drinks about 5 cups of coffee a day. Many Finns drink morning coffee, afternoon coffee and evening coffee. If guests come to the home, the guests are always offered coffee.

Finns like best arabica coffee beans that have been roasted light. Coffee is imported to Finland, for example, from Brazil, Colombia and Kenya.
