# Saturday 24.3.2018 (radio)

The film Ikitie won the most Jussi Awards. Earth Hour switches off lights around the world. Summer time begins. The spring migration of birds is late.

## Domestic films were awarded Jussis

![AJ Annila received the Jussi Award for Best Director for the film Ikitie at the Jussi Gala](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10131778)

Director AJ Annila's film Ikitie won the most Jussi Awards. Photo: Antti Aimo-Koivisto / Lehtikuva

The best domestic films of last year have been awarded. The best films and actors were given Jussi Awards.

The film Ikitie received the most Jussis. Ikitie received 6 Jussis in total. It was, for example, the best film of the year, and its director AJ Annila was the best director of the year.

The second most awards went to The Unknown Soldier, directed by Aku Louhimies. The Unknown Soldier received 5 Jussis. It was, for example, the audience's favourite film.

A total of 18 Jussi Awards were handed out.

## Earth Hour switches off the lights

![The railway station is known especially for Emil Wikström's stone man. Helsinki Railway Station is known especially for Emil Wikström's stone man.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8454735)

The lanterns of the Kivimies statues at Helsinki Central Railway Station will go out during Earth Hour. Photo: Marja Väänänen / Yle

The lights go out on Saturday evening in different parts of the world for the sake of the climate. The world's largest climate event, Earth Hour, is now held for the 12th time.

For example, the Sydney Opera House, Moscow's Red Square, London's Big Ben clock tower, the Pyramids of Egypt and Niagara Falls on the border of the USA and Canada will switch off their lights at 20.30 local time.

In Finland, for example, the ski jump hill in Lahti, Tampere Hall and the Yle transmission tower in Pasila, Helsinki, are taking part in the event. The lanterns of the Kivimies statues at Helsinki Central Railway Station will also go out..

The lights were off for one hour.

## The clocks are moved to summer time

![Clock face](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10042296)

Open image viewer

The clocks are moved to summer time tomorrow night. Photo: Anssi Leppänen / Yle

Tomorrow night we move to summer time. Summer time is used in other EU countries as well as in Finland.

The clocks are moved forward one hour during the night between Saturday and Sunday at 3 o'clock.

Studies show that moving the clocks is not good for people's health. That is why Finland has proposed that the EU give up summer time.

Other member states have not supported the proposal. The European Commission is, however, studying the matter.

## The spring migration of birds is late

![Northern lapwing.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5551996)

Open image viewer

The northern lapwing often returns to Finland already at the turn of February and March. Photo: Juha Kemppainen / YLE

The spring migration of birds to Finland is late this year. The late migration is due to a cold spring.

For example, skylarks and northern lapwings normally return to Finland at the turn of February and March. The bird organisation Birdlife Finland says that at the start of January a few skylarks were already seen in Finland. However, they left again, because the weather was so cold.

Last year at this time, hundreds of barnacle geese and thousands of skylarks, lapwings and bean geese had already arrived in Finland.
