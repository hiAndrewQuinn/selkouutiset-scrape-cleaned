## Carbon dioxide emissions are rising again

![Finnair aircraft at Helsinki-Vantaa airport.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10599791)

Open image viewer

The decrease in air travel has affected carbon dioxide emissions. Photo: Martti Kainulainen / Lehtikuva

The coronavirus pandemic affects carbon dioxide emissions across the whole world.

In the spring, when the coronavirus began to spread, carbon dioxide emissions fell. 17 percent of them disappeared.

Emissions fell because traffic and industrial production decreased because of the coronavirus.

Now carbon dioxide emissions have risen again. They are now almost as high as before the coronavirus.

## The second wave of the coronavirus

![A traffic sign announces a face mask requirement for pedestrians.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1920,h_1080,x_0,y_99/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-7334195f91928a04277/1603375824)

Open image viewer

In some streets in Germany, a mask must be worn.  Photo: Focke Strangmann / EPA

The coronavirus is now spreading fast in the United States and in many European countries.

On Friday, the most new infections ever during the coronavirus period were reported in the United States.

The figure was over 82,000.

Among European countries, almost 15,000 infections were reported in Germany on Friday. That was the highest figure for one day.

In the Czech Republic a similar record was set on Friday. More than 16,000 infections were reported there.

Poland has tightened coronavirus restrictions in many ways. There too a record daily number of infections was set on Friday. There were over 13,000 infections.

Large infection numbers mean that the second wave of the coronavirus is at its worst in the United States and in many European countries.

## Winter time begins

![A clock whose hand is moved back to winter time.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1600,h_900,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/14-svyle-6142553197327452bd/1603530654)

Open image viewer

In winter time the clock is moved back one hour. Photo: Yle

Next night the clocks are moved to winter time. At 4 am the clocks are moved back one hour.

Finland moves to winter time, which is also called standard time. Before 1981 Finland was on winter time all the time.

Nowadays the change of time is easier, because phones, computers and many household appliances update the clock automatically.

Tomorrow, Sunday, it is dry almost everywhere, i.e. no rain falls.

In the middle of the country you may see the sun. Elsewhere the weather is cloudy.

In the afternoon and evening there may be a few showers in the western part of the country.

The day after tomorrow, Monday, there will be rain again, and the weather will warm across the whole country.
