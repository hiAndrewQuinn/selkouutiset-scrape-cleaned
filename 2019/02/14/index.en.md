# Thursday 14.2.2019 (radio)

A big walkout at the Pasila postal centre. The police left many crimes uninvestigated last year. Film director Aki Kaurismäki protested the government's forest and mining policy. Final-year upper secondary students drove around town.

## The police left many suspected crimes uninvestigated

![Police logo, police officer in uniform](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10442812)

Open image viewer

The law says that the police can suspend and end pre-trial investigations.  Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

Last year the police suspended and ended the pre-trial investigation of a very large number of criminal cases. According to Yle's information, the police suspended or ended almost 140,000 pre-trial investigations last year.

Police inspector Jyrki Aho says that a pre-trial investigation can be suspended, for example, if the suspected perpetrator is not known. A pre-trial investigation can also be suspended if it is not known how the investigation can be taken further.

The suspended investigations included, for example, property and violent crime investigations.

Last year the police recorded more than 300,000 crime reports in total.

## Big walkouts at the postal centre

![A postal sorting centre.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9593134)

Open image viewer

Some of the work at the Pasila postal centre is being transferred to a subcontractor. Photo: Aleksi Tuomola / Lehtikuva

In Southern Finland, post may be late in the coming days because of walkouts by Posti employees.

There have been big walkouts at the Pasila postal centre yesterday and today. The walkouts are because some of the postal centre's work is being transferred to a subcontractor. SOL Henkilöstöpalvelut starts work at the postal centre on Monday.

The union of Posti employees, PAU, says that SOL's employees are paid 30 percent less than Posti's own employees.

## Aki Kaurismäki gives up his title

![Aki Kaurismäki](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9972968)

Open image viewer

Film director Aki Kaurismäki. Photo: Jussi Nahkuri / Yle

Film director Aki Kaurismäki is giving up the title of Academician of Arts. Aki Kaurismäki says that he is giving up the title as a protest against the government's forest and mining policy.

Kaurismäki has written a letter to President Sauli Niinistö. In the letter he says that the government has let Finland's timber felling become too large. He also says that foreign mining companies take ore and minerals from Finnish soil, and leave behind a polluted landscape and contaminated lakes for taxpayers to deal with.

The Academician of Arts is a high title that the President of the Republic awards to an artist who has excelled in their work.

Aki Kaurismäki received the title of Academician in 2008.

## Final-year students celebrate the end of school

![Bench-warming lorry in Hämeenlinna](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10646869)

Open image viewer

Upper secondary school graduates celebrate the end of upper secondary school. Photo: Nina Keski-Korpela / Yle

Final-year upper secondary students have today been holding their bench-warming parties. The bench-warming party celebrates the end of the final-year students' upper secondary school.

At the bench-warming party, the final-year students ride on the back of a lorry in the city centres. The sides of the cars have posters that comment on current affairs. Many final-year students are dressed in silly clothes, and they throw sweets to people.

After the bench-warming parties, the final-year students start a study break for the matriculation examinations. The spring matriculation exams begin in mid-March.
