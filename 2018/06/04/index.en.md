# Monday 4.6.2018 (radio)

Many workplaces have started protecting employees from ticks. There will be more electric car charging points. The EU disability card is now in use in Finland too. There is baby news from Korkeasaari.

## An employer can protect an employee from ticks

![A tick attached to human skin.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5419582)

Open image viewer

Protective clothing is the best way to protect against ticks. If a tick does get attached to the skin, the tick must be removed quickly. Photo: Antti Heikinmatti / Yle

Many workplaces have started to protect employees from ticks. A tick can get onto the skin when working in nature. In North Savo's forestry association, for example, more than half of the employees have had a vaccine.

Ticks spread tick-borne encephalitis, against which there is a vaccine. Ticks also spread Lyme disease, against which there is no vaccine.

Day-care centres and schools also talk about the risk of ticks. The most important thing is to stop the tick from getting onto the skin. Clothes and rubber boots, for example, are good for protection. It is worth pulling socks over the trouser legs.

## More electric car charging points

![Charging an electric car.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10236577)

Open image viewer

K-Group wants an electric car to be chargeable while you shop. Photo: AOP

More electric car charging points are coming to Finland. The retail chain K-Group is building a network of charging points that will cover the whole country. The work will be done during this year and next year. After that, K-Group will have about 400 charging points at more than 70 sites.

The charging points will be opened mainly in big cities, next to K-Group's shops. The idea of K-Group is that an electric car can be charged while you do your shopping. All the charging points are free for customers at the start.

At the moment there are 1,300 charging points in Finland in total, at about 500 sites.

## The EU disability card is now in Finland

![Screenshot from the website of the Foundation for Persons with Intellectual Disabilities. A symbol and the text: Equality, inclusion, activity - disability card.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8834983)

Open image viewer

With the disability card, you can prove the need for help across the whole EU. The aim is to promote equality, inclusion and mobility for disabled people.

The European Union disability card is now in use in Finland too. It is possible to apply for the card from today. A disabled person can use the card to show that they need support, for example on trips, at events and in services across the whole EU.

The card can be obtained by people who have a decision granting the right to the card. There are about 300,000 such people in Finland. You can check your right to the disability card on the Kela website and at www.vammaiskortti.fi.

The disability card costs 10 euros, and getting one is voluntary.

## Baby news from Korkeasaari

![A Dorcas gazelle calf being fed with a bottle.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10236921)

Open image viewer

A Dorcas gazelle calf is fed with a teat bottle. Photo: Mari Lehmonen / Korkeasaari Zoo

There is baby news from the Korkeasaari zoo in Helsinki.

The kuhertaja gazelles have had five fawns. The youngest of them is one week old. It still stays hidden, and the mother comes to nurse it a few times a day. One of the fawns is being looked after apart from the rest of the herd. The fawn is fed with a teat bottle. This is done because no fawn is to be lost.

There are fewer than 50 kuhertaja gazelles in the zoos of Europe. That is why the birth of the fawns was a very welcome event at Korkeasaari. Besides Korkeasaari, only Karlsruhe in Germany has a herd that is breeding. Desertification and poaching threaten the kuhertaja gazelle.
