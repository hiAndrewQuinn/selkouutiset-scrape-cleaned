# Tuesday 25.12.2018 (radio)

Christmas has been celebrated quite calmly, but in Helsinki the police are investigating the murder of a child. At Christmas the most flowers bought are hyacinths. The city of Kuusamo fulfilled the wish of 82-year-old Elma Myllymäki: she got to bake gingerbread with children. The weather has milded and the driving conditions have been dangerous.

## Murder overshadows Christmas

The police are investigating the murder of a child in Helsinki. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

Christmas has been celebrated quite calmly. The police have had to calm down some quarrels in homes. Drunk drivers have also been caught.

In Helsinki, however, the police are investigating a murder, the victim of which is a boy under 12 years old. The police have arrested a man of about 35 in the flat where the murder was committed. The Helsinki police will not say more about the matter before Friday.

## Hyacinth is Christmas's favourite

![More and more Finns are buying white Christmas flowers, including hyacinths and tulips.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7682710)

Open image viewer

Hyacinth (on the left) is the favourite Christmas flower. Photo: Kauppapuutarhaliitto

Finns buy flowers at Christmas for an average of 50 euros.

People buy the most hyacinths: about 2.5 million pieces. About half of the Christmas flowers have been grown in Finland.

Over the whole year Finns buy flowers for 500 million euros. 20 percent of this money is spent at Christmas.

## Big flower bouquets are bought less than before

![Heidi Saarinen](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10570971)

Open image viewer

Heidi Saarinen had to close her flower shop. Photo: Jouni Immonen / Yle

Flower shops are doing worse than before.

People buy more cheap flowers from supermarkets and fewer bound flower bouquets. For example, few flowers are bought for many funerals, and big flower bouquets are no longer bought as they used to be. Cremation is now common, and relatives hope that guests bring only one flower. When a person is buried in the ground, a lot of flowers are usually left on the grave. In a cremation this cannot be done.

The custom has changed quickly. The new custom clearly reduces sales of flower shops.

## Elma Myllymäki got to bake with children

![Elma Myllymäki Kuusamo](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10565768)

Open image viewer

3-year-old Annu and 4-year-old Pihla baked with Elma Myllymäki. Photo: Ensio Karjalainen / Yle

The city of Kuusamo turns 150 years old, and in honour of it the city has fulfilled the wishes of 150 elderly people.

The wish of 82-year-old Elma Myllymäki was to bake gingerbread with children. So Kaisa Korhonen from Kuusamo came with 3- and 4-year-old girls to bake at Elma Myllymäki's home shortly before Christmas.

Elma Myllymäki says that it is really nice to be with children. Myllymäki has no children of her own, and her relatives live far away in Sweden. She says that she is sometimes lonely.

## The weather warmed up

![A child plays in a snowdrift.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10571210)

Open image viewer

The sun tempts people out to play on St Stephen's Day. Photo: Rob Watkins / AOP

The weather today, on Christmas Day, is much warmer than yesterday, Christmas Eve.

In a large part of Finland rain or snow has fallen. Roads and streets have been dangerously slippery.

On Wednesday, St Stephen's Day, it no longer rains or snows. Now and then the sun shines, and the driving conditions are good across the whole country on St Stephen's Day. Some Finns drive home by car on Wednesday, when the Christmas holidays end and everyday life begins.
