# Wednesday 25.3.2020 (radio)

All of Uusimaa may be isolated. The coronavirus has caused three deaths in Finland. A forecast says that in the peak months of the epidemic, perhaps 11,300 people in Finland will need hospital care. There is enough food in the shops. The selectors for the Finlandia Prizes have been decided.

## All of Uusimaa may be isolated

![The border between Uusimaa and Southwest Finland.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11275123)

The border between Uusimaa and Southwest Finland. Photo: Jussi Nukari / Lehtikuva

The government is about to isolate the whole Uusimaa region because of the coronavirus. This is what STT and Yle's information says.

New restrictions are coming, because there is more coronavirus in Uusimaa than elsewhere in Finland.

The government is taking section 118 of the emergency powers act to Parliament first. The movement restriction is meant to come into force quickly.

## 3 people have died of corona in Finland

Kanta-Häme Central Hospital in Hämeenlinna. Photo: Anne-Maria Niskanen / Yle

The coronavirus has caused 2 new deaths in Finland. An elderly person died yesterday in the Helsinki and Uusimaa hospital district.

In addition, a middle-aged person who belonged to a risk group died today at Kanta-Häme Central Hospital.

In total, there have now been 3 deaths caused by the coronavirus in Finland. The first death was reported on Saturday.

The Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare forecasts that in the peak months of the epidemic, perhaps 11,300 people in Finland will need hospital care. Of these, perhaps 3,600 will need intensive care.

The forecast says that at the same time, perhaps just over 900 patients will be in hospital care. At the same time, 280 patients will be in intensive care, the forecast says.

## There is enough food in the shops

![S-Market in Sokos Joensuu, Joensuu.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11082208)

Open image viewer

In Finland there is no need to fear that food will run out in the shops. Finland produces enough of its own food. Photo: Ismo Pekkarinen / AOP

There is plenty of food in the grocery stores. The grocery shops have prepared for restaurants and cafes to be closed. For more than a week, there have already been more customers in the grocery stores than normal. In addition, restaurants have already been quiet.

The managing director of the grocery trade, Kari Luoto, says that Finland's self-sufficiency rate is high, about 80 percent.

Self-sufficiency shows how well a country can itself produce food for its residents.

\- Food will not run out in Finland, Kari Luoto reassures those who are stockpiling.

However, there may be delays with imported goods. Imported goods include, for example, fruit.

## The selectors for the Finlandia Prizes are known

![Hannu Lintu](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10015333)

Open image viewer

The chief conductor of the RSO, Hannu Lintu, chooses the winner of the Finlandia Prize for fiction. Photo: Nella Nuora / Yle

The selectors for the Finlandia Prizes have been decided.

This year, the winner of the Finlandia Prize for fiction will be chosen by conductor Hannu Lintu. He will be the chief conductor of the Radio Symphony Orchestra until next spring. After that, Lintu will move to the National Opera.

The winner of the non-fiction prize will be chosen by Nasima Razmyar, deputy mayor of Helsinki.

The winner of the Finlandia Prize for children's and young adults' literature will be chosen by actor Christoffer Strandberg.

The nominees in all categories will be announced in early November. The winners will be revealed on 25 November. The value of each prize is 30,000 euros.
