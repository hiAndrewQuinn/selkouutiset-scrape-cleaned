## President Niinistö spoke about Russia

![Sauli Niinistö.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5203,h_2926,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-114243364b0305e664ef/1689268372)

President Sauli Niinistö says that Finland is prepared for Russia's hostility. Photo: Tiina Jutila / Yle

The President of the Republic, Sauli Niinistö, says that Russia is putting more pressure on Finland than before. Niinistö spoke about the matter on Sweden's radio programme Sommar.

Niinistö said that Russia has not had time to react to Finland's NATO membership before. Now Russia is constantly producing more anti-Finnish propaganda.

The president mentioned, for example, the closing of Finland's consulate in St Petersburg and the harsh statements about Finland by Russia's Defence Minister Sergei Shoigu.

Niinistö said that Finland is prepared for Russia's hostility.

Navalny is currently serving a long prison sentence in Russia. The protesters demand Navalny's release.

## The clock tower rang

![The clock tower of Helsinki Central Station.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_2610,h_1468,x_0,y_149/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-115950764e20ca46238f/1730706720)

Open image viewer

The clock tower of Helsinki Central Station rang for the first time ever on Sunday at 3 pm. Photo: Mikko Ahmajärvi / Yle

History was made at Helsinki Central Station today, when the station's clock tower rang. It has never rung before.

The clock tower rang in the afternoon at 3 o'clock. The sound that was played from the tower was composed by Tuomas Skopa.

The clock tower rang in honour of the fact that it is 150 years today since the architect Eliel Saarinen was born. Eliel Saarinen designed Helsinki Central Station.

The station building was inaugurated in 1919. Its trademarks are the clock tower, about 50 metres high, and the stone men who carry lamps by the main door.

About 200,000 people pass through Helsinki Central Station every day.

## Duck and bear hunting began

![A Labrador retriever carries a mallard shot by a hunter.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3939,h_2215,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-115912364e0931dab4a7/1692505264)

Open image viewer

Waterfowl may be hunted until the end of the year. Photo: Jussi Nukari / Lehtikuva

Waterfowl hunting has begun today. For example, mallards, teal and goldeneyes may be hunted until the end of December.

Just under 100,000 hunters take part in waterfowl hunting in Finland every year.

Bear hunting has also begun today. In the whole country a total of 180 bears may be shot.
