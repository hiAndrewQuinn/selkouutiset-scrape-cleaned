Yle has again asked Finns which party they support. There are no big changes in party support.

The most popular party is the National Coalition Party. Over 24 percent of Finns support the National Coalition Party. The popularity of the National Coalition Party has risen slightly. The National Coalition Party is an opposition party, that is, it is not in the government.

The second most popular party is the SDP. The SDP is a government party. Over 19 percent of Finns support the SDP. The SDP's support has fallen slightly. The reason is perhaps the partying of Prime Minister Sanna Marin, which has been widely reported in the media.

The third most popular party is the Finns Party. Over 16 percent of Finns support the Finns Party.

The next most popular parties are the Centre Party, the Greens, the Left Alliance and the Swedish People's Party (RKP).

## Backup power plants were needed

![Twin-barrelled power plant behind a fence.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3946,h_2219,x_593,y_453/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-10049496319d76ca6685/1662638085)

Open image viewer

Fingrid's backup power plant in Forssa. Photo: Ville Välimäki / Yle

In Finland 2 backup power plants were started on Thursday, so that enough electricity was obtained. Backup power plants were needed, because the Olkiluoto 3 nuclear power plant was not producing enough electricity. There was a valve fault at Olkiluoto. That is why Olkiluoto's electricity production had to be reduced.

Fingrid, Finland's national grid company, decided in the morning that because of the situation the Huutokoski and Forssa backup power plants must be started.

Fingrid says that enough electricity was obtained. There was no shortage of electricity in Finland.

## Firewood trade

![Pile of firewood against a storehouse](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4608,h_2592,x_0,y_864/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/17-6009757b318249f427/1471355027)

Open image viewer

The rise in energy prices has increased wood burning. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

The energy crisis has greatly increased sales of firewood, that is, fuel wood. Some sellers of firewood have already sold all their wood. Some sellers of firewood are not taking new customers, so that there is enough wood for old customers.

The prices of fuel wood have risen because a lot of fuel wood is being bought. The price of raw timber and transport costs have also risen. Delivery of fuel wood may take a long time.

Many Finns want to heat with wood this winter, because the price of energy has risen a lot.

Ukraine has increased its counterattacks against Russia. The Ukrainian army has retaken areas that Russia occupied in the southern and eastern parts of Ukraine. In the Kharkiv region, for example, the Ukrainian army has advanced quickly.

Associate professor Antti Paronen of the National Defence University says that Ukraine has changed its war strategy. Ukraine has prepared well for the counterattack.

What helps Ukraine is that it has received more weapons that can shoot far.
