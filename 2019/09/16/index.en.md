# Monday 16.9.2019 (radio)

The price of crude oil has risen. Less phosphorus from hay fields runs into waters than was thought. A new sustainable and flexible material has been developed in Finland from spider web and cellulose. 60 percent of Finland's doctors are women.

## Oil price rose

![Petrol pump.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9089422)

Open image viewer

The price of petrol has risen by only a few cents. Photo: Oliver Berg / EPA

The price of crude oil has risen by almost 10 percent today.

The reason for the price rise is the attacks on Saudi Arabia on Saturday. In the attacks, oil fields and refineries were attacked with drones. Because of the attacks, Saudi Arabia has cut oil production to less than half of the usual amount. Saudi Arabia is the world's biggest oil exporter.

The fall in the crude oil price has been slowed by the fact that the USA has promised to sell oil abroad from its stocks.

At Finnish petrol stations, the price of petrol has risen by a few cents.

## New information on phosphorus from hay fields

![Cows at the Luke research station in Maaninka.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10970711)

Open image viewer

Cows look at the camera. Photo: Sami Takkinen / Yle

Farms pollute seas and lakes less than was thought. A new study says that half as much phosphorus runs from hay fields into the waters as was thought.

Phosphorus is in cow manure, which is spread on the fields so that the hay grows better. Some of the phosphorus runs off the fields with rainwater into rivers, lakes and finally the sea. Because of this, lakes and the sea become eutrophic.

Eutrophication means that, for example, a lot of nutrients for water plants and algae end up in the sea. Some plants increase, others decrease. The water becomes cloudy and the stones on the shore become slimy. Roach and other coarse fish increase, and good food fish such as whitefish, pike-perch and Baltic herring decrease.

Farms pollute the sea and lakes less than was thought, but still too much. Farms' phosphorus emissions are still too high, the researchers say.

## New material from spider web

![The liquid, syrupy silk protein turns into a strong and flexible fibre.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10972901)

Open image viewer

The researchers developed a new kind of material. Photo: Eeva Suorlahti / Aalto University &amp; VTT

A new, sustainable and flexible material has been developed in Finland from spider web and cellulose.

Aalto University says that the researchers glued wood cellulose fibres together with the silk protein of a spider web. The spider silk was not taken from nature; instead, the researchers made the same substance in a laboratory. This worked because the researchers know the DNA, that is, the genetic information, of spider silk.

The new material could perhaps replace plastic in the future. The new material breaks down in nature. It may also be possible to make cloth from it.

## Doctor's job interests women

![A drop in the foreground of the picture.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6323557)

Open image viewer

More women than men apply for medical studies. Photo: Toni Pitkänen / Yle

The job of a doctor interests young women more than men.

Before 2000, most of Finland's doctors were men, but then the number of women started to grow. Now about 60 percent of doctors and medical students in the field are women. The reason is that more women than men apply for the medical profession.

The universities hope that in the future as many men as women will apply for medical studies. Jyrki Mäkelä from the University of Oulu says that the female majority among doctors is not a problem. However, he says that there is also room in the field for men.
