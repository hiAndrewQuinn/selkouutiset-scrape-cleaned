# Tuesday 13.6.2017 (radio)

The Finns Party is breaking up. Sipilä is building a new government with those who have left. Demonstrators have been arrested in Russia. Helsinki Helpers help tourists. Bedbugs are already a nuisance in Helsinki.

## The Finns Party is breaking up

![MP Simon Elo, chair of the New Alternative group, speaks to the media in Helsinki on 13 June, after MPs who broke away from the Finns Party formed the group.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9667081)

Simon Elo is the chair of the New Alternative group. Photo: Jussi Nukari / Lehtikuva

The Finns Party has broken up. 22 MPs are leaving the Finns Party and forming their own parliamentary group. The former chair of the Finns Party, Foreign Minister Timo Soini, is one of those leaving. The other Finns Party ministers are also moving to the new group, whose name is New Alternative. The group is ready to support Juha Sipilä's government. The new group is the fourth largest in Parliament.

The Finns Party's new chair, Jussi Halla-aho, is surprised by the party's break-up. Halla-aho says that he believed only 1 or 2 MPs would leave. Halla-aho accuses those who left of breaking promises. During the chair campaign, all the candidates were ready to accept the election result.

## The government crisis is over

![Prime Minister and Centre chair Juha Sipilä arriving for an interview at Yle's A-Studio in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9666378)

Open image viewer

Prime Minister Juha Sipilä. Photo: Jussi Nukari / Lehtikuva

Prime Minister Juha Sipilä wants to continue co-operation with the New Alternative group. Sipilä says that the government crisis has been resolved and that he will not ask the President for resignation. The current ministers of the government can continue in their posts. The Prime Minister will give a statement on the new government to Parliament.

## In Russia police used harsh methods against demonstrators

![Police arrested hundreds of demonstrators in Moscow.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9665880)

Open image viewer

Police detained demonstrators in Russia. Photo: EPA/ Yuri Kochetkov

In Russia the police have arrested more than 1,500 demonstrators who oppose the Russian administration. The demonstrations were called by opposition leader Aleksei Navalny, who was sentenced to 30 days in prison.

Demonstrations have been held in more than 100 cities in different parts of Russia. The demonstrations have been the largest in Russia since 2012.

## Helsinki Helpers help tourists

![Young people in green Helsinki guide vests.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9663087)

Open image viewer

Helsinki Helpers speak many languages. Photo: Markku Pitkänen / Yle

Young city guides, known as Helsinki Helpers, are again moving on Helsinki's streets. They help tourists who arrive in Helsinki by ship in the summer for free. About 400,000 cruise passengers will come to Helsinki this summer on large cruise ships.

There are 20 city guides, and they speak many different languages well. They can be recognised by their green vests.

## Bedbugs have become a problem in Helsinki

![Bedbug.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9654716)

Open image viewer

A bedbug is about 5-8 millimetres long. Photo: City of Helsinki material bank, photographer Marjut Räsänen

Bedbugs, that is wall bedbugs, have become a problem in Helsinki's houses. Experts say that the number of bedbugs has clearly grown over the past 10 years. People bring bedbugs with them to Finland from trips abroad. A bedbug is a broad, flat, brown insect, which is quite small.

Bedbugs are hard to get rid of, because they live in small cracks in beds and walls. Bedbugs are unpleasant, because they suck people's blood at night.
