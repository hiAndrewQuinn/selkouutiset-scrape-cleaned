# Sunday 3.9.2017

North Korea has carried out a nuclear test again. The prime minister says that the work of the police must not be disturbed in asylum matters. Training a police dog takes a long time. Cold weather threatens the apple harvest.

## North Korea carried out a nuclear test

![Undated picture released by the North Korean news agency KCNA of Kim Jong-Un examining a metal case in an unnamed location.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9813326)

North Korean leader Kim Jong-Un examines a metal case in a secret place. Photo: KCNA / AFP

North Korea has once again carried out a nuclear test. The country says that it tested a new hydrogen bomb. The nuclear test was big, because an earthquake was measured in the area. The earth tremor was now 10 times stronger than a year ago, when North Korea also carried out a nuclear test. The mood in the region is now tense, because North Korea fired a missile over Japan earlier.

North Korea's 6th nuclear test has been widely condemned around the world. North Korea's neighbours, that is, Japan, China and South Korea, want to punish the country for the nuclear test. For example, they want to stop North Korea's trade. The Finnish state leadership is also worried about the nuclear test. Prime Minister Juha Sipilä wants the UN Security Council to deal with North Korea's actions soon.

## Prime Minister Sipilä says that the work of the authorities must not be disturbed in asylum matters

![Juha Sipilä](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9813488)

Open image viewer

Prime Minister Juha Sipilä. Photo: Jussi Nukari / Lehtikuva

Prime Minister Juha Sipilä says that the government does not forbid helping asylum seekers. Sipilä says that helping and preventing the work of the authorities are different things. For example, the police must not be prevented from giving a negative asylum decision. In Sipilä's opinion, churches must not hide asylum seekers who have to leave Finland, and the police's work must not be disturbed.

The government is planning changes to the law that will make illegal stay in Finland more difficult. The punishments are to be made tougher.

## Police dogs are an invaluable help to the police

![Dogs from left: Nalli, Jussi and Into.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9807288)

Open image viewer

Nalli, Jussi and Into are new police dogs. Photo: Police

Dogs are important helpers for the police. In Finland there are currently 260 police dogs, which search for drugs, explosives and people who have got lost in the forest. A dog can sometimes also be used to subdue a dangerous person.

Training a police dog takes a lot of time. The joint training of the police dog and the dog handler takes 2.5 years. Regular practice continues after that, for the whole life of the police dog.

Every year about 10 new handlers and dogs graduate from the police dog school. The dogs live at the handler's home.

## Cold summer threatens apple harvest

![Apples on a branch.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9811893)

Open image viewer

The apples are still unripe. Photo: Jaana Polamo / Yle

Finland's summer has been too cool for many plants. Cereals, berries and apples do not have time to ripen before autumn. There are many apples on the trees, but they are unripe. In Southern Finland there may be apples, but elsewhere in Finland there is not enough warmth for apples. An expert says that during the day it must be almost 20 degrees, and at night 10 degrees, otherwise the apples will not have time to ripen.
