## Wind power

![Picture of the wind turbine at wpd's Mäkikangas wind farm in Pyhäjoki](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1807,h_1016,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-852260613b2277a8577/1690884194)

Wind power is the cost-effective energy form of the future. Photo: wpd Windmanager Suomi Oy

Wind power is the economically most efficient way to increase electricity production, says a report by Sitra.

Finland can reach its emissions reduction target when transport, heating and industry start to run more on electricity. The report says that electricity consumption will double by 2050.

Sitra says that wind power on land can produce up to two thirds of Finland's electricity in 2050.

It is important to be able to store energy. Hydrogen technology may help.

The Finnish Wind Power Association calculates that Sitra's estimate means some thousands of new wind turbines.

## Electric cars

![electric car charging](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10951904)

Open image viewer

Electrifying transport reduces climate emissions. Photo: AOP

The American car company Ford intends to invest more in electric car production.

Ford is investing over 11 billion dollars, that is 9.8 billion euros, and is building 4 new factories. They will produce electric cars and their batteries.

Ford says that the investments put the company at the forefront as the USA moves towards electric transport. Ford says that 40–50 per cent of its cars may be fully electric by 2030.

## Food prices

![Basic foods in a grocery shop basket.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8609783)

Open image viewer

The price of the food basket is becoming more expensive next year. Photo: Mikko Stig / Lehtikuva

Food prices will rise strongly next year, forecasts Pellervo Economic Research.

Prices may rise by 1.8 per cent. That is more than the general rise in consumer prices.

PTT says that the rise in food prices is due to rising costs. In particular the prices of fertilisers, feed and construction have risen.

## Gambling problems

![A pensioner woman plays a gambling game.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5472,h_3078,x_0,y_316/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-848324612e1b0a12a01/1632830832)

Open image viewer

Gambling can be a big problem for those whose livelihood is weaker than that of wage earners. Photo: Mikko Koski / Yle

Gambling problems are more common among those who receive social benefits. Risky gambling is also more common than among others. This is what a study by the Institute for Health and Welfare shows.

31 per cent of problem gamblers or risk gamblers had received at least one Kela social benefit in 2016. Among all respondents the share was 21 per cent.

In the study, social benefits were unemployment benefit, sickness allowance, disability pension and social assistance.

## Explosion in Gothenburg

![Map of Gothenburg in Sweden](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12118221)

Open image viewer

Photo: Harri Vähäkangas / Yle

In Gothenburg in Sweden more than 20 people have ended up in hospital after an explosion in an apartment block.

Some were seriously injured. Hundreds of people were evacuated from their homes.

The explosion happened early in the morning. The cause is not clear. The police say that the explosion was not due to a natural cause, for example a gas explosion.
