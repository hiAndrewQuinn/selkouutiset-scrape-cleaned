# Wednesday 11.12.2019 (radio)

Finland is debating the Finns who are in the al-Hol camp in Syria. Smoking among young people has decreased. There has been a severe winter storm in Iceland.

## Finns at al-Hol

![A woman carried a child at the al-Hol camp on 9 December.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11110001)

The al-Hol camp holds family members of Isis terrorists. Photo: Delil Souleiman / AFP

Finland is debating the Finns who are in the al-Hol camp in Syria.

The al-Hol camp holds family members of Isis terrorists. Among them are also about 40 Finnish women and children.

Foreign Minister Pekka Haavisto said on Wednesday that Finland has not yet decided to bring the Finns from the al-Hol camp back to Finland.

Yle has seen an official paper which says that the matter is being prepared anyway. The paper says that Finns could be brought back from the al-Hol camp to Finland via Iraq.

The Finnish women travelled to Syria because they wanted to support terrorists themselves. That is why some Finns do not want the women to be helped. Many people also think the women are dangerous.

## Opposition criticism

![Kai Mykkänen (left), chair of the National Coalition parliamentary group, Harry Harkimo of Liike Nyt, and Jussi Halla-aho, chair of the Finns Party, at the press conference on the interpellation in Parliament on 11 December 2019.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11112586)

The leaders of the opposition parties tabled an interpellation to the government about the Finns in al-Hol. Photo: Heikki Saukkomaa / Lehtikuva

The Finns in the al-Hol camp are also being discussed in the Finnish Parliament.

The opposition parties think that Foreign Minister Pekka Haavisto has handled the matter badly. The opposition parties want to know whether family members of terrorists will come from Syria to Finland.

The opposition parties have made an official interpellation to the government about the Finns in al-Hol. The government will answer the interpellation next week.

## Teenage smoking

![Tobacco](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6144073)

Open image viewer

Teenage smoking has decreased. Photo: Yle

Finnish young people smoke less than before. A new study shows that 7 per cent of boys and 6 per cent of girls smoke every day.

10 years ago, young people smoked a lot more. Then 21 per cent of boys and 17 per cent of girls smoked every day.

There are differences between educational institutions. Students at upper secondary schools smoke less than vocational school students.

Although Finnish young people smoke less than before, adults' smoking did not decrease last year.

## Iceland's storm

![Iceland warning map](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11111010)

Open image viewer

Wind warnings have appeared on Iceland's weather map. Photo: Icelandic Meteorological Office

There has been a severe winter storm in Iceland. The storm began on Tuesday, and it continued on Wednesday too.

The storm stopped traffic across Iceland. Airports and main roads were closed. Authorities said that people should stay indoors during the storm.

Because of the storm, schools and libraries have been closed. The storm has torn roofs off and knocked down trees. Power has also been cut in various parts of Iceland.
