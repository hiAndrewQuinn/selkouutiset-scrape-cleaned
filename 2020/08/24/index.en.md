# Monday 24.8.2020 (radio)

Hundreds more workers are needed for corona testing. New travel restrictions have come into force. Viking Line starts co-operation negotiations. There are microplastics in Finnish fish too.

## More corona test workers are needed

![A person at the corona testing sampling point at Helsinki-Vantaa airport on 11 August 2020.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11502721)

Corona testing at Helsinki-Vantaa airport on 11 August. Photo: Roni Rekomaa / Lehtikuva

Hundreds more workers are needed for corona testing. The Ministry of Social Affairs and Health wants to raise the number to 20,000 tests a day.

For example, in Uusimaa about 10,000 samples are needed a day. That is more than three times the current number.

Perhaps 700 more sample takers are needed.

## Travel restrictions came into force

![Border guards in Tornio checking traffic at the Swedish border.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5256,h_2957,x_0,y_271/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-7111895f439fde53246/1598267834)

Finland has started again to restrict travel to Norway and back. Photo: Juuso Stoor / Yle

New travel restrictions have come into force. Border checks have been introduced today on the Norwegian border.

The border between Finland and Norway is over 700 kilometres long. The border can now only be crossed at border crossing points.

A travel document must be carried, for example a passport. Citizens of the Nordic countries are only required to show proof of identity, for example an identity card.

The quarantine recommendations apply to everyone except residents of the border community.

Travel restrictions were tightened because the number of corona infections in Norway has grown.

Internal border control has also been brought back on traffic between Finland and Iceland, Greece, Malta, Germany and Denmark.

## Viking Line starts co-operation negotiations

The ferry company Viking Line is starting co-operation negotiations. The co-operation negotiations concern the land staff, that is, the employees on land.

At most 200 people will be laid off or made redundant, or their work will become part-time.

About 570 people take part in the negotiations in Finland, Sweden, Estonia and the Åland Islands.

The aim is to make savings. Because of the corona pandemic there have been fewer passengers than before.

Viking Line will make a loss this year.

## There are microplastics in Finnish fish too

![A manta net collects micro-litter in the Baltic Sea](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11505113)

Open image viewer

Micro-litter was collected with a manta net, which resembles a manta ray, in the Baltic Sea in summer 2017. Photo: Maiju Lehtiniemi / SYKE

Microplastics are everywhere in Finland's waters. This is what a study by the Finnish Environment Institute and the University of Eastern Finland shows.

Most of Finland's microplastics come from car tyres, when rain washes the small plastic tips into the waters.

There are microplastics in fish, mussels and bottom-dwelling animals.

Open-sea herring, Baltic herring and three-spined stickleback contain small amounts of plastic.

For example, coastal perch, smelt, and perch and vendace from Lake Kallavesi contain more plastic. For example, one in every four vendace from Kallavesi has eaten plastic.

Most microplastics accumulate in blue mussels.

Microplastics end up in people mainly from crayfish and small fish that are eaten whole.

The study says that most fish do not contain microplastics. The Environment Institute says that it is still healthy to eat fish.
