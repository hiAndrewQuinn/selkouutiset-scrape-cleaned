The war in Ukraine came closer to Finland this week.

Ukraine used a drone to attack a Russian warship in the Gulf of Finland on Wednesday. The attack took place near Suursaari, about 40 kilometers from Kotka.

Minister of Defense **Antti Häkkänen** says that Finland has been in contact with Ukraine. Finland has told Ukraine that Finnish territory or airspace must not be used for attacks.

Finland also reminded Ukraine that there are many ordinary ships operating in the Gulf of Finland, which must be able to pass safely.

## Visas for Russian athletes

![Russian national team at the Channel One Cup in 2023.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5329,h_2997,x_0,y_258/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-17310656ae5fae96212a/1793456904)

Finland restricts Russian athletes' entry to the Games in Finland.

Previously, Russians were granted visas for short business trips. Now, a visa can only be obtained if the work is essential to Finland.

The International Court of Justice ruled in October that Russia must return to the World Ice Hockey Championships.

The decision sparked opposition in Finland and many other countries, as Russia continues its war in Ukraine. Therefore, Finland is restricting the entry of Russian athletes to Finland with new visa restrictions.

## Tax information and immigrants

![Three portraits of Miki Kuusi, Risto Siilasmaa and Saber Kordestanchi, with names and numbers next to the fence person.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1997,h_1123,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-17369106af5577d556aa/1794464630)

Miki Kuusi received the most income, Risto Siilasmaa received the second highest income and Saber Kordestanchi received the third highest income. Photo: Petteri Bülow / Yle, Silja Viitala / Yle, Hostaway Oy

Last year's tax data was published in Finland this week.

**Miki Kuusi**, the founder of the food delivery service Wolt, received the most income. He received approximately 132 million euros in income.

Entrepreneurs who have moved to Finland from elsewhere also rose to the top of the income list.

The third highest earner was **Saber Kordestanchi**. She moved from Iran to Finland to study.

Kordestanchi earned around 43 million euros last year. The large amount of income came from selling part of the company he founded.

Among the 20 highest earners, there were 3 people who had moved to Finland from abroad.

Kordestanchi estimates that tightening immigration regulations may reduce the influx of new talent to Finland.

## Fall graduates

![Noora Juusenaho is standing outside on the lake shore holding a white graduation cap in her hand.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5330,h_2998,x_5,y_353/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-17353786af1d68ed3f3f/1794234377)

Noora Juusenaho is this autumn's high school graduate. She has graduated from Parkano High School. At the autumn cap party (4.12.), she will be able to put on her cap. Photo: Kirsi Matson-Mäkelä / Yle

The names of the new graduates for the fall semester were published on Thursday.

Approximately 4,800 high school graduates will graduate in Finland this fall. This is approximately 200 fewer than last fall.

In about 20 percent of upper secondary schools, no students graduate in the fall. In about 10 percent of upper secondary schools, only one student graduates in the fall on average.

The size of the upper secondary school affects the number of students graduating in the autumn. A large upper secondary school can organize its studies so that some of the students graduate in the autumn.

In many small high schools, students study together for three years.

The graduation day for fall high school graduates is Friday, December 4th.
