# Tuesday 22.5.2018 (radio)

Environmental support for agriculture may decrease. Finns buy a lot of clothes from big hypermarkets. Many foreign students want to stay in Finland. Apple trees are blooming in southern Finland.

## Environmental support is decreasing

![In the picture, couch grass is being controlled after threshing in a barley field](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9206174)

Agriculture is both landscape and nature. Photo: Juha Kemppainen / Yle

Environmental support for agriculture may decrease. The European Commission proposes that EU agricultural funding be cut by 5 percent. The cut concerns the years 2021 - 2027.

The cut is bad for farmers and for Finland's nature, because the cut concerns environmental programmes. Many farmers have taken measures that, for example, protect waters.

The Finnish Association for Nature Conservation is worried, because the cut in environmental support threatens the endangered countryside and the countryside's nature.

## Finns buy clothes especially from hypermarkets

![Customers in an H&amp;M store in Stockholm.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10114255)

Open image viewer

Customers in an H&amp;M store in Stockholm. Photo: Jessica Gow / EPA

Finns buy a lot of clothes from big hypermarkets. The Finnish Commerce Federation found that the most clothes are bought from Prisma hypermarkets. Next come K-Citymarkets. The Swedish clothing giant Hennes & Mauritz is only in 3rd place.

But the list is different when the buyers' ages are compared. Women under 35 strongly prefer international fashion chain stores. People over 35 go a lot to hypermarkets and department stores. Men often buy clothes from sports shops and hypermarkets.

Last year, almost 4.6 billion euros was spent on clothes and shoes in Finland. That is an increase of one percent from 2016.

## Foreign students like it in Finland

![a man studying](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10134124)

Open image viewer

Many foreign university students want to stay in Finland after graduating. Photo: AOP

Many foreign students want to stay in Finland. One in four students in their final year of study is planning to stay in Finland after graduating. They plan to stay in Finland for over 2 years.

The student barometer says that 14 percent were planning further studies. Only just under a quarter were planning to move somewhere else.

Students from outside the EU plan to stay in Finland more than those who came from EU countries. 13 universities and 2 universities of applied sciences in Finland took part in the survey. Most of those who answered were studying for a master's degree or a doctorate.

## Apple trees are blooming in southern Finland

![There are impressive apple trees in Kumpula in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10217659)

Open image viewer

Apple trees are already in bloom in southern Finland. Photo: Kari Ahotupa / Yle

The warm and in places hot weather has made apple trees bloom in southern Finland. Usually apple trees do not bloom this early.

Bees and other pollinators are now important. They affect how good the harvest of fruit trees and berries will be.

A home gardener can make bees feel at home in the garden by planting nectar plants. Willows also provide food for bees in early spring. Researcher Terhi Ryttäri says that it is also a good idea to leave nesting places for pollinators in the garden. Good nesting places for insects are corners where, for example, piles of brushwood or rotting wood are left.
