# Saturday 18.3.2018 (radio)

Talks about North Korea continue in Finland. In Kitee there is a ski school for asylum-seeker children. Poet Heli Laaksonen has tested new words in the Finnish language.

## Talks about North Korea continue

![Helsinki Market Square](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10121587)

Helsinki is the place for an important meeting. Photo: Sergio Azenha / AOP

An important meeting will be held in Finland on Sunday. The meeting is linked to the situation in North Korea. Finland itself will not take part in the meeting.

On Sunday, representatives of North Korea are also coming to Finland. In recent days, the situation in North Korea has been negotiated in Sweden.

The situation in North Korea is difficult because the country has been in a dispute with South Korea and the USA. They do not like that North Korea has developed a nuclear weapon.

North Korean leader Kim Jong-un and US President Donald Trump plan to meet this spring.

## A ski school for asylum-seeker children

![Children from the International Ski School in Kitee.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10117626)

Abdullah, Fatima and the other asylum-seeker children like skiing. Photo: Tanja Perkkiö / Yle

Then to Kitee, in eastern Finland.

In Kitee there is a ski school for asylum seekers. Children who live in the Kitee reception centre go to the ski school.

This week, the ski school pupils got to visit the biathlon World Cup races. The races were 100 kilometres away in Kontiolahti.

The visit was arranged because Kaisa Mäkäräinen is the children's sponsor athlete. Mäkäräinen is a famous biathlete. The children also met Mäkäräinen, who is their big idol.

The Kitee ski school pupils have got skis and ski clothes from the International Ski School. It is an organisation that helps asylum-seeker children to settle into the Finnish winter.

## New words in a poet's test

![Poet Heli Laaksonen](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10120531)

Open image viewer

In Heli Laaksonen's opinion, "sydäniskuri" (heart shocker) is a good word. Photo: Pipsa Uutto / Yle

The Finnish language officially gets 600 new words this year. They are going into the Language Office's dictionary. The Language Office looks after the Finnish language.

Yle News asked poet Heli Laaksonen to test the new words. Laaksonen writes poems in dialect, and she loves the words of the Finnish language.

When Laaksonen tests new words, she thinks about what the word means and how the word sounds.

Laaksonen likes many of the new words. One of them is, for example, "sydäniskuri" (heart shocker). Laaksonen is always happy when a Finnish word is found to replace a foreign word. It is much easier for a Finn to say "sydäniskuri" than "defibrillaattori". That has been the name of the device until now. "Sydäniskuri" also tells well that the device starts the heart with electric shocks.

Laaksonen does not like new foreign words. It is hard for a Finn to know what they mean. For example, "drooni" (drone) is now going into the Language Office's dictionary. It means a small device that looks like a helicopter. I suggest the word "minikopteri" (mini-copter) instead of "drooni", poet Laaksonen says.
