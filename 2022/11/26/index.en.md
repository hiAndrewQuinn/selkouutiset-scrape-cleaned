## Electricity

![Four-part power socket](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_2978,h_1675,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-81510460b89ae59ec51/1622711138)

Everyone should save electricity. Photo: Sannimari Lehtilä / Yle

In Finland, people are trying to save electricity. The aim is for Finns to use 10 percent less electricity than before.

Electricity is to be saved so that there is enough electricity in winter too. In frosty weather, heating uses a lot of electricity.

Also, the rise in the price of electricity will slow down if electricity consumption decreases. That is why saving electricity helps everyone.

At the start of November, electricity consumption had already fallen by almost 10 percent. Last week the weather got colder, and electricity consumption fell by only 3 percent.

## Iran

![Protesters have set a fire on the street.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1278,h_717,x_0,y_82/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-10186316342b9f6e2629/1665576072)

Protests and demonstrations continue in Iran. The human rights organisation Human Rights Watch says that police have arrested many thousands of protesters.

Students and journalists, for example, have also been arrested. The human rights organisation says that people have been tortured during detention.

Up to 400 people have died in connection with the protests in Iran. Among them are also children.

The protests in Iran began when police beat a young woman so badly that she died. In the police's view, the woman did not wear the hijab, that is, the headscarf, in the right way.

Finns hope for more services for the elderly. Photo: Hilma Toivonen / Yle

According to Finns, there are not enough elderly care services.

About 50 percent of Finns think that there are too few elderly care services. This is what a new survey says.

Most respondents in the survey thought that there are enough health services. The respondents were especially satisfied with exercise and sports services.

## Prime minister favourites

![Prime Minister Sanna Marin at the oral question time in Parliament in Helsinki on 24 November 2022.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5120,h_2880,x_0,y_221/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-1038977637f7dda7403d/1670408821)

Open image viewer

Prime Minister Sanna Marin. Photo: Antti Aimo-Koivisto / Lehtikuva

Prime Minister Sanna Marin is also Finns' favourite to be prime minister of the next government. This is what a Uutissuomalainen survey says.

Marin is the chair of the SDP. In the Uutissuomalainen survey, the second most popular prime ministerial candidate is National Coalition Party chair Petteri Orpo.

There are elections in Finland next spring, at the beginning of April. In the elections, MPs are elected to the Finnish Parliament, that is, the Eduskunta. After the elections, the parties agree on a new government.

## The weather

On Sunday it will snow a little. Snow falls in southern Finland and western Finland. Less than 5 centimetres of snow is expected. Across the whole country it is a little frosty on Sunday.

On Monday it will snow a little in eastern Finland. The temperature is still below freezing. Later next week the weather will warm up slightly, that is, the frost will weaken.
