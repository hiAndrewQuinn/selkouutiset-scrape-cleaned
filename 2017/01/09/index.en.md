# Monday 9.1.2017 (radio)

The basic income experiment has begun. Most Finns want to lose weight. A snowstorm helped the Saimaa ringed seal. Posti congratulates 100-year-old Finland.

## The basic income experiment has begun

![Juha Järvinen](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9393080)

Open image viewer

Juha Järvinen from Kurikka believes that with basic income he can become an entrepreneur again. Photo: Ville Hakonen / Yle

The basic income experiment has started in practice. For two (2) years, the people in the test group will get 560 euros tax-free into their account every month. 2,000 unemployed job seekers were chosen for the experiment. The first payment day was on Monday.

Among other things, the experiment wants to encourage people to find work. For example, many people do not want to take a job if their take-home income does not grow. Such situations are called incentive traps. In the basic income experiment, a person can keep both their wages and their basic income, and benefits are not reduced.

Unemployed Juha Järvinen says that now his dream of his own company can come true. Järvinen is an entrepreneur who went bankrupt and has been without work for many years. Järvinen believes that it is now worth taking, for example, gig work.

## Most Finns want to lose weight

![Graphic](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9386118)

Open image viewer

Only a few Finns are completely happy with what the scale shows. Photo: Yle News graphics

After Christmas, people talk a lot about losing weight. In a Yle survey, 84 percent of women wanted to lose weight. Also more than 70 percent of men want to lose weight.

Expert Patrik Borg says that weight-loss diets do not solve the problem. Losing weight permanently requires a change in lifestyle and time.

About two thirds of Finnish men and about half of Finnish women are overweight.

## A snowstorm helped Saimaa ringed seals

![Saimaa ringed seal on the ice](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7756046)

Open image viewer

Photo: Eero Korhola

The weekend snowstorm has helped Saimaa ringed seals.

The Saimaa ringed seal gives birth to a pup, or baby, on the ice of the lake. The pup needs a snowdrift for protection. On bare ice, the pups die in the frost or a predator catches them. The Saimaa ringed seal gives birth around the turn of February and March.

Winter nests can also be made for Saimaa ringed seals if not enough snow falls. Metsähallitus will decide next week whether to start making winter nests. In that case, more than 100 volunteer snow shovellers are needed to help.

At the moment there are about 360 Saimaa ringed seals. The goal of protecting the seal is a population of 400 animals by the year 2020.

## Finland gets congratulations

![White rose.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6801424)

Open image viewer

Now it is Finland's jubilee year. Independent Finland turns 100 years old on 6 December. Photo: Yle

100-year-old Finland is being celebrated in many different ways. Posti is collecting a record number of names from people living in Finland, Finns living abroad and other friends of Finland for a shared jubilee year greeting.

You can congratulate Finland on the website www.onnittelesuomea.fi.

Posti is also collecting names at events in different towns. The collection of names started in Lapland on Monday, and the collection is moving towards the south. In addition to your name, you can leave a short congratulation message. In honour of the jubilee year, the message can be 100 characters long.
