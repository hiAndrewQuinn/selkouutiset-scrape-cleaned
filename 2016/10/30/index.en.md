## Older workers

![An empty desk](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5662747)

Open image viewer

Men over 50 are often laid off. Photo: Yle

In working life, a man over 50 has the biggest risk of getting the sack. The risk is many times greater than for young people. Women are also laid off less than men.

For example, in retail and in the service sectors, men over 50 have a clearly greater risk of getting the sack than young people. In industry the risk of the sack is no longer as great as before.

Companies also prefer to hire young rather than old workers for new permanent jobs.

## Children's lying

![The picture shows a child and a soft toy.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6996076)

Open image viewer

A small child says what the adult wants to hear. Photo: Kia-Frega Tyynismaa

An adult does not always notice if a child lies. A new American study says that an adult notices only about 50 percent of a child's lies.

Finnish expert Nina Sajaniemi says that adults believe too easily what a child says. An adult notices lying better if he looks at the child's facial expressions.

Sajaniemi also says that a child often does not intend to lie. The child just thinks about what the adult wants to hear and then says that. A small child may not even understand that he is lying.

Adults themselves think that they notice children's lying fairly easily, but that is not the case.

## The Swedish language

![Tove Jansson.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7136269)

Open image viewer

Tove Jansson wrote the Moomin books in Swedish. Photo: Seppo Sarkkinen / Yle

The week of Swedishness begins on Monday. During the week Swedish-language events are held in different parts of Finland, for example in Helsinki and Turku.

The most important day of the week is Sunday. Then it is Swedish Day, in Swedish Svenska dagen.

Swedish Day is always on 6 November. Then the Swedish language is celebrated, which is Finland's second official language.

![Marshal Mannerheim on the wall of the Lappeenranta Officers' Club.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9218293)

Open image viewer

Mannerheim's mother tongue was Swedish. Photo: Yle

About 300,000 Finns speak Swedish as their mother tongue. Swedish-speaking Finns, or Finland Swedes, live mostly in coastal areas. However, Finland-Swedish culture is important for the whole of Finland.

Many famous Finns are Swedish-speaking. For example, the mother tongue of the writer Tove Jansson and of president and marshal Mannerheim was Swedish.
