## May Day celebrations

May Day is being celebrated in Finland.

May Day is being celebrated again in the same way as before corona. In the 2 previous years, online celebrations were organised because of corona.

Now people have gathered together again to celebrate May Day. Events on May Eve have been organised in many cities.

May Eve is especially a festival day for students. The best-known event of May Eve is at Helsinki's Market Square, where the statue of Havis Amanda, that is, Manta, again got a student cap at 6 p.m.

Manta always gets the student cap from students. This year Manta got the cap from students of the University of Helsinki.

## The Finns Party

![Chair Riikka Purra at the Finns Party's party council meeting.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4000,h_2250,x_0,y_177/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-948465626cfd807a67a/1651310160)

Finns Party chair Riikka Purra. Photo: Antti Aimo-Koivisto / Lehtikuva

The Finns Party wants Finland to join the defence alliance NATO. The party decided on this on Saturday.

Among the parties, the SDP and the Left Alliance have not yet said whether they support NATO membership. The other parties are ready for Finland to apply for membership of NATO.

In Finland, the defence alliance NATO is being discussed because of the war in Ukraine. Europe's security situation changed when Russia attacked Ukraine.

In many people's view, Finland is in a safer position if Finland is in NATO. There are 30 member countries in NATO.

Finland may decide as early as May whether it applies for NATO membership or not.

## Tobacco law

![A woman's hand with a burning cigarette between her fingers.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11960038)

Open image viewer

The tobacco law protects children. Photo: Petteri Bülow / Yle

Smoking on playgrounds and at public beaches will be banned.

The new tobacco law comes into force on Sunday. It bans smoking on playgrounds and stipulates that in summer smoking is not allowed on public beaches.

This is how children are meant to be protected from tobacco smoke.

## South Asian heat

![A person walks across a huge heap of rubbish.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_6008,h_3372,x_0,y_627/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-948423626c16d0465d6/1651251054)

A firefighter at an Indian landfill site. Photo: Idrees Mohammed / EPA

It is hot in South Asia. In India and Pakistan the temperature is over 40 degrees.

Because of the hot weather, for example, air conditioners are used a lot. Electricity is already in short supply, and in the Indian capital Delhi power cuts are being warned of.

Hot weather also causes health problems. Many children have also fallen ill because of the hot weather.

The situation is made worse by the fact that in some cities a fire has broken out at a landfill site. In Delhi, for example, a waste mountain 60 metres high is burning.

In Finland May Day is celebrated in cool weather.
