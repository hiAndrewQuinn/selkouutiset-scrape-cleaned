## The Presidential Palace reception

![The Presidential Palace with festive lighting](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5670,h_3189,x_0,y_9/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6193675de8adba56e05/1575529942)

Finland has celebrated Independence Day.

The most important event of Independence Day is the celebration at the Presidential Palace. This year the Presidential Palace reception was held again.

One year ago and two years ago the Presidential Palace reception was not held because of corona. This year President Sauli Niinistö and his wife Jenni Haukio invited about 1,300 guests to the Presidential Palace.

![A woman sits in an armchair.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12683644)

Jyväskylä nurse Helena Pohjolainen received an invitation from the President to the Presidential Palace reception. Pohjolainen says that at the reception she also represents all her work colleagues. Photo: Marja Roiha / Yle

Among the guests are, for example, nurses and other health care workers. Many of them received an invitation to the Presidential Palace reception last year too, but then the celebration was cancelled.

Normally the guests of honour at the Presidential Palace reception are veterans and Lottas. They served the Finnish army during the war about 80 years ago.

This year the veterans and Lottas celebrated at the Presidential Palace already last Thursday. The presidential couple organised a celebration of their own for the old veterans and Lottas, so that they did not have to fear coronavirus infection.

## Independent Finland 105 years old

![The raising of the flag on Independence Day at Tähtitorninmäki in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4032,h_2268,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-1044234638eeafe1a032/1670311850)

The Finnish flag was raised on the flagpole on Independence Day. Photo: Petteri Lindholm / Yle

Finland's Independence Day is always 6 December. Finland became an independent country on 6 December 1917, that is, 105 years ago.

Before Finland became independent, Finland was part of the Russian Empire for about 100 years. The last Russian emperor tried to make Finns into Russians. However, Finns wanted to remain Finns and decide on their own affairs. That is why independence is very important to Finns.

For most of its history, Finland has been part of Sweden. Finland was part of Sweden for more than 600 years. That is why Finland and Sweden are still very similar countries today.

## Different Independence Day celebrations

![Soup is served to people in the market square.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12683662)

Open image viewer

Independence Day celebration for the poor at Hakaniemi market square. Photo: Juha Kivioja / Yle

Various celebrations have been organised on Independence Day in different parts of Finland. The aim is for everyone to have the chance to celebrate Independence Day.

For example, in Helsinki a celebration for the poor was held at the Hakaniemi market square. In addition, there was a celebration in Pasila, Helsinki, for everyone who has no home.

In Vantaa a big multicultural Independence Day celebration was held. At the Vantaa celebration, new Finnish citizens were also celebrated.

In Tampere a children's Independence Day dance was held. At the dance, children could dance, listen to music and see a circus performance.
