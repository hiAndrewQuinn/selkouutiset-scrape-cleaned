## Finland and Nato

![The steps of Parliament House were largely covered in snow.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5000,h_2812,x_0,y_233/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-1049631639c973917388/1677920580)

Finland's primary aim is to join Nato together with Sweden. The countries submitted the applications at the same time. Photo: Silja Viitala / Yle

Most parties say that Finland must be ready to join Nato also without Sweden. The chairpersons of the parliamentary parties answered Yle's survey.

The National Coalition, the Finns Party, the Centre, the Christian Democrats and Liike Nyt support joining Nato alone.

Only the Left Alliance opposes leaving Sweden behind.

RKP and the Greens cannot say, and the SDP did not want to answer the question.

Still, the primary aim of all parties is that Finland and Sweden join Nato at the same time and quickly.

Of the Nato countries, only Turkey and Hungary have not yet approved the countries joining the defence alliance. Turkey still has demands towards Sweden.

## Greenwashing

![Labels with the words bio, green and eco hang on a clothesline.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3888,h_2185,x_0,y_200/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-412884595628447a7c3/1498818733)

Open image viewer

Companies' claims about environmental friendliness cannot always be trusted. Photo: Mostphotos/Jakub Jirsak

The European Commission is preparing a directive that will address companies' greenwashing. The directive is a guideline for the legislation of EU countries.

Greenwashing means claiming that an activity is environmentally friendly, even though the ways of working are not changed.

Companies often use the words green, environmentally friendly, responsible or carbon neutral. The EU Commission says that 40 percent of environmental claims are false.

The Finnish Environment Institute found that more than half of environmental claims are improper.

More than 200 different environmental labels are in use in the EU area. The Commission says that the labels cannot be trusted.

## Police training

![Police University of Applied Sciences in autumn 2021.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4032,h_2268,x_0,y_359/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-868977616ec6df4193e/1634649944)

Open image viewer

There is only one Police University of Applied Sciences in Finland, where police officers are trained. The institution is located in Hervanta in Tampere. Photo: Miikka Varila / Yle

The Police University of Applied Sciences has had a shortage of applicants for its entrance exams.

More than half of those who have been invited to the tests do not arrive at the entrance exams. Partly because of this, not all the places on the course have been filled.

Usually about 500 applicants take part in the first stage of the entrance exam. In this spring's entrance exams only 250 applicants took part. The reasons were, among others, corona and illnesses.

Last autumn about 300 students were selected for the training. The target was 400.

A normal number of applicants is coming again for the autumn application.

## Shrovetide

![Shrovetide buns.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9440880)

Open image viewer

A Shrovetide bun is a bun that nowadays is filled with whipped cream and either almond paste or jam. It is a variation of the German Heißweck. Photo: Olli Koski / Yle

Today Shrove Sunday has been celebrated.

Traditionally, Shrovetide marks the start of the wait for Easter. The name of the festival comes from entering the fast.

Church and pagan traditions are combined in Shrovetide.

Finnish Shrovetide traditions are the toboggan hill, pea soup and Shrovetide buns.
