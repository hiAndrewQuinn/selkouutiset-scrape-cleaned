# Tuesday 21.3.2017 (radio)

Finns' opinions about asylum seekers. Air traffic strike. Paternity leave. The winter sleep of the brown bears in Ranua has ended.

## Finns are shy about giving their opinion on asylum seekers

![Asylum seekers and those showing their opinion with them at Helsinki's Rautatientori in sunny spring weather.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9519304)

Giving an opinion on asylum seekers is a difficult matter for Finns.  Photo: Antti Aimo-Koivisto / Lehtikuva

A large study on how Finns think about asylum seekers has been published.

A large part of Finns do not dare to say what they think about asylum seekers. People are afraid of being called a racist or a "tolerance-lover". "Tolerance-lover" has become a mocking name for a tolerant person.

Every second respondent said in the study that in Finland you cannot freely give your opinion on asylum seekers. People think that it is hard to discuss asylum matters calmly and matter-of-factly in Finland.

According to the study, Finns are ready to help people who need help.

They think that asylum seekers must adapt to the Finnish way of life. That means that they must give up some of their own way of life.

More than a thousand people took part in the study.

## No air traffic strike is coming

![Finnair planes at Helsinki-Vantaa.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9513484)

Open image viewer

Planes will fly normally at the end of the week.  Photo: Markku Ulander / Lehtikuva

There will be no air traffic strike in Finland at the end of the week. A new strike threatened air traffic on Wednesday, Thursday and Friday.

Employers and employees had been arguing for many weeks about how work is done at airports in a company called Airpro.

The dispute was settled on Tuesday evening. The dispute had been negotiated for almost 30 hours.

Airpro is a big company at Finnish airports. It carries suitcases onto aeroplanes, for example, and does security checks on people.

## Paternity leave is often short

![Heikki Soini outside with his child.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9519036)

Open image viewer

Heikki Soini is on paternity leave.  Photo: Retu Liikanen / Yle

In Finland it is possible for a father to stay at home with the child when the child is small, under 2 years old. This is called paternity leave.

A large part of fathers take paternity leave but only for a short time, about 4 weeks. Paternity leave can be taken for 9 weeks.

It is thought that the reason is money. During paternity leave you cannot get a salary. Instead you get paternity allowance from Kela. The paternity allowance is less than a salary.

## The brown bears of Ranua have woken up

![Bear Jehu resting](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7149603)

Open image viewer

A few years ago, Jehu the bear of Ranua Wildlife Park enjoyed the sunshine like this.  Photo: Jarmo Honkanen/Yle

Ranua Wildlife Park says that the winter sleep of its brown bears has ended. Ranua Wildlife Park is in Southern Lapland.

The bears have slept their winter sleep for almost 5 months. They went to sleep a little earlier than before, at the turn of October and November.

Now the bears have been scratching at the hatch of the den. They want to get out into the open air already.

In Helsinki, the bears at the Korkeasaari Zoo woke up from their winter sleep already a month ago.
