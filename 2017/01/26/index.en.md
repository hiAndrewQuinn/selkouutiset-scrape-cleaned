## Finnish cities are safe

![Street safety graphic. Source: Police / Preliminary data of the Street Safety Index 2016](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9420488)

Open image viewer

It is safe in Finnish cities. According to the police, there are no dangerous suburbs.  Photo: Yle

Finland's big cities are safer than before, police statistics show.

The safest city is Espoo. According to the statistics, the fewest crimes, such as robberies and assaults, happen in Espoo.

The most crimes happen in Helsinki. One reason is that people come to the centre of Helsinki at weekends to party, and many people are drunk, the police say.

However, the police say that there are no dangerous places in Helsinki that people should avoid.

## THL is worried about measles

![A patient is given a measles vaccination at a health centre in Berlin on 23 February.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7824537)

Open image viewer

Small children get the MMR vaccine, which protects against measles. Photo: Lukas Schulze / AFP / Lehtikuva

The Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare THL is worried about measles.

THL says that in Finland there are many areas where too many children go without the MMR vaccine. The MMR vaccine protects against measles as well as mumps and rubella.

THL says that a child can get measles, for example, on a trip abroad if the child has not been vaccinated. In Finland the child can also spread measles to other children who have not been vaccinated. Measles spreads easily and can turn into an epidemic, which means that a big group of people get sick.

Measles is a dangerous disease that can kill. Thanks to the MMR vaccine, measles was almost completely wiped out in Finland.

## Survey: Support for the Centre Party falls, SDP is number one

![Party support in January 2017.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9425049)

Open image viewer

The government parties lost support. The SDP was the most popular party.  Photo: Yle News graphics

Support for Prime Minister Juha Sipilä's Centre Party is falling.

In Yle's survey, 19 percent said that they support the Centre Party. The most support went to the SDP, which is not in the government. Support for the SDP was more than 20 percent. More than 18 percent supported the National Coalition Party.

Finland will hold municipal elections on 9 April. The residents of a municipality vote on which politicians get into the municipal council, that is, to decide on the municipality's matters.

## Mexico rejects the US wall

![The border fence between Mexico and the United States in Tijuana on 10 November. ](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9292148)

Open image viewer

Border fences have already been built between Mexico and the United States. Photo: Alejandro Zepeda / EPA

The President of Mexico says that Mexico will not pay for the wall that the United States wants on the border between the countries.

President Enrique Peña Nieto says that he opposes the wall.

US President Donald Trump ordered yesterday that building the wall will start. The wall costs billions of dollars, and Trump plans to demand the money from Mexico.

Trump wants a wall on the border because many people come from Mexico to the United States without permission.
