# Tuesday 19.11.2019 (radio)

The postal strike continues. A criminal group has tried to open an illegal entry route into Finland from Russia. There is great variation even in babies' sleep rhythms. Men's Day was celebrated on Tuesday.

## The postal strike continues

![Heidi Nieminen (left) and Tuomas Aarto, managing director of the service sector employers Palta ry, surrounded by the media](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11075627)

The postal strike continues.

The postal and logistics sector union rejected the National Conciliator's compromise proposal. PAU did not accept the proposal, because among other things there is a dispute about the collective agreement for parcel sorters.

Posti does not intend to withdraw its decision to move the sorters to another collective agreement.

PAU and the service sector employers Palta will continue mediation on Wednesday at 13:00.

The strike began last Monday. About 9,000 employees are on strike.

## Illegal border crossing discovered on the eastern border

![Border markers on the Finland-Russia border](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3840,h_2160,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6130855dd2b9b6dd1ee/1574146868)

Open image viewer

The criminal group intended to bring Iranians into Finland through the forest. Photo: Kari Saastamoinen/Yle

The Border Guard suspects that a professional criminal group has tried to open an illegal entry route. The route was meant to run from Russia to Finland.

An investigation says that the group's operations are led from Central Europe by an Afghan person. He has previously brought Iranians and Afghans to the EU area through Turkey and Greece.

The group's activity was discovered when it illegally brought Iranians from Russia to Finland in June. 3 Iranian men walked a 15-kilometre route through the forest from near Vyborg to Ylämaa in Lappeenranta.

The criminals followed the journey in real time on a smartphone. The men were caught. The Border Guard says that it was a test trip.

Finland was chosen as the route because Iranians can easily get a visa to Russia. From Finland, on the other hand, you can get to Central Europe, because Finland is in the Schengen area.

The Russian border guard has already this year prevented about 90 Iranians from crossing the border illegally into Finland.

## A baby's sleep is important

![A baby sleeping in a cot.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11051566)

Open image viewer

Even a small baby's sleep rhythm matters. Photo: Simon Denson / AOP

A broad Finnish study says that there is great variation in small children's sleep rhythms and in the quality of their sleep.

The study includes 1700 families, and children's sleep has been monitored from babyhood onwards. Research leader Juulia Paavonen of the National Institute for Health and Welfare says that sleep in infancy is important for the child's development.

Families need support with children's sleep very early, and more than now.

Sleep rhythm can be supported with everyday methods. These include, for example, lighting, the timing of daytime naps and regular routines.

## Men's Day is not yet official in Finland

![Stock photo, a man and a dog at sunset.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11075336)

Open image viewer

The purpose of the day is to draw attention especially to the wellbeing of boys and men. Photo: Paul Marriot / AOP

International Men's Day is celebrated around the world today. In Finland it is not celebrated, at least not officially.

The Almanac Office says that Men's Day is not yet established enough in Finland, and so it is not in the calendar.

Men's Day draws attention especially to the health of boys and men, gender equality and the promotion of good role models.
