# Monday 6.9.2021 (radio)

The Finnish government has negotiated on corona restrictions. Many Finns think that Finland's debt is growing too fast. The Radio Symphony Orchestra has a new chief conductor. Climate change brings health problems. The weather is warming up.

## Corona restrictions

![ Prime Minister Sanna Marin (SDP) answers media questions as she arrives at the negotiations on updating the government's hybrid strategy at Säätytalo in Helsinki on 6 September.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5120,h_2880,x_0,y_328/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-850426613602a5bc517/1630939020)

The Finnish government has negotiated on corona restrictions. The government's goal is that the corona restrictions can be ended completely.

The goal can be reached if the corona vaccinations go well. Prime Minister Sanna Marin estimates that Finland can live normally again when at least 80 per cent of people over 12 years old have received the corona vaccination.

The corona restrictions may be ended as early as October.

## National debt

![A pile of coins is lifted with fingers.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1920,h_1080,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5142935bb5d6cb9bae4/1538643719)

Open image viewer

In the summer Finland already had debt of 130 billion euros. Photo: Seppo Sarkkinen

Many Finns think that Finland's debt is growing too fast. However, there are differences between the supporters of different parties.

In a survey by the newspaper Maaseudun Tulevaisuus, 80-90 per cent of the supporters of the Finns Party, the National Coalition and the Centre were worried about national debt. For example, only about 20 per cent of the supporters of the Left Alliance were worried about national debt.

National debt has grown during the corona crisis. At the end of July Finland had debt of about 130 billion euros.

## Chief conductor Nicholas Collon

![Conductor Nicholas Collon conducts](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3142,h_1767,x_0,y_64/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5529225ca1f855d7281/1554118795)

Open image viewer

RSO's new chief conductor Nicholas Collon. Photo: Chris Christodolou

The Radio Symphony Orchestra, or RSO, has a new chief conductor. The new chief conductor is Nicholas Collon.

Collon starts work in Helsinki this week. The British Collon is the RSO's first foreign chief conductor.

The role of chief conductor is important. He leads and plans the orchestra's performances.

The Radio Symphony Orchestra is Yleisradio's orchestra. Its task is to promote Finnish music culture.

The RSO was founded almost 100 years ago, in 1927. The orchestra has 99 musicians.

## Climate change and health

![An elderly woman's hand holds a coffee cup with a strawberry pattern. The corner of a newspaper is also visible on the table. ](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12037600)

Open image viewer

Heat is taxing an elderly person's health. Photo: Ismo Pekkarinen / AOP

Climate change brings health problems. This is reported by the medical journal The Lancet.

For example, more elderly people die from heat today than before. Heat deaths among the elderly have increased by over 50 per cent in 20 years.

Because of climate change, many other diseases are also more common than before. For example, allergies have increased because of climate change.
