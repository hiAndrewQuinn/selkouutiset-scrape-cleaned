## Regional councils

![A voter puts a ballot slip in the ballot box. An election official opens the lid of the box beside them.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_6036,h_3395,x_0,y_251/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-90513761ed548934c03/1642944937)

In the regional elections, the new regional councils were elected with a female majority. Photo: Juho Liukkonen / Yle

We are still telling about yesterday's regional elections.

In Finland's first regional elections, 21 regional councils were elected. They decide on social and health services and on rescue services. No elections were held in Helsinki or in Åland.

Of all those elected, 53 per cent are women and 47 per cent are men.

Many professionals in the social, health and rescue sectors got into the regional councils.

## Municipalities' own representatives

![A nurse walks along a corridor at Mikkeli Central Hospital.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4442,h_2499,x_0,y_458/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6623835e8c4c8141cfc/1658227741)

In Finland, 51 municipalities were left without their own regional councillor in the regional elections.

292 municipalities took part in the elections. About 16 per cent of the municipalities were left without their own councillor.

For example, in Pirkanmaa, Akaa and Ikaalinen did not get their own councillor. In Southwest Finland, Kustavi and Kemiönsaari were left without one, and in Lapland, Enontekiö and Muonio.

In the regional councils, however, decisions are made about social, health and rescue services across the whole area.

## Shortage of nurses at the children's hospital

![The new children's hospital in Helsinki photographed from the air. It is winter, there is snow on the ground and the sun shines brightly. The children's hospital wall has many different colours.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4657,h_2619,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-89971761d879b4794cb/1661764681)

Open image viewer

The new children's hospital was taken into use in 2018. Although the hospital is located in Helsinki, all the country's sickest children are treated there. Photo: Retu Liikanen / Yle

The intensive care unit of the new children's hospital is in crisis because of a shortage of nurses, Yle's MOT programme says.

MOT's investigation shows that the children's hospital has recently experienced several dangerous situations.

Parents of seriously ill children say that they are worried about the safety of patients.

In a short time, 17 intensive care nurses have left the children's hospital. The cause of the staff shortage is not the corona epidemic. The nurses talk about exhaustion, stress and pressure.

The Helsinki and Uusimaa hospital district does not think the situation is alarming.

## Päivi Räsänen's trial

![Päivi Räsänen smiling in the courtroom.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5120,h_2880,x_0,y_310/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-90577561ee5ea692487/1643011811)

MP Päivi Räsänen's trial has started. Räsänen is a Christian Democrat. Photo: Lehtikuva

The trial of MP Päivi Räsänen has started in the Helsinki District Court.

Räsänen is charged with incitement against a group of people.

The charges concern writings in which Räsänen is suspected of having belittled homosexual people.

Räsänen denies the charges.

## Branded clothes and crimes

![Spring fashion clothes: denim and lace.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7831855)

Open image viewer

Young people have started robbing branded clothes from other young people. Photo: Miia Roivainen / Yle

The East Uusimaa police have become aware of a new crime phenomenon. Minors are robbing branded clothes from other minors, threatening them with violence.

Cases have recently occurred, for example, in Vantaa, Järvenpää and Hyvinkää.

In investigating the cases, the crime categories are robbery or aggravated robbery.

The police urge parents to watch whether new branded products have appeared for their child, or whether such items have suddenly disappeared.
