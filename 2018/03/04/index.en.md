## Tension over the social and health care reform is growing

![The Prime Minister's interview hour at Kesäranta.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10101645)

Prime Minister Juha Sipilä answered journalists' questions on the radio. Photo: Heikki Saukkomaa / Lehtikuva

In Finnish politics, people are now waiting tensely to see what happens to the reform of "sote" services. Sote means social and health services.

The government wants to get the social and health care reform finished soon. Last week, Elina Lepomäki, an MP from the government party the National Coalition Party, said that she plans to vote against the reform. That added to the tension.

Today the Prime Minister said on the radio that the reform must come even before the next parliamentary elections. The elections are in spring next year.

## Oat products interest people

![A handful of oats](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10094068)

Open image viewer

According to research, oats contain many substances that are good for people. Photo: Timo Leponiemi / Yle

Shops are now selling a lot of products made from oats. Experts in Finland have studied oats for decades.

Research has shown that oats are good for human health in many ways. Oats have substances that help heal, for example, inflammation. According to research, oats also lower cholesterol and reduce the risk of heart and blood vessel diseases.

Finland plans to improve research on oats, because oats are so healthy.

## Women priests: 30 years

![Pirkko Lehtiö](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10100169)

Open image viewer

Pirkko Lehtiö is one of Finland's first women priests. Photo: Jaani Lampinen / Yle

Women have been able to be priests in Finland for 30 years now. The first women started as priests in the Lutheran Church of Finland in March 1988.

Women's priesthood was celebrated on Sunday in churches, for example in Helsinki and Tampere. Today, fewer than half of the Lutheran Church's priests are women.

## Saara Aalto's Monsters goes to Eurovision

![Saara Aalto](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10101192)

Open image viewer

Monsters is about little monsters inside a person's head. Photo: Ari Lahti / AOP

Finland's Eurovision song was chosen on Saturday evening. This year the Eurovision song was chosen from three songs performed by singer Saara Aalto. The song was chosen together by the audience and an international jury.

The winner is a performance called Monsters. Saara Aalto says that Monsters is about the negative things in life that leave little monsters in a person's head. Aalto says that you have to be strong and make decisions yourself. You must not let the monsters in your head have an effect.

Eurovision is held in Portugal's capital Lisbon in May.
