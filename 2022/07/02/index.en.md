## Helsinki Pride

Also Prime Minister Sanna Marin was at the Pride march. Photo: Silja Viitala / Yle

Helsinki Pride has organised a big parade. Pride is the biggest human rights event for sexual minorities in Finland.

The parade started from Senate Square and went round the city towards Kaivopuisto. Also Prime Minister Sanna Marin took part in the event. The last time the Helsinki Pride parade was held was 3 years ago.

The police estimate that about 80,000 people took part in the parade.

In Finland the whole week has been Pride week. The President's spouse Jenni Haukio is the patron of this year's Pride events.

The Pride parade can still be watched on Yle Areena and on Yle's website.

## The Finnish army

In the Finnish army, conscripts will in future be told more directly about Russia and the threat from it.

The reason is the change in Finland's security policy environment.

Earlier, in lessons, for example, it has been told about the "yellow state" that threatens Finland.

About 12,000 conscripts and female volunteers will begin military service on Monday.

## The Ukrainian language

![I love Ukraine sign in central Kyiv.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1920,h_1080,x_183,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-97448962b5d331ea5f6/1656083290)

Open image viewer

"I Love Ukraine" sign in central Kyiv. Photo: Maxim Fedorov / Yle

The use of one's own language, that is Ukrainian, has increased in Ukraine because of the war. Russia attacked Ukraine on 24 February.

Many Russian-speaking Ukrainians have switched their language to Ukrainian. They say that they do not want to speak the language of the occupier.

Ukrainian is also studied in language courses.

In the capital Kyiv more and more Ukrainian is spoken. A Kyiv tour guide, Serhij Savtšenko, has noticed that familiar customers now speak to each other and to the guide in Ukrainian. Earlier these customers spoke Russian.

Savtšenko has always spoken Ukrainian at home. Now he also speaks Ukrainian at work.

## Willow warbler

![Marsh tit in a ringer's hand](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4140,h_2329,x_0,y_142/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5475575c7ffd3a231ae/1551891807)

Open image viewer

Marsh tit in a ringer's hand. The breeding populations of the dunnock, garden warbler and common redpoll, for example, are also in steep decline. Photo: Yle/Pirjo Koskinen

Finland's most common bird species is the willow warbler. Bird counts, however, show that this summer the number of willow warblers fell by more than 30 percent.

The willow warbler spends the winter in Africa.

The bird organisation BirdLife Finland estimates that the decline of the species may be due to conditions in Africa. Also during migration to Finland the weather may have been bad for the birds.

## The weather

![Finland's weather map](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12521123)

On Sunday a cold front will bring rain, thunder and drier air to Finland. Behind the front it clears up quickly. The afternoon is already widely sunny in the south and west. There is heat across the whole country. In the east the temperature may rise to around 30 degrees.

Monday is quite sunny and warm, but clearly cooler than recently.
