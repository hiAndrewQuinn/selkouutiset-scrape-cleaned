# Saturday 16.12.2017 (radio)

Blue Reform held a party conference. A crime report has been made because of an article in Helsingin Sanomat. Finns are spending more money on Christmas presents than before. Most Finns have a positive attitude to multiculturalism.

## Blue Reform

![Sampo Terho](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9955142)

Open image viewer

Sampo Terho is the chair of Blue Reform. Photo: Jarno Kuusinen / AOP

There was a big political meeting in Tampere on Saturday. At the meeting there were people from a party called Blue Reform. The meeting was Blue Reform's first party conference.

Blue Reform is a brand-new party. It was born when the Finns Party split. In the summer, a group of people left the Finns Party because they did not accept the Finns Party's new chair, Jussi Halla-aho.

In Tampere, Blue Reform chose its own chair. He is Sampo Terho. In the summer, Terho lost the Finns Party chair election to Jussi Halla-aho.

Political researchers are now waiting for Blue Reform to say more exactly what the party's policies are. It is already known that Blue Reform does not like immigration and multiculturalism. On these matters it thinks roughly the same as the Finns Party.

## Helsingin Sanomat

![Conscripts.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9944515)

Open image viewer

Did someone give secret information to Helsingin Sanomat without permission? Photo: Ari Lahti / AOP

On Saturday, Helsingin Sanomat reported how the Finnish army gathers information. Now the army has made a crime report because of the article.

The army thinks that someone has given secret papers to Helsingin Sanomat without permission.

President Sauli Niinistö also says that Finland's security may be harmed when secret information is written about in a newspaper.

Helsingin Sanomat thinks that the newspaper's readers must be able to know what is happening in society.

## Christmas presents

![Wrapped Christmas presents in a shopping trolley.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9973155)

Finns buy a lot of Christmas presents. People are now spending more money on Christmas presents than before.

This year many people are buying a phone or some smart device as a Christmas present. Shops estimate that most Finns have already bought their Christmas presents.

## Multiculturalism

![Women in headscarves cooking food.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9020342)

Open image viewer

Finns have a more positive attitude to multiculturalism if they get to know immigrants. Photo: Päivi Köngäs / Yle

Most Finns have a positive attitude to multiculturalism. The Ministry of Justice asked people's opinions about multiculturalism. The people who answered lived in places where there is a reception centre.

Over 60 percent of those who answered thought that multiculturalism is a good thing. Women had a more positive attitude to multiculturalism than men.

Those who answered had a more positive attitude to multiculturalism if they knew foreigners. Those most afraid of multiculturalism were those who did not know a single person who speaks a foreign language or a single immigrant.
