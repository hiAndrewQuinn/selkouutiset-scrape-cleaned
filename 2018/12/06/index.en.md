# Thursday 6.12.2018 (radio)

On Thursday Finland's Independence Day was celebrated. The guests of honour at the Palace party are war veterans and Lottas. Finland's history is long.

## Independence Day

Today, Thursday, Finland's Independence Day has been celebrated. Independent Finland turns 101 years old this year.

The biggest Independence Day party is at the Presidential Palace. President Sauli Niinistö and Mrs Jenni Haukio have invited about 1700 guests to the Presidential Palace.

The theme of the Palace party this year is the environment. The theme can also be seen in the food served at the party. Guests are served, for example, vegetables and Finnish fish.

A large part of Finns follow the Palace party on television. Viewers are especially interested in the party dresses.

Mrs Haukio has a dress this year whose fabric is made of birch, that is, wood. The designers of the dress are students at Aalto University.

## War veterans and Lottas

![98-year-old Aune-Leea Huotari and a photo of her from the war.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10540039)

The guests of honour at the Palace party are war veterans and Lottas.

War veterans are men who fought in the Finnish army during the Second World War. Lottas are women who helped the army during the war, for example as nurses or cooks. There are not many war veterans and Lottas left, because it is more than 70 years since the war.

On Independence Day, the news reported on 98-year-old Aune-Leea Huotari. She has received an honourable mention as Lotta of the Year.

During the war, Huotari carried blood to places where soldiers were being treated. If the blood ran out, Huotari gave her own blood to the patients.

## Independent Finland

Finland has been independent for 101 years, but Finland's history is much longer.

Finland was part of Sweden for more than 600 years. That is why Finland is a very similar country to Sweden.

In 1809 Finland became part of Russia. The Russian period lasted about 100 years.

On 6 December 1917, Parliament, that is, the Finnish parliament, decided that Finland is independent. It means that Finns decide about their own affairs themselves.

That is why 6 December is a holiday for Finns.
