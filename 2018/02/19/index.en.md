# Monday 19.2.2018 (radio)

The number of job vacancies is growing. Retail workers are not doing overtime now. Ugandan police are investigating the death of a Finnish businessman. Lappeenranta lower secondary school pupils are not allowed to swear.

## Job vacancies

![A worker at a metal company's machine.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10075249)

Open image viewer

For example, there are job vacancies in industry. Photo: Penpichaya Khonchalad / Yle

The number of job vacancies is growing. In 2017 there were about 20 percent more job vacancies than in 2016.

It is often hard for companies to find workers for vacancies. Companies cannot find enough workers who have a suitable education.

Companies estimate that already at the end of last year, more than 50 percent of vacancies were ones for which it is hard to find a worker. For example, jobs in industry and retail are often hard to fill.

## Overtime ban in retail

![Customers shopping in a shop.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9959769)

Open image viewer

Customers do not yet notice the overtime ban in retail. Photo: Kimmo Hiltunen / Yle

An overtime ban has started in the retail sector. Workers do not do overtime in shops or in shop warehouses. They work only the normal working hours.

Because of the overtime ban, shop shelves may have fewer goods than usual in a few days' time.

The trade union of the workers decided on the overtime ban. The trade union negotiated a new collective agreement with the employers, but the negotiations have not yet succeeded. The collective agreement sets out, for example, wages.

## A businessman's death in Uganda

![Ugandan police spokesperson Emilian Kayima at a press conference in Kampala on 19 February.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10081944)

The Ugandan police told journalists about the investigations on Monday. Photo: Teemu Juhola / Yle

In Africa, in Uganda, the police are investigating the death of a Finnish man. The Ugandan police say that they have arrested many people in connection with the case.

The Finnish businessman was found dead in a hotel in Kampala, the capital of Uganda. This happened on the 6th of February. It is still not certain what the cause of death was.

The businessman's death has been much talked about in Finland, because the man had permission to market, that is, to advertise, Patria's products. Patria is a Finnish company that makes weapons. Patria is owned by the Finnish state.

Patria considers it a mistake that the businessman had permission to market Patria's weapons in Uganda. Two Patria managers resigned last week, because they were responsible for the mistake.

## Swearing by pupils

![Three young ninth-graders with their mobile phones.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10080638)

Open image viewer

Pupils Riika Manninen, Eeva Laine and Teemu Vainikka of Kimppa lower secondary school think that adults' swearing is ugly, too. Photo: Tommi Parkkinen / Yle

Schoolchildren sometimes swear a lot, but Kimpisen lower secondary school in Lappeenranta has decided to fight ugly swear words. The school's pupils are no longer allowed to swear.

Teachers make a mark when they hear a swear word. If a pupil gets 3 marks, the teacher talks with the pupil.

The school has also promised a prize for classes that use clean language.
