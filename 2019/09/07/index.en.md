# Saturday 7.9.2019 (radio)

Katri Kulmuni is the new leader of the Centre. Antti Rinne says that the wages of Posti workers will not be cut. Immigrants are being taught to ride a bicycle. Russia and Ukraine have started a prisoner swap.

## Katri Kulmuni is the new leader of the Centre

![Centre leader candidate Katri Kulmuni spoke before the leadership votes at the Centre's extraordinary party congress in Kouvola on Saturday 7 September.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10960615)

The Centre Party has chosen a new leader. The new leader is Katri Kulmuni. She won the vote at the party meeting. The other candidate was Antti Kaikkonen.

Katri Kulmuni is an MP and minister of economic affairs. She was born in Tornio in northern Finland, and she is 32 years old.

The Centre's supporters hope that the new leader will improve the party's popularity. In the spring parliamentary election, the Centre got very few votes and fell from the position of the largest party.

Last week, in Yle's poll, under 12 percent of respondents supported the Centre. The Centre was only the fifth most popular party.

## Prime Minister: Posti wages will not fall

![Antti Rinne](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10960352)

Open image viewer

Antti Rinne. Photo: Yle

Prime Minister Antti Rinne says that the wages of Posti workers will not be cut. Rinne also criticises Posti's managers for bad work.

Prime Minister Rinne was interviewed on Yle's Ykkösaamu programme. Rinne said that Posti has been firing people for many years and has tried to cut workers' wages. At the same time, the salaries of Posti's own managers have risen quickly. Rinne said that Posti has been managed badly.

Rinne also said that trade unions must negotiate with Posti about agreements that help Posti do well in competition.

Posti is a state-owned company. Posti has a money shortage, because fewer and fewer letters, newspapers and other mail are being sent all the time. People use email and digital newspapers. At the same time, Posti has gained new competitors.

## Immigrants on bicycles

![Bicycle picture](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10955272)

Chahira Zerouki (left) and Rahma El Younoussi. Photo: Anton Rinta-Jouppi / Yle

About 300 immigrants have taken a course that teaches them to ride a bicycle.

The courses have been held in 40 different places around Finland, and more will be held. There are more women than men in the courses. Most of them come from North Africa and the Middle East.

The course organisers say that people's self-esteem gets better and everyday life gets easier when they learn to ride a bicycle.

## Russia and Ukraine swapped prisoners

![In front of a prison in Moscow, buses left for the airport on Saturday. They were believed to carry Ukrainian prisoners.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10960400)

In Moscow, buses left a prison. They probably carried Ukrainian prisoners. Photo: AOL/ Andrei Vasilyev

Russia and Ukraine have swapped prisoners.

Each country freed 35 prisoners. Among them are 24 Ukrainian sailors, whom Russia took prisoner near Crimea last year. Russia also freed film director Oleg Sentsov.

Ukraine, for its part, let go a man to Russia who may know a lot about the shooting down of a Malaysian passenger plane in eastern Ukraine in 2014.

The prisoner swap is a small sign that relations between Russia and Ukraine are improving. Relations broke off when Russia took the Crimean peninsula from Ukraine in 2014. Russia also supports the armed forces fighting in eastern Ukraine.
