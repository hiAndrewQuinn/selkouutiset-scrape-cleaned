# Saturday 23.5.2020 (radio)

Yle made a survey about the effects of corona on Finns. Ever fewer babies in Finland receive a church baptism. Muslims have begun celebrating Eid al-Fitr. Increased movement in nature increases exposure to ticks.

## Corona harms society but may bring people closer

![Frank Martela, Helsinki, 29.3.2018](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_7610,h_4281,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-4751085abcd9368c92f/1522325900)

Philosopher Frank Martela. Photo: Antti Haanpää / Yle

The corona crisis affects society a lot. Yle made a survey about the effects of the corona epidemic.

98 per cent of respondents believe that the corona crisis is harming Finnish society. At the same time, however, people believe that the crisis will make the nation more united and stronger.

36 per cent of respondents believe that people will be stronger after the corona crisis.

39 per cent do not believe that the feeling of unity will change.

Only just under 20 per cent believe that the corona crisis weakens and divides the nation.

Philosopher Frank Martela of Aalto University studies happiness. He says that in crises we are not just individuals but part of a bigger community. Crises remind us that each person's opportunities depend on the society around them.

## Fewer and fewer children are baptised

![A priest baptises a child.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9434614)

Open image viewer

At baptism, the child joins the church. At christening, it is also said what name the child will get. Photo: Mika Kanerva

Ever fewer babies in Finland receive a church baptism. At baptism, the child becomes a member of the Evangelical Lutheran Church of Finland.

Last year, 62 per cent of babies were baptised. Ten years ago, the figure was almost 80 per cent.

The popularity of baptism varies in different parts of Finland. In the Diocese of Helsinki, about 40 per cent of children were baptised last year. In the north, in the Diocese of Oulu, the figure was about 80 per cent.

The decrease in baptisms is affected by the fact that ever fewer Finns belong to the church.

## Ramadan has ended and Eid al-Fitr has begun

![Fireworks at Yas Island harbour in Abu Dhabi.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10817612)

Open image viewer

Fireworks at Yas Island harbour in Abu Dhabi after Ramadan. Photo: Ali Haider / EPA

Muslims have begun celebrating Eid al-Fitr. It lasts 3 days.

Eid al-Fitr marks the end of the holy month, that is, Ramadan. The best-known tradition of Ramadan is fasting. During the fast, nothing is eaten or drunk while the sun is in the sky.

Traditionally, the celebration of Eid al-Fitr includes, among other things, lots of festive food. In addition, people visit friends and relatives and give gifts.

This year, however, the corona situation makes celebrating Eid difficult.

## In nature, remember the tick

![A tick on the skin.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1194,h_672,x_0,y_261/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6294405e17270f202ba/1589927253)

Open image viewer

A young tick is so small that it is hard to notice on the skin. Photo: Sini Ruohonen

People have moved about a lot in nature during the corona epidemic. That increases the chance of getting a tick, that is, a tick in the skin.

Ticks spread tick-borne encephalitis (TBE) and borreliosis, which is also known as Lyme disease. There is a vaccine against TBE, and borreliosis is treated with antibiotics.

Careful tick checks after being outdoors are the best way to protect against borreliosis. The most important thing is to remove the tick from the skin as soon as possible.

The tick's habitat has spread towards the north. There are ticks at least as far north as Oulu.
