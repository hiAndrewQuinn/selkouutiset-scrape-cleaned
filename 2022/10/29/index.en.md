Prime Minister Sanna Marin is worried about the economic situation in Europe. Marin was a guest on Yle's Ykkösaamu programme. Marin said that a recession may come in Europe, because energy prices and interest rates are rising. A recession means that the economy shrinks for many months in a row.

Ykkösaamu also talked about the value increase tax, that is, the exit tax. Marin criticised the government party, the Centre Party, for starting to oppose the value increase tax.

Marin says that the tax has been agreed in the government groups. Marin says that the government must discuss ways of working.

## Satisfaction with Post

![Main entrance of a postal centre.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5183,h_2910,x_0,y_433/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-966097629efafb16e94/1654586457)

Open image viewer

Dissatisfaction with Posti's operations has increased. Photo: Matti Myller / Yle

Finns' satisfaction with Post has fallen. A new survey says that over 40 percent of Finns think that Post's operations are fairly bad or very bad.

Finns' satisfaction with Post has fallen by 8 percentage points in a few years, which is quite a lot. Less than 30 percent of Finns are satisfied with Post's operations.

The Local Government Development Foundation asked Finns about their satisfaction with various everyday services. Grocery shops and pharmacies received the best grades. Over 80 percent of Finns are satisfied with their own grocery shop. Almost 80 percent of Finns are satisfied with the pharmacy.

There has been a shortage of children's pain and fever medicines in the autumn. Photo: Helena Korpela / Yle

The availability of children's pain and fever medicines has returned to almost normal. Many popular children's pain and fever medicines ran out of pharmacies in the autumn, because they were bought a lot.

For example, flu has again increased among children and adults, because there are no corona restrictions and people meet many other people.

Medicine companies say that almost all children's pain and fever medicines are again available from pharmacies.

## Summer time ends - remember to change the clock

![Clock with the hand being moved back to winter time.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1600,h_900,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/14-svyle-6142553197327452bd/1603530654)

Open image viewer

Summer time ends. The clock is set back one hour. Photo: Yle

Finland is moving from summer time to winter time, that is, standard time, tonight.

The clock is set back one hour early in the morning. Officially summer time ends on Sunday morning at 4 o'clock, when the clock is turned back so that it is 3 o'clock.

Summer time ends and begins at the same time everywhere in the EU. Summer time starts again at the end of March.
