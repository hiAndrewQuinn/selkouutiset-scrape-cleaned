## Violence by children

![Shadows cast on the ground in central Helsinki in August 2020.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4000,h_2250,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-7218755f69d5c1a0491/1600772490)

Children commit more assaults than before

This year almost 3,000 assaults where the suspect is under 15 years old have already come to the police's attention. Five years ago there were about 1,700 cases.

The police say that in violent crimes by minors the perpetrator often has an immigrant background.

Violent crimes by children include, for example, hitting, kicking and threatening with a knife. The perpetrators also often film the assault and share it on social media.

![Corona home testing.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5083,h_2859,x_0,y_465/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-90215861e18164e17af/1642169145)

Corona test. The result shows negative. Photo: Mårten Lampén / Yle

The corona situation in Finland is calming down.

The Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare says that serious forms of the disease are probably not seen very much. There are also fewer patients in hospitals who have come there specifically because of corona.

The number of corona infections may, however, vary during the winter, THL says. Infections come in waves, sometimes there are more and sometimes fewer.

## Red Nose Day

![Red Nose Day logo](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8433491)

Open image viewer

Red Nose Day collects money for the world's children. Photo: Ville Välimäki / Yle

Red Nose Day is being celebrated at Yleisradio today. During Red Nose Day money is collected on the radio and television for the poorest children in the world.

The UN says that more than 300 million people need humanitarian aid this year. The Church's foreign aid says that the war in Ukraine and inflation, that is, the rise in prices, have increased the need for help.

Last year Finns donated over 3 million euros on Red Nose Day

## Clear News turns 30

![Journalists in a group photo.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12676227)

Open image viewer

Yle's Clear News journalists. Margit Alasalmi, Markku Lehtimäki, Pertti Seppä, boss Petri Kejonen, Merja Windia, Marko Tammi and Pälvi Tammi. Photo: Heli Savola / Yle

Yle's Clear News turns 30 today. Yle makes news in easy Finnish for TV, radio and the internet.

Originally Clear News was made for Finns who lived abroad. The purpose of Clear News was to help these Finns abroad keep up ties to Finland and the Finnish language.

Today Clear News is made, for example, for students of Finnish and for all people for whom following standard-language news is difficult for some reason.

In Finland there may be about 750,000 people who benefit from easy Finnish.
