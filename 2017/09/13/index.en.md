# Wednesday 13.9.2017 (radio)

Emergency response centres get many unnecessary calls. Favouring local food is difficult for municipalities because of the procurement law. The Finnish Immigration Service is reducing reception centres. Espoo is making English a language of service. The summer allergy season will soon be over.

## Emergency centres often get unnecessary calls

![The Central Finland emergency response centre closed in November 2014.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7636688)

Emergency response centres get many unnecessary calls. The calls have come especially after the stabbing attack in Turku.

People call emergency response centres when, for example, they hear noise. Some call if they see people who look suspicious.

The emergency response centres say that many people overreact, which means they get worried and call for no reason.

## The procurement law can be difficult

![School lunch.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9826866)

Open image viewer

School food is not always local food. Photo: Hanna Othman / Yle

Many municipalities want to offer local food in schools and day-care centres. However, it is difficult because of the procurement law.

Because of the procurement law, municipalities cannot buy, for example, meat and potatoes directly from farmers in their own municipality; instead, the purchases must be put out to competitive tender.

In competitive tendering, offers are requested and the municipality must take the cheapest one. In the tendering, it is not allowed to say that only products from the municipality itself are acceptable.

However, the municipality can say that it wants to buy, for example, bread so fresh that it is still warm. Then the local bakery has a good chance to win the tender.

## Asylum seekers do not want to leave Evitskog

![Residents of the Evitskog reception centre demonstrating against the closing of the centre.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9829195)

Open image viewer

Residents of the Evitskog reception centre do not want to leave Kirkkonummi. Photo: Antti Kolppo / Yle

The Evitskog reception centre in Kirkkonummi is being closed. The Finnish Immigration Service is reducing reception centres because the need for them has decreased.

Evitskog is one of the largest reception centres in Finland. More than 300 asylum seekers have lived there.

The plan has been to move the residents to Espoo and to Kauhava in South Ostrobothnia. However, most of the asylum seekers do not want to leave Kirkkonummi.

## English becomes a language of service in Espoo

![The coat of arms of Espoo](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5540904)

Open image viewer

In the future, people can deal with Espoo in Finnish, Swedish or English. Photo: YLE Helsinki

The city of Espoo plans to make English an official language of service of the city.  English will become Espoo's 3rd language of service alongside Finnish and Swedish.

In the future, all the city's services and forms will be available in Finnish, Swedish or English.

More than 14 percent of Espoo residents speak some language other than Finnish or Swedish as their mother tongue.

## Allergy sufferers' symptoms are easing

![An allergic person blows their nose.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9566186)

Open image viewer

Allergic people get, for example, a runny nose from plant pollen. Photo: AOP

The summer allergy season is ending.

At the end of summer there has still been grass pollen in the air, but now the grasses are stopping flowering. That is why the symptoms of allergic people are also ending. Allergic people get, for example, a runny nose from plant pollen.

However, people who have a mould allergy can still get allergy symptoms. The University of Turku says that there are small amounts of mould spores in the air.
