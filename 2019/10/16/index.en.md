# Wednesday 16.10.2019 (radio)

The government intends to speed up the processing of work permits. HSL will soon sell public transport tickets to Tallinn. In Sotkamo, they are trying out whether nature can help young people with problems. More than 400 Saimaa ringed seals live in Saimaa.

## Getting a work permit will be faster

![Michelle Kreuss](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11014621)

Open image viewer

South African Michelle Creuss waited for a work permit to come to Finland for 5 months. Photo: Annu Passoja / Yle

The government intends to make it easier for foreign workers to move to Finland. The government promises to speed up the processing of work permits.

Workers who come to Finland from outside the EU area need a work permit. Right now, getting a work permit can take half a year.

The government's goal is that a work permit is processed within one month. If the goal is met, it will be a big relief, for example, for tourism companies in Lapland. A lot of tourists come to Lapland in winter, and for example, hotels and restaurants in Lapland have a severe shortage of workers.

## HSL will soon sell tram tickets to Tallinn

![Public transport in Tallinn, a tram.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9353201)

Open image viewer

You can also ride trams in Tallinn. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

It will soon be easy for Finns to travel on the trams and buses in Tallinn. Tallinn tram and bus tickets can already be bought next spring with HSL's mobile app, that is, the Helsinki Region Transport app.

Travel for Estonians in the Helsinki region will also become easier. Estonians will be able to buy Helsinki region travel tickets with the pilet.ee mobile app.

The cooperation between Helsinki and Tallinn only concerns mobile apps, for example on a phone. Travel cards cannot be used in the other country.

## Nature helps young people with problems

![A forest covered in moss.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11017049)

Open image viewer

People calm down in the middle of nature. Photo: Richard Newton / AOP

In Sotkamo in Northern Finland, a new way of helping young people who have problems is being tried. A youth worker can take a young person into nature. In nature, the young person can calm down and talk with the youth worker.

Studies show that nature prevents mental health problems.

However, a young person does not have to go to the forest. People go to nature only if the young person wants to.

Sotkamo is cooperating with the Tyynelä Wellbeing Nature Centre. There, young people can also take care of animals, for example cats, dogs and horses.

New ways are needed to prevent mental health problems, it is reported from Sotkamo.

## The number of Saimaa ringed seals is growing

![Saimaa ringed seal on a rock](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10219346)

Open image viewer

A little over 400 ringed seals live in Saimaa. Photo: Petri Vironen / Yle

The number of Saimaa ringed seals is growing little by little.

Now a little over 400 Saimaa ringed seals may live in Saimaa. This year, almost 90 pups, that is, baby seals, were born.

Metsähallitus says that the number of seals has grown because their breeding has not been disturbed. Fishing restrictions have also strengthened the seal population.

The number of Saimaa ringed seals has grown since 2010. At that time, about 270 seals lived in Saimaa.

Even though the number of seals has grown, the Saimaa ringed seal is still very endangered.
