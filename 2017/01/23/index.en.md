# Monday 23.1.2017 (radio)

Meetings have been held on the situation in Syria. An art exhibition by Japanese artist Yayoi Kusama interested Finns. The Times writes about Finnish sisu. Videos and 3D pictures have come to housing ads. Finland is presenting Finnish culture to the French in wooden cabins in the middle of Paris.

## Syrians hope for peace

![People queueing for water.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9408624)

Open image viewer

The meeting in Helsinki is thinking about how Syrians can be helped. Photo: Youssef Badawi / EPA

2 meetings have been held on the situation in Syria.

One meeting is in Astana, the capital of Kazakhstan. There they are discussing how the war in Syria can be ended. The Syrian government and the government's opponents are taking part in the Astana meeting.

The other meeting is in Finland, in Helsinki. The Helsinki meeting is discussing how ordinary people can be helped in Syria. Help is also needed in Syria's neighbouring countries, where there are many Syrian refugees.

Finland wants the Helsinki meeting to talk especially about helping women and girls. The Helsinki meeting continues on Tuesday.

## Kusama interested Finns

![Mirror room (Pumpkin)](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9356969)

Open image viewer

Yayoi Kusama's art is known for its dots. Photo: Hanna Kukorelli / HAM

A new record has been set at the Helsinki Art Museum. The exhibition of Japanese artist Yayoi Kusama got more visitors than any other exhibition before it.

225,000 people visited Kusama's exhibition in 4 months. Sometimes there were almost 7000 visitors a day.

There are a lot of dots in Yayoi Kusama's art. Kusama's exhibition in Helsinki ended on Sunday.

## Finnish sisu

![bearded man with an axe](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9417987)

Open image viewer

Finnish sisu means that we do not give up. Photo: AOP

Finnish sisu has been noticed around the world again. The British newspaper The Times writes that Finnish sisu will be a trend this year. The Times thinks that sisu is really needed this year.

The newspaper also tells its readers how to practise Finnish sisu. It is done by a person repeating different sentences without facial expressions and unnecessary sounds.

## Online housing ads

![A remote-controlled drone camera in the sky.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7976279)

Open image viewer

The surroundings of a home can be filmed with a drone camera. Photo: Risto Koskinen / Yle

There are new kinds of housing ads on the internet.

Many ads have video. For the videos, the area around the home can be filmed from high in the air with a drone camera.

Sometimes the ads also have 3D pictures. The viewer feels as if they are inside the home.

Often the seller of the home orders the videos and 3D pictures from a company. It is easier to find a buyer for the home when the ad has been made well.

## Finnish cabins in Paris

![A Finnish design cabin.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9416700)

Open image viewer

You can now stay overnight in a Finnish wooden cabin in Paris. Photo: Juha Nurminen / Yle

Finnish wooden cabins can now be found in the middle of Paris.

The Finnish Cultural Institute has brought the cabins to Paris. With the cabins, it wants to present Finnish culture and design to the French.

Lena Lutaud, a journalist at the newspaper Le Figaro, says that Finland is now in fashion in France. The journalist thinks the cabins are a fun idea.

People can also stay overnight in the cabins, where there is a temporary hotel.
