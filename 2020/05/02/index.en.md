# Saturday 2.5.2020 (radio)

May Day was quiet. Students have to go back to school when schools open. The number of layoffs is growing. A shortage of workers threatens the harvest in Europe.

May Day was quiet in Finland. People did not gather in parks or in city centres. The police had much fewer jobs to do than usual on May Day.

In some towns there were shootings, which means someone shot with a gun. The police are investigating the shootings in Vantaa, Kokkola, Turku and Åland.

The most serious shooting was in Vantaa. The police investigated it as an attempted murder. However, nobody died in the shootings on May Day.

## Going to school is important

![Tuomas Pöysti](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11332727)

Open image viewer

Chancellor of Justice Tuoma Pöysti. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

The Chancellor of Justice reminds us that it is important for children to go to school. Schools open in two weeks, and face-to-face teaching will continue there then.

Some parents are wondering whether they should let their children go to school because of the corona situation.

Chancellor of Justice **Tuomas Pöysti** said on Saturday in an interview with Yle that, according to the law, adults must make sure that children complete their compulsory education. Children must learn the things that are taught at school.

## Many layoffs

![Chairs raised onto tables in an empty restaurant in a shopping centre.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11319731)

Open image viewer

Because of the corona situation, there is no work for many service jobs right now. The workers have been laid off. The work will continue when the company is open again. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

Because of the corona situation, many people in Finland have been laid off. In March, 65,000 people were laid off. In April, many more layoffs came.

Many people who work in service jobs have been laid off, for example in restaurants, cafés, clothing shops or in tourism, for example in hotels. Many of the laid-off people are under 30 years old. More women than men have been laid off. Many young women work in service jobs.

An expert says that perhaps more than 90,000 people in Finland will be laid off.

## Shortage of seasonal workers is hurting the harvest

![Tomatoes in a greenhouse in Närpiö](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3024,h_1701,x_0,y_1199/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6363265e314d1a34b40/1614714435)

Tomatoes. Photo: Jarkko Heikkinen / Yle

A shortage of workers is hurting the harvesting of fruit and vegetables in different parts of Europe. Usually seasonal workers come to Europe from elsewhere to help on farms with the harvest. Because of the corona situation, it is difficult for workers to travel. There is a shortage of seasonal workers.

Italy and Spain are the largest producers of vegetables and fruit in the EU. For example, about 150,000 seasonal workers are missing from Spain's fields. If workers cannot be found, some of the crop may perhaps rot in the field.

There is also a shortage of seasonal workers on berry farms and vegetable farms in Finland.
