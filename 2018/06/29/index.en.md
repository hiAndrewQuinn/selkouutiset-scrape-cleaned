# Friday 29.6.2018 (radio)

EU leaders have agreed on a common asylum policy. The Minister of Justice wants to ban marriages of minors. Nature is important to Finns. A new method exposes sellers of fake strawberries.

## The EU is setting up centres for migrants

![Juha Sipilä and flags](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10280091)

Open image viewer

Prime Minister Juha Sipilä thought the EU leaders' discussion on immigration was constructive. Photo: Ludovic Marin / AFP

EU leaders have agreed on a common asylum policy. At the meeting in Brussels it was agreed that migrant centres will be set up in Mediterranean countries. It is not yet known in which countries the centres will be set up.

People who have the right to asylum will be moved from the centres to EU countries that volunteer. Others will be returned directly from the centres.

Prime Minister Juha Sipilä says that the solution can prevent people smuggling and dangerous routes.

## The Minister of Justice wants to ban marriages of minors

![Antti Häkkänen](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10281299)

Open image viewer

Minister of Justice Antti Häkkänen wants Finland to stop giving special permits for marriages of minors. Photo: Jarno Kuusinen / AOP

Minister of Justice Antti Häkkänen wants to ban marriages of minors. In Häkkänen's opinion, religious and cultural reasons, for example, are not good enough reasons.

Every year, 10 - 30 special permits are applied for so that minors can marry. Last year there were 11 applications. 7 got a special permit. Most of those who got a permit have been 17-year-old girls. Permits given to people under 17 have been based on pregnancy.

The Ministry of Justice says that marriages of minors have been stopped, or are being stopped, in almost all the other Nordic countries.

## Nature is important to Finns

![Rubbish at the foot of a tree](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5378448)

Open image viewer

Finns are worried about litter in nature and about climate change. Photo: Anna Sirén / Yle

Over 90 percent of Finns say that nature is important. Just as many say that nature is part of the national identity and increases well-being and health.

The Ministry of the Environment did a survey about Finns' relationship with nature. Finns most like to exercise or relax in nature. Almost 70 percent go out in nature at least every week. Women go out in nature more often than men.

People over 65 are more worried about the state of Finland's nature than younger people. Finns think that every person is responsible for nature. Most say that the authorities are also responsible.

Finns think that climate change and litter are the biggest threats to nature.

## Science exposes strawberry cheats

![A device shared by Luke and Evira](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10280631)

Open image viewer

A device shared by Luke and Evira. The devices are isotope mass spectrometers. Photo: Eija Tammisalpa / Evira

A new method exposes sellers of fake strawberries. An isotope study has created a profile and a database of Finnish strawberries.

Food inspectors can now take samples at market stalls. If a strawberry in a spot test does not match the profile, it is from abroad.

The method also helps honest berry sellers: now they can say that they are definitely selling Finnish berries. Researchers say that the method works. It can also be extended to other natural products.
