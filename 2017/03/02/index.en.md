# Thursday 2.3.2017 (radio)

Employment offices are interviewing unemployed people. A record number of women want to join the army. In Lapland about a thousand reindeer work in the tourism business. Swedes know little about Finland.

## Interviews of the unemployed have started

![A sign showing the opening hours of the TE office](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8919368)

Open image viewer

Employment offices must interview every unemployed person every 3 months. Photo: Heikki Rönty / Yle

Employment offices are keeping in closer contact with unemployed people than before.

From the start of this year, TE offices must interview every unemployed person every 3 months. So far about 40 percent of the unemployed have been interviewed.

Jarmo Ukkonen, head of the Uusimaa TE office, says that the interviews have already helped. Ukkonen says that many unemployed people have found work or a study place.

In an Yle online poll, 90 percent of the unemployed say that the interviews are hardly any help.

## The army interests women

![Navy recruits taking their oath on the parade ground at Upinniemi, Kirkkonummi, on 18 February 2011.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9488669)

Open image viewer

The Defence Forces have already trained over 7000 women for the reserve. Photo: Sari Gustafsson / Lehtikuva

A record number of women have applied to the army. By the start of March, over 1100 women had applied for voluntary military service.

The previous record was set last year, when about 850 women applied to the army.

Captain Timo Miettinen from Army Command says that women's interest in the army may have many causes. For example, the Defence Forces being visible in the media and the security policy situation may have increased interest in the army.

In recent years, over 60 percent of the women have received leadership training in the army.

## A tourism reindeer takes tourists on a sleigh ride

![reindeer arctic reindeer farm rovaniemi](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9487630)

Open image viewer

The tourism reindeer Musti pulls a sleigh and poses in photos.  Photo: Sauli Antikainen / Yle

In Finnish Lapland about 1000 reindeer already work in the tourism business. A tourism reindeer's job is to take tourists on sleigh rides.

Every year about 100 new reindeer come into the field. Training a tourism reindeer takes 3 years.

A good tourism reindeer must not be scared of people, because tourists often want to take photos of themselves and the reindeer. It is also an advantage for a tourism reindeer if it has handsome antlers.

A tourism reindeer is a valuable animal. One tourism reindeer earns maybe about 10,000 euros during the winter.

On the other hand, tourism reindeer cost more than ordinary reindeer. A tourism reindeer needs, for example, much more food, because otherwise it does not have the strength to pull a sleigh.

## Swedes know Finland poorly

![A summery lake landscape and a lakeside sauna.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9349067)

Open image viewer

Swedes know Finland best for its nature and the sauna. Photo: AOP

Swedes' knowledge of Finland is weak.

When people talk about Finland, the first things Swedes think of are lakes, the sauna, Helsinki and vodka. Many do not know, for example, that Swedish is an official language of Finland or that people whose mother tongue is Swedish live in Finland.

The Finnish Institute in Stockholm has asked Swedes about their knowledge and images of Finland.

Over 60 percent of Swedes said that their image of Finland is positive. Positive ratings are given, for example, to Finnish humour, diligence and sisu.

The negative ratings are related to Finns' alcohol use.
