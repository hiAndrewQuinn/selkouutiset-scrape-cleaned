# Friday 28.12.2018 (radio)

In Tampere, prostate cancer screening is being studied. Minister Tarasti wants voting to become a civic duty. The SDP uses the most money on parliamentary elections. The suspect in the Arabianranta murder was the victim's uncle. Presidents Niinistö and Putin talked on the phone.

## Prostate cancer is to be screened

![Blood sample tubes in a laboratory.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9855641)

Open image viewer

Prostate cancer screening can begin with a blood test. Photo: AOP

A large study of prostate cancer screening has started in Tampere.

15,000 test participants are being invited to the study from Tampere and Helsinki.

The studies are looking for the best way to carry out prostate cancer screening.

Prostate cancer is the most common cancer in men. Each year prostate cancer is diagnosed in about 5,000 men.

## Voting as a civic duty

![Lauri Tarasti.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9957032)

Open image viewer

Minister Lauri Tarasti. Photo: Jarno Kuusinen / AOP

Minister Lauri Tarasti proposes that voting in elections become a civic duty. Tarasti believes that in this way the voting activity of Finns will rise.

Minister Lauri Tarasti is an expert on electoral legislation.

In the magazine Kanava, Tarasti proposes that it be written into the constitution that voting is a civic duty. Tarasti does not want compulsory voting, but he believes that a mere mention of the civic duty will encourage people to vote.

However, if a person does not vote, Tarasti says that they should not be punished for it.

## The SDP has the biggest election budget

![SDP election balls.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8842557)

Open image viewer

The SDP will spend about 1.5 million euros on the parliamentary elections. Photo: Ismo Pekkarinen / AOP

The SDP, that is, the Social Democrats, will spend more money on the parliamentary elections in the spring than the other parties. The SDP's election budget is about 1.5 million euros, according to a report by the Finnish News Agency STT.

The next biggest election budgets belong to the National Coalition Party and the Centre Party.

The least money for election campaigning is spent by the Blue Future. The Blues intend to spend at most 100,000 euros on the elections.

All parties intend to campaign online and on social media more than before this time.

The parliamentary elections will be held on 14 April.

## The Arabianranta murder suspect is the victim's uncle

![ Chief investigator Kirsi Kanth.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10574211)

Open image viewer

Chief investigator Kirsi Kanth of the Helsinki Police Department is investigating the Arabianranta murder. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

The Helsinki District Court has had a man held in custody who is suspected of the murder of a child in Arabianranta, Helsinki.

The hearing was secret.

The man is suspected of murdering a 7-year-old boy on Christmas Eve.

The suspect was born in 1982. He has a previous criminal record.

The man is the uncle of the little boy who died, that is, the boy's mother's brother.

## Niinistö and Putin talked on the phone

![Sauli Niinistö and Vladimir Putin](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10363073)

Open image viewer

Finland's President Sauli Niinistö and Russia's President Vladimir Putin. Photo: Kirill Kudryavtsev / EPA

President Sauli Niinistö and Russia's President Vladimir Putin have talked on the phone today. The Presidential Office says that the call lasted one hour.

During the call the presidents talked about, for example, current issues in the Arctic Council and the Council of Europe. They also talked about the situation of Russia and Ukraine in the Sea of Azov, and about nuclear arms control.

Niinistö and Putin also wished each other a happy new year.
