# Sunday 24.11.2019 (radio)

The postal dispute has not been resolved. On Monday, many support strikes start that will disrupt traffic. In Finland, cash is still accepted. Autoliitto criticises where the speed cameras are placed.

## Postal dispute

The postal dispute has not been resolved in the weekend talks.

Yesterday a proposed solution was put forward for the dispute about parcel sorters. Others thought the proposal was good, but the Media Union did not accept it. The Media Union represents the employer.

Because the dispute over parcel sorters was not resolved, the PAU union in turn rejected the National Conciliator's proposal for a new collective agreement.

![Buses and passengers at Elielinaukio in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11084569)

Because no agreement was reached, the postal strike continues. Tomorrow, Monday, many new strikes will also start, to support postal workers.

Because of the support strikes, Finnair will cancel about 300 flights for today and tomorrow. Bus traffic in the capital region will also stop for a day. In addition, ships that sail under the Finnish flag will stay in port.

## Cash payments

![Jone Salmela, restaurant manager, O'Learys Tripla](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5034,h_2832,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6117635dcd3b2621ed2/1574594290)

The O'Learys restaurant in Tripla in Helsinki does not accept cash, says restaurant manager Jone Salmela. Photo: Markku Pitkänen / Yle

In Finland, cash is still accepted as a means of payment almost everywhere.

The tourism and restaurant industry and the Finnish Commerce Federation say that only a few shops and restaurants here have stopped accepting cash.

In Sweden and Norway, cash is used much less than in Finland. Sweden also aims to stop using cash completely at some point in the future.

Paying by card or mobile device is also becoming common quickly in Finland. However, 10 per cent of Finns still use only cash.

The Bank of Finland thinks it is important that people can also use cash. Older people and children, for example, need cash.

## Speed cameras

![The first new speed camera on Highway 4 went up at Onkiniemi in Sysmä.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10665368)

A speed camera takes pictures of cars that drive too fast. Photo: Markku Lähdetluoma / Yle

Autoliitto says that the places for traffic speed cameras, that is, speed cameras, need to be considered more carefully. In Autoliitto's view, road safety must be taken into account better when placing the cameras.

Cameras have now been put in places where a driver could accidentally speed for a short time, but road safety is not endangered by this.

In Autoliitto's view, Finland should follow the model of Sweden. In Sweden, the cameras are at known danger spots. Shortly before the camera there is a sign by the road that says there is camera monitoring. When drivers see the sign, they slow down. So the state's fine income falls, but road safety improves, Autoliitto says.
