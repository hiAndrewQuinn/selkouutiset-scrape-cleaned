# Friday 31.8.2018 (radio)

The amount of solar power will double this year in Finland. Recycled fertilisers can reduce the nutrient load on the Baltic Sea. A separate summer time and winter time may be abandoned. Next week's weather will be warmer than usual.

## Solar electricity is becoming more common quickly

![Solar panels on a detached house.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9832392)

Open image viewer

Finns are installing more and more solar panels. They can reduce the electricity bill. Photo: Kalevi Rytkölä / Yle

The amount of solar power will double this year in Finland. More and more panels are going on the roofs of detached houses, cottages and companies.

In a detached house, solar electricity can perhaps replace about 20 percent of the year's electricity consumption. In a detached house, your own solar electricity also reduces transfer fees and taxes.

In apartment blocks and terraced houses, solar electricity can only be used for the housing company's shared electricity consumption. A change in the law and replacing the meters are needed before the electricity can be divided among the flats.

About 2.6 million Finns live in apartment blocks and terraced houses.

## Recycled fertiliser reduces the load on the Baltic Sea

![Cereal field in Jokioinen, Forssa.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10378545)

Open image viewer

Recycled fertiliser alone will not save the Baltic Sea, but it helps reduce the load. Photo: Elise Tykkyläinen / Yle

Recycled fertilisers can help protect the Baltic Sea. Ordinary fertilisers are harmful to the Baltic Sea. The sea has become eutrophic, that is, a lot of nitrogen and phosphorus, for example, has run into it.

In recycled fertilisers, the nutrients are taken from industry and agriculture, for example. Then a non-renewable natural resource does not need to be mined separately. At the same time, energy is saved.

The Natural Resources Institute has tested a recycled fertiliser that comes, for example, from a pulp mill. It uses an organic, that is, biological by-product of the paper industry.

Recycled fertilisers reduce the load from agriculture on the sea. However, many other measures are needed to save the Baltic Sea.

## The EU may give up changing the clocks

![Alarm clock next to a bed.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10043722)

Open image viewer

Changing the clocks in spring and autumn may become history. Most Finns support switching to permanent summer time in Finland. Photo: AOP

The European Commission proposes that the separate summer time and winter time be abandoned.

In an EU survey, 84 percent of people opposed changing the clocks in spring and autumn. 4.6 million people responded to the Commission's online survey. Finland has wanted the changes to be abandoned.

The matter requires an EU-level decision. However, each member state can decide for itself which time zone it chooses. The Finnish government does not yet have a position on the time zone. It is not yet known how quickly the actual proposal for the change will be made.

## The weather stays warm

![Yellow leaves.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10380549)

Open image viewer

Summer-like weather continues, and autumn may be warm. Photo: AOP

Next week starts with warm weather. The weather is warmer than usual. The temperature is over 20 degrees across almost the whole country. In the central and northern parts of the country it is about 5 degrees warmer than usual at this time of year.

Yle meteorologist Toni Hellinen says that the warm weather may continue for the first week of September and perhaps also the second week. On Saturday there will be showers in the southern and central parts of the country and in Kainuu. Sunday will be sunnier across the whole country.
