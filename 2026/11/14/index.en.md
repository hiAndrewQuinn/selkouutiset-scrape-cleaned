## Introducing the topic of the week

This time the topic of the program is telephone company sales work.

Yle investigated how telephone companies sell subscriptions and devices.

Many customers buy unnecessary and overly expensive services.

Vendors sell them especially to the elderly and people who don't understand what they are buying.

We will now tell you what is happening in sales and what telephone company executives are saying about it.

## Selling useless products

Former employees of telephone companies, namely Elisa, Telia and DNA, tell Yle about sales work.

They say that customers are often sold products that they don't need or know how to use.

For example, a customer can be sold an expensive 5G subscription even though the customer's phone does not work on the 5G network.

A tablet can also be sold to a customer even if they don't need one.

The sales package can also include a television service or data security that no one installs on the phone.

Sometimes the salesperson says that the subscription price will decrease, even though in reality the customer's bill will increase.

Former employees say that elderly and insecure customers in particular are pressured to buy unnecessary products.

## Commission

The problem is caused by the commission, or extra pay, that the salesperson receives for selling. The salesperson wants to sell a lot because he or she gets more money.

This causes many problems for customers. For example, people pay every month for services they know nothing about.

Often, children of elderly people have to come to the store to clarify and cancel unnecessary contracts made by the elderly.

The Consumer Authority receives approximately 1,500 calls from telephone companies per year. The number of calls has increased significantly in recent years.

## “Individual errors”

The leaders of Elisa, Telia and DNA deny the problem.

They say that these are isolated errors and that the guidelines prohibit pressuring customers and selling unnecessary products.

Telephone companies say that the salesperson should always check what kind of phone the customer has.

However, DNA's director admits that additional bonuses can sometimes lead to overselling.
