# Wednesday 30.8.2017 (radio)

Police leaders are suspected of a crime. Finland's top prosecutor is not allowed to work. The government is considering next year's budget. Heavy rain is troubling Mumbai.

## Police leaders suspected of a crime

![Police Commissioner General Mikko Paatero, Helsinki Police Department chief Lasse Aapio and Helsinki police commander Jukka Riikonen.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9805458)

Mikko Paatero, Lasse Aapio and Jukka Riikonen. Photo: AOP / Lehtikuva

Senior police leaders in Finland are suspected of a crime. The former Police Commissioner General Mikko Paatero is suspected of a crime, as are the former and current Helsinki police chiefs, Jukka Riikonen and Lasse Aapio.

They are suspected of a breach of official duty. It is said that they have not supervised police work as the law requires. The case is connected to the criminal investigation of Jari Aarnio, the former head of the Helsinki drug police.

It is suspected that Aarnio removed the names of whistleblowers from the police register. All the police leaders say that they have not committed a crime.

## Finland's top prosecutor is not allowed to work

![State Prosecutor Matti Nissinen](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9235472)

Open image viewer

State Prosecutor Matti Nissinen is Finland's top prosecutor. Photo: Juha Kivioja / Yle

The Ministry of Justice has decided that the State Prosecutor, that is, Finland's highest prosecutor, Matti Nissinen, is not allowed to do his work. The ministry says that Nissinen must stop working, because he is suspected of a crime.

The suspicion of a crime arose from training that was ordered for prosecutors. The training was organised by a company led by Nissinen's brother.

The training cost over 74,000 euros. Nissinen has said that he has not committed a crime.

## The government considers next year's budget

![Prime Minister Juha Sipilä, Minister of Finance Petteri Orpo and Minister for European Affairs, Culture and Sport Sampo Terho at the government budget session at Kesäranta in Helsinki, 30 August.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9805791)

Open image viewer

The government is considering the budget at Kesäranta in Helsinki. Photo: Martti Kainulainen / Lehtikuva

The Finnish government has started to think about how money will be spent next year. The government has met together to consider what the state will give money to next year, and how money can be found for the state.

The government has disagreed, for example, about whether Finland must pay off its debts.

Finland's finances are looked after by the Minister of Finance, Petteri Orpo. He is pleased that Finland's economy has begun to improve. Prime Minister Juha Sipilä, for his part, says that Finland cannot start to waste money, even though the economy is improving.

It is expected that on Thursday the government will say what it has decided.

## Heavy rain is troubling Mumbai

![People wade through water in Mumbai.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9806858)

Open image viewer

People in Mumbai have had to wade through water in the heavy downpour.

India's largest city, Mumbai, has been thrown into chaos because of heavy downpours. It has been raining hard in Mumbai for four days already. The water has risen onto the streets and disrupted traffic. There are disruptions in train and air traffic.

Schools and offices have also had to be closed. Mumbai has over 20 million inhabitants. South Asia is now in the monsoon season.

This year it has rained a great deal during the monsoon season. The rains have caused floods, and more than 1,200 people have died in India, Nepal and Bangladesh.
