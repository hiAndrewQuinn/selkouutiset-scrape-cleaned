# Monday 5.9.2016 (radio)

Teachers do not want to become principals. The government plans to reduce benefits for immigrants. Almost all wage earners are now part of the competitiveness agreement. Birches suffer from the wet summer. The number of giant pandas in China has grown.

## Teachers do not want to become principals

![A sign for the office and the staff room in a school corridor.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6642308)

Open image viewer

Principals have really a lot of work nowadays. Photo: Yle

Teachers are no longer interested in the job of principal. It can be hard to find a principal especially for schools in remote areas.

A principal, that is, the head of a school, has really a lot of work nowadays.

For example, Agneta Torsell is the principal of 3 schools in Espoo. She says that all the school's matters and tasks belong to the principal. The principal even changes the lamps if needed.

Because of principals' big workload, teachers no longer want to become principals. Teachers would rather just teach.

## The government saves on benefits for immigrants

![Twenty-euro notes.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8925809)

Open image viewer

The government plans to reduce the integration allowance next year. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

The government plans to reduce benefits for unemployed immigrants.

An unemployed immigrant is to be paid an integration allowance that is 10 percent smaller than an unemployed Finn's basic daily allowance. At minimum, an immigrant may be left with 500 euros a month in hand.

The smaller benefit is planned to be introduced next year.

The aim is to save money. In addition, the government hopes that reducing the benefit lowers asylum seekers' wish to come to Finland.

## The competitiveness agreement is now ready

![Markku Jalonen, Vuokko Piekkala, Antti Palola, Jarkko Eloranta, Jyri Häkämies, Juha Sarkio and Sture Fjäder at the signing of the competitiveness agreement.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9132360)

The municipal sector organisations signed the competitiveness agreement on Monday. In the front of the picture, labour market director Markku Jalonen and EK's CEO Jyri Häkämies shake hands, while Akava's chairman Sture Fjäder watches from the side. Photo: Petteri Paalasmaa / AOP

Municipal sector workers have signed the competitiveness agreement.

Over 90 percent of wage earners are now part of the agreement.

The agreement means that Finns' working hours get a little longer, even though pay does not go up. For example, the annual pay of municipal workers goes down because of the agreement.

In return, the government lowers taxes a little.

The government hopes that the agreement improves Finland's economy.

## Fungal diseases threaten birches

![A sick birch.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9137646)

Open image viewer

Birch rust turns birch leaves yellow. Photo: Kyösti Vaara / Yle

Birches are in poor condition because of the rainy summer.

The Natural Resources Institute says that because of the warm July and the rainy August, birches now suffer from many fungal diseases.

For example, in the north you can see many trees that suffer from birch rust. In city parks, on the other hand, you can see a birch virus disease, says researcher Risto Jalkanen.

Risto Jalkanen is worried about how the birches will survive the winter. But he does not believe in large-scale birch damage.

## Giant pandas are now doing better

![A panda eats bamboo in a zoo.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6192399)

Open image viewer

Giant panda Ying Ying eats bamboo in the Hong Kong zoo. Photo: Ym Yik / EPA

Giant pandas are now doing well. The International Union for Conservation of Nature says that the number of giant pandas has grown so much that the species is no longer as endangered as before.

The species is still vulnerable, so panda bears must still be protected.

According to new estimates, over 1800 adult giant pandas now live in the wild

The giant panda, or great panda, lives in deciduous and coniferous forests in central China. The giant panda eats bamboo as its food.
