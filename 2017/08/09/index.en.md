## Discrimination because of names

A foreign name, or a name that sounds foreign, can make looking for work difficult in Finland. In job seeking, there is more discrimination because of ethnic background than because of gender.

Many people have even translated their name into Finnish or chosen a Finnish surname. Nicoletta Kekoniemi from Lappeenranta did this. She changed her Italian surname to her husband's Finnish surname when she got married. Nicoletta Kekoniemi says that now people no longer wonder about her foreign background so much.

## Enontekiö is expecting new residents

In the municipality of Enontekiö, in the very northernmost part of Finland, new residents are expected. Three Syrian refugee families will soon move to the municipality.

Residents of Enontekiö have collected furniture and household items for the families. In Enontekiö the families have an Arabic-speaking assistant who helps them with everyday life.

## The United States and North Korea in a war of words

The United States and North Korea are threatening each other with harsh words.

In the United States, the newspaper the Washington Post reported that North Korea has a nuclear warhead that fits on a missile. US President Donald Trump warned that North Korea must not threaten the United States. North Korea's state news agency then replied that North Korea's missiles can fly as far as the US island of Guam.

## Graffiti got into a museum

Street art can now also be seen in a museum. Finland's first graffiti exhibition is on show at the Kerava Art Museum.  Graffiti has been made in Finland since the 1980s,  but many people have thought of it as just scribbles.
