## Student protests

The student protest continues in Helsinki. Students have taken over the main building of the University of Helsinki. The protest has already continued for 10 days.

Students were allowed to continue the protest also today, Thursday, even though an important event is being held in the main building of the university. President Sauli Niinistö also takes part in the event.

Student protests have also spread to other educational institutions in Finland. Students are angry because the government intends to reduce the amount of money that goes, for example, to study grants and housing allowance.

## Housing prices

Housing prices have fallen in Finland. Old housing company apartments were over 6 percent cheaper in August than a year earlier. This is what Statistics Finland says.

Prices fell most in the Helsinki region. There, old housing company apartments became cheaper by over 8 percent in a year.

Housing prices have fallen more in apartment blocks than in terraced houses.

## Studying Finnish

![Wassana Khangkheree takes a picture of the situation she wants to discuss with her work supervisor.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1919,h_1080,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-1176997651281c53db59/1695712190)

Wassana Khangkheree studied Finnish by photographing her own work. Photo: Jukka Lehto / Yle

In Helsinki a new way of studying Finnish is being tried. In it the immigrant does not sit in a classroom. They start their studies at the workplace.

At the workplace, the immigrant photographs their own work and discusses the pictures with the teacher. In this way the words and tasks of the workplace become familiar.

A group of students from the food sector and day-care centres is trying this new way of studying. For example, Wassana Khangkheree learned the work and the Finnish language at a day-care centre. Now she has a job there.

The new kind of study suited Khangkheree well. She remembers and learns better when she gets to move around and work with children.

## Hunger Day collection

![A young man collects money in a box on the street during the Hunger Day collection.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1920,h_1080,x_0,y_33/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-7150305f50d764610f6/1695912960)

Open image viewer

The Hunger Day collection is held every autumn. Photo: FRK/ www.malkamaki.com

The Finnish Red Cross's Hunger Day collection has started. The Red Cross is collecting money so that it can help people in emergency situations.

The Red Cross says that help is now needed, for example, in Libya because of floods. Help is also needed in Morocco, where there was a big earthquake.

The Hunger Day collectors are now moving on the streets. They are collecting money until Saturday.

On Friday a little rain falls in the north. In the south it is dry, meaning it does not rain. Almost across the whole country it is cloudy.
