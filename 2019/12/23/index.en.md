# Monday 23.12.2019 (radio)

Many nurses are considering changing careers. Pork sales from Finland to China may grow. Artificial light disturbs sleep in cities. Lapland has record amounts of snow.

## Problems in nursing

![Sari Äkäslompolo and Reeta Vähämäki](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11132616)

Nurse Sari Äkäslompolo and midwife Reeta Vähämäki say that they intend to leave health care. Photo: Heikki Haapalainen and Juha Kivioja / Yle

Many nurses intend to stop working in health care. They are dissatisfied with working conditions in health care.

Studies show that nurses are dissatisfied, especially because of poor management. Nurses think that the big bosses at hospitals do not listen to nurses.

Temporary work also frustrates many nurses. It is often hard for a nurse to get a permanent job.

Especially nurses aged 30 to 50 are considering changing careers. That is a bad thing, because they already have a lot of work experience.

Health care is being talked about in Finland, because the sector will need more workers in the coming years.

## Pork to China

![Pork sales at a wholesale market in Beijing.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10985986)

Open image viewer

The Chinese like to buy Finnish pork. Photo: Wu Hong / EPA

Pork sales from Finland to China may grow. The reason is that China is lowering tariffs on pork.

The tariff on frozen pork has been 12 per cent. Now the tariff is falling to 8 per cent. That is why Finns hope that Chinese people will buy more Finnish pork.

Meat companies HKScan and Atria, for example, sell Finnish pork to China.

China needs pork from abroad, because there is swine fever in China. Swine fever is a dangerous animal disease that kills pigs.

## Artificial light in cities

![Christmas lights in Sysmä](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4160,h_2340,x_0,y_215/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6240415df9fe9d52770/1576665070)

Open image viewer

Even small towns have a lot of light. Photo: Mika Moksu / Yle

Now is the darkest time of the year. However, there is a lot of artificial light in cities. Artificial light comes from street lamps, advertisements and also Christmas lights.

Research professor Timo Partonen says that too much light can even be a health hazard. If it is light all the time, it disturbs the body's internal clock.

The internal clock is a group of nerve cells in the brain. It controls the sleep rhythm. Light and darkness affect the internal clock, Professor Partonen says.

In the evening, darkness makes people tired and helps them sleep. That is why it is good to make sure too much artificial light does not come in through the window.

## Snowy Lapland

![Car buried in snow.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11133770)

Open image viewer

This is what Lapland looks like now. Photo: Vesa Moilanen / Lehtikuva

There is a lot of snow in Lapland this Christmas. There is more snow than usual at Christmas time.

The Finnish Meteorological Institute says that in some places in Lapland there is already over 80 centimetres of snow.

At Christmas a little more snow may fall in Lapland. So Christmas in the north is beautifully white.

In Southern Finland, Christmas looks quite different. In the Helsinki region, for example, there is no snow at all.
