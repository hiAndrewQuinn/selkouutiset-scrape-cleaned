# Wednesday 24.5.2017 (radio)

Mass murder charges against Iraqi brothers were dismissed. Arrests over the Manchester bomb attack. Many Finns have moved abroad. Finns like chocolate. President Koivisto's funeral will be held on Ascension Day.

## Iraqi brothers cleared of mass murder charges

![Detention hearing of a terrorism suspect in Tampere](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8521822)

The detention hearing of an Iraqi man was held in Tampere in December 2015. Now the man and his brother have been released.  Photo: Leena Peltokangas / Yle

The Pirkanmaa District Court did not convict the Iraqi brothers who were accused of mass murder. The men have been released.

The prosecutor demanded in court that the brothers be sentenced to life imprisonment for a mass murder that was committed in Iraq in 2014.

According to the prosecutor, the brothers took part in the mass murder. The brothers came to Finland in September 2015, and the police arrested them in December.

## Arrests made because of the Manchester attack

![Two police officers walk on a street](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9628954)

Open image viewer

There are many armed police on the streets of Manchester after the bomb attack. Photo: Andy Rain / EPA

British police have arrested some people because of the Manchester bomb attack. The police suspect that they are connected to the bomb attack. The bomber died in the attack.

Britain has raised the terrorism threat level after the Manchester attack. It is now the highest possible, which means a new terrorist attack could happen at any time.

The Finnish Ministry for Foreign Affairs also says that people must be careful in Britain, although Britain is still quite a safe country for travellers.

The bomb attack was carried out on Monday evening at the Manchester Arena, where there was a concert. There were many children and young people at the concert. Over 20 people died in the attack and dozens were injured. Some people were seriously injured.

## There are thousands of Finns in many cities abroad

![The Finnish empire](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9627774)

Open image viewer

Finns have moved abroad for things like work, warmth and love. Photo: Henna Ryynänen

There are many cities abroad where thousands of Finns live.

Most Finns live in Sweden. 46,000 Finns live in Stockholm and 18,000 Finns in Gothenburg.

In the 1900s many Finns moved abroad because they had to find work.

Nowadays many Finns also move abroad because of the sun and warmth. For example, over 20,000 Finns live on Spain's Costa del Sol.

## Finns like chocolate

![In the picture, a shop's sweets shelf with chocolate and bags of sweets.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8790037)

Open image viewer

The favourite product on the shop's sweets shelf is chocolate. Photo: Jarmo Nuotio / Yle

Finns are eating more sweets than before.

Shops say that sales of sweets have increased at the beginning of the year.

Sweets are now cheap, because no tax is paid on them any more.

The Finns' favourite sweet is chocolate, the shops say.

All five of the most popular sweets are chocolate.

## President Koivisto will be buried on Ascension Day

![President Mauno Koivisto smiles.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9630935)

Open image viewer

President Mauno Koivisto in 2003. Photo: Mauri Ratilainen / AOP

President Mauno Koivisto's funeral is on Thursday in Helsinki.

A president's funeral is a big event. On Thursday there are many police in Helsinki, and streets have been closed.

As many as 100,000 people may follow the funeral procession.

The procession carries the president's coffin. The procession sets off from Helsinki Cathedral.

President Mauno Koivisto will be buried in Hietaniemi Cemetery.

You can follow the funeral on TV1 and on Yle's website.

Mauno Koivisto was President of the Republic of Finland from 1982 to 1994. He died at the age of 93.
