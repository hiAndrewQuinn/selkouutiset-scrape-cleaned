# Monday 13.8.2018 (radio)

The head of the National Bureau of Investigation goes to court. According to a new book, President Sauli Niinistö uses more power than the law says. The Ministry of the Environment wants more information about what houses are built from. Young people are much more worried about climate change than 10 years ago.

## The head of the National Bureau of Investigation goes to court

![The head of the National Bureau of Investigation Robin Lardot at the bureau's press conference in Turku on 19 August 2017.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10284926)

Robin Lardot. Photo: EPA

A senior police chief, Robin Lardot, is being charged in court.

Lardot is accused of not properly supervising how the police use informants. Many police informants are criminals who give the police information about other people's crimes.

Lardot is now the head of the National Bureau of Investigation. He goes to court together with four other police chiefs. They are all accused of the same thing. They all say that they have not committed offences in office. Some of the accused police chiefs are now retired or have been dismissed.

The trial starts on Tuesday.

## Book: The president uses too much power

![Sauli Niinistö](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10262050)

Open image viewer

Sauli Niinistö. Photo: Yle

The journalists Matti Mörttinen and Lauri Nurmi have written a book about President Sauli Niinistö.

The book says that the president has used more power than the law says. Niinistö has also used power in domestic politics. The book says, for example, that Niinistö persuaded the employees' and employers' organisations to continue negotiations on the government's competitiveness pact. This happened about 2 years ago.

Finland's constitution says that the government handles domestic politics and EU politics. They do not belong to the president, says Professor Tapio Raunio.

## Building materials of houses for everyone to know

![A builder tiles a bathroom.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10348693)

Open image viewer

A man builds a bathroom. Photo: Ismo Pekkarinen / AOP

The Ministry of the Environment wants to collect more information about what houses are built from.

The ministry wants information about what supplies and materials have been used in a house, and how much, to be available to everyone. The information is useful, for example, when people want to find out why a house has unhealthy indoor air. The information also helps recycling of the supplies and materials.

Building firms already have the information on their computers. The industry organisation says that sharing the information with others is quite a good idea.

## Young people worry about climate change

![A protester in the Netherlands who has covered his face with moss and lichen.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10311538)

Open image viewer

A protester in the Netherlands in 2015. Photo: Bart Maat / EPA

Young people are much more worried about climate change than 10 years ago.

A study says that almost 70 percent of young Finns are worried about climate change. Girls are more worried than boys. Young people with a lot of schooling are more worried than those with little schooling.

Many young people also want to slow down climate change themselves, for example by eating vegetarian food and using a bicycle or a bus and not their own car. But they do not always have the money to buy products as sustainable as they would like.
