# Sunday 11.3.2018 (radio)

Many young people have too much debt. Many want to protect their own forest. A Sámi film won a prize. Finns have 120,000 different first names.

## Some young people have a lot of debt

![Coins on a hand.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10111162)

Open image viewer

It is easier to take out a loan than to pay it back. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

Many young people have too much debt.

Some young people have so much debt that almost all their pay goes on paying the debt. Then the young person may stop working and live on social assistance, because they do not have to pay debt from it. Today it is much easier to borrow money than before. Loans are advertised and you can get them fast. But many young people do not have a permanent job, from whose pay it would be easier to pay back a loan.

The Guarantee Foundation helps people who have too much debt. Juha Pantzar from the Guarantee Foundation says that loan advertisements must be reduced.

## People want to protect their own forest

![Edge bog of the southern forest islet in the northern basin of Kivisuo.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10107844)

Open image viewer

Forest and bog in northern Finland. Photo: Ari-Pekka Auvinen / Finnish Nature Conservation Foundation

Many forest owners want to protect their own forests.

Forests are protected, for example, by ordinary Finns who live in a city and inherit forest from relatives. Many want to keep the forest's nature and do not want to cut down and sell the trees. Last year people set up at least 170 new protected areas, a total of 30 square kilometres of forest.

At the same time, the state is reducing the money that is used to protect forests. The government also hopes that more trees will be cut down. New factories that use a lot of wood have been set up in Finland.

Forests are cut down so much that Finland's nature is becoming poorer: plant and animal species are disappearing completely.

## Nature and ballet in a Sámi film

![Eatnanvuloš lottit – Birds in the Earth.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10111630)

Open image viewer

Birit and Katja Haarla in the film Birds in the Earth. Photo: Marja Helander / Eatnanvuloš lottit – Birds in the Earth

The Sámi film Birds in the Earth has won the Risto Jarva Prize.

Marja Helander's film asks who owns the land and waters in the northern areas where the Sámi live. The main roles in the film are played by the nature of Lapland and two Sámi ballet dancers, Birit and Katja Haarla.

The Risto Jarva Prize was given by the Finnish Film Foundation at the Tampere Film Festival.

## There are many more first names than before

![The naming-day cake of Ukko Vilho Valtteri.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6178941)

Open image viewer

A cake to celebrate Ukko getting his name. Photo: Yle

Finnish citizens use 120,000 different first names. As late as the 1980s, there were half as many first names.

Today it is fashionable to give a child an unusual first name. A lot of names are also taken from nature, for example Lumi (snow), Kaisla (reed) and Meri (sea). Parents often give a name that is familiar and popular in many countries at the same time, for example Maria, Emilia, Oliver and Daniel. Many parents do not want to give a child a name with the letter ä or ö, because it is difficult abroad.

In 2016, the most popular girls' first names were Emma, Sofia and Aino, and the most popular boys' names were Onni, Elias and Leo.
