# Saturday 23.11.2019 (radio)

The investigation of the postal dispute continues. Finland is sending asylum seekers back to Venezuela. Finns think the voting age should not be lowered. A sea eagle has been shot at Pöytyä. Many towns open the Christmas season.

## A proposed solution to the postal dispute

![PAU chair Heidi Nieminen comments on the negotiation situation after arriving at the National Conciliator's office on Saturday](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11083577)

A proposed solution has been made to the postal dispute.

The working group that is investigating the postal dispute has made a proposal on how the dispute is to be resolved. The content of the proposal is not disclosed.

The parties to the dispute, that is, the employee organisation PAU and the employers' organisation Palta, must give their answer to the proposal tomorrow afternoon at 3 pm.

The postal dispute concerns 700 people who sort parcels. Posti moved them to a cheaper collective agreement, and PAU does not accept this.

The postal strike has lasted almost 2 weeks. On Monday, several other strikes that support the postal workers are also due to begin.

## Finland returns people to Venezuela

![Finland's former ambassador to Venezuela 2006-2013 Mikko Pyhälä](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1920,h_1080,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5597495cc9e0436ab02/1556734284)

Finland's former ambassador to Venezuela, Mikko Pyhälä. Photo: Yle

Finland has returned asylum seekers to Venezuela.

Finland's former ambassador to Venezuela, Mikko Pyhälä, says that Finland has made returns to Venezuela, even though the UN refugee agency UNHCR has called on all countries to stop returns. UNHCR says that the humanitarian situation in Venezuela is very bad. For that reason, no one should be returned until conditions in Venezuela improve.

South American Venezuela has shortages of food and medicine, clean water and electricity. The country's political situation is confused, and crime and violence have increased.

## The voting age is not to be lowered

![A man writes a number on a voting slip.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10746346)

Open image viewer

A voting slip from the last Parliamentary election. Photo: Kalle Mäkelä / Yle

Finns think that the current voting age limit, 18 years, is good. Most Finns think that the voting age should not be lowered.

The Municipal Sector Development Foundation asked Finns whether the voting age should be lowered to 16. Only just over 25 percent of respondents supported lowering the age limit. Almost 60 percent opposed it.

## Police investigate the shooting of a sea eagle

![A sea eagle flies in a cloudless sky.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_2100,h_1182,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/14-svyle-29871357a0defab9f2a/1683879934)

Open image viewer

The sea eagle is a protected bird. Photo: Yle, Risto Salovaara

The police suspect a nature conservation crime in Pöytyä in Varsinais-Suomi.

In the middle of this month a sea eagle was found in Pöytyä, injured and in poor condition. A shotgun pellet was found in the sea eagle's wing, that is, the bird had been shot. The bird's wing was also broken when the bird fell to the ground.

The police remind that the sea eagle is a protected bird in Finland. A protected bird may not be killed or harmed, nor even deliberately disturbed.
