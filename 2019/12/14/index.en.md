# Saturday 14.12.2019 (radio)

The President answered citizens' questions. Ukraine has overtaken Estonia in work-based immigration. Finns do not want Finland to join NATO. Operation Snowflake helps with traffic during the Christmas period.

## The people asked, the President answered

![The President of the Republic, Sauli Niinistö, answered questions and talked with listeners on Yle Radio 1 on 14 December.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11118022)

Yle Radio Yhdessä broadcast the President's question hour today. President Sauli Niinistö answered citizens' questions for an hour.

The President was asked about many kinds of things. For example, he was asked about Britain's exit from the EU, the marginalisation of young people, and food production.

The President was also asked about bringing back Finnish women and children from the al-Hol camp in Syria. The President said that he is waiting for the government's line on the matter. He said that a line is needed, because very different information is in circulation. The government is due to decide on the line on Monday.

The President's question hour can be listened to on Yle Areena.

## Defence Minister also comments on al-Hol

![Defence Minister](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5472,h_3078,x_0,y_282/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6125025dcfe1f816c9c/1573904992)

Defence Minister Antti Kaikkonen also says that the situation of the Finns in the al-Hol camp must be resolved quickly. Kaikkonen said on TV1's Ykkösaamu that the problem only gets worse when it drags on.

Kaikkonen said that the greatest attention must now be paid to helping the children.

The al-Hol camp in Syria holds family members of Isis terrorists. There are 11 Finnish women in the camp, and about 30 children with them.

## Ukraine brings a lot of labour

![A worker's hands laying paving stones.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10309281)

Open image viewer

A Ukrainian worker lays paving stones. Photo: Elisa Kinnunen / Yle

More people are coming to Finland from Ukraine to work than before. More people are already coming to work in Finland from Ukraine than from Estonia.

The Finnish Immigration Service says that last year over 1,800 Ukrainians were hired to work in Finland. At the same time, about 1,200 Estonians were hired.

Fewer workers are now coming to Finland from Estonia than before, because average wages in Estonia have risen. In Ukraine, on the other hand, the average wage is only 350 euros a month.

## Finns reject NATO membership

![Row of NATO country flags on a parade street in London.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5448,h_3065,x_0,y_332/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6184245de581cbbcd1b/1575322154)

A row of NATO country flags. Photo: All Over Press

Finns do not want to join the NATO military alliance.

47 per cent, almost half of Finns, think that Finland should not join NATO. 22 per cent of Finns support NATO membership.

Taloustutkimus carried out the survey on this, and over 2,000 people answered.

## Operation Snowflake helps drivers

![Autoliitto roadside service text on the back of a boiler suit](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6426217)

Open image viewer

Autoliitto's volunteers come to help if a car breaks down during a journey. Photo: Miki Wallenius / Yle

Operation Snowflake has started again.

Operation Snowflake is an aid operation run by Autoliitto's road service. In it, volunteers help drivers who get into trouble in some way. A car can, for example, get a puncture or run out of petrol.

Operation Snowflake lasts until New Year's Day.
