# Thursday 29.11.2018 (radio)

The government proposes a new law on dual citizens. Finland does well when the well-being of different countries is compared. The new road traffic law reduces the number of speeding fines. Christmas tree growers are now busy.

## Dual citizens

![Jussi Niinistö](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10532528)

Open image viewer

Defence Minister Jussi Niinistö. Photo: Vesa Moilanen / Lehtikuva

The government proposes a new law on dual citizens. Dual citizens have the citizenship of Finland and of another country.

The government's proposal concerns Finnish military posts. The government does not want to ban all dual citizens from Finnish military posts.

However, the government proposes that a dual citizen cannot get a military post if having dual citizenship could harm Finland's security.

Defence Minister Jussi Niinistö does not say which country's citizenship could be a danger to Finland.

Next, Parliament, that is, the Finnish parliament, will handle the government's law proposal.

## Well-being comparison

![School class](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10202041)

Open image viewer

The Finnish school was the best in the comparison. Photo: Terhi Varjonen/Yle

Finland has done well in a new international comparison. A London institute compared well-being in different countries.

149 countries were part of the comparison. Finland was in 3rd place.

The well-being of the countries was studied in 9 different areas. Finland was the best in education and administration.

Finland's worst result came when health was studied. Finland was only in 25th place in health.

The other Nordic countries also did well. Norway was the best country in the comparison.

## Road traffic law

![A speed camera watches speeds.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9720373)

Open image viewer

A speed camera takes pictures of cars. Photo: Ismo Pekkarinen / AOP

The road traffic law is changing. The new law comes into force in summer 2020.

The new law reduces the number of speeding fines. The police can give fines only if the picture from the surveillance camera, that is, the speed camera, is good. The driver's face must be clearly visible in the picture. For example, in snowfall, the pictures from the speed camera are often bad. That is why not everyone who drives too fast will get a fine notice.

The number of other fines grows when the new law comes into force. The police can give fines if the speed camera picture shows, for example, that the driver drives through a red light or without a seatbelt.

## Christmas trees

![Janne Muhonen from Ähtäri grows Christmas trees on a small scale in his own yard.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10531437)

Many Finns want a real Christmas tree. Photo: Mirva Ekman / Yle

Christmas tree growers are now busy. Growers cut down about one million Christmas trees every year.

Few trees are grown in Finland. That is why Christmas trees are brought in from abroad, for example from Denmark and Estonia.

Finns like real trees, although there are also plastic trees today.

Trees grow slowly. A Christmas tree grows for about 10 or 15 years before it is cut down.
