# Sunday 19.8.2018 (radio)

Many children from poor families are bullied. There is a serious shortage of border guards. The hunting of waterfowl and bears begins. The heat is over for now.

## Poverty can be a reason for bullying

![Friends filming themselves with a mobile phone.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9305942)

Open image viewer

Many bullied people say that schools have not tried to stop bullying or discrimination. Photo: Tiina Jutila / Yle

Many children from poor families experience bullying.

An online survey by the child rights organisation Save the Children says that almost 70 percent of children from poor families have experienced bullying. Of children from well-off families, 40 percent have experienced bullying.

The survey shows that the reason for bullying can be poverty itself. Children can recognise poverty. They pay attention, for example, to the quality of things, the brand, the age and the model.

More than 30 percent of bullied people said that schools have not in any way tried to stop bullying and discrimination.

## There is a shortage of border guards

![Information board for passengers at an airport.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10357721)

Open image viewer

Many passengers are now arriving at Helsinki-Vantaa Airport from Asia. Photo: Kalevi Rytkölä / Yle

There is a serious shortage of border guards at Helsinki-Vantaa Airport. Several dozen more border guards are needed at the airport.

Attempts have been made to ease the staff shortage by lending border guards to Helsinki-Vantaa from the eastern border and from Lapland.

More border guards are needed because the number of long-distance passengers is growing quickly. Many passengers are now coming, especially from Asia. EU rules say that border guards must check air passengers who come, for example, from China.

## The hunting of waterfowl and bears begins

![two tufted ducks](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10179272)

Open image viewer

Tufted ducks are critically endangered. Photo: Kari Kosonen /Yle

The waterfowl hunting season begins tomorrow, Monday.

The bird organisation Birdlife and the Hunters' Association say that hunters should not shoot birds whose populations have declined, that is, decreased a lot in recent years. For example, the long-tailed duck, the tufted duck and the common moorhen are critically endangered, and they should not be shot.

Bear hunting also begins tomorrow.

The bear population in Finland is strong. About 1800 bears over one year old are roaming in the forests.

This year, there is permission to hunt, that is, to kill, at most 355 bears.

## The heat is over for now

![Weather graphics.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10359995)

Open image viewer

The temperature is falling below 20 degrees across almost the whole country. Photo: Joonas Koskela / Yle Sää

This summer has been the second warmest in Finland's measurement history.

So far there have been 62 hot days in total. Only yesterday, Saturday, the 25-degree heat limit was exceeded in Lappeenranta and Porvoo, for example.

The record for hot days is from 2002. Then there were 65 hot days in total.

Meteorologist Joonas Koskela says that the heat is over for now. The weather early in the week is unsettled, and the temperature is falling below 20 degrees across almost the whole country.
