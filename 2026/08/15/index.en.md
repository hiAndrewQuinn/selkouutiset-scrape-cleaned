## The problem with economic forecasts

The Kalevi Sorsa Foundation studied how Finnish experts predict economic growth and economic turnarounds.

Predictions rarely come true.

The problem is that forecasters make predictions based on statistical data. However, the statistical data is often old or inaccurate.

Because experts do not know the exact current state of the economy, it is impossible for them to predict the future economic situation well.

Predictions are sometimes correct only by chance.

The Finnish government makes important decisions based on economic forecasts.

That is why incorrect economic forecasts are a big problem for society.

For example, the Ministry of Finance makes forecasts that the government uses when it plans the state budget.

The government reads forecasts when it assesses adjustment needs, i.e. makes decisions on taxes and cuts.

Researchers warn that politicians may make the wrong decisions because the forecasts are inaccurate.

Researchers say politicians should not rely too much on economic forecasts.

Researchers recommend that politicians use recent figures and information from tax authorities.

Many different institutions make economic forecasts in Finland.

Forecasts are made by, for example, the Ministry of Finance, the Bank of Finland and Pellervo Economic Research.

They defend economic forecasts.

Forecasters admit that it is difficult to estimate exact figures and economic changes in advance.

However, forecasters say that forecasts are helpful to decision-makers.

Forecasters say that what matters is the overall big picture of the economy, not the small numbers.
