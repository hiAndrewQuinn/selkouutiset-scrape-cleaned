## New government starts

![Finland's new government 2023.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5120,h_2880,x_0,y_400/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-11328246491b9e31f6ff/1687271941)

The new government has 19 ministers. Photo: Antti Aimo-Koivisto / Lehtikuva

Finland has got a new government. President **Sauli Niinistö** granted the resignation of Sanna Marin's government on Tuesday. After that, President Niinistö appointed the new government.

The new government is led by **Petteri Orpo**. Orpo is the chair of the National Coalition. He is also the prime minister of the new government.

The new government has 19 ministers. The ministers of the new government are from the National Coalition, the Finns Party, the Christian Democrats and the Swedish People's Party, that is, RKP. The new ministers took the official oath or affirmation of office on Tuesday.

## Employment

![A woman stands and picks plastic parts from a moving conveyor belt by a robot in a factory.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3740,h_2104,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-97031462a99187b7025/1655280455)

The share of people in work is growing. Photo: Anna Wikman / Yle

The share of Finns in work is growing gradually. In May, 74.3 per cent of working-age Finns were in work.

The share grew a little from the previous month, that is, April.

In May last year, the share of people in work was 73.8 per cent. So the share of people in work has risen by half a percentage point in a year.

## Savings

![A sandy beach full of people.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1920,h_1080,x_0,y_36/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6943385ef9a8fb4dcd7/1593420079)

Many Finns are saving on holiday trips, for example. Photo: Andy Rain 7 EPA

Finns are now saving on many things. The reason is rising prices.

The Consumers' Association has asked Finns what they have saved on this year. More than half of Finns have saved on restaurant visits, grocery shopping costs and holiday trips.

A little under half of Finns have cut back on driving cars. About 15 per cent have saved on medicine costs.

## Pregnancy discrimination

![A pregnant belly under a knitted jumper. A woman and a man hold their hands on it.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5178,h_2912,x_0,y_355/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-106486563d3a76b7ce17/1674817048)

Open image viewer

Tehy says that there is pregnancy discrimination in the health care sector. Photo: Meri Lantela / Yle

There is pregnancy discrimination in the social and health care sector. This is what the nurses' organisation Tehy says. Pregnancy discrimination means, for example, that a person is not offered work when she becomes pregnant.

An employer can also ask that a person comes back to work straight after maternity leave.

Pregnancy discrimination affects fixed-term workers, who are not always taken back to work because of pregnancy. Pregnancy discrimination is illegal.

## Estonia's equal marriage law

![A rainbow flag flutters.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_6000,h_3375,x_0,y_178/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-7493405fc7e0df56a78/1656299805)

Open image viewer

The equal marriage law allows same-sex couples to marry. Photo: Petteri Bülow / Yle

Estonia has approved an equal marriage law. An equal marriage law means that two men or two women can marry each other. They can also adopt children together.

The equal marriage law will come into force in Estonia next year. In Estonia's parliament, 55 MPs voted for the law. 34 MPs voted against it.

Estonia is the first Central European country to allow people of the same sex to marry.
