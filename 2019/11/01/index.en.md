# Friday 1.11.2019 (radio)

The effect of the active model is unclear. The Industrial Union and Pro start an overtime ban. A citizens' initiative on an air travel tax goes to Parliament. October was cool.

## There are no exact figures on the active model

The effect of the active model on the unemployed is unclear. There are no exact figures. This is what a working group that has studied the active model says.

The active model did not increase the number of unemployed people getting jobs. Some of the unemployed receive earnings-related daily allowance. The active model improved their employment.

The active model increased the participation of the unemployed in various courses.

The active model for the unemployed came into force at the beginning of 2018. In the active model, an unemployed person gets less money if they do not take part in courses, for example.

Prime Minister Antti Rinne's government intends to end the model at the beginning of next year.

## The Industrial Union starts an overtime ban

![Technology Industries of Finland and the Industrial Union started negotiations on renewing the collective agreement at Eteläranta in Helsinki on 29 August 2019.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5176,h_2912,x_0,y_267/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5867485d67dd62b2020/1567088132)

Technology Industries of Finland and the Industrial Union started negotiations on renewing the collective agreement on 29 August 2019. Photo: Laura Hyyti / Yle

The Industrial Union is starting an overtime ban. The ban starts next Monday and ends on Friday. The overtime ban concerns about 100,000 employees.

The trade union Pro is also starting an overtime ban, which concerns the white-collar employees of the technology industry.

An overtime ban means that employees do not do any overtime at all. The aim is that the collective agreement negotiations may progress faster.

The collective agreement negotiations between the Industrial Union and Technology Industries of Finland are ongoing. The industry agreements expired at the end of October, that is, on Thursday.

The Industrial Union says that there has been a dispute in the negotiations, especially about working time.

## The citizens' initiative on air tax goes to Parliament

![Aeroplane](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3594,h_2022,x_70,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/17-39653392b6d4305e/1561378107)

The signatories of the citizens' initiative demand that Finland introduces an air travel tax. Photo: EPA/Daniel Reinhardt

The citizens' initiative on an air travel tax goes to Parliament. The signatories of the citizens' initiative demand that Finland introduces an air travel tax.

The signatories say that air trips must cost more. The aim is to reduce emissions. Flying speeds up climate change.

An air travel tax is already in place in Sweden, for example.

A citizens' initiative is, for example, a bill that citizens make. Parliament must deal with a citizens' initiative if more than 50,000 people have signed it.

## October was cool

![Snowy autumn landscape in Heinävaara, Joensuu.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11026161)

Open image viewer

Snowy autumn landscape in Heinävaara, Joensuu. Photo: Ismo Pekkarinen / AOP

October was cooler than usual.

The average temperature in October was between -3 and -4 degrees in Northern Lapland, and a little over 7 degrees in the south-west archipelago.

The Finnish Meteorological Institute says that in October it also rained more than usual. Especially in North Karelia it rained a great deal.

At the end of October there was snow in the east and the north.
