# Saturday 17.8.2019 (radio)

Many minor crimes are never solved. The new nutrition recommendations counter climate change. Finnish children's weekly money is bigger than in other Nordic countries. Mmiisas is Finland's most important social media influencer.

## Criminal investigation

![Table of clearance rates for crimes.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10924014)

Open image viewer

Photo: Jyrki Lyytikkä / Yle

Minor crimes are being solved less and less often.

For example, the clearance rate for assaults and property crimes has fallen across the whole country over the last 5 years.

Regional differences are nevertheless big. In Yle's comparison, assaults and property crimes have in recent years been solved most often in Ostrobothnia and least often in Eastern Uusimaa.

In Eastern Uusimaa the police's way of working has changed. Not all minor crimes are investigated any more, or an investigation is left unfinished. In this way the police have time to solve serious crimes.

## Nutrition recommendations

![A tray with a plate of school food.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3843,h_2162,x_0,y_216/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5841665d57e663b2b86/1566041803)

School meals closely follow nutrition recommendations. Photo: Anni Härkönen / Yle

The Nordic countries are currently making new nutrition recommendations. The new recommendations show concern about climate change for the first time.

The new recommendations say that people must eat less meat and more vegetables. It is also good to eat food that is organic and produced nearby. Such food is good for people's health, but also for the climate.

In Finland nutrition recommendations are followed closely, for example in schools and day-care centres. Because of this, the recommendations can really teach people to eat in a new way.

## Children's monthly money

![Pink piggy bank](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10512665)

Open image viewer

Finnish children's monthly money is about 28 euros on average. Photo: Mikko Koski / Yle

Finnish children get more pocket money from their parents than children in other Nordic countries.

In Finland 8–14-year-old children get pocket money of about 28 euros a month on average. The smallest monthly money is in Sweden. There children's monthly money is about 22 euros on average.

A survey by Danske Bank says that in Finland parents want to teach children to work with a weekly or monthly allowance. In return for the money, children must, for example, do the dishes or take out the rubbish.

Swedish parents, on the other hand, hope that the weekly money teaches the child to save money.

## Social media influencers

![Miisa Rotola-Pukkila](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10725919)

Open image viewer

Miisa Rotola-Pukkila is better known by the name Mmiisas. Photo: Minna Perovuo / Yle

24-year-old Miisa Rotola-Pukkila is Finland's most important social media influencer. Rotola-Pukkila is better known by the name Mmiisas.

Mmiisas is a YouTuber, that is, she makes videos for YouTube. The videos reach an average of 100,000 viewers. Mmiisas also has an Instagram account, which more than 400,000 people follow.

In the list by the analytics company Melwater, the next most important social media influencers are the YouTubers Lakko, that is Eric Savolainen, and Roni Bäck.
