# Sunday 21.4.2109 (radio)

Eight bomb attacks have been carried out in Sri Lanka. The attacks have been condemned around the world. Only a few municipalities provide early childhood education in Sami. Home gardening trends are organic growing and useful plants.

## At least 200 dead in Sri Lanka bombings

![St Anthony's church, Kochchikade, Colombo, Sri Lanka, 21 April](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10749943)

Police are investigating traces of the explosion in front of St Anthony's Shrine church in Colombo, the capital of Sri Lanka.. Photo: Pushpa Kumara / EPA

In Sri Lanka in South Asia, churches and hotels were attacked. Evening information said that at least 200 people have died in the bomb attacks.

About 30 of the victims were foreigners, including citizens of the United States, Britain and the Netherlands. More than 500 people were injured.

The Foreign Ministry has no information about Finnish victims.

The attacks were carried out on 8 sites in the capital Colombo and its surroundings. Investigation of the attacks is still ongoing. The news agency AP reports that 7 people have been arrested.

Christians make up 6 percent of Sri Lanka's population. Most of them are Catholics. 74 percent are Buddhist Sinhalese and 18 percent are Hindu Tamils. Muslims are 7 percent.

## Attacks condemned around the world

![Juha Sipilä at the election night event on 14 April.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10743746)

Open image viewer

Prime Minister Juha Sipilä. Photo: Jarno Kuusinen / AOP

Leaders in various parts of the world have condemned the attacks in Sri Lanka.

Among others, German Federal President Frank-Walter Steinmeier, the President of the European Commission Jean-Claude Juncker and French President Emmanuel Macron have condemned the attacks.

Prime Minister Juha Sipilä writes on Twitter that the bomb attacks in Sri Lanka "are horrific attacks". Sipilä writes that the situation is being followed closely.

Foreign Minister Timo Soini also condemns the attacks on the Foreign Ministry's Twitter account. Soini says that the attacks are cowardly violence.

## There is a shortage of Sami-language teaching

![Sámi álbmotbeaivi 2019. / Sámi National Day 2019.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10633241)

Open image viewer

Sami flag. Photo: Vesa Toppari / Yle

Only a few municipalities in Finland offer in-person teaching or early childhood education in the Sami language.

Only about 10 percent of Sami children receive teaching in the Sami language outside the Sami home region. The home region is in the northern parts of Lapland.

The reason is above all a lack of supply, because demand keeps growing.

The law requires the municipality to make sure that a child can receive early childhood education also in the Sami languages. Three Sami languages are spoken in Finland.

## Usefulness and organic growing are trends in home gardens

![Herbs in a greenhouse.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8814335)

Open image viewer

Photo: Suvi Turunen / Yle

Sun and warmth tempt home gardeners in southern Finland to spring work.

Timo Taulavuori, managing director of the Finnish Horticultural Society, says that organic growing can now be seen in home gardens. In the gardens, plastic and chemical pesticides are avoided. Many people choose plants that attract butterflies and bees.

Useful plants are also a strong trend. Young people in particular grow herbs, chillies and tomatoes. Some also try growing maize.

Growing does not always need soil. Plants can also be grown on a balcony.
