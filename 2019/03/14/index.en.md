# Thursday 14.3.2019 (radio)

Amphetamine use has grown to record levels. The number of foreign-language speakers in the Helsinki region is growing quickly. You can get to know the election candidates on Yle's election machine. A young climate activist is proposed for the Nobel Prize.

## Amphetamine is being used at a record level

![A plastic bag of amphetamine](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10457698)

Open image viewer

Amphetamine use has grown by more than 50 percent since 2018. Photo: Customs

Amphetamine is being used in Finland at a record level.

Amphetamine is an extremely dangerous drug.

A new study says that in 10 cities a total of more than 300 kilos of amphetamine were used last year. 2 years ago the amount was about 200 kilos.

The use of cocaine has also increased. Cocaine is used especially in the big cities of Southern Finland.

In a study by the Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare, drug use was studied in city wastewater. About 40 percent of Finns live in the study area.

## The number of foreign-language speakers is growing

![Anti-racism day in Hämeenlinna in 2014, with delicacies from different countries on offer.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7150137)

In about 15 years, 25 percent of the Helsinki region's population will speak a language other than Finnish, Swedish or Sami as their mother tongue. Photo: Markku Karvonen / Yle

The number of foreign-language speakers is growing in the Helsinki region.

A foreign-language speaker means a person whose mother tongue is some language other than Finnish, Swedish or Sami.

There are now just over 200,000 foreign-language speakers in the 14 municipalities of the Helsinki region. A new population forecast estimates that by 2035 there will already be almost 440,000 foreign-language speakers.

If the estimate comes true, the share of foreign-language speakers in the Helsinki region's population will grow from the current 14 percent to 25 percent.

## The election machine shows the candidates' views

![Election machine 2019. A smartphone with the election machine open in front and Parliament House in the background.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1920,h_1080,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5488525c8911cc7a073/1552486914)

Open image viewer

You can search the election machine for a candidate that suits you.

The views of parliamentary election candidates on climate policy are contradictory.

Almost all candidates say that Finland must be a pioneer in fighting climate change. However, many candidates are not ready for practical measures.

For example, restrictions on the sale of diesel and petrol cars do not get support. A large part of the candidates also do not want to reduce forest felling or raise taxes on meat.

An election machine has been opened on Yle's website. From it you can see what the candidates think, in addition to climate matters, about, for example, education, immigration or elderly care.

## A young climate activist is a Nobel candidate

![Greta Thunberg took part in a climate march in Hamburg at the beginning of March.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10688002)

Open image viewer

Greta Thunberg took part in a climate march in Hamburg at the beginning of March. Photo: Focke Strangman / EPA

Swedish climate activist Greta Thunberg is being proposed for the Nobel Peace Prize.

Thunberg is 16 years old. She became known when she started a school strike for the climate. Every Friday, Thunberg protests for the climate in front of the Swedish parliament building. She has also spoken about climate issues to world leaders, for example in the EU.

Thunberg has inspired schoolchildren to hold climate demonstrations around the world. For example, tomorrow schoolchildren will demonstrate for the climate in at least Helsinki, Tampere and Turku.
