There is good news about corona.

Director Mika Salminen of THL says that the corona epidemic is easing in Finland. More than 50 per cent of all people in Finland have received 2 corona vaccine doses. More vaccines are being given all the time.

The government's goal is that 80 per cent of people over 12 years old receive 2 doses. Mika Salminen of THL says that this goal will be reached quite soon.

The vaccine gives good protection against the disease caused by the coronavirus. Mika Salminen says that strict corona restrictions probably will no longer be needed.

In Uusimaa the corona restrictions are now milder than before.

From Saturday, people do not need to keep the 2-metre safety distance. In theatre and concert auditoriums, audiences no longer need to be divided into blocks.

More people may be let into public events than before.

![Sebastian Aho in the picture](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3994,h_2247,x_0,y_337/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-81803960c06917a7365/1623222696)

Now a sports news item.

NHL ice hockey players will be able to go to the Olympics. The International Ice Hockey Federation and the NHL have agreed that NHL players will take part in the Beijing Olympics.

The Beijing Olympics are in February 2022. No NHL games will be played during the Games. The Finnish ice hockey team plays in the same group in Beijing as Sweden, Slovakia and Latvia.

The NHL ice hockey league is played in the USA and Canada. The world's best ice hockey players play in the NHL. There are also many Finnish players in the NHL.

NHL players did not take part in the 2018 Winter Olympics in South Korea.

One more weather forecast.

The weather is getting a little warmer. The sun shines at times. At the weekend it only rains in the north.

At the start of next week it may also rain a little in Southern Finland and Central Finland.

During the day it is warm, 13...20 degrees. In the east and north it is warm, 8...15 degrees. At night there may be frost, that is, the temperature may be slightly below freezing.
