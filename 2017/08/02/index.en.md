# Wednesday 2.8.2017 (radio)

Many Finns have debt problems. Britain's Prince Philip has retired. Finland is now getting a lot of rain. Teachers from Finnish schools abroad have been at training days in Helsinki.

## Debt problems are common

![a pocket calculator, hands and a pile of money](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9603548)

Many Finns have debt. Many take on so much debt that they cannot pay all their bills.

If a person does not pay their bills, they may get a payment default entry. Already 375,000 Finns now have a payment default entry.

A payment default entry makes life difficult. Because of the entry, it is hard for a person to, for example, rent a flat.

Many pay off an old loan with a new loan. Often, however, this means that the amount of the loan just grows.

## Prince Philip retired

Britain's Queen Elizabeth and Prince Philip. Photo: Will Oliver/Epa

News from Britain: Prince Philip has retired. He is 96 years old.

Prince Philip's last official duty was on Wednesday, when he received a military parade in London. The parade was in front of Buckingham Palace.

Prince Philip is Queen Elizabeth's husband. Queen Elizabeth is 91 years old, but she continues to work.

Queen Elizabeth and Prince Philip have already passed many duties on to Britain's younger royals.

## It is raining a lot

![Rain.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9751762)

The weather is rainy in different parts of Finland. Photo: Uula Kuvaja / Yle

It is now raining a lot in Finland. The Finnish Meteorological Institute warns of heavy rain.

It is raining most in eastern Finland. In 5 days, as much rain may fall in eastern Finland as usually falls during the whole of August.

It may also rain a lot elsewhere in Finland.

## Teachers from Finnish schools abroad got training

![Pupils at the Finnish school in Basel.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9662755)

Open image viewer

Pupils at the Finnish school in Basel. Photo: Yle

Teachers from Finnish schools abroad have been at training days in Helsinki. In the training they got ideas for teaching the Finnish language.

These Finnish schools teach the Finnish language to Finns abroad, that is, Finns who live in other countries.

The Finnish schools give lessons at weekends. On weekdays, the children of Finnish families abroad go to an ordinary school, where different subjects are taught in the language of the country they live in.

Teacher Maila Eichhorn has long been involved in the work of the Finnish schools. Eichhorn says that a Finnish child abroad's Finnish language skills are the parents' responsibility. The most important thing is that Finnish is spoken at home. The task of the Finnish schools is to support the language upbringing that the parents give, Eichhorn says.

This year, the Finnish schools are also making video greetings for Finland, which is turning 100. The greetings can be found on the Clear News website and Facebook page.
