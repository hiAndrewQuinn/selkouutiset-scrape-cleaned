# Tuesday 8.5.2018 (radio)

More jobs have been created in Finland this year than expected. The citizens' initiative for free second-level education has moved on to Parliament. Rare humanoid robots are teaching Finnish to immigrants in Kajaani. Finland's representative Saara Aalto competes in the semi-final of the Eurovision song contest this evening.

## More new jobs have been created than expected

![cleaning](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9990642)

Finland's economy has improved, and more new jobs have come than was expected. Photo: AOP

More jobs have been created in Finland this year than was expected. Employment is growing in all age groups, but employment among people over 55 has improved the most.

The employment rate is now 71.1 percent. The government's target is a 72 percent employment rate. The employment rate tells how many percent of people of working age have a job.

Employment has improved because Finland's economy has improved. Exports abroad are busy, and citizens are now spending more. In addition, companies are investing, that is, putting capital into things, more boldly than before.

## A citizens' initiative on second-level education was made

![Hanna Markkula-Kivisilta and Speaker Paula Risikko.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10196365)

Hanna Markkula-Kivisilta handed the initiative to Speaker Paula Risikko. Photo: Yle

The citizens' initiative on free second-level education has moved on to Parliament. The initiative was handed to the Speaker of Parliament, Paula Risikko. After being handled by a committee, the initiative can move on to be handled by the whole of Parliament.

So far, 2 citizens' initiatives have been approved. The first one approved was a law change that allowed same-sex couples to marry.

## A robot teaches Finnish in Kajaani

![Two men talk with a humanoid robot.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10189205)

Open image viewer

Many people dare to talk to a robot better than to a human. Photo: Niko Mannonen / Yle

Rare humanoid robots are teaching Finnish to immigrants in Kajaani. Edukai Oy has two robots, Nano and Nono. Edukai provides training and coaches people for working life. A group for integration into society also studies there.

Student Hamza Triki says that it is nice to talk with the robot. Then it does not matter if you are scared or shy. Students say that you do not need to be stressed with the robot. Loknath Trimsina says that the robot speaks and understands plain Finnish. That is why he understands the robot better than people, who often speak too fast.

Humanoid robots are still rare in adult education. The robots in Kajaani started work as assistant teachers in immigrant integration training at the start of the year. There are robots in basic education too, in Tampere and Espoo, for example.

## Saara Aalto competes in the Eurovision semi-final

Finland's representative Saara Aalto competes this evening in the semi-final of the Eurovision song contest. In it, competitors are selected for the Eurovision final, which is on Saturday.

19 countries compete in this evening's semi-final. Saara Aalto's song has number 15 in the running order. The Eurovision is held in Lisbon, Portugal.
