# Sunday 20.1.2019 (radio)

Too few Finns make a will. Artificial intelligence is used more and more to moderate online discussions. A crow can estimate the weight of an object just by looking at it. The new frost record is 34.1 degrees. On Monday morning there will be a total lunar eclipse visible in the sky.

## Too few wills are made

![Signing a will.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9269408)

Open image viewer

A badly made will can be impossible to carry out. Photo: Tiina Kokko / Yle

Too few Finns make a will.

Inheritance lawyers say that perhaps just under 20 percent have made a will that is valid. That is why inheritance disputes are increasing all the time. In addition, the deceased more and more often have several marriages behind them.

Wills cause disputes if they are made badly and carelessly. There can be mistakes in wills if people make them themselves. Sometimes the mistakes are so big that the will cannot be carried out.

However, about 90 percent of Finnish inheritances are divided peacefully after the death of the deceased.

## Artificial intelligence moderates effectively

![artificial intelligence illustration](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10252325)

Open image viewer

Artificial intelligence is effective at moderating online discussions. Photo: AOP

Artificial intelligence is used more and more to moderate online discussions. In moderation, someone monitors online discussions. Discussions can also be removed from the internet.

Artificial intelligence can handle larger amounts of messages than a person. Artificial intelligence is also faster.

Artificial intelligence can be more democratic and more impartial than a person. A computer can be taught that moderation looks only at the content of a message, not at the writer.

Yle also began moderating discussions with the help of artificial intelligence last year. Yle's computer is taught Yle's journalistic principles. \_\_

## The crow is an intelligent bird

![New Caledonian crow](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7162773)

Open image viewer

New Caledonian crow. Photo: John Gerrard Keulemans

Crows are more intelligent than was known until now.

The latest research says that a crow can estimate the weight of an object just by looking at it. The bird estimates the weight from the way the wind affects the object.

In the test, the crows understood cause and effect as well as 7- to 10-year-old human children.

The researchers tested New Caledonian crows.

## The frost record was broken

![ice crystals in a glass](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10602484)

Open image viewer

Photo: AOP

A new winter frost record has been measured in Lapland. The Finnish Meteorological Institute says that a frost of 34.1 degrees was measured at Meltosjärvi in Ylitornio on Sunday morning.

However, the all-time frost record is still far off: in 1999, in Pokka in Kittilä in Lapland, it was minus 51.5 degrees.

The frost will continue in the coming days too.

## On Monday morning there is a total lunar eclipse

![Lunar eclipse.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10327085)

Open image viewer

A lunar eclipse in Kirkkonummi. Photo: Risto Rautee

On Monday morning a total lunar eclipse will be visible in the sky. It begins at 6.41 and ends at 7.43.

The eclipse can be seen completely in Northern Finland. Part of it can also be seen in Southern and Eastern Finland.

The lunar eclipse can be followed with the naked eye, with binoculars or with a telescope.
