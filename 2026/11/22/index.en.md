# News of the week in plain Finnish | Saturday 21.11.2026

Hazard information test for phones.

Finland tested a method of warning of danger directly to phones on Tuesday. The test message was supposed to arrive at 12 noon, but not everyone received it.

Some Finns received the message later when the Ministry of the Interior resent it. Many first had to allow test messages in their phone settings.

We will now tell you more about the hazard information test.

The Ministry of the Interior sent a test message to phones on Tuesday. It was trying out a new way to quickly warn people of danger.

Many had to allow test messages in their phone settings. Some received the message first in the three Sámi languages and only then in Finnish and Swedish.

The new method is called FI-Alert.

Finland began considering sending warnings to phones at least as early as 2005. At that time, a devastating tsunami occurred in Asia.

The alert became relevant this year when Ukrainian drones were found in Finland. The drones were part of Russia's war of aggression in Ukraine.

## There were unexpected problems with the message

![Two people stand behind podiums at an official event on a blue background.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4000,h_2250,x_0,y_314/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-17290776ae1d54927ae6/1793185717)

Tuesday's hazard information experiment caused an unpleasant surprise for the Ministry of the Interior. Doro phones began to malfunction after the test.

Doroja is especially prevalent among the elderly and to some extent in healthcare.

The problems may have been caused by the special characters in the Sámi languages. Not all phones recognized them.

It was also a surprise that the alarm system does not work on children's alarm phones.

The system supplier, telecom operators and the Emergency Response Centre are investigating the cause of the disruption. However, the test showed what needs to be improved in the hazard announcements to phones.

## The test will be repeated on Tuesday

Next Tuesday, November 24, the hazard bulletin will be tested again.

The notification is sent at 12 o'clock, which overrides the phone's settings. The phone also keeps sounding an alarm for 10 seconds.

The sound is heard even though the phone is on silent.

The Ministry of the Interior is considering whether to include Sámi languages in Tuesday's test.
