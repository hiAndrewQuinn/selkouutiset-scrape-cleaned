Israel's defence minister has assessed the situation in Gaza. Defence Minister Yoav Gallant estimates that the military operation in Gaza will last 3 months.

However, the defence minister does not say when the Israeli army plans to attack the area by land into Gaza. Israeli air strikes on Gaza continue all the time.

The defence minister says that Israel intends to completely destroy the militant group Hamas.

Hamas attacked Israel from Gaza a little over 2 weeks ago. The war started from that, and many thousands of people have died in it.

There is a crisis in Gaza, because people in the area do not have enough food, water or medicines.

## Aira Samulin

![Aira Samulin teaches children to dance (1998).](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_7030,h_3954,x_0,y_211/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-91894462150e081977e/1645547090)

Open image viewer

Aira Samulin taught Finns of all ages to dance. Photo: Jyrki Valkama

Dance teacher Aira Samulin has died.

Samulin was 96 years old. She died today, Monday, at Meilahti Hospital in Helsinki.

Aira Samulin was well known all over Finland. Many people knew Samulin from television.

Samulin influenced Finnish entertainment and culture for many decades.

Samulin was known especially for the dance school that she founded in the 1960s.

## Climate change and mental health

![A man walks in sleet with an umbrella in his hand.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3456,h_1944,x_0,y_76/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6090825dc3bb3859061/1698066315)

Open image viewer

Winter is dark when there is no white snow. Photo: Mostphotos

Climate change also affects people's mental health in Finland.

Because of climate change, winters in the future will be warmer than now. That is why winters will also become darker. There is little snow, and there are many grey rainy days.

Research Professor Timo Partonen of the Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare says that the lack of light increases mental health problems. In particular depression and sleep problems become more common.

However, Partonen reminds us that exercise and healthy food can help with the problems that the lack of light causes.

## Most positive Finn of the year

![Ella Kanninen smiles in the back yard in Tuscany.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1600,h_899,x_0,y_1/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-11890706532500969dbc/1698046827)

Open image viewer

Ella Kanninen is the Most Positive Finn of the Year. Photo: Ella Kanninen / Yle

The Most Positive Finn of the Year has been chosen. It is the journalist and presenter Ella Kanninen. Many people know Kanninen from the TV1 programme Half Past Six.

The Most Positive Finn of the Year was chosen in a public vote. The choice is part of Red Nose Day, which is celebrated this year on 10 November.

According to Ella Kanninen, being positive does not mean laughing. A positive person takes other people into account. Small things are important. For example, you can smile at people you do not know, Kanninen advises.
