## Sweden re-assesses the human rights situation in Iraq

![Iraqis at the Jadaa refugee camp near Mosul in Iraq, 17 November.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9304199)

Iraqis at the Jadaa refugee camp near Mosul, 17.11. Photo: Ahmed Jalil / EPA

The Swedish Migration Agency estimates that the human rights situation in Iraq has got worse.

According to the agency, the armed conflict is now in more areas of Iraq than last year. That is why it is difficult to escape violence within the country.

The new assessment may affect Sweden's asylum decisions. For example, some negative decisions may perhaps be reviewed again.

In Finland the authorities will decide in January whether Iraq's security assessment must be changed.

## Many asylum seekers have given up Islam

![Inside Kaustinen church.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5092907)

Open image viewer

According to the church, many asylum seekers have given up Islam and joined the Lutheran church. Photo: Tuula Nyberg / EPA

The Lutheran Church of Finland estimates that hundreds of asylum seekers have given up Islam and joined the Lutheran church.

New members must go to confirmation school and be active in the parish for at least 3 months.

Some may experience religious persecution, because they have given up Islam. In that case it can be a reason for getting asylum.

## Pessimists more easily get a heart attack

![A man with pursed lips.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7119709)

Open image viewer

A dissatisfied and pessimistic person more often gets coronary heart disease than others. Photo: Hanna Lumme / Yle

A pessimistic attitude to life may increase the risk of dying of a heart attack.

Researchers followed the lives of more than 2,300 Finns for 11 years. The researchers noticed that pessimism was a risk factor for coronary heart disease. Pessimists died of heart attacks more often than optimistic people.

## Anna Järvinen is the Swedish Finn of the year

![Singer Anna Järvinen plays music in the reddish light of the setting sun.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6566038)

Open image viewer

Swedish Finn singer Anna Järvinen has strongly promoted the Finnish language in Sweden. Photo: Klaffi Productions

The singer and songwriter Anna Järvinen has been chosen as the Swedish Finn of the year. Järvinen has strongly promoted the Finnish language in Sweden this year.

Anna Järvinen moved from Helsinki to the north of Stockholm when she was 6 years old.

The choice is based on a public vote. It was organised by the Finnish-language departments of Swedish radio and television.
