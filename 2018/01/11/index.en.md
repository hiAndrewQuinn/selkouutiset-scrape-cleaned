# Thursday 11.1.2018 (radio)

Technical skills in Finland are declining. Domestic train travel grew last year. It is now easier to get treatment at health centres than before. Measuring caffeine may help identify Parkinson's disease.

## Technical skills are threatened with decline

![The new tram is in the familiar yellow-green colour.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6167949)

Transtech builds trams. The company needs many more mechanical engineering workers. Photo: Harri Oksanen / Yle

In Finnish export companies and universities there is a fear that technical skills in Finland are declining. For example, the number of professors in the technology field is only half of what it was before.

Companies already find it hard to find top experts in Finland for work. Then Finland's competitiveness is at risk. A large part of Finland's exports are based on mechanical engineering skills. About half of goods exports are, for example, engines, paper machines or power plants.

Exports are also the economic foundation of Finnish well-being.

## Train travel has increased

![The VR logo on the door of a railway station.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9988792)

Open image viewer

Photo: Ismo Pekkarinen / AOP

Domestic train travel grew last year. Travel grew by 8 percent.

The transport company VR says that the reasons are the falling ticket prices, the speed of the routes and the increase in the number of train services.

In domestic long-distance train traffic, 12.5 million journeys were made last year. The company says that on the Helsinki - Oulu route, travel grew as much as 48 percent.

## Getting to a doctor has eased slightly

![A doctor walks along the corridor of a health centre.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9759211)

Open image viewer

There are big regional differences in the shortage of doctors. Photo: Mikael Kokkola / Yle

It is now easier to get treatment at health centres than before. Getting treatment has eased slightly over the past three years.

In the Finnish Medical Association's population survey, 41 percent say that there are problems getting a doctor's appointment. In the previous survey, three years ago, the share was 48 percent.

There is still a shortage of doctors in health centres. Almost five (5) percent of doctors were missing last year. That is a little more than in 2016. In October, about 190 doctors' posts were unfilled.

The biggest shortage of doctors is in Kainuu, Eastern Savo, Vaasa and Southern Savo. Nowadays, one in every five doctors' posts is filled by substitutes.

## Caffeine may help identify Parkinson's disease

![Coffee beans in a coffee measuring spoon.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9238796)

Open image viewer

Caffeine is found in coffee, among other things. Photo: Ismo Pekkarinen / Yle

Measuring caffeine from the blood may help identify Parkinson's disease. It was already known earlier that people who drink a lot of coffee have a lower risk of getting Parkinson's disease than those who do not consume caffeine.

A new study says, however, that in the blood of Parkinson's patients the caffeine level does not rise in the usual way. This information may help when a patient is diagnosed with Parkinson's disease.

In Finland about 14 000 people have Parkinson's disease. It is an incurable nervous system disease whose cause is not known. The disease causes, for example, tremor, muscle stiffness and slowing of movements. It is usually diagnosed in people aged 50 to 70.
