# Tuesday 12.11.2019 (radio)

Denmark has started border checks at the Swedish border. Women's pensions are about 20 percent smaller than men's pensions. The postal strike has now lasted 2 days. Village shop support is of interest.

## Denmark started border checks at the Swedish border

![A police officer looks at a woman's identity card on a train at the border between Sweden and Denmark.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11048133)

Denmark has started border checks at the Swedish border.

The checks are made two days a week. The checks are spot checks. Sweden has had border checks at the Danish border since 2015.

Thousands of people cross the border to go to work every day.

Denmark wants to prevent organised crime from spreading into the country from Sweden. In Denmark, there were at least 2 attacks during the summer that involved Swedish citizens.

## Women's pensions are a fifth smaller than men's pensions

![Pensioner's hands counting euros](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10134449)

Open image viewer

The EU is worried about the poverty of older women. Photo: AOP

Women's pensions are about 20 percent smaller than men's pensions. The Ministry of Social Affairs and Health says that the difference is at the average level of EU countries.

The pension gap between the sexes has slowly narrowed over 20 years.

There is no big difference nowadays in the employment rate, the length of the career or the retirement. Instead, the pay gap between women and men is big in Finland. Pay affects the future pension.

In Finland, women also take more family leave than men. Many women also look after children at home with the home care allowance.

The EU is worried that many women are poor in old age. In Finland too, the risk of poverty in retirement is double for women compared with men.

## Postal workers held a demonstration and get support

![The Posti and logistics sector union PAU held a demonstration against the scrapping of Posti's working conditions at Posti's head office in Pasila, Helsinki, on 12 November.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11064500)

Open image viewer

Postal workers' demonstration in Ilmala, Helsinki. Photo: Markku Ulander / Lehtikuva

The postal strike has now lasted 2 days. Posti's employees held a demonstration in Helsinki. About 200 postal workers gathered during the day to protest at Posti's head office.

The background to the strike is a dispute over moving some of the employees to a cheaper collective agreement.

At least 3 other trade unions have promised to support the postal workers' strike with their own support strikes.

Representatives of the employees and the employer will meet on Wednesday, led by the National Conciliator.

## Village shop support has been applied for eagerly

![Vehkalahti village shop in Koitti, Hartola.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10964278)

Open image viewer

Vehkalahti village shop in Koitti, Hartola. Photo: Mika Moksu / Yle

Almost all village shops in the countryside are interested in the new village shop support. One million euros will be distributed next year.

There are 221 village shops in Finland. Perhaps about 100 shops meet the criteria for the support.

The shop must be in the countryside, where the population is sparse. The shop must be open all year round. In addition, the nearest shop must be at least 7.5 kilometres away.

In Sweden, village shop support has been paid since 2016.

Senior inspector Tommi Alanko of the Food Agency says that decisions on who receives the support will be made this year.

Every year, 20 to 30 shops stop operating.
