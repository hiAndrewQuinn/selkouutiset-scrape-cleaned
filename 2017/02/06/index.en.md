# Monday 6.2.2017 (radio)

The Sámi have a national day. A strike threatens air traffic. Speeding has decreased as the fixed fine has gone up. Trade restrictions are hurting Finland. Fewer music CDs are being borrowed from libraries.

##

## The Sámi celebrate their national day

![Sauli Niinistö at the Sámi Parliament](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9444072)

Open image viewer

President Sauli Niinistö congratulated the Sámi in Inari. Photo: Vesa Toppari / Yle Sápmi

The national day of the Sámi is being celebrated today.

The President of the Republic, Sauli Niinistö, visited Inari, where he congratulated the Sámi.

The Sámi are an indigenous people who have their own language and culture. The Sámi homeland is in northern Finland, northern Sweden and northern Norway, and in north-west Russia.

The Sámi also have their own flag. The Sámi Parliament is the Sámi's own parliament, which defends the Sámi way of life. The Sámi way of life includes, for example, reindeer herding.

States have oppressed the Sámi for a long time, and land has been taken away from them, for example.

## A strike threatens air traffic

![Security checkpoint area at Helsinki-Vantaa airport.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9444229)

Open image viewer

The strike is affecting security checks. Photo: Mauri Ratilainen / AOP

A strike threatens air traffic during the skiing holiday week.

The school skiing holidays start on 20 February, and many families intend to fly abroad then.

Aviation workers warn that a strike will start on Friday 17 February, if the collective agreements of the employees of Airpro are not improved.

If the strike starts, it will affect security checks at the airport and flights organised by the travel companies Tui and Tjäreborg and by Norwegian.

## Speeding has decreased

![An automatic traffic enforcement camera at the side of the road.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9444152)

Open image viewer

Automatic cameras monitor speeding. Photo: Heikki Saukkomaa / Lehtikuva

Speeding by cars has decreased since the fixed fine was raised.

Automatic camera monitoring has also reduced speeding.

Speeding has decreased by about 10 per cent, studies show.

The fixed fine was raised in 2015.

You have to pay a fixed fine if you drive just a little too fast.

## Trade restrictions are felt in Finland

![Finnlines cargo ship at the Vuosaari port in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9100487)

Open image viewer

Finland exports a lot of goods abroad. Photo: Jarno Kuusinen / AOP

International trade restrictions threaten to raise prices and increase unemployment in Finland too.

Because of the restrictions, Finnish companies lose income, as exporting goods abroad becomes harder.

Russia, for example, has started paying subsidies for goods produced in Russia. That reduces the export of Finnish goods to Russia.

Finland sells a lot of machinery and equipment abroad, but many countries have restricted its import into their countries.

## Borrowing music from libraries is decreasing

![A woman browses CDs placed on a bookshelf.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9445129)

Open image viewer

Fewer CDs are borrowed than before. Photo: Joona Haarala / Yle

You can also borrow music on CDs from a library, but borrowing music has become rare.

The reason is that internet streaming services have become popular. A lot of music is listened to on Spotify and YouTube, for example.

Mostly older people borrow CDs from libraries.

Sheet music and music books, on the other hand, are borrowed from libraries just as much as before.
