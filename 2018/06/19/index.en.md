# Tuesday 19.6.2018 (radio)

Jari Aarnio, former head of the Helsinki drugs police, has been released from prison. Elderly people are more and more often becoming victims of fraud. The police have investigated a series of crimes involving fake police officers. France bans mobile phones in schools.

## Jari Aarnio got out of prison

![Jari Aarnio](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10264154)

Open image viewer

Jari Aarnio Photo: Yle

Jari Aarnio, former head of the Helsinki drugs police, has been released from prison. The Helsinki Court of Appeal decided that Aarnio may wait for the Court of Appeal's ruling while free.

Aarnio was sentenced in 2016 to ten years in prison for drug crimes. Aarnio appealed the sentence to the Court of Appeal.

The Court of Appeal will not give its own verdict until next year. Aarnio has been in pre-trial detention for 3 and a half years in total.

## Many elderly people can be cheated online

![Hanna Laaksonen and Maija Frantsila](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10257722)

Open image viewer

Hanna Laaksonen (left) and Maija Frantsila are keen internet users. They have not met any major problems in the digital world, and they encourage other elderly people to go online. Photo: Jaani Lampinen / Yle

Elderly people are more and more often becoming victims of fraud. Services are moving online, and many frauds are carried out online. Scammers take advantage of the fact that many elderly people are inexperienced on the internet.

There are now 3 times as many fraud victims over 65 as there were 7 years ago. The number of frauds has grown in all age groups.

Despite these side effects, the police encourage elderly people to use the internet. The risk is small when a person recognises the places where danger lies. The police say that the internet has a lot of good in it and that it is modern.

## Fake police officers have carried out many frauds

![Police car](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10147165)

Open image viewer

Criminals posed as police and asked elderly people for their bank codes. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

The police have investigated a series of crimes in which frauds were committed and fake police officers were involved. The case is moving to the prosecution's consideration. In total, there are more than 120 crimes. The crimes were committed this year and last year. The victims are elderly people from all over Finland.

Fake police officers have, for example, asked victims for their bank details over the phone. The police say that the crimes were planned and professional. The investigation has a few main suspects and dozens of other suspects. The profit from the crimes has been more than 800 000 euros. The police and banks have been able to return about 430 000 euros to the victims.

The police remind people that police officers never ask people for their online banking user IDs or passwords. Social services, insurance companies or banks do not ask for such information either. Online banking user IDs and passwords must be kept carefully, and they must not be disclosed to outsiders.

## France bans mobile phones in schools

![President Emmanuel Macron bending down to speak to small pupils sitting at desks.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10261630)

Open image viewer

The mobile phone ban was one of President Emmanuel Macron's election promises. Photo: Philippe Wojazer / EPA

France is banning the use of mobile phones in schools completely.

The law will officially come into force in the autumn. Many schools have already tried the ban. In September, pupils may still bring a phone to school, but they may not use it even during breaks.

The school mobile phone ban was an election promise of President Emmanuel Macron. Most lower secondary school pupils in France have a mobile phone.
