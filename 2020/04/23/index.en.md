# Thursday 23.4.2020 (radio)

Public events and festivals of over 500 people are banned until at least August. Parks may be closed on May Day. Nurses' unions criticise elderly care homes. Eye contact on a video call causes the same emotional reactions as in real life

## Summer's big public events have been cancelled until August

![A picture of Sanna Marin.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11318383)

Open image viewer

Prime Minister Sanna Marin says that the government is preparing for a long corona crisis. Restrictions can only be lifted gradually. Photo: Silja Viitala / Yle

The government says that returning to normal life after the corona crisis may take a long time yet.

For example, public events and festivals with more than 500 people are banned until at least August.

Next week the government will decide whether schools will stay closed until the end of the term. The government will say more about lifting restrictions on 3 May.

Prime Minister Sanna Marin says that not all restrictive measures can be dropped at once. Marin says that the situation is still serious.

The current restrictions therefore remain in force. For example, gatherings of more than 10 people are banned, and safe distances are important.

## Parks may be closed on May Day

![May Day 2019](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3936,h_2214,x_0,y_123/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5598965ccac82393e30/1556793420)

The May Day crowd in Helsinki last year. Photo: Laura Railamaa / Yle

Parks may be closed on May Day to curb the celebrations.

Interior Minister Maria Ohisalo says that people must keep safe distances from other people on May Day too. For this reason, the police and municipalities may close parks to use on May Day.

The government intends to publish a virtual May Day campaign, which will tell about different ways to celebrate May Day remotely.

## Nurses' unions criticise care homes

![A doctor puts on protective gloves.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_6240,h_3510,x_0,y_322/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6605455e84badfecc18/1585758399)

Open image viewer

In many care homes and even in hospitals there is a shortage of proper protective equipment. Photo: Pasi Takkunen / Yle

Nurses' unions criticise elderly care homes. The unions say that care homes do not always follow the corona guidelines. There are failures both in the public sector and in private care homes.

The unions say that, for example, some care homes keep their doors open, even though a visiting ban is in force. People from outside the house come to eat in the care homes' dining rooms. In addition, the restrictions on gatherings of more than 10 people have not been followed.

The unions also say that the guidelines on protective equipment are poor in many places. Nurses have been told to use, for example, fabric neck tubes when there have not been face masks.

## Direct eye contact can bring a smile

![Gaze](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3072,h_1727,x_0,y_158/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/17-36609562f78834d3b2/1445951744)

Illustrative image. Direct eye contact does a person good: it brings a smile and relaxes. Photo: Yle / Jukka Lintinen

Because of the corona epidemic, a large part of people do not see relatives or friends face to face. That is why video calls, where you can see the face of the person you are talking to, are now talked about a lot.

Researchers say that eye contact on a video call causes the same emotional reactions as in real life. Even seeing direct eye contact has made people smile. This has been studied at the University of Tampere.

Researchers recommend that the device's camera is pointed so that in the call you can look straight at the person. In all apps this is not possible. Then it looks as if the person you are talking to is looking upwards to the side. It is still nice to see the face of a familiar person.
