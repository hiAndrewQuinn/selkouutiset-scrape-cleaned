# Monday 11.11.2019 (radio)

The postal strike has begun. The use of plastic in drink bottle caps may increase. The seal population continues to grow. A citizens' initiative for permanent summer time has been handed over to Parliament.

## The postal strike began

The postal strike has begun this morning. About 9,000 Posti employees are on strike.

Letter post may be late by many weeks. Parcels may arrive faster.

The strike does not affect the early delivery of newspapers. In addition, post related to safety and health is delivered. Such post includes, for example, hospital letters and laboratory samples.

The strike may last about a month, unless an agreement is reached. The employees' union PAU says that Posti intends to change the collective agreement of many postal workers to a cheaper one.

The parties will meet the National Conciliator on Wednesday. The work of the National Conciliator is to help settle labour disputes.

## Reducing plastic may increase plastic use

![Used plastic bottles baled](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_2300,h_1294,x_0,y_119/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6089985dc2d73ad0f12/1573051179)

Open image viewer

In the new directive, the cap must stay attached to the plastic bottle. At the moment, the cap comes off the bottle when the bottle is opened. Photo: Riikka Pennanen / Yle

The use of plastic in drink bottle caps may increase. The reason is a European Union directive, that is, a guideline in legislation.

The directive aims to reduce single-use plastic. Now, however, it increases the use of plastic in the brewing industry.

In 5 years, in EU countries, caps must stay attached to the plastic bottle for the whole time of use. That means the cap design must be changed.

In addition, bottle packaging must be changed. That increases the use of plastic and costs the breweries a lot of money.

Finns are conscientious about recycling. About 90 percent of plastic bottles are recycled, and almost all with the cap. In that case, the caps are not harmful to nature.

The EU directive aims to ensure that plastic caps do not end up in the seas. In all countries, plastic bottles are not recycled the way they are in Finland.

## The grey seal population continues to grow

![grey seal on a grey sea](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10782047)

Open image viewer

The number of grey seals continues to grow strongly. Photo: AOP

There are now many grey seals in the Baltic Sea. The seal population continues to grow.

In this year's counts, more than 14,000 grey seals, that is, halic, were seen in Finland's sea areas. Across the whole Baltic Sea area, the population is already over 38,000.

The population is especially strong in Finland's south-western sea areas.

The Baltic grey seal population has grown on average about 5 percent a year since the beginning of the 2000s.

## The initiative on summer time was handed to Parliament

![Parliament's legislative director Tuula Kulovesi received the Permanent Summer Time citizens' initiative from Katri Niittymäki (left), Hanna-Kaisa Siekkinen and Ville Niemelä for Parliament on 11 November](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11062887)

Parliament's legislative director Tuula Kulovesi received the citizens' initiative from Katri Niittymäki (left), Hanna-Kaisa Siekkinen and Ville Niemelä. Photo: Vesa Moilanen / Lehtikuva

The citizens' initiative for a permanent move to summer time has been handed to Parliament.

Staying on summer time is justified by the fact that in winter time there is less daylight in the evening.

An extra hour of light in the evening gives better opportunities, for example, for exercise after work days and school days.

The initiative collected almost 54,000 supporting signatures.
