# Sunday 13.9.2020 (radio)

Traffic across the western border opens next Saturday despite Sweden's corona figures. Adults' sleep has decreased only a little. Genealogy is a popular hobby. The weather at the start of the week is mostly dry.

## Sweden's corona figures and the western border

![THL Director Mika Salminen at the ministers' press conference on the steps of the Senate House in Helsinki on 11 September.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11541962)

Sweden's corona figures will at least not soon limit border traffic again. Traffic across the western border opens next Saturday. After that, people may travel to Finland from Sweden without restrictions.

In Sweden, in the last 2 weeks, there have been 25.7 new coronavirus infections per 100,000 inhabitants.

On Friday the government decided that people may come to Finland from countries where there are at most 25 cases of the disease. In that case, no voluntary quarantine is needed.

Still on Friday, Sweden had fewer than 25 cases per 100,000 inhabitants. Finland will look at the situation again on Thursday.

## Adults' lack of sleep is smaller than thought

![A woman sleeping under a blanket.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11037345)

Open image viewer

Poor sleep increases the risk of various diseases over time and can also weaken the results of treatment of illnesses. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

In Finland, adults' sleep has decreased only a little. Until now it was thought that lack of sleep had increased, that is, adults sleep less than they need.

The Finnish Institute of Occupational Health and the University of Helsinki followed the length of Finns' sleep over 36 years.

The sleep of the people studied became shorter, but the explanation was that people were getting older. Sleep gets shorter when a person ages.

In the study, women's sleep became shorter on average by about 32 minutes, and men's sleep by about 18 minutes.

At the end of the long study, the length of adults' sleep was on average just under seven and a half hours a night.

## Genealogy interests younger and younger people

![A magnifying glass on top of a genealogy book.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7728007)

Open image viewer

The genealogy boom came to Finland in the 1970s. Photo: Jarkko Riikonen / Yle

Genealogy is a popular hobby in Finland.

The number of genealogists has grown bigger in 10 years than ever before. This is what the Finnish Genealogical Society says.

Church books, that is, population records, are now in digital form. That is why genealogy can be done on your own computer. You no longer need to go to a provincial archive or library to study papers or microfilms.

Various family tree services and DNA mapping also help.

The most active genealogists are Finns aged 50 to 70. Nowadays there are also many people under 40.

## The start of the week is mostly dry

![The sun shines on Töölönlahti.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10522522)

Open image viewer

Photo: Mesut Turan / Lehtikuva

The weather at the start of the week will be mostly sunny and dry across a large part of the country.

The Finnish Meteorological Institute says that there will also be showers early in the week in Central Lapland and Northern Lapland.

Also in southern Finland, there may be showers around Tuesday.

Temperatures stay between 12 and 17 degrees in the south and between 8 and 14 degrees in the north.

During Wednesday and Thursday a low pressure system will arrive in Finland, bringing rain and winds.

Towards the end of the week the low pressure moves away, and then the weather is a little clearer and better.
