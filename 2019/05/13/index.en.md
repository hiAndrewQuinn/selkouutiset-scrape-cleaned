## EU career no longer interests young people as before

![Iina Lietzén](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10779981)

Iina Lietzén is building a career in the European Parliament. Photo: Anna Karismo / Yle

Young Finns' eagerness to apply for EU jobs has fallen. That is why the number of Finns in the EU's institutions is falling.

The situation worries Finland's EU representation. Finland's special circumstances may not be taken into account as well as before. Such matters include, for example, agriculture, forestry and transport policy.

At the moment Finns have been placed in good EU jobs better than average. There are many Finns in EU jobs compared with the size of the country.

About 1,300 Finns work in the EU.

## Police tackle honour violence in a new way

![Anonymous young people in the picture](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10250792)

Open image viewer

Some immigrant families have honour violence. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

In Finland about 30 young immigrants have fled home from violence linked to family honour. This is the estimate of Sopu-work expert Johanna Aapakallio. She does family crisis work at the Loisto Settlement association.

The Helsinki police have started a completely new way of working. The police meet young people who face honour violence or the threat of it. The police meet young people in the secret premises of the Loisto Settlement association in Helsinki.

A risk assessment is made for the young people. An important principle is also to meet the whole family, not only the victim of honour violence.

Last year Sopu-work dealt with about 60 cases connected with honour and violence.

## More and more elderly people drive cars

![Tuovi Niemelä driving her car](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10773060)

Open image viewer

For Tuovi Niemelä from Jalasjärvi, a car is an important help in everyday life. Niemelä is 80 years old. Photo: Pasi Takkunen/Yle

There are already over 400,000 people over 70 with a valid driving licence on Finland's roads. Each year more elderly people drive on the roads.

There are about 85,000 people over 80 on the roads.

However, the number of elderly people killed or injured is still at the same level as 10 years ago.

## Poste restante must be free

![A homeless person collects his post from a poste restante pick-up point in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_2672,h_1503,x_542,y_209/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5413985c5c241e9416b/1557738181)

Open image viewer

Homeless Markus collects his post once or twice a week from the poste restante pick-up point at Kasarmitori. Photo: Yle / Vesa Marttinen

Poste restante parcels must not be charged for if they are the only way to receive post. The communications regulator Traficom made the decision.

For example, homeless people use the poste restante service, that is, pick-up post. The service is also important for people who do not have a permanent address.

Posti said in February that the poste restante service would become a paid service. However, Posti postponed the reform, because it wanted a statement from an authority.

## Paavo Väyrynen also leaves the Star Movement

![Paavo Väyrynen](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10765176)

Open image viewer

Paavo Väyrynen wants to stand in the next presidential election. Photo: Mikko Stig / Lehtikuva

Paavo Väyrynen is asking to leave the Star Movement. Väyrynen founded the Seven Stars Movement and was its chairman. However, he intends to continue as the party's leader until the European elections.

Väyrynen is aiming for the presidential election of 2024.

Last spring Väyrynen left the Citizens' Party, which he had founded himself.
