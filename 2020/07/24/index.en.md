The number of coronavirus tests has grown during the summer.

The director of the Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare, Mika Salminen, says that the number of tests has grown, because the normal common cold is also going around. People who have even a small symptom want to go for a coronavirus test, just in case.

Salminen thinks it is good that people go for tests. Salminen says that especially if a person loses their sense of smell, they must go for a corona test straight away.

## An excellent lingonberry harvest

![Lingonberries in a bucket and on a bog in Pudasjärvi.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4145,h_2332,x_0,y_492/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6997095f10001938117/1594884447)

Open image viewer

The lingonberry harvest is excellent this year. Photo: Jari Peltoperä / Yle

The lingonberry harvest this summer is really good.

The lingonberry is an orange berry that grows on bogs. It is also known by the names cloudberry and dwarf cornel.

There are really a lot of lingonberries this year. Rainer Peltola, a special researcher at the Natural Resources Institute, says that in Ranua, for example, there are as many as 60 berries per square metre. A lingonberry harvest is usually already considered very rich when there are 10 berries per square metre.

There are now a lot of lingonberries, because during the summer there has been a suitable amount of rain and sunshine.

This has been a good summer for forest berries. Besides lingonberries, a good harvest is also expected for bilberries and lingonberries.

## Spot a bat

![A northern bat lying in a cave. ](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11303910)

Open image viewer

The northern bat is the most common bat species in Finland. Photo: Håkan Söderström / AOP

The national bat spotting starts today. The spotting lasts for the weekend.

Bat spotting means that people in different parts of the country spot, that is, observe bats. Observations can be made anywhere you like, for example in the yard of a summer cottage, in a forest or in a city park.

Bats are observed for one hour. Observations can be made with the naked eye or by using technical tools.

The Finnish Bat Research Society hopes that people will report their bat observations to the Finnish Species Information Centre. The web address is laji.fi.
