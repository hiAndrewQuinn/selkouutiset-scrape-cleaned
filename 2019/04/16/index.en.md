# Tuesday 16.4.2019 (radio)

Centre chairman Juha Sipilä is leaving his post. Fighting the grey economy has been effective. 30 Finnish war dead will be buried in Lappeenranta in May. Finns living abroad made voting history.

## Juha Sipilä resigns as Centre leader

![Juha Sipilä](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10742561)

A new chairman will be chosen for the Centre in September in place of Juha Sipilä. Photo: Heikki Saukkomaa / Lehtikuva

Juha Sipilä is resigning as leader of the Centre.

Sipilä says that the poor election result left him no other options. The Centre lost 18 seats in Parliament.

An extraordinary party conference will choose the Centre's new chairman in September. Sipilä will carry out the chairman's duties until then.

The party board says that the Centre's direction is opposition and the threshold to government is high. The Centre nevertheless intends to answer the questions of the government formateur.

## Fighting the grey economy is progressing

![Accounting folder, money and calculator.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10494042)

Open image viewer

Photo: Ismo Pekkarinen / AOP

Fighting the grey economy has been effective. The grey economy avoids, among other things, taxes.

The Tax Administration says that undeclared wages worth 42 million euros were found last year. The police, meanwhile, collected criminal profits of over 27 million euros. That is 10 million more than in 2017.

The Tax Administration says that cooperation between authorities has improved. For example, immigration control and the police carried out 220 joint inspections last year.

The current programme for fighting the grey economy ends next year. The next government will decide on what comes after.

## War dead to be buried in Lappeenranta

![Coffins of unknown Finnish soldiers are lowered into the ground.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9625291)

Open image viewer

Cadets lowered the small coffins of unknown soldiers into the ground in spring 2017 at the Lappeenranta Heroes' Cemetery. Photo: Ulla Ylönen/Yle

30 Finnish war dead will be buried in Lappeenranta in May. They will be buried on the Remembrance Day for the Fallen, 19 May.

The dead have waited to be buried for over 70 years. The remains of the dead have been found over the last couple of summers in Russia.

The identities of the Finnish soldiers could not be confirmed. It is only known of them that they were Finnish soldiers. All Finnish soldiers who cannot be identified will be buried in the Lappeenranta Heroes' Cemetery.

About 13,000 Finnish soldiers were left in the war areas during the Winter War and the Continuation War. So far 1,300 soldiers have been found.

Finnish volunteers have been searching for Finnish soldiers for over 20 years. Russians too have found remains of Finnish soldiers.

## Finns abroad voted actively

![Vote counting.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10736731)

Open image viewer

Vote counting. Photo: Mikko Stig / Lehtikuva

Finns living abroad have made voting history.

The voting percentage of Finns abroad in the parliamentary elections was 12.7 percent. The voting percentage rose by 2.6 percentage points. In Finland voter turnout rose by 2 percentage points.

Almost 255,000 Finns who have the right to vote live abroad. Of them, more than 41,000 people voted in the parliamentary elections in 86 countries.

Sunday's elections were also the first postal voting elections in Finnish history: Finns around the world were able to vote by post. More than 6,000 Finns living abroad voted by post.
