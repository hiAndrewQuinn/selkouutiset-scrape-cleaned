Coronavirus vaccinations are progressing at different speeds.. The Ministry of Social Affairs and Health is preparing a new model for entry into the country. ## There are differences between regions in corona vaccinations

![People and staff at the corona vaccination centre in Oulu Hall.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11914494)

The corona vaccination point at Oulu Hall. Photo: Paulus Markkula / Yle

Corona vaccinations are progressing at different speeds in different regions of Finland.

For example, in the Helsinki and Uusimaa hospital district, in Lohja, people aged 40 are already being vaccinated. In North Karelia, only those who turn 65 are being vaccinated.

The distribution of vaccines has not been weighted according to risk groups. The Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare says that the size of the groups is not known exactly.

The pace of the first vaccine doses is slowing down a little. Second doses are going to be given at a faster pace.

## Border control is about to be tightened

![Signs reminding people about corona preparedness and safe distances at the border inspection point at Helsinki's Länsisatama on 23 February.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11832992)

Open image viewer

At Helsinki's Länsisatama, people are reminded about the corona epidemic. Photo: Antti Aimo-Koivisto / Lehtikuva

The Ministry of Social Affairs and Health is preparing a new model for entry into the country.

The aim is to tighten border control, especially at ports and airports. The number of passengers is increasing as corona restrictions are lifted.

One option is that transport companies check corona certificates already in the country of departure. Then passengers do not need to be directed to a health check in Finland.

After that, test certificates and vaccination certificates can be checked with spot checks. This can prevent congestion at the border crossing.

At the moment, Finland does not require a negative test certificate from passengers arriving in Finland.

## The EU recovery package moves forward in Parliament

![Bullet train in Venice.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11896964)

Open image viewer

Italy intends to direct a large part of the EU recovery package money to building new high-speed train connections. Photo: AOP

Parliament's Finance Committee supports the approval of the EU recovery package. The committee emphasises that the package must be a one-off.

Of the committee members, 12 supported approving the package, 5 opposed it, and 3 voted blank. The opponents were from the Finns Party and the Christian Democrats. The National Coalition members voted blank.

The purpose of the recovery package is to help EU member states cope with the effects of the coronavirus pandemic.

The decisive vote is in Parliament next Wednesday.

Vocational schools are preparing for free basic education.

Teaching will be free for students from next autumn. However, there are problems in obtaining study equipment.

There are differences, for example, in whether students get their own computers.

## Half of Finns save for retirement

![Banknotes in an envelope.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10886343)

Open image viewer

For many people, the smallness of the pension comes as a surprise. Many others already save money in advance for their retirement years. Photo: Tomi Natri / / AOP

Already nearly every second Finn saves money for their retirement years.

A study by the Finnish Centre for Pensions says that the most active retirement savers are entrepreneurs. Those of working age and university graduates who will soon retire are also active.

A survey says that over 50 percent of men save for their retirement days. Only about 40 percent of women save.
