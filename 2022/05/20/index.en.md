# Friday 20.5.2022 (radio)

Russia stops gas imports to Finland. Russia sets up bases in the western parts of the country. The state helps waterbirds. The weather is mostly dry.

## Gas from Russia

![Gas processing plant chimneys and buildings in grey winter weather.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12423236)

Open image viewer

In the picture, Gazprom's gas processing plant near Moscow in early April. Photo: Maxim Shipenkov / EPA

Natural gas will soon no longer come from Russia to Finland. Russia will stop gas imports to Finland on Saturday morning at 7 o'clock. This is reported by the gas company Gasum. The reason is that Finland refuses to pay Russia for gas in roubles.

Just over 5 per cent of Finland's energy is natural gas. Industry uses natural gas the most.

Finland is renting an LNG ship, so that there will be enough gas in the future. Finland is renting the ship from the USA for 10 years. The ship may be in use next winter. An LNG ship carries natural gas that has been turned into a liquid.

Russia is setting up new military units in the western parts of the country.

This is reported by Russia's Defence Minister Sergei Shoigu. He says that the reason for the new military units is the expansion of NATO. Shoigu says that Russia will set up 12 new military units by the end of this year.

Finland and Sweden officially applied for NATO membership on Wednesday.

## Waterbirds

![Red-crested pochard](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1920,h_1080,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5609695cd121ea381d0/1557209677)

Open image viewer

The population of the red-crested pochard has collapsed to a fraction of its former size in 30 years. Photo: Asko Hauta-Aho

The Finnish state is helping waterbirds. The aim is that the disappearance of birds will stop. Many waterbirds are much fewer than before.

The Finnish state is building wetlands for the birds. The state is also repairing old wetlands. A wetland is a wet area in nature that lies between water and dry land. Wetlands are important for waterbirds.

The state also traps raccoon dogs and minks. Raccoon dogs and minks are predators that destroy birds' nests. Raccoon dogs and minks are alien species, that is, they do not belong to Finland's nature.
