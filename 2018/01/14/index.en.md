# Sunday 14.1.2018 (radio)

The presidential election may affect who leads the Centre Party. People were frightened by a false missile alert in Hawaii. Finns do not want to forget the civil war.

## The Centre Party and the presidential election

![Juha Sipilä](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10022285)

Prime Minister Juha Sipilä is the chairman of the Centre Party. Photo: Martti Kainulainen / Lehtikuva

The presidential election may affect who leads the Centre Party.

The chairman of the Centre Party, Prime Minister Juha Sipilä, says that the presidential election may affect whether he continues as chairman. Sipilä says that the chairman's support may fall if the party does badly in the election.

The Centre Party's presidential candidate is Matti Vanhanen. Vanhanen has had only a little support in opinion polls. One reason for this is that Paavo Väyrynen is also a candidate in the election. Väyrynen is an old Centre Party politician.

On Sunday, Sipilä was a guest on the radio programme Pääministerin haastattelutunti (The Prime Minister's Interview Hour). The presidential election will be held in 2 weeks.

## False missile alert in Hawaii

![A false missile alert for Hawaiians.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10022135)

Open image viewer

Hawaiians got a frightening text message. Photo: Yle Uutisgrafiika

People have been frightened by a false missile alert in Hawaii, in the USA.

The authorities sent Hawaiians a text message on Saturday. The message said that a missile was approaching Hawaii. The same warning also came on television.

The warning said that people must take shelter. The warning also said that this was not a drill.

About 40 minutes later, Hawaiians got a new text message. In it, the authorities cancelled the warning.

People got the false warning because of a mistake. An employee pressed the wrong button. The authorities have apologised.

## Finns and the civil war

![Whites preparing for war, Civil Guard members from Lammi.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9961280)

Open image viewer

White soldiers. Photo: Yle arkisto

100 years have passed since the Finnish Civil War. Yle News studied what Finns think about the civil war today.

Finns think it is important that there is accurate information about the civil war. Finns do not want to forget what happened 100 years ago.

Most Finns think that the civil war still divides the nation. Researcher Marko Tikka thinks that Finns have a healthy understanding of history. They also want to know about the difficult things.

In the civil war, Finns fought against each other. The war began in January 1918 and ended in May.

In the war, the Whites and the Reds were on opposite sides. The Reds' goal was a socialist Finland.

The Whites supported Finland's bourgeois government. The Whites won the war.

Many Reds died in prison camps after the war. In total, more than 36,000 people died because of the civil war.
