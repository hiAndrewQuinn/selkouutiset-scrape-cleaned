## People with intellectual disabilities and police use of force

First, news about people with intellectual disabilities.

The Finnish Association for Developmental Disabilities is concerned about the police actions in Espoo on Friday.

A man with a developmental disability died when the police used a remote control on him here at Rinnekodi in Lakisto, Espoo.

The man behaved aggressively.

The Finnish Association for People with Developmental Disabilities hopes that the authorities will better understand people with disabilities.

Expert **Susanna Rieppo** says that the case is sad and exceptional. Therefore, it must be investigated thoroughly.

The Finnish Association for People with Developmental Disabilities hopes that the authorities will better understand people with disabilities.

(Susanna Rieppo, advocacy expert, Finnish Association for Developmental Disabilities)

*"It would be really important that all actors, officials, and society have knowledge and understanding about intellectual disabilities."*

The Finnish Association for People with Developmental Disabilities also reminds us of the law.

The law states that the reasons for the behavior of a person with a developmental disability must be determined so that they can be supported.

## Artificial intelligence concern

Now news about artificial intelligence.

Yle has asked Finns what they think about artificial intelligence.

Slightly more than half of Finns fear that artificial intelligence will take away a lot of jobs, meaning that it will cause mass unemployment.

16 percent of Finns believe this a lot and 35 percent of Finns believe this quite a lot.

Slightly less than half of Finns are not afraid that artificial intelligence will cause mass unemployment.

Experts say that many jobs will be taken over by artificial intelligence.

However, the job may remain, but the work is changing. Therefore, employees need to learn new skills.

## Pasture-raised broilers

Finally, some news about agriculture.

This is the Karhula farm in Nastola.

Here, broilers are raised on outdoor pastures. There are only a few such farms in Finland.

Free-range broilers eat fresh plants and insects from the ground.

The cage is always moved to a new location. This way the birds always have fresh plants to eat.

The chicken poop fertilizes the soil and the grass soon grows back.

On the Karhula farm, broilers are raised in a mobile breeding facility.
