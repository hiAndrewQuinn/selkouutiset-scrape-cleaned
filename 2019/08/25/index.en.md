# Sunday 25.8.2019 (radio)

Shots have been fired at police in Porvoo. Prime Minister Antti Rinne says that the shooting in Porvoo is serious. A Yle journalist has met a young Finn who is in a youth prison in Syria. Skolt Sami people have held a celebration over the weekend.

## Shooting in Porvoo

![In Porvoo there was a large police operation early on Sunday 25 August. The investigation was carried out in the Ölsten industrial area.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10937487)

Shots have been fired at police in Porvoo. Nobody died, but 2 police officers were taken to hospital.

The shooting happened during the night between Saturday and Sunday. The shooting took place in the Eestinmäki industrial area of Porvoo.

Because of the shooting there was a big police operation in Porvoo. The police have searched for the shooters all Sunday. The police estimate that the shooters are dangerous.

## Prime Minister's interview hour

![Prime Minister Antti Rinne before the live broadcast of the Prime Minister's interview hour on Yle Radio Suomi at Kesäranta in Helsinki on 25 August.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10937790)

Prime Minister Antti Rinne in a radio interview. Photo: Roni Rekomaa / Lehtikuva

Prime Minister Antti Rinne says that the shooting in Porvoo is serious.

In Rinne's view something has changed in society if the police are attacked. Rinne reminds us that the police protect us all.

Prime Minister Rinne commented on the events in Porvoo on the radio programme Prime Minister's Interview Hour. Rinne was a guest on the programme for the first time. Rinne began as prime minister in June.

The Prime Minister's Interview Hour has been made for almost 60 years. The programme is broadcast once a month. Antti Rinne is the 25th prime minister to be interviewed on the programme.

## Finn in a youth prison in Syria

![A man seen from behind, sitting on a chair.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10934870)

Open image viewer

The Finnish young man in the youth prison does not want to reveal his name publicly. Photo: Antti Kuronen / Yle

Next, news from Syria. A Yle journalist has met a young Finn who is in a youth prison in Syria.

The Finnish young man is now 18 years old. He was 14 when he travelled with his family to the area controlled by the terrorist organisation ISIS.

The Finn was caught when ISIS lost the war. Now he is in a youth prison, where young people are helped to return to normal life.

The prison is run by Kurds, who fought against ISIS. The Finn says that conditions in the prison are good.

A longer version of the interview, in everyday language, can be found on Yle's website at yle.fi.

## Skolt Sami

![Festival for the settlement of Skolt Sami people, 24 August 2019.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4032,h_2268,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5856525d617c2d3f89b/1566669941)

A big celebration was held in Sevettijärvi in Lapland at the weekend. Skolt Sami people took part in the celebration.

Skolt Sami are one group of the Sami people. The Sami are the indigenous people of Lapland.

At the celebration the Skolt Sami remembered how new homes were sought for them 70 years ago. At that time the Second World War was over, and Finland lost Skolt Sami areas to Russia.

Many Skolts lost their own language and culture when they moved elsewhere in Finland. Now the Skolt Sami language and Skolt culture are on the rise again and are doing better.
