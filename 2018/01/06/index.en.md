# Saturday 6.1.2018 (radio)

In Kainuu, snow is still cutting off the power. What things do you need at home if the power is off for many days? The energy industry opposes a ban on coal. Tellervo Koivisto and Pentti Arajärvi tell in a Yle interview what it is like to be the spouse of the President of Finland.

## More power cuts in Kainuu

![A man sweeps snow off the steps of a house in Hyrynsalmi.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10009661)

In Kainuu in Eastern Finland, more snow is falling. The snow is pressing trees onto power lines, and that is why more than 5 000 homes have been without power today, on Saturday.

There have been power cuts in Kainuu for about a week. People have coped quite well, because in the countryside people are used to services not always working. Many have a wood stove, with which they can heat the house and cook. Because it is not very frosty outside, people have not got too cold at home. Many also have a standby generator, which makes electricity from diesel oil.

When the power is off, phones do not always work either. Mobile phone masts do not work for long without electricity.

## Important things in a power cut

![Wood heating, dry wood, matches, storm lantern, candles, camping stove, torches, spare batteries, battery-powered radio, a package that helps you get through a power cut.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10009618)

A radio, batteries, candles and matches are needed if the power is off for a long time. Photo: Pekka Sakki / Lehtikuva

In Finland, everyone should keep some things at home that help them cope, even if the power is off for many days.

Such things are a torch, candles and matches. A radio is also important, one that works on batteries. From the radio you can hear the news and other important information, even if the television and the internet do not work. There are chargers that can charge a mobile phone even when there is no power. It is good to have cash.

At home it is always a good idea to keep food, water and medicines, enough to cope for many days.

## The energy industry opposes a coal ban

![The Hanasaari power plant and a pile of coal in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10009600)

Open image viewer

Coal still heats houses in Helsinki. Photo: Vesa Moilanen / Lehtikuva

The energy industry opposes a ban on coal.

Environment Minister Kimmo Tiilikainen proposes that the use of coal be banned by 2025. The energy industry says that the use of coal will fall in any case within 10 years. If coal is banned, the ban will be expensive for consumers and the energy markets will be thrown into chaos, the energy industry says.

Minister Tiilikainen wants to find out whether municipalities could give up coal within 7 years and start using natural gas, for example.

## The President's spouse has to leave home and work

![Tellervo Koivisto.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10009198)

Open image viewer

Tellervo Koivisto. Photo: Markku Pitkänen / YLE

Tellervo Koivisto and Pentti Arajärvi tell in a Yle interview what it is like to be the President's spouse in Finland.

Tellervo Koivisto's husband, Mauno Koivisto, was President of Finland in the 1980s and 90s. Tellervo Koivisto says that it was an interesting time. However, she says that it was hard to move away from her own home. The official residence was a bit like a hotel. There were guests there all the time. The summer cottage was an important place for Tellervo Koivisto, where she could be herself.

![Pentti Arajärvi.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10009197)

Pentti Arajärvi's wife, Tarja Halonen, was President in the 2000s. Pentti Arajärvi had to give up his own job. He also had to be in the public eye more than he wanted. Arajärvi also says, however, that the President's spouse gets to go to exciting places and meet interesting people.
