# Tuesday 6.9.2016 (radio)

More cruise ships will be built at the Turku shipyard. From October, it will be easier to get a fixed fine. The Norwegian royal couple is on a state visit to Finland. The television antenna network goes dark during the day in Southern Finland.

## Turku got a new cruise ship order

![The gate of the Turku shipyard](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9147155)

2 cruise ships will be built at the Turku shipyard for the Carnival shipping company. Photo: Ari Welling / Yle

The Turku shipyard has received a big ship order.

The Turku shipyard will build 2 cruise ships for the Carnival shipping company. The ships will be ready by 2022.

The new ships are environmentally friendly. They use liquefied natural gas as fuel.

The German company Meyer owns the Turku shipyard. Meyer says that the deal is worth about 800 million euros.

Carnival is one of the biggest cruise shipping companies in the world. Carnival has already ordered 2 cruise ships from Turku before.

## It is easier to get a fixed fine

![Speed camera](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7530738)

Open image viewer

There are almost 1000 camera posts around the country. But most of them have no camera. Photo: Tommi Parkkinen / Yle

Finnish drivers very rarely get a speeding fine based on police camera surveillance. On average, only a couple of percent of drivers are caught speeding by a police surveillance camera.

There are almost 1000 speed cameras, that is, police camera surveillance posts, along roads around the country. The posts have about 90 cameras in total, but only about 50 cameras are in use at the same time.

The police say that Finns no longer speed as much as before. The change happened a year ago, when the fixed fine was raised considerably.

From the beginning of October, the lower limit for a fixed fine goes down. You can get a fine for going just 7 kilometres over the speed limit.

## The Norwegian royal couple came on a state visit

![Jenni haukio, Sauli Niinistö, Harald V and Sonja ](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9146752)

Mrs Jenni Haukio and President Sauli Niinistö welcomed King Harald and Queen Sonja of Norway.  Photo: Tiina Jutila / Yle

King Harald V and Queen Sonja of Norway have started a state visit to Finland. President Sauli Niinistö and Mrs Jenni Haukio welcomed the royal couple in the morning in front of the Presidential Palace.

During the day, the royal couple has, for example, visited Parliament and met Prime Minister Juha Sipilä at Kesäranta.

The Norwegian royal couple's visit lasts 3 days. The programme of the visit focuses especially on the economy, health and learning.

Besides Helsinki, Harald and Sonja are visiting Oulu.

## Changes are being made to the TV antenna network

![No signal](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6417072)

Open image viewer

There are long breaks in television broadcasts in Southern Finland because of work on the antenna network. Photo: Timo Nykyri / Yle

There are long breaks in television broadcasts today and in the coming days in Southern Finland.

TV broadcasts may be cut off for as long as 7 hours between 9 and 16 o'clock.

The breaks are caused by changes to the television antenna network. The work continues until Thursday.

In antenna households, the television channels must be searched for again after the work. The changes do not affect cable and satellite households.

During the break, you can watch Yle's channels, for example, on Areena on Yle's website.
