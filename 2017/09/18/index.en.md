## Intoxicants take away the right to practise

![Hospital beds in a corridor at the emergency department of Oulu University Hospital.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9790226)

Many doctors, nurses and other health professionals lose their right to practise because of alcohol or drugs. Photo: Wasim Khuzam / Yle

Every year, dozens of health care workers lose their right to practise their profession. Then they may no longer do that work. The National Supervisory Authority for Welfare and Health, Valvira, says that the reason is most often a substance abuse problem. The reason can also be, for example, a memory disorder or poor professional skills.

However, the cases have decreased in recent years. Valvira believes that workplaces now step in earlier than before, for example in substance abuse problems.

Preliminary figures show that this year about 40 people have lost their right to practise. More than half of them are practical nurses.

## Rains put the grain harvest at risk

![Barley.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9828681)

Open image viewer

Because of the rain, some of the barley, oats and wheat may be left in the field. Photo: Simo Pitkänen / Yle

The farmers' organisation MTK says that the continuous rains may be a disaster for farms. MTK demands crisis measures from the government for the financial trouble of farms. Especially on grain farms, the harvest is at risk because of the rains.

The agricultural advisory organisation ProAgria says that the last time there was such a bad grain summer was 30 years ago, in 1987. In the southern parts of the country, farmers have been able to try threshing the grain. However, the grain is very wet, and drying it with a machine is expensive.

The weather this week will decide very much how much of the grain harvest can be threshed in time.

## About half ride a bike without a headlight

![A bicycle light and a hand.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7588063)

Open image viewer

Without a headlight, a cyclist cannot see and cannot be seen. Photo: Kimmo Gustafsson / Yle

About half of cyclists ride in the dark without lights. This is what the road safety organisation Liikenneturva says. However, a proper light helps the cyclist see the road better. It also helps other people see the cyclist.

More than 60 percent of drivers told Liikenneturva that a cyclist without lights is hard to notice.

In the dark, a bicycle must have a front light that shows a white light. A new law proposal plans that cyclists must also use a red rear light.

## The Finnish school in Dublin greets 100-year-old Finland

![A drawing of the Finnish flag.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9837669)

Open image viewer

In Finnish schools abroad, children study the Finnish language and Finnish culture.

A large number of Finnish children and young people live in different parts of the world, and they study the Finnish language and Finnish culture in Finnish schools.

Now that independent Finland turns 100, the Finnish schools are sending greetings to Finland on video.

The newest greetings come from the Finnish school in Dublin, Ireland.
