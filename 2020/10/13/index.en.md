# Tuesday 13.10.2020 (radio)

The government parties have reached agreement on the social and health care reform. Face masks have become compulsory at airports. Alko's sales have grown a lot during the coronavirus epidemic. The body mass index in healthcare is being criticised.

## The government reached agreement on the social and health care reform

![Press conference on the social and health care reform.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11592515)

The government parties have agreed on the new social and health care reform. Photo: Silja Viitala / Yle

The government parties have reached agreement on the social and health care reform. Sote means social and health care reform.

Finland will set up 21 social and health care areas. During the transition period, the name of the areas is wellbeing area.

In addition to the areas, the City of Helsinki is also an organiser.

In the reform, the organising of social and health care services moves from municipalities and joint municipal authorities to the wellbeing areas. The areas also include rescue services, for example the fire brigade.

In the reform, Itä-Savo will be joined to South Savo.

The purpose of sote is to improve basic services.

The social and health care reform failed in Parliament last term. In the new sote, for example, public health care has a strong role.

## Face masks became compulsory at airports

![Passengers wearing face masks at Helsinki-Vantaa airport.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11592768)

Open image viewer

Face masks must now be worn at Finnish airports. Photo: Silja Viitala / Yle

The airport company Finavia says that all passengers must wear face masks at airports.

The face mask requirement came into force today. It applies to everyone who moves around in the airport.

Until now, Finavia's recommendation has been in force. It was given already in May.

Finavia wants to protect passengers and airport staff. Finavia also wants to stress that safe travel is possible.

## Alko's sales have grown

![Bottle shelves of Alko in the Sokos store in Kuopio.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10839622)

Open image viewer

Alcohol sales in Alko stores have grown sharply during the coronavirus. Photo: Petra Husu / Yle

Alko's sales have grown a lot during the coronavirus epidemic. From January to September, Alko's sales rose by more than 12 percent compared with the same time last year.

In September, sales by litres rose 15.5 percent from September last year.

Last month, nearly 9 percent more customers visited Alko stores than a year ago.

Total alcohol consumption has, however, fallen, because restaurants have been closed for part of the time. In addition, the number of passengers bringing alcohol in from abroad and from ships has decreased because of the restrictions.

## Body mass index does not tell enough

![Fashion show in Canada.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11568121)

Open image viewer

The picture is from a fashion show in Toronto, Canada, from 2012. The fashion show encouraged plus-size women to recognise both inner and outer beauty.  Photo: AOP

The body mass index in healthcare is being criticised. For example, Canada has already decided to stop using body mass index in the treatment of obesity.

Body mass index is a measure used to assess the relationship between a person's weight and height.

Most often, body mass index is calculated from a person's weight, divided by the square of their height.

In Finland, obesity in adults is assessed by working out the body mass index and measuring the waist circumference.

Specialist doctor Reita Nyberg says that body mass index does not tell anything about the body's composition. For example, muscles weigh more than fat. This can be the case for athletes, for example.
