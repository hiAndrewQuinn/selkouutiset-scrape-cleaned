# Monday 14.1.2019 (radio)

President Niinistö and Mrs Haukio are visiting China. The police are getting more reports of child sexual abuse than before. A citizens' initiative to deport foreign sex offenders has received many signatures. Saimaa ringed seals may succeed in nesting this year without help from snowdrifts.

## The president's state visit

![About 80 children greeted the presidents at the reception ceremonies.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10596016)

A group of children greeted the presidents, waving Finnish and Chinese flags. Photo: Matti Porre / Office of the President of the Republic

President Sauli Niinistö and Mrs Jenni Haukio have started a state visit to China. The visit lasts 2 days.

President Niinistö and Mrs Haukio were received by the President of China, Xi Jinping.

At the reception ceremony, a military orchestra played the national anthems of Finland and China. After that, the presidents inspected the guard of honour. A large group of children was also there, waving Finnish and Chinese flags.

The presidents had a conversation in which they talked about, for example, relations between Finland and China. Human rights issues were also discussed for a long time, President Niinistö says.

During the visit, Niinistö and Xi will open the joint Finland-China winter sports year, whose purpose is to strengthen cooperation in the field of winter sports.

This is already the 3rd time Niinistö and Xi have met. The year before last, Xi visited Helsinki.

## Sexual abuse of children

![Silhouette of a young girl in a tunnel](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10595331)

Open image viewer

Last year the police received about 1,400 reports of child sexual abuse. Photo: AOP

More reports of child sexual abuse were made to the police last year than the year before.

According to the preliminary statistics of the Police Board, about 1,400 reports of child sexual abuse were made last year. That is about 18 percent more than the year before.

In sexual abuse cases, about 20 percent of the suspected perpetrators had a foreign background.

## Citizens' initiative on sex offenders

![Memorial candles in front of the Valkea Ovi shopping centre in Oulu.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10595446)

Open image viewer

A citizens' initiative to deport foreign sex offenders was launched when the first suspected sex crimes in Oulu became public. Photo: Timo Heikkala / Lehtikuva

A citizens' initiative on deporting foreign sex offenders has received over 50,000 signatures.

The initiative proposes that a foreigner automatically loses their residence permit in Finland if they commit a sexual crime here. In addition, the Finnish Immigration Service could deport them immediately.

However, Parliament will not have time to deal with the citizens' initiative before its term ends because of the elections. The initiative also cannot be moved to the new Parliament.

## Ringed seals nesting

![Ringed seal](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10070762)

Open image viewer

The Saimaa ringed seal gives birth to its pups in a snow cave. Photo: Eero Korhola

The Saimaa ringed seal may succeed in nesting this year without help from snowdrifts. On Wednesday, Metsähallitus will say whether the seal needs help.

The seal gives birth to its pups in a nest that it digs in a snowdrift. In many previous years there has been so little snow that no proper snowdrifts formed. Because of this, people have shovelled snow into help snowdrifts for the seals.

This year more snow has fallen. It also seems that the wind has perhaps blown and piled up enough snow onto the nesting places of the seals on the shores of Saimaa.
