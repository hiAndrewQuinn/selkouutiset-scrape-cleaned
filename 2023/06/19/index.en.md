## Budget for a dignified life

![Luggage of Maarit Sivonen and Marko Sivonen.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_6000,h_3375,x_0,y_625/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-11217666475b24710f30/1685435747)

Travelling from time to time is also part of a dignified life, say researchers at the University of Turku. Photo: Matti Myller / Yle

The University of Turku researchers have calculated how much money is needed for a dignified life in Finland.

Food, clothes and a home are only enough for survival. The researchers say that a chance to, for example, have hobbies and go out sometimes is also needed. A small holiday trip once a year is also part of a dignified life.

The researchers calculate that in Helsinki a family of 4 needs over 3,600 euros a month. A person living alone gets by on a little over 1,500 euros.

This includes housing, food, clothes, phone, health, hobbies, hygiene and things for the home.

In Finland, 8 per cent have monthly incomes that are smaller than the sum needed for a dignified life.

The future government will strengthen relations with the USA. This is what the future foreign minister Elina Valtonen and defence minister Antti Häkkänen say.

The National Coalition's Valtonen and Häkkänen are important ministers in foreign policy and military relations.

They also promise that Finland's support for Ukraine will continue, because it is also about Finland's security. Valtonen says that membership of the NATO defence alliance has opened new opportunities for Finland towards the USA.

The new government of Finland will perhaps start its work this week.

## Pensioners' gig work

![A care home resident sits on their bed with the help of a carer and a rollator.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3000,h_1687,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-96984162a866fce6fff/1655204694)

More and more pensioners are doing gig work. Photo: Antti-Petteri Karhunen / Yle

In Finland, more and more pensioners are working.

Pensioners can now look for work online. The Omakeikka.fi website opened this month. Pensioners can look on the site for gig work that suits them.

Employers can also look for gig workers there.

The service is free for pensioners.

Over 130,000 Finns had their main job in a cultural profession last year. That is almost 6 per cent more than the year before last.

However, the number is still not as high as before the corona pandemic.

At the Sodankylä film festival in Lapland, more people came than ever before in the festival's 38-year history. Photo: Antti Ullakko / Yle

At the Sodankylä film festival in Lapland last week, a record was set. There were 35,000 visitors in total.

There were 64 sold-out screenings.

One of these was Aki Kaurismäki's film Fallen Leaves, which won a prize at the Cannes film festival in France. The Finnish premiere was at the festival.
