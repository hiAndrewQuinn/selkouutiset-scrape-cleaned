# Tuesday 31.7.2018 (radio)

37 percent of Finns want to ban plastic bags in shops completely. The price of petrol may rise further. Drought shows in Finland's groundwater. Roundworms may have been woken from eternal ice.

## Plastic bags get criticism from Finns

![Plastic bags in a pile](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10311084)

Open image viewer

Many Finns are ready to ban the sale of plastic bags in shops completely. Photo: Antti Haanpää / Yle

Many Finns say that plastic bags should not be sold in shops.

Taloustutkimus carried out a survey for Yle on the price of plastic bags. The current price is 20 cents. 21 percent of respondents say that half a euro is a good price. Only 8 percent support a price of 2 euros.

The surprise was that 37 percent want to ban plastic bags in shops completely. Women support a ban more than men do. The manufacturer of plastic bags does not support a ban. Amerplast's sales director Ari-Pekka Pietilä says that most of the plastic bags in grocery shops are already made from recycled plastic.

The EU, the European Union, wants to reduce plastic waste. Making plastic bags cost money is one way to do this.

## Petrol is expensive

![A man refuels his car at a petrol station.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10032325)

Open image viewer

Petrol may become even more expensive. Photo: Kalle Mäkelä / Yle

The price of petrol may rise further in the coming months.

Petrol has been expensive this summer. In May it was forecast that the price of crude oil may rise significantly in the autumn. That may raise the price of petrol further.

Jan von Gerich, chief analyst at Nordea, says that the price of a barrel of oil is unlikely to rise to 90 dollars, which has been feared.

## Groundwater is low

![A well in the yard of an allotment garden plot.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9073523)

Open image viewer

If the garden has to be watered with well water, it is worth doing it only at night. Then the water does not evaporate straight away. Photo: Kyösti Vaara / Yle

Extreme weather also affects groundwater. That is why drought shows in Finland's groundwater.

In the southern parts of the country, in the south-west and in Central Finland, the groundwater may have dropped by almost half a metre. The Finnish Environment Institute reports that groundwater may fall to record lows.

Some water utilities have already issued recommendations that water use should be reduced. Users of yard wells should shorten their showers and the watering of yards.

## Roundworms have been woken from eternal ice

![A worm shaped like a spindle, magnified greatly.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10330444)

Open image viewer

One roundworm of today, Caenorhabditis elegans, is the same length and lives in soil, like the relatives found in Siberia. Tens of thousands of roundworm species are known. Some also live as parasites in humans, for example the pinworm. Photo: Kbadnam / CC BY-SA 2.5

Researchers say that they have woken roundworms from eternal ice. The worms had been frozen in Siberia for tens of thousands of years.

Russian researchers say that the worms had been in the warm laboratory for a few weeks. After that the worms began to move and to look for food.

Small roundworms froze into the Siberian permafrost almost 42 000 years ago. At that time there were mammoths in the north, but there were not yet any people. The worms were only a millimetre long but were still multicellular animals. It was already known that roundworms are a very tough life form. Before this, worms had only been revived from about 40 years of icy sleep.

The scientific community is cautious about the Siberian discovery.
