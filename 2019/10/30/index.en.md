# Wednesday 30.10.2019 (radio)

Denmark gives permission for the Nord Stream 2 gas pipeline. A dispute over "kiky hours" is stalling pay negotiations. There are still few women on company boards. Finns value Yle Areena.

## Nord Stream 2 moves forward

![A worker wearing a yellow high-visibility vest and a helmet walks next to the gas pipeline.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5472,h_3078,x_0,y_77/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6060115db98c3f7567c/1572441321)

Construction work on the Nord Stream 2 gas pipeline progressed in early June near Kingisepp in Russia. Photo: Alexander Demianchuk / Itar-Tass / All Over Press

Denmark has given permission for the Nord Stream 2 gas pipeline. Denmark has delayed the project. The Danish Energy Agency says that the pipe may be laid from the south-east side of the island of Bornholm.

The Russian gas company Gazprom is the biggest owner of the gas pipeline. The pipeline carries Russian gas from Northwest Siberia to Europe.

The pipeline runs along the bottom of the Baltic Sea from Russia to Germany. More than 2100 kilometres of pipe have already been installed.

Nord Stream 2 has sparked fierce debate.

## Dispute over kiky hours stalls pay talks

![Collective agreement negotiations start in the technology industry in 2019](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5472,h_3078,x_0,y_282/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5868665d68c1ea9661b/1567146577)

Open image viewer

The collective agreement negotiations between the technology industry and the Industrial Union started at the end of August. Photo: Laura Hyyti / Yle

The autumn pay negotiations are under way. In the negotiations between the Industrial Union and the technology industry, a solution is being sought on Thursday. The collective agreement expires on Thursday.

The negotiators have been arguing about extra working hours, which were agreed on in the 2016 competitiveness agreement. Pay rises have not even been discussed yet.

The employers' side stresses that extending working hours and pay rises are linked to each other.

## Still few women on company boards

![Shoes of men in suits.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8771142)

Open image viewer

In Finland, almost a third of the members of company boards are women. Growth over two years has been over seven percentage points. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

There are still quite few women on company boards in different parts of the world.

Women make up 17 percent of board members. The share of women has grown by less than 2 percentage points since the previous measurement.

Among chief executives, women are only a little over 4 percent, and among financial managers less than 13 percent. Among leaders under 40 years old, there are more women than in the older age groups.

The report says that the reason for the small number of women is, for example, old-fashioned company culture.

The report studied the number of women in companies in 66 countries. Only 6 countries have more than 30 percent women in company management. Finland is among these countries.

## Yle Areena is Finland's most respected online brand

![A mobile phone in hand. On the phone's screen is Yle's monthly letter.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4986,h_2805,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-4929375b226dde34bea/1528983464)

Finns value Yle Areena. Yle Areena has been Finland's most respected online brand for the 7th time in a row.

The study says that 92 percent of Finns know the Areena brand. The next most respected brand is the Finnish Meteorological Institute, and third is Kanta.fi of the National Archive of Health.

The study assessed 150 of the best-known domestic and foreign online brands. Yle is 6th in the study, and Elävä arkisto is 7th.
