# Tuesday 6.9.2022 (radio)

Fortum agreed with the state on loan terms. Olkiluoto 3 may increase its output. Finnair flies to the regional airports again. The weather is cool.

## Fortum

![The Fortum flag flutters in the yard of the head office.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4240,h_2385,x_0,y_2/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-98441562d81c2919c98/1658330213)

Fortum's head office in Keilaniemi. Photo: Rinna Härkönen / Yle

The energy company Fortum has agreed on the terms on which it will borrow money from the Finnish state. Fortum can borrow a total of over 2 billion euros from the state. However, Fortum is not withdrawing the money straight away now.

Fortum may need loan money from the state, because electricity is expensive. The electricity market works so that electricity companies now have to have much more money in their accounts than before.

The Finnish state owns over 50 percent of Fortum.

## Olkiluoto

![Picture of a nuclear power plant.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3566,h_2006,x_2,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-98273262d173afc8e8f/1657894047)

The third reactor at Olkiluoto is meant to produce electricity for the national grid at full power in December. Photo: Lucas Ekblad / Yle

The new reactor at the Olkiluoto nuclear power plant may produce more electricity than before. The Radiation and Nuclear Safety Authority gave permission. Teollisuuden Voima may increase the output of the third reactor at the Olkiluoto nuclear power plant.

Olkiluoto 3 is Finland's newest nuclear power plant. At the moment the new reactor at the Olkiluoto nuclear power plant is only being tested. The new reactor at the Olkiluoto nuclear power plant is meant to operate normally in December this year.

## Flights to the regions

![Finnair's ATR at Joensuu airport.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1841,h_1036,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-7833646049fe938658e/1714224093)

Open image viewer

Finnair's ATR at Joensuu airport. Photo: Sami Jumppanen / Korpipaja

The airline Finnair is again flying from Helsinki to Finland's small airports. From the end of October Finnair will fly from Helsinki to, for example, the airports of Joensuu, Jyväskylä and Kajaani.

Finnair flies to Finland's small airports quite rarely, because aviation fuel is now expensive.

So few passengers fly to Finland's small airports that the airline does not make a profit from the flights. That is why the Finnish state supports the flights with almost 14 million euros. The state wants the small airports to stay open.

## Drowning deaths

![Lake landscape of Lake Kallavesi.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1600,h_900,x_0,y_138/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-7241665f72ee3eb49fa/1601367732)

Open image viewer

Lake landscape of Lake Kallavesi. Illustrative image. Photo: Keijo Salokangas / Yle

In Finland 15 people died in August by drowning in the water.

Between January and August, a total of 71 people drowned in Finland this year. In the summer 53 people drowned, which is 34 people fewer than in summer 2021.

In Finland 100–150 people drown every year. About 50 percent of them drink alcohol before drowning.
