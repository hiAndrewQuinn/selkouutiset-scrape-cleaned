# Tuesday 13.8.2019 (radio)

Online grocery shopping is growing fast this year. President Niinistö does not care about the criticism from the Hungarian prime minister. The state's budget for next year is being planned. Two little boys saw a bear on the way to school near central Mikkeli.

## Food from the internet

![Robert Himanen](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10920241)

Open image viewer

Robert Himanen takes grocery shopping to customers. Photo: Pekka Tynell / Yle

Online grocery shopping is growing fast. Online shopping means that the customer orders food on the internet and the shop brings it to the customer's home.

For example, K-stores have sold food through the internet more than 100% more this year than last year. Many families with children in particular buy food on the internet. Families save time, because they do not need to go to the grocery shop.

Although online shopping is now growing fast, its share is less than half a per cent of all grocery sales in Finland.

## Niinistö does not care about Orbán

![Sauli Niinistö](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10920043)

Open image viewer

President Sauli Niinistö. Photo: Roni Rekomaa / Lehtikuva

President Sauli Niinistö does not care about the criticism from the Hungarian prime minister.

Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán has said that there is something wrong with Finland's judicial system. Orbán criticises Finland perhaps because Finland is now the holder of the EU presidency.

Finland wants the EU to reduce funding to Hungary and other member states that do not respect EU principles. One important principle is that politicians do not influence how the judicial system works. Other principles include, for example, freedom of speech and democracy.

President Niinistö says that the speaking style of Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán is well known in Europe, and many people do not take Orbán's speeches seriously.

## The state's finances are being planned

![Minister of Finance Mika Lintilä arriving at the negotiations on the 2020 budget proposal.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10919766)

Open image viewer

Mika Lintilä. Photo: Martti Kainulainen / Lehtikuva

Finland's state budget for next year is being planned at the moment.

The Ministry of Finance started budget negotiations today, Tuesday. Minister Mika Lintilä says that Finland's economic growth is slowing and therefore the state must be thrifty. Lintilä says that it is important to keep as many Finns as possible in work. Then people pay taxes, and the state's money is not spent too much on unemployment benefits.

The government will decide in a month what kind of state budget it proposes to Parliament.

## A bear frightened little boys in Mikkeli

![A bear builds up a layer of fat before going into hibernation.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9395200)

Open image viewer

This is a different bear from the one in Mikkeli. Photo: Tuomo Björksten / Yle

Little boys saw a bear during the day near central Mikkeli.

Two 10-year-old boys were cycling home from school when they saw a bear in the forest. The boys were frightened and rode home very fast. One boy's mother called the police and also warned the boys' school.

The police came to the place and found bear tracks. The police asked people not to go to Kalevankangas in Mikkeli, where the bear was, without good reason.
