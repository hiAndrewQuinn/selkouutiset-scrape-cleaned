A study has been made of the lives of young people born in 1997. In some institutions children are treated badly. Pensioners talk about their own sex lives in a theatre. Someone may have forgotten that they own shares in a telephone company.

## The lives of young people born in '97 were studied

![A girl looks out of a window.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10442691)

Information was collected from 60,000 young people for the study. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

A big study has been made in Finland of young people born in 1997. Information was collected for the study from 60,000 young people.

The study says that things have gone badly for some young people. 20 per cent have had mental health problems. Authorities have taken 6 per cent of young people into care, that is, moved them away from home, at least for a while.

Most 20-year-old Finns are doing well. Those young people whose parents are also doing well do best. Children who go to university are usually those whose parents have also studied a lot.

## Bad treatment in institutions

![Maija Sakslin, Deputy Parliamentary Ombudsman](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10500795)

Open image viewer

Maija Sakslin. Photo: Markku Pitkänen / Yle

Maija Sakslin, Deputy Parliamentary Ombudsman, says that in some institutions in Finland people are treated badly.

Sakslin visits institutions that care, for example, for children taken into care. She says that in some institutions children are punished by being locked in on their own behind locks. Children are also ordered to take off their clothes when, for example, they are searched for drugs. Finnish law forbids this.

Sakslin says that the Niuvanniemi psychiatric hospital in Kuopio knows how to care for children and young people well. There, children do not need to be tied up, isolated, held down or ordered to take off their clothes.

## Real-life stories about sex from the elderly

![A bed with crumpled sheets](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10355655)

Open image viewer

Sex is not only for young people. Photo: AOP

In Espoo a theatre show is coming, in which 6 pensioners tell the audience about sex. The pensioners tell about their own lives, things that are true. They tell what sex was like when they were young and what it is like when they are old. For example, 86-year-old Osmo Tuorila says that he still wants sex and that it is very important.

A Canadian theatre group has made similar shows with ordinary people in other parts of the world too.

## Shares moved from account to account

![A man speaks on a landline phone.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10499743)

Open image viewer

Have you forgotten your shares? Photo: Getty Images

Many Finns may have forgotten that they own shares in a telephone company.

Shares in telephone companies have for a long time been in accounts at the Finnish Central Securities Depository. The accounts will be closed soon, and that is why the shares must be moved elsewhere, for example to some bank.

There are still about 55,000 accounts at the Central Securities Depository. The accounts hold shares worth 800 million euros in total.
