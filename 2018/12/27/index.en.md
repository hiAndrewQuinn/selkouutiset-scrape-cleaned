# Thursday 27.12.2018 (radio)

Seasonal work in Lapland attracts foreign workers. Sales of fireworks have started. The police demand that the man suspected of the schoolboy's murder be kept in custody. The Election Sofa is a new kind of election programme. Almost all households have the internet.

## Lapland attracts workers from abroad

![Winter landscape in Lapland.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8261687)

Open image viewer

A beautiful, snowy landscape attracts foreign workers to Lapland for the winter season. Photo: Pekka Kauranen / Yle

Seasonal work in Lapland interests foreign workers.

Entrepreneurs and staffing companies say that people come to work in Lapland for the winter season from abroad. This year there are 10 percent more arrivals than last year. The arrivals represent several dozen different nationalities.

There is a shortage of Finnish seasonal workers in Lapland. Because of this, more workers have to be hired from abroad than before.

Foreigners come to Lapland to work for the winter season, because they are attracted by the northern lights and the beautiful winter nature.

## Fireworks sales have started

![Fireworks for sale.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5693867)

Open image viewer

Fireworks may only be set off on New Year's Eve, after 6 pm. Photo: Yle

Sales of New Year's fireworks and rockets have started.

Fireworks are already on sale now, but they may only be set off on New Year's Eve, from 6 pm to 2 am. However, many towns have completely banned fireworks in the city centre areas.

New Year's Eve is next Monday.

Not everyone likes New Year's rockets. That is why a citizens' initiative has been started that wants to ban private use of fireworks. More than 25,000 people have now signed the "Limits on the Bangs" citizens' initiative.

If 50,000 people sign the initiative, it moves on to be handled by Parliament.

## The murder suspect is to be held in custody

![Candles were lit on Christmas Eve evening in Arabianranta for the boy of primary school age who was killed.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10572734)

Residents of Arabianranta lit candles on Christmas Eve for the schoolboy who was murdered. Photo: Heikki Saukkomaa / Lehtikuva

The police want to have the man who is suspected of the schoolboy's murder held in custody.

A primary school-aged boy died in Arabianranta in Helsinki on Christmas Eve evening. The police suspect that a 36-year-old man murdered the boy.

The hearing on the man's detention will be tomorrow in the Helsinki District Court.

## Election talk from the home sofa

![Coffee cups on a table, sofa in the background](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10550257)

Open image viewer

In Yle's new programme, a candidate for Parliament can be interviewed on your own home sofa. Photo: Jarno Tahvanainen / Yle

A new kind of election programme will come to Yle Areena in the spring. The programme series is called The Election Sofa.

In the programme, candidates for Parliament visit the homes of ordinary Finns. People can discuss with the candidates in their own home on any topic they want.

One family or group of friends is chosen from each electoral district for The Election Sofa programme.

The programme will be published on Yle Areena in March. The parliamentary elections are held in April.

## The internet is in almost every home

![A man sits with a laptop.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10462726)

Open image viewer

Over 90 percent of households have the internet. Photo: AOP

Almost all Finnish households have the internet.

Statistics Finland says that in November more than 90 percent of households had the internet in use.

Statistics Finland also says that 86 percent of households had at least one smartphone in use in November. 82 percent of households owned a passenger car.
