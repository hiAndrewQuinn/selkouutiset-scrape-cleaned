The national swimming skills survey shows that the swimming skills of Finns have deteriorated.

Ten years ago, 76 percent of sixth-graders in comprehensive school could swim. Four years ago, only 55 percent of sixth-graders could swim.

Also, only a little over half of adults can swim. In a survey in 2022, about 40 percent of adults said they could not swim properly.

The Finnish Swimming Instruction and Lifesaving Association says that immigrants have the weakest swimming skills.

## What do you need to know?

Finland has the same swimming skills requirements as other Nordic countries. So what do you need to know?

A person must first jump into deep water, so that their head is below the surface. Then they must swim 200 meters without stopping. Of this distance, at least 50 meters must be swum backstroke. When a person can do all this, then they can swim.

In Finland, swimming lessons often begin as children. Many children attend swimming schools even before they reach school age.

Swimming lessons are part of the curriculum in primary schools. The goal is that all children will be able to swim after completing primary school.

## Water qualification

The Finnish Swimming Instruction and Lifesaving Association issued new guidelines for swimming instruction last autumn. Water competence is now important.

Water competence means that a person knows how to act safely in and near water.

Swimming skills are part of water competence, but they are not enough on their own. It is also a good idea to learn lifesaving skills, swimming with clothes on, and wearing a life jacket.

A person who is water-competent knows how to prevent drowning.
