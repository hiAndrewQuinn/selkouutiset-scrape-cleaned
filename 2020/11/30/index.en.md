Coronavirus vaccinations may start in Finland even before Christmas.

The drug companies Pfizer and BioNTech may get permission to sell the vaccine in the early part of December. The companies developed the vaccine together. In Finland, the plan is to vaccinate risk groups and healthcare staff first, for example.

Experts say that almost all Finnish adults may be vaccinated by next summer. The timetable is still uncertain. Children will not be vaccinated straight away, at least.

## Drug company Moderna is applying for permission to sell a coronavirus vaccine

Drug company Moderna is also applying for permission to sell a coronavirus vaccine. Moderna intends to apply straight away for permission to sell the vaccine in Europe and the USA.

Permission is being applied for because Moderna's studies of the vaccine have finished. The studies confirm that the vaccine's effectiveness is about 94 per cent. Moderna says that the vaccine is safe.

## The number of coronavirus infections is growing quickly

There have been 6 new coronavirus deaths in Finland.

175 people are in hospital care because of the coronavirus. 18 people are in intensive care. The number of people in hospital care has grown.

283 new coronavirus infections have been found in Finland. The number of coronavirus infections has grown a lot. Almost 5,500 infections have been found in 2 weeks. This is almost 2,500 more than 2 weeks ago.

Most new infections are in the Helsinki and Uusimaa hospital district area. The next most new infections are in North Ostrobothnia and Varsinais-Suomi.

The coronavirus situation has got worse in Kymenlaakso, that is, in the Kouvola and Kotka area. New restrictions are coming to the area, and they will be explained in more detail tomorrow, on Tuesday.

## Living in a rented flat is expensive in Finland

![The picture shows a block-of-flats suburb in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4000,h_2250,x_0,y_419/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6681445ea3e0325166d/1587798702)

Living in a rented flat is expensive in Finland.

A study by the University of Turku says that people in Finland spend more money on rent than elsewhere in Europe. In Finland, people spend on average about 32 per cent of all their income on rent. Many people spend more than 40 per cent of their income on rent.

If you are paying a housing loan, housing costs on average about 15 per cent of your income.
