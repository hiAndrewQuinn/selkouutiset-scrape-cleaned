## Changing the rules of homeschooling

Minister of Education Anders Adlercreutz is launching preparations to change the rules for homeschooling.

In the future, homeschooling will require a permit. The application must also include a teaching plan.

In addition, the authority has the opportunity to interrupt homeschooling if the child is not learning sufficiently.

The Minister of Education also wants every child to receive a comprehensive school leaving certificate.

Many homeschoolers now do not take exams at all. However, the reforms will not have time to be completed during this election period.

## The growth of homeschooling

Homeschooling has become rapidly popular in Finland.

In 2018, there were just over 300 children being homeschooled. This year, there are already well over 1,000 children being homeschooled. The number has more than tripled in less than 10 years.

Homeschooling is still rare: only about 0.2 percent of primary school-age children study at home. More than 40 percent of them live in Uusimaa.

An expert from the Ministry of Education and Culture says that municipalities face challenges when homeschoolers lack certificates for further studies.

## Problems

Yle's MOT program previously interviewed research teachers from across Finland. Many of them were concerned about learning, marginalization, and the fact that some families choose homeschooling because of ideology.

Principal **Jari Andersson** from Sastamala strongly stated that some homeschooling is “legalized abandonment.” Abandonment means that a child is left without care.

However, the Ombudsman for Children **Elina Pekkarinen** has said that homeschooling should not be banned completely, because for many families, homeschooling is a good option.
