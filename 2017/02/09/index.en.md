# Thursday 9.2.2017 (radio)

Seinäjoki is the best town for companies. Clear News could win an important prize. The weather warms up next week. The Finnish school in Zürich sends greetings to 100-year-old Finland.

## Seinäjoki is a good place for companies

![Metal piling around the city centre of Seinäjoki at the Vekseliaukio car park construction site in January 2017.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9433556)

Companies are doing well in Seinäjoki. Photo: Pasi Takkunen/Yle

The areas of Seinäjoki, Turku and Hämeenlinna are the best areas for companies. The Confederation of Finnish Industries has compared different areas and also asked the managers of companies where in Finland a company should be located.

Seinäjoki is number one in the comparison for the third time in a row. The Turku area rose to second place, when last year it was only 9th. In third place was Hämeenlinna. The capital area, that is, Helsinki and its neighbouring municipalities, came fourth.

The comparison is meant to find the good sides of different areas and the means by which municipal decision-makers may be able to help companies. When a company is doing well, it can offer jobs to the residents of the municipality.

## Clear News is a prize nominee

![The Clear News team (Margit Alasalmi, Monna Tervo, Merja Windia, Jan Fredriksson, Pälvi Tammi, Petteri Löppönen, Pertti Seppä and Krista Taubert).](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9450664)

Open image viewer

The Clear News team: Margit Alasalmi, Monna Tervo, Merja Windia, Jan Fredriksson, Pälvi Tammi, Petteri Löppönen, Pertti Seppä and Krista Taubert. Photo: Bonnier

Yle News in plain Finnish is nominated for the Grand Journalism Award. Clear News is nominated in the category "Journalistic Deed of the Year".

Plain-language TV news began at the end of 2015, when many asylum seekers came to Finland.

Clear News got nominated because it supports the integration of immigrants and the learning of the Finnish language. Because of its calm rhythm and easy language, Clear News is also watched by, for example, elderly people and people with learning difficulties.

It is estimated that there are about half a million people in Finland who need plain language.

The winners of the Grand Journalism Award will be announced on 14 March.

The Grand Journalism Award supports and promotes good journalism.

## The weather warms up next week

![Winter landscape](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9447595)

Open image viewer

The frost ends next week. Photo: Henrietta Hassinen / Yle

The weather will change next week.

In recent days there has been hard frost in Finland. There is frost at the weekend too, although less than on previous days.

Next week the weather will warm up, because the wind direction will change. Now the wind has brought cold air from the east, but next week the wind will bring warm air from the west. In southern Finland it may be up to 5 degrees warmer on Monday and Tuesday.

So next week it may be almost 20 degrees warmer than now.

## Greetings from the Zürich Finnish school

![Minun Suomeni still.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9449464)

Open image viewer

Pupils of the Zürich Finnish school start a new series on Clear News, "Greetings from the Finnish school". Photo: Yle

A large number of Finnish children and young people live in different parts of the world. They study the Finnish language and culture at Finnish schools. As independent Finland now turns 100 years old, the Finnish schools are sending greetings to Finland by video. The first greetings come from the Zürich Finnish school in Switzerland. The greetings can be watched on the Clear News website.
