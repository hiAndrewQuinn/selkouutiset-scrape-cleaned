# Friday 4.1.2019 (radio)

Municipal mergers have become less popular. Those who disappeared in the Norwegian mountains have been buried under an avalanche. The price of electricity has risen close to the previous peak. Finns travel more and more.

## No municipal mergers were made last year

Municipal mergers have become less popular. Municipal mergers are made when, for example, organising services is too expensive for one municipality.

No municipal mergers were made at all last year. No mergers are coming this year either.

Municipal researchers say that the reason is uncertainty about the social and health care reform and the regional reform.

10 years ago, 32 municipal mergers were made in one year. 101 municipalities took part in the mergers. There are now 311 municipalities in Finland.

## The skiers in Norway have not been found alive

![An army helicopter arrived at Tamokdalen on 4 January.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10581888)

Helicopter in Tromsø in Northern Norway. Photo: Terje Bendiksby / AFP

In Norway, the Tromsø police say that those who disappeared in the mountains have been buried under an avalanche. The police believe that they will not be found alive.

3 Finnish men and 1 Swedish woman have been missing since Wednesday.

A helicopter flew over the missing area today. The chopper detected signals from two different devices, which locate avalanches.

This confirms the assessment that the group has been buried under the snow. The police say that there is no chance of survival. The medical assessment is also that the skiers are not alive.

## The price of electricity has risen

![Coal power plant at Hanasaari in Helsinki.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5472,h_3078,x_0,y_427/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5174665bc84d445ee6d/1539853666)

Coal power plant in Helsinki. Photo: Sasha Silvala / YLE

The price of electricity has risen close to the previous peak. Instead, consumption has returned to the level it was before the recession.

In Finland, 2% more electricity was used last year than in 2016. Electricity use grew most in industry. The wholesale electricity price rose by an average of 41 percent last year.

Finland still imports a large part of its electricity. The share of renewable energy sources was 47 percent. Even so, carbon dioxide emissions rose by 15 percent. A cold winter increased the need for heating.

However, emissions have fallen a lot over 10 years. Emissions from electricity and district heating may halve in the next decade.

## Finns travel a lot

![Big Ben in London.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8979280)

Open image viewer

Big Ben in London. Photo: Hannah Mckay / EPA

Finns are travelling more and more. Advance information on last year's sales has been received from travel agencies.

Greece, Bulgaria and Egypt, for example, have grown in popularity. Finns also travelled a lot to the United Kingdom and Turkey. A weak currency adds to their popularity. The value of the Turkish lira has fallen by half in just over 2 years.

Anxiety about climate change is not showing at least yet in air travel.

Travel within the country was also popular. The reasons were a strong economy and good weather.

London is, after Bangkok, the most popular city destination in the world. Almost 20 million international tourists visit London each year.
