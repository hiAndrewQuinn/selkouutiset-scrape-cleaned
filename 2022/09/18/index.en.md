## Worry about electricity prices

![Electricity cables in a socket.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5467,h_3075,x_5,y_274/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-100838363230216cb865/1714572534)

Many Finns are worried about the electricity price. The electricity price has risen a lot this year.

A new survey says that more than half of Finns are worried about the rise in the electricity price.

The most worried are Finns with a small salary, and Finns who use electric heating.

Many Finns have already started to save electricity. For example, they use electrical devices less than before.

## Monster typhoon in Japan

![A person walks through a big city in the rain.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12629123)

Open image viewer

In Japan it has rained as much as half a metre. Photo: Philip Fong / AFP

There is a strong storm in Japan. Typhoon Nanmadol hit Japan on Sunday.

The storm has brought strong winds and rain. The wind has blown as much as 64 metres per second, and as much as over half a metre of water has fallen.

Because of the storm, many homes have been without electricity. Many Japanese have left their homes for storm shelters.

Typhoons are common in Japan at this time of year. Japan usually has about 20 typhoons a year.

## Big lottery win

![Lotto machine just before the draw.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-6458734)

Open image viewer

Photo: Yle

A money game in Lotto has made history.

On Saturday one player won almost 16 million euros. That is more than ever before in Finnish Lotto.

The winner played Lotto in Siilinjärvi in Eastern Finland. The winner was the only one who had all 7 numbers right.

People in Siilinjärvi have had luck before too. 3 years ago over 90 million euros of Eurojackpot winnings went to Siilinjärvi. Then the win went to a group of 50 people.

## The queen's funeral on Monday

![President Sauli Niinistö in front of the Kultaranta granite castle on 13.6.2022.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5472,h_3078,x_0,y_262/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-97166762ac7cc339555/1655960937)

Open image viewer

President Sauli Niinistö represents Finland at Queen Elizabeth's funeral. Photo: Jani Saikko / Yle

Britain will hold Queen Elizabeth's funeral on Monday. The queen died on 8 September.

The funeral is held in London, and guests from around the world are present. Finland's President Sauli Niinistö and Mrs Jenni Haukio have also travelled to London, and they are taking part in the queen's funeral.

On Sunday President Niinistö and Mrs Haukio visited Queen Elizabeth's coffin. In this way they paid their respects to the queen's memory.

Queen Elizabeth's funeral will also be followed on Yle's channels.

The Finnish-language broadcast of the funeral starts on Monday at 12.30. The broadcast can be followed on TV1, Yle Areena and the website yle.fi.
