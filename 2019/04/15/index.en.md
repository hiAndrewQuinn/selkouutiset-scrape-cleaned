# Monday 15.4.2019 (radio)

A record number of women were elected as MPs. The work of the new Parliament starts after Easter. The SDP does not believe in governing together with the Finns Party. Finnish Post is cutting jobs. A new human relative has been found.

## Record number of women MPs

![Iiris Suomela at the Greens' election night event in Tampere's Kehräsaari.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10739082)

24-year-old Iiris Suomela is the youngest in the new Parliament, that is, the youngest MP. Photo: Matias Väänänen / Yle

A record number of women were elected to Parliament in yesterday's elections. 92 women have now been elected to Parliament.

The previous record is from the 2011 elections. Then 85 women were elected to Parliament.

Parliament also gets a little younger. The average age of the new Parliament is 46 years. The youngest in Parliament is 24-year-old Tampere student Iiris Suomela. She was elected as an MP for the Greens.

Finns elected 83 new people to Parliament in yesterday's elections. That means that dozens of former MPs now have to look for new work.

## Work starts next week

The election result will be confirmed the day after tomorrow, on Wednesday.

The new Parliament starts work after Easter. A week from now, on Wednesday, the first plenary session will be held, where Parliament elects the speakers.

The opening of the parliamentary session is on Thursday, 25 April.

## Which parties in government?

![SDP chairman Antti Rinne at the party's thank-you coffee at the party office in Helsinki on Monday](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10741236)

SDP chairman Antti Rinne. Photo: Vesa Moilanen / Lehtikuva

SDP chairman Antti Rinne does not believe that the SDP and the Finns Party will fit in the same government. Rinne says that the values of the parties are so different that cooperation may not work.

Rinne may be Finland's next prime minister, because the SDP became the biggest party in Parliament in yesterday's elections. Rinne is now negotiating with the other parties and finding out which parties cooperation can work with.

Antti Rinne says that he is trying to get the new government ready by the end of May.

## A new human ancestor

![Homo luzonensis was found in this cave on Luzon Island in the Philippines, researchers say.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10733037)

Open image viewer

The bones of the Luzon human were found in this cave on Luzon Island in the Philippines. Photo: AFP

A new relative has been found for modern humans. The relative lived about 50,000 years ago. That is a short time in the history of the Earth.

The name of the relative is Homo luzonensis, that is, the Luzon human. It lived on Luzon Island, which is today part of the Philippines.

Researchers found the bones of Homo luzonensis 12 years ago. Now they have studied the bones.

Researchers say that Homo luzonensis was small. It was perhaps less than 120 centimetres tall.

Researchers believe that Homo luzonensis walked upright. It did not live in trees.

One thing the researchers do not know: how this little human got to Luzon Island, which is in the middle of the sea.
