# Tuesday 10.10.2017 (radio)

Drugs have been smuggled into prisons with remote-controlled drones. Metals from the Kevitsa mine can get into the groundwater. Eeva Kilpi has received a valuable literary prize. Northern Lapland may get its first snow.

## Drugs have been smuggled with remote-controlled drones

![Radio-controlled camera drone.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7858055)

Remote-controlled drones are used, for example, for aerial photography. Photo: Ilkka Kemppinen / Yle

Attempts are being made to smuggle drugs into Finnish prisons with remote-controlled drones, that is, drones.

In May a remote-controlled drone landed in the yard of a prison, with a sock hanging from a string. Inside the sock there was a mobile phone, a charger and a large amount of drug tablets.

Many smuggling attempts have been noticed in prisons over the last 2 years.

Finnish law prevents suspicious drones from being caught. Technically, it is possible. The drones work on a radio signal, which can be detected and taken over.

## Metals are seeping from the Kevitsa mine into the groundwater

![Kevitsa mine, Sodankylä, Lapland](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9874688)

Open image viewer

Photo: Eemil Friman / Yle

Metals can get into the groundwater from the Kevitsa mine in Sodankylä. Kevitsa is Finland's largest mine, and it is owned by the Swedish company Boliden. Kevitsa's main products are nickel and copper.

Even before the mine, nickel was measured in some groundwater in the Kevitsa area. The cause was thought to be a natural nickel deposit. Now the nickel content exceeds the limit allowed for drinking water.

Boliden denies that it is a question of polluting the groundwater. However, Boliden admits that there are clear changes in the groundwater. A consultant is currently making an assessment of the company's risks to the groundwater. There is also a plan to improve the quality of the groundwater.

The supervising authority cannot yet say whether the situation is serious.

## Eeva Kilpi received a valuable literary prize

![Eeva Kilpi receives the Aleksis Kivi Fund prize of the Finnish Literature Society (SKS), arm in arm with Esko from Nummisuutari.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9875161)

Open image viewer

"I have myself been wounded in my soul by war and my own exile. That is why I have an open mind about receiving refugees into Finland," Eeva Kilpi says. Photo: Markku Ulander / Lehtikuva

The writer Eeva Kilpi has received Finland's oldest literary prize. Kilpi received the Aleksis Kivi Fund prize of the Finnish Literature Society (SKS). The prize sum is 25,000 euros. The prize committee says that Kilpi has done a varied and socially significant life's work.

Kilpi, who was born in Karelia on Lake Ladoga in 1928, has written a lot about Karelian identity and evacuees. She dealt with the subject already in the 1960s, when the subject was politically sensitive.

The justification for the prize says that Kilpi's works are very topical now. They give a voice to those who were displaced by the war. Kilpi says that because she herself is an evacuee, she finds it hard to understand hostile and even violent attitudes towards refugees.

## Northern Lapland may get its first snow

![Cars on the road in the dark](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9481461)

Open image viewer

On Wednesday morning it will become clear whether so much snow has fallen in Northern Lapland that it meets the definition of first snow. Photo: Sointu Ketola

Northern Lapland may get its first snow in places during the evening and night.

The Finnish Meteorological Institute estimates that the amount of snow will not be very large, however. Perhaps a few centimetres of snow will fall.

First snow has fallen when the morning measurement shows at least one centimetre of snow.
