# Tuesday 31.12.2019 (radio)

Prime Minister Sanna Marin has appealed on behalf of the most vulnerable in her New Year's greeting. There are fewer and fewer young women in the regions. 2019 has been a record year for safety on the roads. More seats will be added to Allegro trains at the turn of the year. The decade is changing.

## The Prime Minister's greeting

![Prime Minister Sanna Marin in the Parliament café in Helsinki on 17 December 2019](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11131529)

Prime Minister Sanna Marin (SDP). Photo: Heikki Saukkomaa / Lehtikuva

The new prime minister, Sanna Marin, has given the New Year's greeting. The Prime Minister's New Year's greeting is a tradition.

Prime Minister Marin appealed on behalf of those in the weakest position. Marin said that Finland's strength lies in its people and their skills.

She also talked about government decisions that will improve the position of people on low incomes. Marin said that more than 70 per cent of Finns will have more money to spend next year than this year.

Marin also emphasised measures against climate change. She repeated that the government is committed to a carbon-neutral Finland by 2035.

## Young women move to big towns

![Village shop and two older women](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11133248)

Open image viewer

Hartola municipality in Päijät-Häme has lost over half of its women of childbearing age in the 2000s. Photo: Mika Moksu / Yle

There are fewer and fewer young women in the regions.

Many young women move to big towns. This wave of women moving explains why fewer and fewer children are born in Finland.

The most young women have moved away from Kainuu and South Savo. There, the number of women under 45 has fallen by about 30 per cent this century.

In Kymenlaakso and Satakunta the decline is about 20 per cent.

Only in Uusimaa and Pirkanmaa has the number of young women grown.

## Road safety improved

![Christmas traffic on Highway 4 in Vantaa on 21 December](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11132837)

2019 has been a record year for safety on the roads.

The road safety organisation Liikenneturva says that 204 people have died on Finnish roads this year. This year, 15 fewer people died in traffic than last year.

The estimate is based on preliminary information.

Especially in November and December, exceptionally few people died in traffic.

## Allegro is busy

![Passengers arrive in Helsinki on an Allegro train from St Petersburg on 2 January](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10592145)

Open image viewer

Trips between Helsinki and St Petersburg on Allegro trains are growing strongly. Photo: Markku Ulander / Lehtikuva

Allegro trains get more seats at the turn of the year. Allegro runs between Helsinki and St Petersburg. Allegro's passenger numbers have grown 15 per cent since last year.

Russians are now in their New Year holiday season. About 60,000 Russian tourists are arriving in Finland. The Allegro train has 330 seats. Now that number is being raised to over 600.

About half of Allegro's passengers are Russian. Finns make up 30 per cent and tourists from other countries 20 per cent.

The Russian Tolstoy train also gets 20 extra services at the turn of the year.

The year will soon change in Finland too.

On the other side of the world, people have already been living in 2020 for many hours. The year changed first on the Pacific islands. For example, in the atoll nation of Kiribati the year changed at midday Finnish time.
