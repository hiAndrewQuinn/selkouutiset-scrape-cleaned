# Wednesday 19.4.2017 (radio)

The Finnish Security Intelligence Service and the army will soon be able to read some people's emails. The new taxi law was approved. The first Finnish satellite went into space. 15-year-old boys are very satisfied with life. A fingerprint, a face image and an eye image are biometric identifiers.

## More rights for the police and the army

![A finger points at a laptop screen.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9096347)

Open image viewer

The police get the right to read emails. Photo: Ralf Hirschberger / EPA

The Finnish Security Intelligence Service and the army will soon get more rights to gather intelligence. That means, for example, that the police and the army can read some people's emails and other online messages. Permission for intelligence gathering must be asked from a court every time.

The Security Intelligence Service and the army want more intelligence rights because they want to prevent terror attacks in Finland. They also want to catch criminals, and they want to prevent threats from other states to Finland.

## There will be more taxis

![Taxi drivers jammed traffic at Asema-aukio square in Helsinki in their demonstration on Tuesday 18 April.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9571542)

Open image viewer

Taxi drivers oppose there being many more taxis. Photo: Markku Ulander / Lehtikuva

Parliament has approved a law that increases the number of taxis and their competition. More taxi licences will be given than before. The state will no longer set the price of a taxi ride. But the state can prevent the price of a taxi ride from rising far too much.

Taxi entrepreneurs oppose there being more taxis. The new law comes into force in a little over a year.

## Students' satellite into space

![A box-shaped satellite with antennas sticking out of it. ](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9572133)

Open image viewer

The Aalto 2 satellite. Photo: Aalto University

The first Finnish satellite has been taken into space.

The satellite's name is Aalto 2, it weighs only 2 kilos and it was made by students. The satellite studies gas that is between the Earth's atmosphere and space.

The satellite flew into space in a rocket that left Florida in the USA on Tuesday evening. Aalto University students have made other satellites too, which will be taken into space later.

## 15-year-old boys are satisfied

![Pupils of class 9C at Viitaniemi in Jyväskylä are satisfied with their lives.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9571436)

Open image viewer

The pupils of Viitaniemi school in Jyväskylä are satisfied with life. Photo: Jaana Polamo / Yle

In Finland 15-year-old boys are more satisfied than girls, says the international Pisa study.

More than half of the boys said in the study that they are very satisfied with life. About 30 percent of the girls were very satisfied. Only 4 percent of the boys were not satisfied, and 9 percent of the girls.

Even more satisfied young people were found in the Dominican Republic, Mexico, Costa Rica and Croatia.

6,000 Finnish young people and 72 countries were included in the study.

## A machine recognises you by your face

![The holder's fingerprint is now put in the passport.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5002723)

Open image viewer

A fingerprint is a biometric identifier. Photo: Arja Lento

A fingerprint, a face image or an eye image tells who a person is.

For example, in an online shop you can prove your identity by sending your own face image or fingerprint, that is, a biometric identifier, with your mobile phone.

Many people think that a biometric identifier is certain and safe. But an identifier can also be stolen and misused. For example, from an image of the eye you can see much more information than a person wants to give. From the eye you can see, for example, some illnesses.
