# Monday 26.11.2018 (radio)

There is a dispute between Ukraine and Russia. Cash is being used less and less. The number of hate crimes has grown. Finns want to decide on parental leave themselves.

## Efforts are being made to stop the dispute between Russia and Ukraine

![Ukrainian navy boats heading towards the Kerch Strait on 25 November.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10525781)

Ukrainian navy vessels heading towards the Kerch Strait on 25 November. Photo: Russian Security Service / Sputnik / Lehtikuva

A dispute has arisen between Russia and Ukraine.

The dispute began late on Sunday, when Russia seized 3 Ukrainian navy ships in the Kerch Strait. Russia says that Ukraine entered Russian territorial waters.

Ukrainian President Petro Poroshenko has proposed a state of emergency in Ukraine.

Efforts are being made to stop the dispute from spreading. That is why the UN Security Council is holding an emergency session. The European Union demands that Russia release traffic in the Kerch Strait, as well as the Ukrainian ships and their crews.

## Cash use is decreasing

![Man paying with coins at a café cash desk.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4745,h_2669,x_376,y_205/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-5223875be6fdde7f63c/1541865140)

Finns use more cash than in other Nordic countries. Photo: Kimmo Hiltunen / Yle

Cash is being used less and less all the time.

In Sweden coins and notes are already used so little that the central bank is worried. The costs of handling cash for shopkeepers grow compared with the benefit, if only a few customers use cash.

Among the Nordic countries Finns use cash the most. Even so, just over 10 per cent of Finns mainly use cash. On average 25 per cent pay with cash in a restaurant or café.

In Sweden cash is no longer accepted in all cafés or museums.

## More hate crimes have been reported than before

![Graphic of a fist and a shouting mouth.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10051298)

Open image viewer

Photo: Yle News Graphics

Almost 1,200 criminal reports were made to the police last year about crimes whose motive is suspected to be hatred.

Hate crimes were reported 8 per cent more than in 2016. This is what the report of the Police University College says.

The most common motive is prejudice or hostility based on the victim's ethnic or national background. Nearly 70 per cent of criminal reports of this kind were made in 2017.

Most criminal reports concern verbal threats, harassment or insults.

## Finns want to decide parental leave themselves

![A child reads a book in her mother's lap.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9770251)

Open image viewer

Photo: Elisa Kinnunen / Yle

Finnish parents want to decide for themselves how they divide parental leave. This is shown by a study by the Family Federation of Finland.

Most parents do not support quotas for fathers and mothers. Most want paternity leave to be extended.

The government tried to reform family leave, but the proposal failed. Several parties and labour market organisations have proposed that leave be shared more evenly between partners. Then women can work better.

The government cut the right to take a child to day care if the other parent is at home. Most Finns think that this should not be done.
