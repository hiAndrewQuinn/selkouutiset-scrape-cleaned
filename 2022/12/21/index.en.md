## Finland's climate policy

![Thin tree trunks being handled by a forest machine.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_3000,h_1687,x_0,y_110/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-10076916321a9ee0ce37/1663152283)

Open image viewer

A lot of forest is felled in Finland for industry. Photo: Antti-Petteri Karhunen / Yle

Finland's climate policy is in a difficult situation. This is what a report by the Natural Resources Institute Finland (Luke) says.

The report says that more carbon dioxide gets into the air than was previously estimated. Finland's forests no longer bind as much carbon dioxide as before.

There are at least 2 reasons for the situation. Forests are being cut down too much. In addition, the growth of forests has slowed down.

It is important that forests bind carbon dioxide. When carbon dioxide gets into the air, for example from industry, the climate warms and climate change speeds up.

The European Union wants to reduce carbon dioxide emissions. If Finland does not reach the targets, it must pay compensation to the EU. The bill may be several billion euros.

## The shortest day of the year

![Winter solstice in Utsjoki](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9366308)

Open image viewer

Photo: Jonne Järvinen / Yle

Wednesday was the shortest day of the year. It is said that now is the winter solstice.

In southern Finland the sun is in the sky for only a few hours. In Helsinki, for example, the day is a little under 6 hours long.

In the far north the sun does not rise at all at this time of year. In Utsjoki, the northernmost municipality in Finland, the sun will rise next in mid-January.

After the winter solstice the day starts to lengthen slowly again. The longest day of the year is 6 months away.
