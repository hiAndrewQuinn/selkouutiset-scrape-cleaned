## Putin's speech

![Vladimir Putin gives a speech in a large hall.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4887,h_2748,x_0,y_24/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-107561663f49a78ca060/1676974729)

The President's policy speech was listened to closely also outside Russia. Photo: Dmitry Astakhov / EPA

Russian President Vladimir Putin has given a speech to the Russian parliament.

The main topic of the speech was Russia's military special operation, that is, Russia's attack on Ukraine.

Putin again claimed that the war is the fault of Western countries. He also said that despite Western sanctions, things are going well for Russia and its economy.

Putin also said that Russia will stop following the Start agreement. The Start agreement limits nuclear weapons.

Putin's speech lasted more than an hour and a half. The speech was listened to more carefully than usual also outside Russia, because Russia's war in Ukraine has lasted almost a year. The anniversary of the attack is in three days' time, on 24 February.

## Finland's Nato membership

![Session hall.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4999,h_2811,x_0,y_160/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-105755363bd65861794d/1673356940)

Open image viewer

Parliament discussed Finland's Nato membership. Photo: Silja Viitala / Yle

Finland's Parliament has begun to deal with Finland joining the military alliance Nato.

On Friday the Foreign Affairs Committee completed a report, in which it supports approving the membership.

Finland's national Nato legislation is to be dealt with fully in Parliament still during this parliamentary term.

However, Finland will not become a full member of Nato before all the current Nato countries have approved the new member. Turkey and Hungary have not yet approved Finland's Nato membership.

## Parliamentary elections

![Voting booths at a polling station.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4031,h_2268,x_0,y_275/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-90081861de96768e20d/1641977604)

Open image viewer

Parliamentary elections are in April. Photo: Antro Valo / Yle

The candidate nomination for the spring parliamentary elections has ended today.

In the afternoon the parties and electoral associations submitted candidate lists to the electoral districts. Next week the candidates will get their own election numbers.

Advance voting for the parliamentary elections begins in Finland in a month, on 22 March. The actual election day is Sunday, 2 April.

## Joint application for upper secondary education

![Three soon-to-graduate vocational school students in a photo collage.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1919,h_1080,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-106852263dd2f761cb6f/1675440051)

In the joint application for upper secondary education you can apply, for example, to a vocational school. Photo: Teemu Kammonen / Yle

The joint application for upper secondary education has begun today.

You can apply, for example, to upper secondary school or vocational education. The joint application lasts for a month.

In Finland just over 100,000 young people are currently studying in upper secondary schools, and over 300,000 students are in vocational education.
