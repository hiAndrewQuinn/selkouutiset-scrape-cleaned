## 3 young people died in a road-off crash

In Nokia, near Tampere, a serious road accident has happened. 3 young people died and 1 was injured when a car drove off the road and hit a tree.

The accident happened during the night between Saturday and Sunday. The police say that no other vehicles were involved in the accident.

## A grim year on the roads

![A crashed car.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9948556)

Open image viewer

Many fatal crashes have happened this year. Photo: Petri Lassheikki / Yle

Many people have died on the roads this year.

For example, on Friday there were fatal crashes in Espoo and on the border between Kirkkonummi and Siuntio. On Thursday, fatal crashes happened at least in Parkano, Tornio and Ikaalinen.

The goal has been that at most 136 people die on the roads this year. Juha Valtonen, research manager at Liikenneturva, says that this number may be exceeded already this month.

## Corona advice for ship passengers

![Western Harbour Terminal 2](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5501,h_3094,x_0,y_284/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6898365ee784a5d7311/1600771307)

Open image viewer

A health advice point is being opened at the Western Harbour terminal. Photo: Otso Ritonummi / Yle

The city of Helsinki is opening a health advice point for ship passengers tomorrow. The advice point is opened at the Western Harbour terminal.

At the advice point, information about the coronavirus is given, and passengers who may have corona symptoms are directed to a test.

Later, advice points will also be opened at the Olympia terminal, the Katajanokka terminal and the Hansa terminal in Vuosaari.

The Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare says that 11 new corona infections have been found in Finland during the last day. It is the smallest daily number this week.

## Easy-to-read language makes reading easier

![Easy-to-read books](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10711415)

Open image viewer

Easy-to-read books can be borrowed from the library. Photo: Terhi Varjonen / Yle

A very large number of people need easy-to-read language to make reading easier. The Finnish Association for People with Intellectual Disabilities says that as many as 750,000 people need easy-to-read language to make reading easier.

The content, vocabulary and structure of easy-to-read language are easier than in standard language, and that is why it is easier to read and understand.

About 10 to 15 new easy-to-read books are published in Finland every year. Easy-to-read books are made for all ages, also for children and young people. Easy-to-read literature can be recognised by the arrow symbol.
