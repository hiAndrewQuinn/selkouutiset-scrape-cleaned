## Domestic tomatoes

![Tomatoes and cucumbers in the greengrocery section of a shop.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_6000,h_3375,x_0,y_309/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-79351560740e0489976/1637828706)

Domestic tomatoes may become a luxury product this winter.

Tomato production in Finland may fall by as much as 50 percent this winter. When production falls, the price of tomatoes per kilo rises.

The fall in production is due to energy becoming more expensive. Because energy is expensive, tomato production is also falling in the rest of Europe.

Tomatoes have been imported to Finland mainly from Spain and the Netherlands. In future, tomatoes may also be imported here from Morocco.

## Free contraception

![Free contraception campaign calls on municipalities to offer free contraception to young people under 25.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9527239)

Free contraception is offered to young people under 25 years old. Photo: Antti Ojala / Yle

Most young people under 25 now get free contraception.

The Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare has a trial in which young people under 25 are offered the means to prevent pregnancy and sexually transmitted diseases. In addition, young people are offered advice,

The trial includes 16 wellbeing services counties and Helsinki.

The aim of the trial is that everyone should have equally good opportunities to get contraceptive services.

## Climate conference

![Sanna Marin.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5472,h_3078,x_0,y_419/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-1022327634eab9504852/1666527821)

The UN climate conference has begun in Egypt.

The conference lasts two weeks, and it begins with a meeting of heads of state. Prime Minister Sanna Marin will give Finland's speech there.

Finnish civil society organisations expect that the negotiations will make decisions on questions that are important to African countries. These include, for example, adapting to climate change and sharing the costs of climate damage.

## Swedish Heritage Day

![Finnish flag at half mast on the flagpole](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_1919,h_1080,x_0,y_0/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-915944620ce004e656d/1645011057)

Today flags have been flown in honour of Swedish Heritage Day. Photo: Paulus Markkula / Yle

Finnish flags have flown on flagpoles today in honour of Swedish Heritage Day.

Swedish Heritage Day is meant to celebrate Finland's bilingualism and the right of Finland-Swedes to use the Swedish language in Finland.

Swedish Heritage Day has been celebrated in Finland for over a hundred years, since 1908.

## First snow

![First snow on a lake.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_6198,h_3486,x_1,y_285/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-770460601d044918cfa/1612514454)

Open image viewer

Winter is coming little by little. Photo: All Over Press

First snow has fallen in many places in Finland. Snow fell last night and this morning, says the Finnish Meteorological Institute.

In the morning the ground was white in Eastern Finland, for example, and in places also in Central Finland and on the west coast. However, only a little snow fell and it melted during the day, when the temperature rose above zero.

The Finnish Meteorological Institute says that at this stage the snow will stay on the ground only in Northern Finland, where winter has already begun.
