# Tuesday 27.11.2018 (radio)

A new on-call number will be introduced in healthcare. Many cities are dropping restrictions on children's day care. Helsinki wants more anonymous job applications. The tick is the most frightening animal in Finland's nature.

## New on-call number advises the sick

![Telephone number](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10513571)

Open image viewer

Photo: Helsinki and Uusimaa Hospital District

A new on-call number is being introduced in healthcare.

The new on-call number is 116 117. A health nurse or a nurse answers the number. They assess whether the caller is so ill that they must go to a doctor immediately.

The new number is meant to reduce unnecessary calls to the 112 emergency number. In addition, the aim is to ease crowds in the emergency departments of hospitals and health centres, especially during the influenza season.

The new on-call number will be introduced across the country during next year. In Helsinki, Uusimaa, North Savo and South Karelia the number is already in use now.

## Many cities are ending day-care restrictions

![Children in a day-care centre](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10507898)

Open image viewer

Dropping day-care restrictions is justified, for example, by equality for children. Photo: Mikko Stig / Lehtikuva

Many cities are giving up restrictions on children's right to day care.

A couple of years ago municipalities were given permission to restrict, that is, reduce, children's day care if one parent is at home. Day care could be reduced to 20 hours a week if the child's father or mother is at home, for example due to unemployment or parental leave.

Some big cities decided right at the start that they would not restrict the right to day care. Now many other cities also intend to give up the restrictions. Giving up the restrictions is justified, for example, by equality for children.

## Helsinki wants more anonymous job applications

![Woman holding a pile of application forms in her hands.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9521103)

Open image viewer

In an anonymous job application only information about the applicant's skills remains. Photo: Liisa Leinonen / Yle

The City of Helsinki wants to increase anonymous job applications. Adding anonymous job applications is proposed by the Helsinki City Board.

Anonymous job application means that details such as the applicant's name, age, gender and ethnic background are removed from job applications. Only information about the applicant's skills remains in the application, that is, information about education and work experience.

The purpose of anonymous job applications is to reduce discrimination. It gives applicants of different backgrounds the same chance of getting a job.

Helsinki is Finland's largest employer. The city employs about 38,000 people.

## Finns fear ticks the most

![Close-up of a tick.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-10258966)

Open image viewer

A tick bite can make you ill. Photo: Artyom Geodakyan / AOP

According to Finns, the tick is the most frightening animal in Finland's nature.

In the new Arjen katsaus survey, over 30 per cent of respondents say that the tick is the most frightening animal.

Ticks are feared because they spread diseases. A person can get borreliosis or encephalitis from a tick bite. There is a vaccine against encephalitis, but no cure. There is no vaccine against borreliosis, but it can be treated with antibiotics.

Besides the tick, Finns fear the bear, the adder and the wolf.
