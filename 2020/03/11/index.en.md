# Wednesday 11.3.2020 (radio)

Finland will not ban public events because of fear of the coronavirus. Coronavirus restrictions are being gradually lifted in Hubei province in China. Turkey is keeping the border open until the EU agrees to its demands. The Finns Party has submitted an interpellation.

## Finland will not ban public events

![A crowd at a rock festival.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5266414)

Audience at a rock festival. Photo: Mika Kanerva / YLE

Finland does not intend to restrict the holding of various public events because of the coronavirus.

In many countries, gatherings of large crowds have been restricted. For example, Italy has banned all public events across the whole country. The ban is meant to prevent the spread of the coronavirus.

In Finland the Ministry of Social Affairs and Health has considered restrictions on events and gatherings that more than 1,000 people attend. However, in the ministry's view, restricting them would not be of much use.

In Finland the coronavirus infection has now been found in about 60 people.

## In China the coronavirus is starting to ease

![A temporary hospital being cleaned in Wuhan, China.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11249254)

Open image viewer

The temporary hospital in Wuhan was closed when no new patients came any more. Photo: Chine Nouvelle / AOP

China is putting into quarantine all passengers who arrive in the Chinese capital, Beijing, from abroad. The quarantine lasts 2 weeks.

Earlier, China had already put into quarantine passengers who come from countries where there are many coronavirus infections.

In China the coronavirus infections have decreased. The infections have also decreased in Hubei province, from where the virus started to spread in December.

China intends to ease the strict restrictions in Hubei. The province has been isolated, and for example schools and factories have been closed. China now intends to allow people to gradually go back to work.

## Turkey keeps the border open

![Recep Tayyip Erdogan.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_4159,h_2339,x_0,y_208/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-6510115e65079056dfa/1583679453)

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan. Photo: Pavel Golovkin / EPA

Turkey says that it is keeping the border open to Greece, to the EU side. Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan says that the border is open to all migrants who want to get to the EU side.

According to Erdogan, the border will stay open for as long as the EU has not agreed to all of Turkey's demands. Turkey demands, for example, financial help and visa freedom from the EU.

Turkey opened the land border to Greece at the turn of February and March. Greece has prevented migrants from entering across the border.

## Interpellation against Marin's government

![Finns Party chairman Jussi Halla-aho, Nyt movement chairman Harry Harkimo and Finns Party parliamentary group chairman Ville Tavio at the press conference on the interpellation in Parliament.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-11251463)

The Finns Party has submitted an interpellation to the government. The interpellation concerns the government's lines on asylum policy and the situation at the Greece-Turkey border.

The Finns Party also demands, for example, that border checks between EU countries are brought back into use.

The National Coalition Party and the Christian Democrats did not join the Finns Party's interpellation. Instead, the Nyt movement is taking part in the interpellation.
