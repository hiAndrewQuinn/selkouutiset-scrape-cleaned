# Thursday 24.11.2016 (radio)

Finnish literature was honoured with the Finlandia Prizes. Forestry firms own a lot of land. Migri's line on Iraq changes a little. A use is being sought for tree leaves.

## The Finlandia Prizes for literature were awarded

The Finlandia Prizes have been awarded. The Finlandia Prizes are the most important prizes of the year for Finnish books.

![Akvarelleja Engelin kaupungista began as a radio play.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9313327)

Photo: Yle / Matti Hämäläinen

Jukka Viikilä got the prize for the book Akvarelleja Engelin kaupungista (Watercolours from Engel's City). In the book, Viikilä imagines what the life of the architect Carl Ludvig Engel was like in Helsinki 200 years ago.

The Finlandia Prize for non-fiction went to Mari Manninen's book Yhden lapsen kansa (A Nation of One Child). The book is about China and its one-child policy.

In addition, the Finlandia Prize for children's and young people's books was awarded. It went to Juuli Niemi for the book Et kävele yksin (You Do Not Walk Alone). The book is about first love.

The Finlandia Prizes are worth 30,000 euros.

## Forestry firms own a lot of land

![Tree plantations of Stora Enso's joint venture Veracel in the state of Bahia in Brazil in 2011.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9308485)

Open image viewer

Tree plantations of a Stora Enso joint venture in Brazil. Photo: Markus Kröger

Finns own a lot of land outside the EU.

A new study ordered by the EU says that Finns own the fourth most land outside the EU of all EU countries.

Finns' large land ownership is because the Finnish forestry firms Stora Enso and UPM own big tree plantations in South America and China. On the plantations, trees are grown for the firms' pulp and cardboard mills.

Development researcher Markus Kröger says that the land ownership is legal, but there are ethical problems with it. When an international firm owns a lot of land, the local residents cannot use it.

## Migri's line on Iraq changes a little

![The Finnish Immigration Service's logo on the outside wall of a building](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-8514134)

Open image viewer

The Finnish Immigration Service's change of line may slightly improve some Iraqis' chances of getting asylum in Finland. Photo: Yle

The Finnish Immigration Service is easing its line a little on Iraqi asylum seekers.

The change is because an administrative court has overturned the Finnish Immigration Service's negative decision for an Iraqi asylum seeker.

In the Finnish Immigration Service's opinion, a Sunni Muslim who comes from Mosul can move to Baghdad. The court said that it is not safe for the man to move to Baghdad.

## A use is being sought for autumn leaves

![Partly reddened maple leaves.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9200006)

Open image viewer

VTT is studying how dyes can be made from autumn leaves. Photo: Juha Kemppainen / Yle

A new use is being sought for tree leaves.

The technology research centre VTT is studying how dyes or other useful ingredients can be made from autumn leaves.

In the future, nature's own dyes could perhaps be used, for example, in dyeing textiles, in the cosmetics industry or in food.

Senior scientist Liisa Nohynek got the idea of using tree leaves when she was cycling home through the autumn landscape.
