Ukraine. Russian speakers in Finland. Economy. Nature conservation. Weather forecast.

Russia has fired missiles at Ukraine's capital, Kyiv. Apparently, however, no one died.

Russia says that it destroyed, with missile strikes, military equipment that other countries have sent to Ukraine. The information has not been possible to verify from reliable sources.

Ukraine says that Russian missiles destroyed railway structures in Kyiv.

## Russian speakers in Finland

![View over a street in the bombed-out port city of Mariupol, photographed on Monday 9 May.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5472,h_3078,x_0,y_328/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-9525666279c07be4f27/1652146487)

Open image viewer

War damage in Mariupol, Ukraine. Photo: EPA-EFE/All Over Press

Many Russians who live in Finland do not like Russia's attack on Ukraine. Yle has asked Russian-speaking people in Finland what they think about Russia's attack on Ukraine.

About 30 per cent of Russian-speaking people say that Russia's attack violates Ukraine's territorial integrity. Just as many Russian speakers say that they do not know what the Ukraine crisis is about.

Only 13 per cent of Russian speakers trust Russian media.

## Students' finances

![HOAS student housing on 7 August 2015.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5120,h_2880,x_0,y_160/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-9493946270cfd4b5ff2/1651560459)

Open image viewer

A young person in a student flat. Photo: Roni Rekomaa / Lehtikuva

The bad economic situation in the world makes it harder for many young people to move into their own home. For example, many new students move into their own flat in the town where they study.

Many things are now getting more expensive quickly because of the war in Ukraine. For example, housing is getting more expensive, because heating energy and electricity are getting more expensive. The price of food is also rising quickly.

Experts say that the rise in prices may make the livelihood of students and other people living alone much more difficult.

## Nature conservation

![Spruce forest on the right and a moss-covered rock.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_extract,w_5310,h_2986,x_0,y_252/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,ar_16:9,dpr_1/f_auto/39-965172629a51883be63/1654281283)

Finns want more nature reserves. Yle has asked Finns for their opinion on the need for nature reserves. 80 per cent of Finns support adding nature reserves.

Young people and women support new nature reserves the most.

Men and older people support new nature reserves the least.

## UV radiation from the sun and the weather

![Monday's weather map.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-12477526)

Open image viewer

On Monday it is warm.

Summer has begun and the sun's radiation is again strong at times. The Finnish Meteorological Institute issued the first summer warning about UV radiation on Sunday. In strong sunshine skin can burn if it is not protected. The sun's UV radiation was strong on Sunday, for example in Uusimaa and Southwest Finland.

On Monday the weather is widely sunny and dry. UV radiation may be strong in Åland. In Lapland there is more cloud, and in places rain showers may come.

The temperature during the day is between 15 and 21 degrees, in Northern Lapland it is cooler.
