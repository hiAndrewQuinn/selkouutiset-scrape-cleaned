# Thursday 2.2.2017 (radio)

In Finland almost a tenth of people have experienced hate speech. New internet police officers are being hired. The President spoke at the opening of Parliament. Games and friends prevent memory from getting weaker.

## Many people in Finland have experienced hate speech

![Hate](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-5412689)

Open image viewer

Yle commissioned a study on hate speech, which was done by Statistics Finland.  Photo: Seppo Sarkkinen / Yle

Nine (9) percent of people living in Finland have experienced hate speech. Taloustutkimus studied the matter for Yle. Behind hate speech there is often ethnicity or religion.

Almost a third of those who answered say that incitement against an ethnic group must be punished more harshly than now.

62 percent of those who answered say that the law on breaching the sanctity of religion is important. The answer surprised the researchers, because the law is difficult to apply. That is why many researchers and politicians say that the law should be removed. This has been done, for example, in Sweden and Norway.

Hate speech targets especially 15 - 24-year-olds. There is a lot of hate speech on the internet and in social media.

## New internet police officers are being hired

![Internet police, Facebook](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-7704716)

Open image viewer

The internet police work in the same way as other police: they give advice, requests and orders. When a certain limit is crossed, the police start investigating the matter. Photo: Heidi Hannukainen / Yle

The police are hiring 25 new internet police officers. The aim is to prevent hate speech and crime online. An internet police officer can, for example, step into a heated discussion on Facebook. The police's presence online is meant to restrain hate speech and rowdiness there.

Finland has 11 police departments. Each will get at least one (1) internet police officer. A special group of ten (10) police officers will be set up in Helsinki. The rest of the police posts will go to the National Bureau of Investigation.

In addition, police officers will be hired to identify and stop illegal activity by extremist movements.

## Parliament has been opened again

![President Sauli Niinistö at the opening of the 2017 parliamentary session at Finlandia Hall in Helsinki on 2 February 2017.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9438423)

President Sauli Niinistö at the opening of the 2017 parliamentary session at Finlandia Hall in Helsinki on 2 February 2017. Photo: Martti Kainulainen / Lehtikuva

On Thursday the opening of the parliamentary session was celebrated. It is always celebrated when Parliament begins its spring session.

The President of the Republic, Sauli Niinistö, spoke at the opening at Finlandia Hall in Helsinki. He said that closing economic and other borders is not a good thing. Niinistö said that no nation will survive if it forgets the others.

Niinistö said that domestic politics is dominated by the economy. The most important thing is that respect for democracy is kept, President Niinistö said. It is also important that Finland can fight growing inequality, he said.

## Playing games and friends are important for memory

![Elderly people play bingo at a wellbeing centre.](https://img.img-cdn.yle.fi/crop_fill,w_767,h_431,dpr_1/f_auto/13-3-9437452)

Open image viewer

Playing bingo and other games, as well as friends, prevent memory diseases Photo: Lauri Heino / Lehtikuva

Games and friends keep an old person's memory in good shape. This has been found in a new study.

Playing games, using a computer and handicrafts help against dementia. They also slow down other memory diseases.

Some memory diseases can be inherited. The risk of getting ill also gets smaller for such people if they use their brain in many ways and are socially active.

In Finland about 120,000 people have some degree of dementia.
